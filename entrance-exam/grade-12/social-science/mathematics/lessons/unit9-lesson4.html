💵 Wages

Master the mathematics of earning: salary, hourly pay, overtime, piecework, commissions and net pay after deductions. Every worker needs these calculations — and they are frequently tested on the entrance exam.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions

Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

Wages are the money earned from work. Different jobs pay in different ways — some workers earn a fixed salary, others get paid by the hour, some by the piece they produce, and some by commission on their sales. Once earnings are computed, deductions like income tax and pension contributions reduce the amount the worker actually receives (net pay).

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Compute gross pay from salary, hourly wage, or piecework
  • Calculate overtime pay using standard time-and-a-half rules
  • Compute commission from sales, including graduated rates
  • Determine net pay after deductions (income tax, pension, etc.)
  • Apply the Ethiopian income tax structure to a monthly income
  • Compare job offers with different pay structures
📖
Explanation
Complete theory with practical examples

2.1 Types of Wage Systems

Salary
A fixed amount paid per year or month, regardless of hours worked.
Hourly Wage
Paid for each hour worked. Extra hours may earn overtime pay.
Piecework
Paid per unit produced (e.g. per shirt sewn, per item packed).
Commission
Paid as a percentage of sales made. Often combined with a base salary.

2.2 Gross Pay Calculations

📌 Gross Pay Formulas

Salary: Gross = annual salary / pay periods per year

Hourly: Gross = Hourly rate × Hours worked

Piecework: Gross = Rate per piece × Pieces produced

Commission only: Gross = Sales × Commission rate

Base salary + commission: Gross = Base + (Sales × Commission rate)

2.3 Overtime Pay

In most countries, hours worked beyond the standard working week (usually 40 hours) are paid at a higher rate — often 1.5× (time-and-a-half) for normal overtime and 2× (double time) for weekend or holiday work.

📌 Overtime Formula

Regular Pay = Hourly Rate × Regular Hours (up to 40)
Overtime Pay = Hourly Rate × 1.5 × Overtime Hours
Total Gross = Regular Pay + Overtime Pay

📝 Example of the difference

At 50 ETB/hour, a worker who works 45 hours earns:
Regular: 40 × 50 = 2,000 ETB
Overtime: 5 × 50 × 1.5 = 375 ETB
Total: 2,375 ETB — not 45 × 50 = 2,250 ETB.

2.4 Deductions and Net Pay

Gross pay is what you earn before deductions. Net pay (take-home pay) is what you actually receive after the government and other institutions take their shares.

📌 Net Pay Formula

Net Pay = Gross Pay − Total Deductions

Common deductions:
• Income tax (based on progressive tax brackets)
• Pension contribution (usually 7% employee + 11% employer)
• Health insurance premium
• Union dues (if applicable)
• Loan repayments (salary advances)

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Basic Hourly Wage
Problem: A worker is paid 45 ETB per hour and works 38 hours in a week. What is the gross weekly pay?
Formula
Gross = Rate × Hours
Calculate
Gross = 45 × 38 = 1,710 ETB
✅ Weekly gross pay = 1,710 ETB
Example 2Hourly Wage with Overtime
Problem: A factory worker earns 60 ETB/hour for the first 40 hours and 1.5× that rate for overtime. If she works 47 hours this week, find her total gross pay.
Regular pay
40 × 60 = 2,400 ETB
Overtime pay
OT hours = 47 − 40 = 7
7 × 60 × 1.5 = 630 ETB
Total
2,400 + 630 = 3,030 ETB
✅ Total gross pay = 3,030 ETB
Example 3Piecework Pay
Problem: A seamstress is paid 12 ETB for each shirt she sews. In one week she completes 145 shirts. What is her weekly pay?
Formula
Pay = Rate per piece × Pieces = 12 × 145
Calculate
Pay = 1,740 ETB
✅ Weekly pay = 1,740 ETB
Example 4Commission Plus Base Salary
Problem: A salesperson earns 3,500 ETB base salary per month plus 4% commission on sales. If total sales for the month are 85,000 ETB, what is the gross monthly pay?
Commission
Commission = 85,000 × 0.04 = 3,400 ETB
Total
Gross = 3,500 + 3,400 = 6,900 ETB
✅ Gross monthly pay = 6,900 ETB
Example 5Net Pay After Tax and Pension
Problem: An employee earns a gross monthly salary of 5,000 ETB. Compute: (a) income tax using the Ethiopian tax bracket, (b) 7% pension contribution, (c) net pay.
(a) Income tax
5,000 falls in the 20% bracket (3,201–5,250) with deduction 302.50.
Tax = (5,000 × 0.20) − 302.50 = 1,000 − 302.50 = 697.50 ETB
(b) Pension
Pension = 5,000 × 0.07 = 350 ETB
(c) Net pay
Net = 5,000 − 697.50 − 350 = 3,952.50 ETB
✅ Tax = 697.50 ETB · Pension = 350 ETB · Net pay = 3,952.50 ETB

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 4
✅ Key Points — Wages
  • Gross pay is earnings before deductions. Net pay is what you take home after deductions.
  • Salary = fixed amount per period. Hourly wage = rate × hours worked.
  • Overtime beyond 40 hours is usually paid at 1.5× the base rate. Total = Regular + Overtime.
  • Piecework = rate per unit × units produced.
  • Commission = sales × commission rate. Often combined with a base salary.
  • Income tax in Ethiopia is progressive — higher income → higher rate. Use Tax = (Income × Rate) − Deduction.
  • Pension contribution is 7% of gross salary for the employee.
  • Net pay = Gross − Income Tax − Pension − Other Deductions.
  • When comparing job offers, always compute the gross monthly pay for a typical month to compare fairly.
