🏠 Real Estate Expenses

Learn the mathematics behind one of life's biggest purchases. Master property tax, mortgage payments, rent calculations, and the true cost of home ownership — practical skills that apply to every adult life decision.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked

Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

Buying or renting a home involves many financial calculations. Beyond the sticker price, there are property taxes, mortgage interest, insurance, maintenance costs, and for renters, security deposits and utilities. Understanding these numbers helps you make informed decisions and avoid financial surprises.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Compute property tax based on assessed value and mill rate
  • Calculate mortgage payments using the amortization formula
  • Determine the total interest paid over the life of a loan
  • Compare renting vs. buying costs
  • Compute real estate commission on a sale
  • Understand closing costs and other one-time expenses

🛡️

🔧

Ongoing repairs and upkeep (rule: ~1% of value/year).

One-time fees when buying a property (2–5% of price).

Percentage paid to the real estate agent when selling.

2.2 Property Tax

Property tax is usually calculated on the assessed value of the property (which may differ from the market value). It is paid annually.

📌 Property Tax Formula

Property Tax = Assessed Value × Tax Rate

The tax rate may be given as a percentage (e.g. 1.5%) or as a mill rate (dollars per 1,000 of value).

If the mill rate is given: Tax = (Assessed Value / 1000) × Mill Rate

2.3 Mortgage Payment

A mortgage is a long-term loan for buying property. The monthly payment is computed using the amortization formula.

📌 Monthly Mortgage Payment Formula

M = P × [i(1 + i)ⁿ] / [(1 + i)ⁿ − 1]

where:
• M = monthly payment
• P = principal (loan amount)
• i = monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100)
• n = total number of payments (years × 12)

📝 Understanding the payment

Each mortgage payment has two parts: principal (reducing the loan) and interest (the cost of borrowing). Early payments are mostly interest; late payments are mostly principal. Total interest over the life of the loan = (M × n) − P.

2.4 Down Payment

When buying property, buyers usually pay a down payment — a percentage of the purchase price paid upfront. The rest is financed through a mortgage.

📌 Down Payment Formula

Down Payment = Purchase Price × Down Payment %

Loan Amount (Principal) = Purchase Price − Down Payment

2.5 Closing Costs

Closing costs are one-time fees paid when buying a property. They typically include:

  • Loan origination fee (1–2% of loan)
  • Title search and insurance (0.5–1%)
  • Appraisal fee (fixed amount)
  • Legal fees
  • Prepaid property tax and insurance

Rule of thumb: budget 2–5% of the purchase price for closing costs.

2.6 Renting vs Buying — Comparing Costs

To decide between renting and buying, compare the total cost of ownership per year against annual rent.

📌 Annual Cost of Ownership

Annual Ownership Cost = Mortgage Payments + Property Tax + Insurance + Maintenance

If ownership cost is higher than rent → renting is cheaper financially (short-term).
If ownership cost is lower than rent → buying may be better (long-term).

2.7 Real Estate Commission

When a property is sold, the seller usually pays a commission to the real estate agent — a percentage of the sale price.

📌 Commission Formula

Commission = Sale Price × Commission Rate

Net Proceeds to Seller = Sale Price − Commission − Other Costs

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Property Tax Calculation
Problem: A house has an assessed value of 2,400,000 ETB. The property tax rate is 1.2%. What is the annual property tax?
Formula
Tax = Assessed Value × Rate
Substitute
Tax = 2,400,000 × 0.012 = 28,800 ETB
✅ Annual property tax = 28,800 ETB
Example 2Down Payment and Loan Amount
Problem: A house is priced at 3,000,000 ETB. The buyer pays 20% as a down payment. How much is the down payment, and what is the loan amount?
Down payment
Down = 3,000,000 × 0.20 = 600,000 ETB
Loan amount
Loan = 3,000,000 − 600,000 = 2,400,000 ETB
✅ Down payment = 600,000 ETB · Loan = 2,400,000 ETB
Example 3Monthly Mortgage Payment
Problem: A 20-year mortgage of 2,400,000 ETB has an annual interest rate of 9%. Find the monthly payment.
Compute i and n
Monthly rate: i = 0.09 / 12 = 0.0075
Number of payments: n = 20 × 12 = 240
Compute (1+i)ⁿ
(1.0075)²⁴⁰ ≈ 5.9958
Apply formula
M = 2,400,000 × [0.0075 × 5.9958] / [5.9958 − 1]
= 2,400,000 × 0.044969 / 4.9958
= 2,400,000 × 0.0090015
≈ 21,604 ETB
✅ Monthly payment ≈ 21,604 ETB
Example 4Total Interest Paid
Problem: Using the mortgage from Example 3 (M ≈ 21,604 ETB, P = 2,400,000 ETB, n = 240), find the total amount paid and the total interest.
Total paid
Total = 21,604 × 240 = 5,184,960 ETB
Total interest
Interest = 5,184,960 − 2,400,000 = 2,784,960 ETB
Interpret
Over 20 years, the buyer pays more than double the loan amount in interest! This shows why a lower interest rate matters enormously.
✅ Total paid = 5,184,960 ETB · Total interest = 2,784,960 ETB
Example 5Real Estate Commission and Net Proceeds
Problem: A house sells for 4,000,000 ETB. The real estate agent charges 4% commission. The seller also pays 15,000 ETB in legal fees. What are the seller's net proceeds?
Commission
Commission = 4,000,000 × 0.04 = 160,000 ETB
Net proceeds
Net = 4,000,000 − 160,000 − 15,000 = 3,825,000 ETB
✅ Net proceeds = 3,825,000 ETB (commission + fees = 175,000 ETB)

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 3
✅ Key Points — Real Estate Expenses
  • Property tax = Assessed Value × Tax Rate. Paid annually, based on the assessed value (not market value).
  • Down payment = Purchase Price × Down Payment %. Typically 10–20% of the price.
  • Loan principal = Purchase Price − Down Payment.
  • Monthly mortgage payment: M = P × [i(1+i)ⁿ] / [(1+i)ⁿ−1], where i = monthly rate and n = number of payments.
  • Total paid over the life of the loan = M × n. Total interest = (M × n) − P.
  • Closing costs are 2–5% of the purchase price — one-time fees at purchase.
  • Maintenance rule of thumb: budget about 1% of property value per year.
  • Real estate commission = Sale Price × Commission %, usually paid by the seller.
  • To compare renting vs buying: calculate the full annual ownership cost and compare to yearly rent.
  • Over the life of a mortgage, the total interest paid often exceeds the loan amount — a strong argument for lower interest rates and shorter terms.
