Unit 9 · Business and Consumer Mathematics · Social Science

🛒 Applications to Purchasing

Learn the mathematics every consumer needs: discounts, mark-ups, sales tax, VAT, and invoice calculations. Master the essential buying calculations that appear on the Ethiopian entrance exam and in everyday life.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

Every time you buy something — a shirt in a shop, a phone online, or supplies for a business — you are involved in purchasing mathematics. Discounts reduce the price, mark-ups add to the cost for profit, and taxes like VAT add to the final amount. Understanding these calculations protects you as a consumer and helps you as a business person.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Calculate trade discounts and cash discounts on purchases
  • Compute net price after a series of discounts
  • Calculate mark-up and the resulting selling price
  • Determine the cost price given the selling price and mark-up percentage
  • Compute VAT (Value Added Tax) and total price including tax
  • Understand invoice math — subtotal, tax, shipping, and grand total
📖
Explanation
Complete theory with practical examples

2.1 Discounts — Reducing the Price

A discount is a reduction from the original price, usually expressed as a percentage. There are two common types:

Trade Discount
Given by a seller to a business buyer (like a wholesaler to a retailer). Usually expressed as a percentage off the list price.
Cash Discount
Given to a buyer who pays immediately or within a short period. Encourages quick payment.
📌 Discount Formulas

Discount Amount = List Price × Discount Rate

Net Price = List Price − Discount Amount
    = List Price × (1 − Discount Rate)

Discount Rate is usually expressed as a decimal (e.g. 15% = 0.15).

📝 Successive discounts

If two discounts are applied in sequence (e.g. 20% then 10%), multiply them, don't add them. Total factor = (1 − 0.20) × (1 − 0.10) = 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, meaning 28% total discount — not 30%.

2.2 Mark-up and Selling Price

A mark-up is the amount added to the cost price to determine the selling price — the seller's profit margin.

📌 Mark-up Formulas

Mark-up Amount = Cost Price × Mark-up Rate

Selling Price = Cost Price + Mark-up Amount
    = Cost Price × (1 + Mark-up Rate)

Mark-up Rate (as % of cost) = (Selling Price − Cost Price) / Cost Price × 100%

📝 Mark-up vs Margin

Mark-up is calculated on the cost price.
Margin (profit margin) is calculated on the selling price.
Example: cost = 100, sell = 150. Mark-up = 50/100 = 50%. Margin = 50/150 ≈ 33.3%.

2.3 Value Added Tax (VAT)

VAT (Value Added Tax) is a tax on the sale of goods and services. In Ethiopia, the standard VAT rate is 15%. It's added to the price you pay.

📌 VAT Formulas

VAT Amount = Price (before tax) × VAT Rate

Total Price (incl. VAT) = Price × (1 + VAT Rate)

Price before VAT = Total Price ÷ (1 + VAT Rate)

📝 Removing VAT from a price

If a product's total price (with VAT) is 1,150 ETB and VAT is 15%, the pre-VAT price is 1,150 ÷ 1.15 = 1,000 ETB. The VAT amount = 150 ETB. Don't just subtract 15% from the total — that gives the wrong answer (1,150 × 0.85 = 977.5).

2.5 Profit and Loss

📌 Profit and Loss Formulas

Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price (when SP > CP)
Loss = Cost Price − Selling Price (when CP > SP)

Profit % = (Profit / Cost Price) × 100%
Loss % = (Loss / Cost Price) × 100%

By convention, profit and loss percentages are always calculated on the cost price.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Single Discount — Net Price
Problem: A shirt has a list price of 800 ETB. A shop offers a 20% trade discount. What is the net price the buyer pays?
Discount
Discount = 800 × 0.20 = 160 ETB
Net price
Net Price = 800 − 160 = 640 ETB
Or directly: 800 × 0.80 = 640 ETB
✅ Net price = 640 ETB
Example 2Successive Discounts
Problem: A laptop's list price is 25,000 ETB. It is subject to a 10% trade discount and then a further 5% cash discount for immediate payment. What is the final net price?
Method
Multiply the factors, don't add the rates:
Net = LP × (1 − 0.10) × (1 − 0.05)
Calculate
Net = 25,000 × 0.90 × 0.95
= 25,000 × 0.855 = 21,375 ETB
Check
Total discount = 25,000 − 21,375 = 3,625 ETB
Effective discount rate = 3,625 / 25,000 = 14.5% (not 15%)
✅ Final net price = 21,375 ETB (14.5% total discount)
Example 3Mark-up and Selling Price
Problem: A retailer buys a phone for 4,000 ETB and marks it up by 25%. What is the selling price, and what is the profit?
Formula
Selling Price = CP × (1 + m) = 4,000 × 1.25
Calculate
SP = 5,000 ETB
Profit = SP − CP = 5,000 − 4,000 = 1,000 ETB
✅ Selling price = 5,000 ETB · Profit = 1,000 ETB (25% of cost)
Example 4VAT Calculation
Problem: A purchase has a net price (before VAT) of 3,200 ETB. If the VAT rate is 15%, what is the total price the buyer pays?
VAT amount
VAT = 3,200 × 0.15 = 480 ETB
Total
Total = 3,200 + 480 = 3,680 ETB
Or directly: 3,200 × 1.15 = 3,680 ETB
✅ Total price = 3,680 ETB (VAT portion = 480 ETB)
Example 5Invoice Math — Complete Purchase
Problem: A business buys 20 boxes at 450 ETB each. They receive a 12% trade discount. VAT is 15%. Shipping costs 250 ETB. Find the grand total.
Subtotal
Subtotal = 20 × 450 = 9,000 ETB
Discount
Discount = 9,000 × 0.12 = 1,080 ETB
Net = 9,000 − 1,080 = 7,920 ETB
VAT
VAT = 7,920 × 0.15 = 1,188 ETB
Grand total
Total = 7,920 + 1,188 + 250 = 9,358 ETB
✅ Grand total = 9,358 ETB

✅ Key Points — Applications to Purchasing

  • Net price = List Price × (1 − Discount Rate).
  • Successive discounts are multiplied, not added: Net = LP × (1−d₁)(1−d₂)…
  • Mark-up is the amount added to cost. Selling Price = CP × (1 + Mark-up Rate).
  • Profit = SP − CP. Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100%.
  • VAT in Ethiopia is 15%. Total price with VAT = Pre-tax × 1.15.
  • To remove VAT from a total: Pre-tax = Total ÷ 1.15. Never subtract 15% directly.
  • Invoice total = Net Price + VAT + Shipping. Shipping is not taxed.
