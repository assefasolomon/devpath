📈 Percent Increase and Percent Decrease

Master one of the most practical topics in mathematics: measuring change. Learn to compute percent increase, percent decrease, and apply these to real-world problems like inflation, depreciation, and price changes.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

Prices rise (inflation). Cars lose value (depreciation). Salaries go up. Populations grow. Every one of these changes can be measured with percent increase or percent decrease. Understanding these calculations is essential for budgeting, business planning, and interpreting the news.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Compute percent increase and percent decrease from two values
  • Calculate the new value after a percent change
  • Find the original value given the new value and percent change
  • Apply percent change to inflation and depreciation problems
  • Compute compound percent changes over multiple periods
  • Distinguish between percentage points and percentage change
📖
Explanation
Complete theory with practical examples

2.1 The Percent Change Formula

The percent change from an original value to a new value is always measured relative to the original.

📌 Percent Change Formula

% Change = [(New Value − Original Value) / Original Value] × 100%

• If the result is positive → percent increase
• If the result is negative → percent decrease

Key rule: always divide by the original value, never the new one.

📝 A common mistake

Don't confuse the change amount with the percent change. If a price rises from 200 to 220, the change amount is 20 ETB, and the percent change is 20/200 = 10%. If it fell from 220 to 200, the change is 20 ETB but the percent change is 20/220 ≈ 9.09% (because the original was 220).

2.2 Finding the New Value After a Percent Change

📌 New Value Formulas

After p% increase: New = Original × (1 + p/100)
After p% decrease: New = Original × (1 − p/100)

Example: A 500 ETB item increases by 15% → New = 500 × 1.15 = 575 ETB.
A 500 ETB item decreases by 15% → New = 500 × 0.85 = 425 ETB.

2.3 Finding the Original Value

Sometimes you know the new value and the percent change, but need to find the original.

📌 Reverse Formula — Find the Original

If a value increased by p% and the new value is N:
Original = N / (1 + p/100)

If a value decreased by p% and the new value is N:
Original = N / (1 − p/100)

📝 Exam tip

Do NOT just subtract the percentage from the new value. A price after a 20% increase is 1.2× the original — so the original is the new value ÷ 1.2, not the new value minus 20%.

2.4 Compound Percent Changes

When a value undergoes multiple successive changes, don't add the percentages. Multiply the factors.

📌 Successive Percent Changes

For changes p₁%, p₂%, p₃% (each positive = increase, negative = decrease):

Final = Original × (1 + p₁/100)(1 + p₂/100)(1 + p₃/100)…

Common mistake — why adding percentages is wrong
A 500 ETB item increases 20% then decreases 20%.

❌ Wrong: 500 × (1 + 0.20 − 0.20) = 500 (unchanged)
✅ Right: 500 × 1.20 × 0.80 = 500 × 0.96 = 480 ETB

The order of the percentages doesn't matter — multiplication is commutative. But the result is NOT the original.

2.5 Applications — Inflation and Depreciation

Inflation
+%
Prices rise over time. Applied as compound increase.
Depreciation
−%
Value falls over time. Applied as compound decrease.
📌 Inflation and Depreciation Formulas

Inflation (n years at r%):
New Price = Original × (1 + r/100)ⁿ

Depreciation (n years at r%):
New Value = Original × (1 − r/100)ⁿ

These use the compound interest formula with n periods.

📝 Real-world example

Inflation of 20% per year for 3 years multiplies prices by (1.20)³ ≈ 1.728 — a 72.8% total increase, not 60%. Compounding makes a big difference over time.

2.6 Percentage Points vs. Percent Change

These two concepts are often confused in news reports and business documents.

Percentage Point Change
The simple arithmetic difference between two percentages.

Example: rate goes from 5% to 8% → change of 3 percentage points.
Percent Change
The relative change, computed against the original.

Example: rate goes from 5% to 8% → percent change = 3/5 = 60% increase.
📝 In the news

When a news report says "unemployment fell by 2%" — it could mean either a 2 percentage point drop (e.g., 7% → 5%) or a 2% relative drop (e.g., 7% → 6.86%). Always clarify.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Basic Percent Increase
Problem: A kilogram of sugar costs 60 ETB. The price rises to 75 ETB. What is the percent increase?
Step 1
Identify: Original = 60, New = 75. Change = 75 − 60 = 15 ETB.
Step 2
% Change = (15 / 60) × 100% = 25%
✅ Percent increase = 25%
Example 2Percent Decrease — Depreciation
Problem: A car worth 800,000 ETB depreciates to 680,000 ETB after one year. What is the percent decrease?
Step 1
Change = 800,000 − 680,000 = 120,000 ETB decrease.
Step 2
% Decrease = (120,000 / 800,000) × 100% = 15%
Check
800,000 × 0.85 = 680,000 ✓
✅ Percent decrease = 15%
Example 3Finding the Original Price
Problem: After a 12% increase, a book costs 560 ETB. What was the original price?
Setup
New = 560, percent increase = 12%.
560 = Original × 1.12
Solve
Original = 560 / 1.12 = 500 ETB
✅ Original price = 500 ETB
Example 4Successive Percent Changes
Problem: A product's price is 1,000 ETB. It rises by 20% one year, then falls by 15% the next year. What is the final price? What is the total percent change from the original?
Step 1
After 20% rise: 1,000 × 1.20 = 1,200 ETB
Step 2
After 15% fall: 1,200 × 0.85 = 1,020 ETB
Total change
% Change = (1,020 − 1,000)/1,000 × 100% = 2%
Alternative
Multiply factors directly: 1,000 × 1.20 × 0.85 = 1,020 ETB
✅ Final price = 1,020 ETB · Total increase = 2% (not 5%!)
Example 5Inflation Over Multiple Years
Problem: Bread costs 25 ETB today. Inflation runs at 30% per year for 2 years. What will the bread cost in 2 years? What is the total percent increase?
Step 1
New = 25 × (1.30)² = 25 × 1.69 = 42.25 ETB
Step 2
Year 1: 25 × 1.30 = 32.50
Year 2: 32.50 × 1.30 = 42.25 ✓
Total increase
(42.25 − 25) / 25 × 100% = 69%
✅ Cost after 2 years = 42.25 ETB · Total increase = 69% (not 60%)
