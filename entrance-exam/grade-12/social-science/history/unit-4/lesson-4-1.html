👑 The Reign of Haile Selassie I (1930–1936)

From his 1930 coronation to the eve of the Italian invasion, Haile Selassie I launched Ethiopia's first comprehensive modernization program — a constitution, a modern army, schools, roads, and international diplomacy. This lesson covers the formative first six years of his reign.

📖 Reading: ~30 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Haile Selassie I came to power at a critical moment. Ethiopia had defeated Italy at Adwa (1896) and entered the League of Nations (1923), but internally the country was still deeply conservative — dominated by regional lords, the Orthodox Church, and a subsistence economy. Haile Selassie's project was to transform Ethiopia into a centralized, modern nation-state within a single generation. This lesson covers the first phase of that project: the 1930 coronation, the 1931 Constitution, and the modernization reforms that followed.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Describe the significance of the 1930 coronation
  • Analyze the 1931 Constitution — its provisions and limitations
  • Identify the main modernization reforms of 1930–1936
  • Explain Haile Selassie's approach to education and institutions
  • Understand the tensions that his reforms created
👑
The 1930 Coronation
A new era begins
📌 Coronation of Haile Selassie I

On 2 November 1930, Ras Teferi Mekonnen was crowned Emperor Haile Selassie I in Addis Ababa. The coronation was a grand international event attended by representatives from across Europe and the Middle East — a deliberate statement that Ethiopia was a modern, sovereign nation. Haile Selassie took the throne name meaning "Power of the Trinity."

Why the Coronation Mattered

  • Symbol of modernity — a public, international event announcing Ethiopia's place in the world
  • Legitimacy — confirmed his position after years of power struggle
  • Propaganda — carefully staged to present Ethiopia as equal to European nations
  • Inspiration — celebrated across the African diaspora, from Harlem to the Caribbean
📝 Global impact

The coronation was covered by media worldwide and became a symbol of African pride. For many in the African diaspora — especially in the United States and the Caribbean — Haile Selassie was seen as a fulfillment of biblical prophecy and a symbol of Black sovereignty. This was the origin of the Rastafari movement.

📜
The 1931 Constitution
Ethiopia's first written constitution
📌 1931 Constitution

Ethiopia's first written constitution, promulgated on 16 July 1931. It created a bicameral parliament (Chamber of Deputies and Senate), but real power remained with the Emperor. It was a cautious document — meant to introduce modern institutions without threatening the imperial system.

Key Provisions

ProvisionEffect
Bicameral ParliamentChamber of Deputies (elected) + Senate (appointed by Emperor)
Executive powerEmperor retains full executive authority
SuccessionImperial succession limited to Haile Selassie's line
Judicial reformIntroduced modern courts and legal codes
SlaveryCommitted to gradual abolition (completed 1942)

Limitations

  • Power remained with the emperor — the parliament was advisory, not legislative
  • No political parties — elections were indirect and controlled
  • Nobles retained huge power — the constitution didn't break feudal structures
  • No bill of rights in the modern sense — a few protections but no real guarantees
📝 A balance

The 1931 Constitution was a careful compromise. Haile Selassie wanted to modernize but could not afford to alienate the church and nobles who had supported his rise. The result was a document that created modern institutions on paper without distributing real power.

🏗️
Modernization Reforms
Building a modern state

Between 1930 and 1936, Haile Selassie introduced a broad reform program. The overall goal was to centralize power and modernize institutions — moving Ethiopia from a collection of semi-autonomous regions to a unified nation-state.

Major Reforms

🏛️
Central Government

Created modern ministries — Finance, Foreign Affairs, War, Interior, Education, Public Works, Commerce.

⚔️
Modern Army

Built a professional army with modern weapons, trained by European advisors (Belgian, Swedish).

🏫
Education

Founded the first modern secondary schools; sent hundreds of students to Europe for study.

🏦
Banking & Currency

Established the Bank of Ethiopia (1931) and introduced a national currency (the birr).

🛣️
Infrastructure

Built roads, telegraph lines, telephone networks, and the first modern hospital in Addis Ababa.

⚖️
Legal Reform

Introduced modern courts, a penal code, and began to standardize the legal system.

📝 The pace of reform

Haile Selassie was in a hurry. He knew that a European invasion was possible at any moment — Italy was already in Eritrea and Libya, and Fascist leader Mussolini openly spoke of avenging Adwa. The reforms of 1930–1936 were designed to prepare Ethiopia for the coming storm.

📚
Education & Institutions
Building human capital

Haile Selassie believed that education was the key to Ethiopia's future. His government founded modern schools, sent students abroad, and invited foreign teachers and advisors. The goal was to create a new generation of educated Ethiopians who could staff modern institutions.

Key Educational Developments

  • Tafari Makonnen School (1925) — the first modern secondary school in Ethiopia
  • Menelik II School — expanded in 1930s
  • Ethiopian students abroad — hundreds sent to France, Switzerland, Britain, and the United States
  • Foreign teachers — invited from France, Egypt, and elsewhere
  • Military academy — trained a new officer corps

Timeline: 1930–1936

Haile Selassie crowned Emperor
2 November — the beginning of a 44-year reign.
1931

First written constitution; first modern bank.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 4.1
