💥 World War I, the Russian Revolution & the Interwar Period

The Great War shattered the old order. Revolution swept Russia. And between the wars, new ideologies — fascism and communism — reshaped Europe.

📖 Reading: ~30 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction

The period 1914–1945 was one of unprecedented global conflict and transformation. Two world wars, a communist revolution, and the rise of totalitarian ideologies changed the course of human history. This lesson covers the first half: World War I, the Russian Revolution, and the fragile peace of the interwar years.

  • Explain the causes of World War I
  • Describe the main events and consequences of WWI
  • Explain the Russian Revolution (1917)
  • Analyse the interwar period and the rise of fascism
💥
World War I (1914–1918)

WWI was the deadliest conflict the world had seen up to that point — over 16 million dead. It started as a European conflict between two alliances: the Triple Entente (Britain, France, Russia) and the Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire).

Underlying Causes

  • Militarism — arms race between European powers
  • Alliances — rigid system of alliances turned a local crisis into a world war
  • Imperialism — competition for colonies
  • Nationalism — especially in the Balkans (the "powder keg of Europe")

Trigger

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary in Sarajevo (June 1914) set off a chain reaction: Austria declared war on Serbia, Russia mobilized, Germany declared war on Russia and France, Britain entered after Germany invaded Belgium.

Consequences

  • Fall of four empires: German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman, and Russian
  • Treaty of Versailles (1919) — Germany forced to accept full responsibility for the war, pay massive reparations, and lose territory
  • Creation of the League of Nations
  • Harsh terms of Versailles → resentment that fueled the rise of Nazism
🔴
The Russian Revolution (1917)

The Russian Revolution was one of the most important events of the 20th century. It toppled the 300-year-old Romanov dynasty and created the world's first communist state.

Background

  • Russia was ruled by an autocratic Tsar (Nicholas II) with a backward economy
  • Widespread poverty, inequality, and political repression
  • WWI casualties and food shortages aggravated the crisis
  • Growing revolutionary movements, especially the Bolsheviks led by Vladimir Lenin

Two Revolutions in 1917

  • February Revolution — protests in Petrograd (St. Petersburg) led to the abdication of the Tsar; a Provisional Government was formed
  • October Revolution — Lenin's Bolsheviks seized power from the Provisional Government

Consequences

  • Creation of the Soviet Union (USSR) — the world's first communist state
  • Russia withdrew from WWI (Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, 1918)
  • Civil war between Reds (Bolsheviks) and Whites (anti-communist forces)
  • Inspired communist movements worldwide
📉
The Interwar Period (1919–1939)

After WWI, the world was supposed to be rebuilt on peace and democracy. Instead, the interwar period was a time of instability, economic crisis, and the rise of totalitarian ideologies.

Key Features

  • Great Depression (1929) — a worldwide economic collapse with massive unemployment and poverty
  • Weakness of the League of Nations — could not prevent aggression
  • Rise of totalitarianism — fascism in Italy, Nazism in Germany, communism in the USSR
  • Failure of democracy in many countries — replaced by dictatorships
⚡
Fascism and Nazism

Fascism — an authoritarian, nationalistic ideology — emerged in Italy under Benito Mussolini (1922) and in Germany as Nazism under Adolf Hitler (1933).

Key Features

  • Extreme nationalism and racism
  • Rejection of democracy and individual rights
  • Cult of the leader (Il Duce / Der Führer)
  • Aggressive foreign policy and expansionism
  • Use of propaganda, terror, and violence
  • In Germany: anti-Semitism and the persecution of Jews
📝 The road to WWII

The aggressive policies of fascist Italy, Nazi Germany, and militarist Japan in the 1930s — including Italy's invasion of Ethiopia (1935) — set the world on the path to the Second World War. That's the subject of the next lesson.

Summary
  • WWI (1914–1918) — caused by militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism; triggered by the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand
  • Treaty of Versailles (1919) — harsh terms on Germany; created resentment
  • Russian Revolution (1917) — Bolsheviks led by Lenin overthrew the Tsar; created the USSR
  • Interwar Period (1919–1939) — marked by the Great Depression, weak League of Nations, and rise of totalitarianism
  • Fascism and Nazism — extreme nationalism, rejection of democracy, cult of the leader, aggressive foreign policy
  • These developments set the stage for World War II
