⚔️ World War I & the Russian Revolution

Between 1914 and 1917, the world witnessed two earth-shattering events: the devastating global conflict of World War I, and the Bolshevik Revolution that created the first communist state. This lesson covers the causes, course, and aftermath of both.

📖 Reading: ~30 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The early 20th century was defined by two monumental upheavals. World War I (1914–1918) was the first truly global conflict, drawing in empires across Europe and beyond and redrawing the map of the world. In the midst of this chaos, the Russian Revolution (1917) overthrew centuries of tsarist rule and established the world's first communist state. Together, these events set the stage for the rest of the 20th century.

🔥
Causes of World War I
The powder keg of Europe
📌 The MAIN Causes

Historians often use the acronym M.A.I.N. to explain the underlying causes of WWI: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, and Nationalism.

  • Militarism: The massive buildup of armies and navies, especially the naval arms race between Britain and Germany.
  • Alliances: The Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) vs. the Triple Entente (Britain, France, Russia).
  • Imperialism: Competition for colonies in Africa and Asia created tension between European powers.
  • Nationalism: Intense pride in one's nation, especially in the Balkans, which was known as the "powder keg of Europe."
  • The Spark: The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary in Sarajevo on June 28, 1914.
⚔️
Course of the War
1914–1918

The war was fought on multiple fronts, but the most famous was the Western Front, where trench warfare resulted in a deadly stalemate.

  • Total War: Entire economies and civilian populations were mobilized for the war effort.
  • New Technology: Machine guns, poison gas, tanks, submarines, and airplanes were used on a massive scale for the first time.
  • Trench Warfare: Soldiers lived in miles of muddy, rat-infested trenches, facing constant artillery barrages.
  • US Entry: The United States entered the war in 1917 on the side of the Allies, tipping the balance.
  • Armistice: Germany signed an armistice on November 11, 1918, ending the fighting.
📜
The Paris Peace Settlement
The Treaty of Versailles (1919)

The victorious Allies met in Paris to dictate the terms of peace to the defeated Central Powers. The most famous (and controversial) was the Treaty of Versailles with Germany.

  • War Guilt Clause: Germany had to accept full responsibility for starting the war.
  • Reparations: Germany was forced to pay massive financial compensation to the Allies.
  • Territorial Loss: Germany lost significant territory to France, Poland, and others, and its colonies were confiscated.
  • Demilitarization: The German army and navy were severely restricted.
  • The League of Nations: An international organization was created to maintain peace (though the US Senate refused to join).
🚩
The Russian Revolution (1917)
The birth of the Soviet Union

While WWI raged, Russia was collapsing from within. Poor living conditions, food shortages, and massive casualties in the war led to widespread unrest, culminating in the revolution of 1917.

  • February Revolution: Strikes and riots in Petrograd forced Tsar Nicholas II to abdicate, ending the Romanov dynasty.
  • October Revolution: Led by Vladimir Lenin, the Bolsheviks seized power from the provisional government.
  • Treaty of Brest-Litovsk: Lenin pulled Russia out of WWI, ceding large territories to Germany.
  • Russian Civil War: A brutal civil war (1918–1921) between the Red Army (communists) and the White Army (anti-communists).
  • The USSR: By 1922, the Soviet Union was officially formed, introducing a state-planned communist economy.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 4.1
What you should remember
  • M.A.I.N. causes of WWI: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism.
  • The spark was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in 1914.
  • WWI introduced trench warfare and new deadly technologies.
  • The Treaty of Versailles (1919) ended WWI but harshly punished Germany, planting the seeds for WWII.
  • The Russian Revolution (1917) overthrew the Tsar and brought the Bolsheviks (Lenin) to power.
  • The Russian Civil War led to the creation of the USSR, the world's first communist state.
