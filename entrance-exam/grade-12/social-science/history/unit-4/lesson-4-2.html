💥 The Interwar Crisis & World War II

Following the devastation of WWI, the world plunged into economic depression and the rise of totalitarian regimes. This lesson covers the Interwar period, the rise of Fascism and Nazism, and the global conflict of World War II.

📖 Reading: ~30 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The period between the two World Wars (1919–1939) was a time of profound crisis. The harsh terms of the Treaty of Versailles, combined with the global Great Depression of the 1930s, created desperate conditions that led to the rise of extreme political ideologies like Fascism in Italy and Nazism in Germany. These aggressive regimes ultimately plunged the world into an even deadlier conflict: World War II (1939–1945).

📉
The Interwar Economy
Capitalism in Crisis

The 1920s saw a brief period of prosperity (the "Roaring Twenties"), but it was built on fragile foundations. In 1929, the bubble burst.

  • The Great Depression: The US stock market crash of 1929 triggered a worldwide economic depression.
  • Massive Unemployment: Millions lost their jobs, farms, and homes in Europe and America.
  • Failure of Capitalism: Many began to lose faith in democratic capitalism, looking to extreme left-wing (Communism) or extreme right-wing (Fascism) solutions.
  • Isolationism: Countries like the US and Britain turned inward, focusing on domestic recovery.
🪖
Rise of Fascism & Nazism
Totalitarian regimes
📌 Fascism

A political ideology characterized by extreme nationalism, dictatorial power, forcible suppression of opposition, and strong control of society and the economy.

  • Benito Mussolini (Italy): Took power in 1922, establishing a Fascist dictatorship and promising to restore the Roman Empire.
  • Adolf Hitler (Germany): Leader of the Nazi Party, took power in 1933. He promised to overturn the Treaty of Versailles, rebuild the economy, and create a "Third Reich" (Empire).
  • Characteristics of Nazism: Extreme racism, anti-Semitism, and the belief in Aryan racial superiority.
  • Appeasement: Britain and France adopted a policy of appeasement in the 1930s, allowing Hitler to annex territories (like Austria and the Sudetenland) in hopes of avoiding war. This failed.
💥
World War II
1939–1945
  • The Spark: Germany invaded Poland on September 1, 1939. Britain and France declared war two days later.
  • The Axis Powers: Germany, Italy, and Japan.
  • The Allied Powers: Britain, France (until 1940), the Soviet Union (after 1941), and the United States (after 1941).
  • Blitzkrieg: Germany used "lightning war" tactics to quickly overrun much of Europe.
  • The Holocaust: The systematic, state-sponsored genocide of six million Jews and millions of others by Nazi Germany.
  • Turning Points: The Battle of Stalingrad (1942–1943) in the East, and the D-Day invasion (1944) in the West.
  • The Atomic Bomb: The war in the Pacific ended after the US dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945.
🌍
Impact of WWII
A new world order
  • Devastation: 40 to 50 million people died, making it the deadliest war in human history.
  • The Cold War: The world was divided into two rival superpowers: the capitalist USA and the communist USSR.
  • Creation of the UN: The United Nations was founded in 1945 to maintain international peace and security.
  • Decolonization: The weakening of European powers (Britain, France) accelerated independence movements across Asia and Africa.
  • Nuclear Age: The world entered a new era where nuclear weapons could destroy humanity.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 4.2
What you should remember
  • The Great Depression (1929) caused massive economic devastation and loss of faith in democracy.
  • Fascism (Mussolini) and Nazism (Hitler) rose to power by promising order and national revival.
  • The policy of Appeasement failed to stop Hitler's aggression.
  • WWII began in 1939 with the German invasion of Poland.
  • The Holocaust was the horrific genocide of Jews and other minorities by the Nazis.
  • WWII ended in 1945. It resulted in the Cold War, the creation of the UN, and the acceleration of Decolonization in Africa and Asia.
