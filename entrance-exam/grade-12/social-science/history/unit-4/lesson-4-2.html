💥 World War II (1939–1945)

The deadliest conflict in human history — over 70 million dead. It ended the age of empires and reshaped the global order.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction

World War II was the most destructive conflict in human history. It involved almost every country in the world, resulted in an estimated 70–85 million deaths, and culminated in the Holocaust and the use of atomic bombs. This lesson covers the causes, key events, and aftermath of the war.

⚠️
Causes of World War II

The causes of WWII can be traced directly to the unresolved issues of WWI and the failures of the interwar period.

  • Treaty of Versailles (1919) — the harsh terms imposed on Germany created deep resentment and a desire for revenge.
  • The Great Depression — economic collapse destabilized democracies and fueled extremist movements.
  • Rise of Totalitarianism — aggressive fascist and militarist regimes in Germany, Italy, and Japan sought expansion.
  • Appeasement Policy — Britain and France allowed Hitler to annex Austria and Czechoslovakia in the hope of avoiding war.
  • Failure of the League of Nations — the international body had no power to stop aggression.

Key Events
YearEvent
1939Germany invades Poland (1 Sept); Britain and France declare war on Germany.
1940Germany conquers France; Battle of Britain begins.
1941Germany invades the USSR (Operation Barbarossa); Japan attacks Pearl Harbor (7 Dec); the US enters the war.
1942–43Turning points: Battle of Stalingrad, Battle of Midway, Battle of El Alamein.
1944D-Day (Allied invasion of Normandy); Allies advance towards Germany.
1945Germany surrenders (May); atomic bombs dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki (Aug); Japan surrenders (Sept).
🌍
Aftermath and Consequences
  • Massive human cost — 70–85 million dead, including the systematic murder of 6 million Jews in the Holocaust.
  • Europe divided — the continent was split between communist East and capitalist West; the Cold War began.
  • End of Empires — the war accelerated the collapse of European colonial empires.
  • Creation of the United Nations (1945) — to prevent future world wars.
  • Nuclear Age — the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki began the nuclear era.
  • Superpowers — the US and the USSR emerged as global superpowers.
📝 Ethiopia in WWII

Ethiopia played a role in WWII. After the Italian invasion (1935), Ethiopian patriots (the Arbegnoch) resisted the occupation. In 1941, Ethiopian forces and Allied troops liberated Ethiopia from Italian rule, and Emperor Haile Selassie returned to his throne.

Summary
  • WWII (1939–1945) — the deadliest conflict in human history, starting with Germany's invasion of Poland.
  • Causes: Treaty of Versailles, Great Depression, rise of totalitarianism, appeasement, failure of the League of Nations.
  • Key turning points: Stalingrad, Midway, D-Day, and the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
  • Aftermath: 70–85 million dead, the Holocaust, the Cold War, the creation of the UN, and the end of European empires.
