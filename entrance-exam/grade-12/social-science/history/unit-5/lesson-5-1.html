🌍 Aftermath of WWII & the United Nations

How the Second World War reshaped the global order and gave birth to the United Nations.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction

World War II (1939–1945) was the deadliest conflict in human history. When it ended, the world faced a choice: return to the unstable nationalism of the interwar years, or build a new international order. The answer was the United Nations — an organisation designed to prevent another global war. This lesson examines the war's human and material cost, the wartime conferences that shaped the peace, the founding and structure of the UN, and the first war-crimes tribunals.

💥
The War's Devastation

By 1945 the scale of destruction was unprecedented. Estimates place total deaths at 70–85 million, including some 6 million Jews murdered in the Holocaust and millions of civilians killed in bombing campaigns, famine and reprisals.

RegionKey Impact
EuropeCities flattened; Germany divided; millions of displaced persons (DPs)
USSR~27 million dead; vast industrial and agricultural loss
AsiaJapan devastated by atomic bombs; China and Southeast Asia scarred
AfricaColonial troops and resources mobilised; nationalist sentiment grew
The war also ended Europe's global supremacy. The USA and USSR emerged as superpowers, and the old colonial empires began to unravel.
🤝
Wartime Conferences

The Allied powers met repeatedly to plan the post-war world:

  • Tehran (1943) — Stalin, Roosevelt and Churchill agreed on the invasion of Europe and discussed a future UN.
  • Yalta (February 1945) — decided the division of Germany, Soviet entry into the war against Japan, and the veto power of the Big Five.
  • Potsdam (July–August 1945) — Truman, Stalin and Attlee settled occupation zones and demanded Japan's unconditional surrender.

These meetings revealed growing mistrust between the Western Allies and the Soviet Union — a foretaste of the Cold War.

🕊️
Founding the United Nations

The UN replaced the failed League of Nations. Its Charter was drafted at San Francisco (April–June 1945) and signed on 26 June 1945 by 50 nations; it came into force on 24 October 1945 — now celebrated as UN Day.

Core purposes:

  • Maintain international peace and security
  • Promote human rights and fundamental freedoms
  • Foster economic and social development
  • Promote self-determination of peoples
Unlike the League, the UN could authorise military action and had the great powers as permanent members — but this also gave them the veto, which could paralyse the Security Council.
🏛️
UN Structure & Agencies
OrganRole

Key specialised agencies:

  • UNESCO — education, science and culture
  • WHO — world health
  • FAO — food and agriculture
  • UNICEF — children's welfare
  • UNHCR — refugees
⚖️
Nuremberg & Tokyo Trials

For the first time, leaders were tried for crimes against humanity and war crimes before international tribunals.

  • Nuremberg Trials (1945–1946) — 22 Nazi leaders tried; 12 sentenced to death. Established that individuals, not just states, are accountable under international law.
  • Tokyo Trials (1946–1948) — Japanese wartime leaders tried for aggression and atrocities.
These tribunals laid the foundation for modern international criminal justice, later extended through the International Criminal Court (2002).
