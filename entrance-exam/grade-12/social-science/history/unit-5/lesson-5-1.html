🌐 Aftermath of WWII & The United Nations

World War II left the globe in ruins, but it also gave birth to a new international order. This lesson explores the devastating consequences of the war, the creation of the United Nations to prevent future conflicts, and the massive socio-economic recovery that followed.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The end of World War II in 1945 marked a major turning point in global history. The world had just witnessed unprecedented destruction, but from the ashes emerged a determination to build a better, more peaceful future. This lesson covers the immediate aftermath of the war, the establishment of the United Nations, and the early efforts to rebuild the global economy and society.

💥
The Aftermath of WWII
Section 5.1

WWII was the deadliest conflict in human history. It left deep physical, economic, and psychological scars across the globe.

  • Massive Casualties: An estimated 40 to 50 million people died, including 6 million Jews in the Holocaust.
  • Physical Destruction: Cities, infrastructure, and entire economies across Europe and Asia were destroyed.
  • Displaced Persons: Millions of refugees were left homeless and displaced from their countries.
  • The Holocaust: The systematic genocide of Jews and other minorities by the Nazis shocked the world's conscience.
  • Nuclear Age: The use of atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki marked the beginning of the nuclear era.
🕊️
The United Nations
Section 5.2
📌 The United Nations (UN)

An intergovernmental organization established on October 24, 1945, to maintain international peace and security, develop friendly relations among nations, and promote social progress and better living standards.

Principal Organs of the UN

🌍
General Assembly

The main deliberative body where all member states have one vote.

🛡️
Security Council

Responsible for maintaining peace. Has 5 permanent members with veto power.

📈
Post-War Socio-Economic Recovery
Section 5.3
  • The Marshall Plan (1948): The US provided billions of dollars to rebuild Western European economies.
  • Bretton Woods Institutions: The World Bank and the IMF were created to manage the global economy.
  • The Welfare State: Western European countries expanded social programs (healthcare, education, pensions).
  • Decolonization: The war weakened European empires, leading to independence in Asia and Africa.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 5.1
What you should remember
  • WWII left 40–50 million dead and caused massive physical and economic destruction.
  • The United Nations was founded on October 24, 1945, to maintain global peace.
  • The UN Security Council has 5 permanent members with veto power (US, UK, France, Russia, China).
  • The Marshall Plan provided massive US financial aid to rebuild Western Europe.
  • The World Bank and IMF were created to stabilize the global economy.
