🔥 Nationalism & the Unification of Italy and Germany

In the 19th century, nationalism swept across Europe — binding people together by language, culture, and history. Two of the most dramatic results were the unification of Italy (1861–1870) and Germany (1871).

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The 19th century was the century of nationalism. Across Europe, peoples who shared a common language, culture, and history began to demand their own nations and states. Between 1859 and 1871, two great nations were born from this wave: Italy and Germany. Both changed the balance of power in Europe and set the stage for World War I.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define nationalism and explain its rise in 19th-century Europe
  • Describe the unification of Italy (1859–1870)
  • Identify the key figures of Italian unification
  • Describe the unification of Germany (1862–1871)
  • Explain Bismarck's role and his policy of "blood and iron"
  • Compare the Italian and German unification processes
🔥
What is Nationalism?
MoE Textbook §1.3
📌 Nationalism

Nationalism is the belief that a group of people who share a common language, culture, history, and territory should form their own independent nation-state. It is both a political ideology and a powerful emotional force that binds people together.

Why Did Nationalism Rise?

📖
Romanticism

Artists and writers celebrated folk culture, language, and history — creating a sense of shared identity.

🇫🇷
French Revolution

Showed that people could overthrow kings and create their own nation based on citizenship, not royalty.

⚙️
Industrialization

Created new classes (bourgeoisie, workers) who demanded political rights and national unity for markets.

🏛️
Congress of Vienna

1815 settlement imposed by monarchs suppressed national aspirations — creating resentment and resistance.

Two Types of Nationalism

TypeMeaningExample
Unification nationalism Peoples of the same nation divided into many states seek to unite Italy, Germany
Separation nationalism Ethnic groups within an empire seek independence Greece, Serbia, Belgium
📝 Why this matters

Nationalism was a double-edged force. It inspired people to fight for freedom and self-rule — but also led to aggressive expansionism, imperial rivalry, and eventually the two World Wars.

🇮🇹
The Unification of Italy
MoE Textbook §1.4 · 1859–1870

In 1815, "Italy" was not a country — it was a patchwork of nine separate states, most of them ruled by foreign powers or the Pope. The Kingdom of Sardinia (Piedmont) became the leading force for unification under King Victor Emmanuel II and his brilliant prime minister Count Cavour.

Steps to Unification

1852
Cavour becomes Prime Minister
Count Cavour takes power in Piedmont-Sardinia. He modernizes the economy, strengthens the army, and wins international support for Italian unification.
1859
War with Austria
Cavour allies with France (Napoleon III) and defeats Austria in northern Italy. Lombardy is annexed to Piedmont.
1860
Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand
Giuseppe Garibaldi and his 1,000 Red Shirts sail to Sicily, defeat the Bourbon army, and conquer southern Italy. He hands it all to King Victor Emmanuel II.
1861
Kingdom of Italy proclaimed
Victor Emmanuel II is crowned King of Italy. Rome and Venetia remain outside the new kingdom.
1866
Venetia joined
Italy gains Venetia after siding with Prussia in the Austro-Prussian War.
1870
Rome became the capital
With French troops withdrawn during the Franco-Prussian War, Italy seizes Rome. The Papal States are annexed. Rome becomes the capital of a fully united Italy.
👤
Key Figures of Italian Unification
The architects of a new nation
👑
Victor Emmanuel II

King of Piedmont-Sardinia. Became the first King of a united Italy. The symbolic head of the Risorgimento.

🎩
Count Camillo di Cavour

Prime Minister of Piedmont. The political brain of unification — used diplomacy to isolate Austria and win French support.

⚔️
Giuseppe Garibaldi

The military hero. Led the Expedition of the Thousand (1860) that conquered southern Italy.

✍️
Giuseppe Mazzini

The prophet of Italian nationalism. Founded "Young Italy" (1831) and inspired the movement with his writings.

📝 The Risorgimento

The Italian word Risorgimento means "resurgence" or "revival." It refers to the whole movement — political, cultural, and military — that led to Italian unification. It was driven by writers, revolutionaries, and statesmen over more than 40 years.

🇩🇪
The Unification of Germany
MoE Textbook §1.5 · 1862–1871

After 1815, "Germany" was a loose confederation of 39 separate states, dominated by Austria and Prussia. The German states shared a common language and culture but were divided politically. The question of unification — the German Question — dominated the century. Would it be "Little Germany" (Kleindeutschland) without Austria, or "Big Germany" (Grossdeutschland) with Austria? Prussia's victory settled the question.

Steps to Unification

1862
Bismarck becomes Chancellor
Otto von Bismarck is appointed Minister-President of Prussia by King Wilhelm I. He declares that German unification will be achieved by "blood and iron" — not by speeches or majority votes.
1864
War with Denmark
Prussia and Austria defeat Denmark and seize the duchies of Schleswig and Holstein. This was Bismarck's first step — and set up conflict with Austria over the spoils.
1866
Austro-Prussian War (Seven Weeks' War)
Prussia defeats Austria decisively at Königgrätz. Austria is expelled from German affairs. The North German Confederation is formed under Prussian leadership.
1870–71
Franco-Prussian War
Bismarck manipulates France into declaring war. Prussia and the German states defeat France decisively. The southern German states (Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden) join the new German Empire.
1871 · January 18
German Empire proclaimed at Versailles
King Wilhelm I of Prussia is crowned Kaiser (Emperor) of Germany in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles — a deliberate humiliation of France. A unified, powerful German Empire is born.
🎩
Bismarck's Role & Realpolitik
The Iron Chancellor
📌 Realpolitik

Realpolitik — "the politics of reality" — is the practice of pursuing national interests through practical means rather than ideological or moral principles. Bismarck was its master. He used war, diplomacy, and manipulation as tools — whatever worked to achieve his goals.

Bismarck's Key Tactics

  • "Blood and iron" — unification through military force, not parliamentary debate
  • Isolate the enemy — keep potential opponents from forming alliances against Prussia
  • Manipulate events — start conflicts on his terms, so Prussia appeared the victim
  • Unite through war — use external threats to bring German states together
  • Know when to stop — after victory, treat the defeated generously (except France) to avoid future revenge
📝 The Ems Dispatch (1870)

Bismarck edited a telegram from King Wilhelm I to make it sound insulting to France. This provoked France into declaring war — exactly what Bismarck wanted, because it made Prussia appear the victim and united all German states behind it.

📝 The aftermath

Both unifications changed the European balance of power. Germany's rise as a great power after 1871 destabilized Europe — Bismarck tried to manage it through a complex alliance system, but after his dismissal in 1890, Germany pursued aggressive policies that contributed to the outbreak of World War I in 1914.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.2
What you should remember
  • Nationalism = belief that people sharing language, culture, and history should form their own nation-state
  • Nationalism rose due to Romanticism, the French Revolution, industrialization, and resentment against the Congress of Vienna
  • Two types: unification nationalism (Italy, Germany) and separation nationalism (Greece, Serbia, Belgium)
  • Italian Unification: led by Piedmont-Sardinia under King Victor Emmanuel II and PM Cavour
  • Key figures: Victor Emmanuel II · Cavour · Garibaldi · Mazzini
  • Garibaldi's Expedition of the Thousand (1860) conquered southern Italy
  • Kingdom of Italy proclaimed 1861; Rome joined 1870
  • German Unification: led by Prussia under Bismarck, who declared "blood and iron"
  • Bismarck's wars: Denmark (1864) · Austria (1866) · France (1870–71)
  • German Empire proclaimed at Versailles, 18 January 1871
  • Realpolitik = politics based on practical interests, not ideology — Bismarck's method
  • The German Question: Grossdeutschland (with Austria) vs. Kleindeutschland (without) — settled in favor of the latter
  • Both unifications destabilized Europe and set the stage for World War I
