🏭 Industrial Revolution & the Rise of Capitalism

Between 1750 and 1900, Britain and then Western Europe transformed from agrarian economies into industrial powerhouses. This lesson explores the origins of that transformation, the rise of capitalism, and its impact on society.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The Industrial Revolution was one of the most important transformations in human history. Beginning in Britain in the mid-18th century, it moved production from farms and workshops into factories, replaced human and animal power with steam and machinery, and reshaped how people lived, worked, and traded. Out of this transformation emerged industrial capitalism — the economic system that would dominate the modern world.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain the causes of the Industrial Revolution
  • Describe the key inventions that drove industrialization
  • Define capitalism and its main features
  • Analyze the social and economic effects of industrialization
  • Understand how capitalism spread from Britain to continental Europe
⚙️
The Industrial Revolution
MoE Textbook §1.1 · c. 1750–1900
📌 Industrial Revolution

The Industrial Revolution was the transition from an agrarian, handicraft economy to one dominated by machine manufacturing, factory production, and industrial capitalism. It began in Britain around 1750 and spread to Western Europe and North America throughout the 19th century.

Why Britain First?

  • Abundant coal and iron — the raw materials of industrial power
  • Agricultural revolution — increased food production, freed up labor for factories
  • Colonial trade and capital — profits from colonies financed investment
  • Political stability — no major wars on British soil during the critical period
  • Strong banking and credit system — funded new enterprises
  • Large domestic market — internal demand for manufactured goods
  • Skilled craftsmen and engineers — inventors and mechanics who built the machines

Key Innovations

📝 Why it mattered

These inventions did more than increase output — they reorganized society. People moved from farms to cities, from workshops to factories. A new working class emerged, and with it new social tensions, new political ideas, and new economic systems.

💰
The Rise of Capitalism
A new economic system
📌 Capitalism

Capitalism is an economic system in which the means of production are privately owned, goods and services are produced for profit, and prices are set by market forces (supply and demand). Industrial capitalism — capitalism driven by mechanized production and wage labor — took shape in 19th-century Europe.

Main Features of Capitalism

🏭
Private Ownership

Factories, land, and capital are owned by individuals or companies, not the state.

💵
Profit Motive

Producers invest and produce in order to earn profit.

⚖️
Market Forces

Prices, wages, and production are determined by supply and demand.

🆓
Free Competition

Multiple producers compete for customers; competition drives efficiency.

👷
Wage Labor

Workers sell their labor to owners in exchange for a wage.

🌐
Expansion

Capitalism constantly seeks new markets, raw materials, and investment opportunities.

Key Thinkers

  • Adam Smith (1723–1790) — argued for free markets and the "invisible hand" in The Wealth of Nations (1776)
  • David Ricardo (1772–1823) — developed theories of wages, rent, and international trade
  • Karl Marx (1818–1883) — critiqued capitalism, arguing that it exploits workers; his ideas inspired socialism and communism
🌍
Effects on Society
Economic, social and global consequences

Economic Effects

  • Rapid growth in production and national wealth
  • Rise of large corporations and financial markets
  • Growth of international trade and global markets
  • Increased urbanization — cities grew rapidly

Social Effects

  • Emergence of two new classes: the bourgeoisie (owners) and the proletariat (workers)
  • Poor working conditions in factories — long hours, low pay, child labor
  • Growth of labor movements, trade unions, and socialist politics
  • Rise of public education and new social reforms

Timeline of the Industrial Age

c. 1750
Industrial Revolution begins in Britain
Textile innovations, steam power, and the factory system transform production.
1776
Adam Smith publishes The Wealth of Nations
The intellectual foundation of free-market capitalism.
c. 1830
Railway age begins
Steam-powered railways spread across Europe and North America.
1848
Communist Manifesto published
Marx and Engels publish a powerful critique of industrial capitalism.
c. 1870–1914
Second Industrial Revolution
Steel, electricity, chemicals, and oil reshape industry again.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.1
What you should remember
  • The Industrial Revolution (c. 1750–1900) transformed production from handicraft to machine-based factory output
  • It began in Britain due to coal, capital, colonial trade, political stability, and skilled workers
  • Key inventions: spinning jenny, steam engine, power loom, cotton gin, railway, telegraph
  • Capitalism — private ownership, profit motive, market forces, free competition, wage labor
  • Key thinkers: Adam Smith (free markets), David Ricardo (wages/trade), Karl Marx (critique of capitalism)
  • Two new classes emerged: the bourgeoisie (owners) and the proletariat (workers)
  • Industrialization brought urbanization, factory labor, labor movements, and global trade
  • By 1900, industrial capitalism dominated Europe and was spreading to the rest of the world
