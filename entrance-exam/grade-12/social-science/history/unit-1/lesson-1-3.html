⚔️ The American Civil War & the Eastern Question

Two conflicts shaped the second half of the 19th century — the American Civil War (1861–1865) reshaped a rising power, while the Eastern Question destabilized Europe as the Ottoman Empire declined.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Two great dramas dominated the mid-19th century. In North America, the American Civil War (1861–1865) tested whether a nation built on the promise of liberty could survive its deepest contradiction — slavery. In Europe and the Near East, the slow collapse of the Ottoman Empire created the Eastern Question: what would happen to Ottoman territories, and which great power would fill the vacuum?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain the causes of the American Civil War
  • Describe the main events and outcome of the war
  • Explain the aftermath and Reconstruction
  • Identify the causes of Ottoman decline
  • Define the Eastern Question and its significance
  • Describe the Crimean War and its consequences
🇺🇸
Causes of the American Civil War
MoE Textbook §1.6

By the 1850s, the United States had grown into two very different societies: an industrial North with free labour and growing cities, and an agricultural South with a plantation economy dependent on slavery. The tension between these two worlds grew for decades and finally exploded in 1861.

Main Causes

⛓️
Slavery

The central issue. The South's economy depended on enslaved African labour; the North increasingly opposed slavery on moral and political grounds.

🗺️
Westward Expansion

As the US expanded west, the question of whether new states would be free or slave-holding became explosive.

⚖️
States' Rights

Southern states argued they had the right to decide their own laws — including slavery — without federal interference.

💰
Economic Differences

The industrial North wanted tariffs to protect its factories; the agricultural South wanted free trade to export cotton.

📜
Political Crisis

The election of Abraham Lincoln (1860) — an anti-slavery Republican — convinced Southern states to secede.

🏛️
Secession

Eleven Southern states left the Union and formed the Confederate States of America under President Jefferson Davis.

📝 The pivotal election

When Abraham Lincoln won the presidency in November 1860 without a single Southern electoral vote, Southern leaders saw it as proof that their way of life was under attack. South Carolina seceded in December 1860, and ten more states followed. The war began in April 1861.

⚔️
The War (1861–1865)
Brother against brother

The Civil War was the deadliest conflict in American history — over 600,000 soldiers died. The North (Union) had more men, factories, and railroads; the South (Confederacy) had better generals and fought on home soil.

Key Turning Points

1861
Fort Sumter
Confederate forces fire on a Union fort in South Carolina — the war begins.
1863 · January
Emancipation Proclamation
Lincoln declares all slaves in Confederate territory free. The war becomes explicitly a war against slavery.
1863 · July
Battle of Gettysburg
The bloodiest battle of the war — a decisive Union victory. Combined with the fall of Vicksburg, it turns the tide.
1864
Sherman's March to the Sea
Union General Sherman marches through Georgia, destroying Confederate infrastructure and morale.
1865 · April 9
Lee surrenders at Appomattox
Confederate General Robert E. Lee surrenders to Union General Ulysses S. Grant. The war ends.
1865 · April 14
Lincoln assassinated
President Abraham Lincoln is shot by John Wilkes Booth — days after the war's end.
🏛️
Aftermath & Reconstruction
1865–1877

The war settled the question of slavery — the 13th Amendment (1865) abolished it. But rebuilding a divided nation proved enormously difficult.

Key Constitutional Amendments

AmendmentYearWhat it did
13th1865Abolished slavery throughout the United States
14th1868Granted citizenship and equal protection to all persons born in the US, including former slaves
15th1870Gave African American men the right to vote

Consequences for the United States

  • Preserved the Union — the US remained one country
  • Abolished slavery — the "peculiar institution" was ended
  • Federal power strengthened over the states
  • Industrial North triumphant — the US became a leading industrial power
  • Reconstruction (1865–1877) tried to rebuild the South and integrate freed slaves, but was eventually abandoned — leading to almost a century of segregation
📝 Global significance

The Civil War was a turning point in world history. It ended slavery in the largest slave-holding republic, and it set the United States on a path toward becoming a global power by 1900. The cotton that fed the British textile industry had come from Southern slave plantations — so the war also disrupted the global economy.

🏰
Decline of the Ottoman Empire
MoE Textbook §1.7

For centuries the Ottoman Empire had ruled southeastern Europe, the Middle East, and North Africa. But by the 19th century it was in steady decline — once a great power, now the "sick man of Europe."

Causes of Decline

📉
Military Decline

Could not keep pace with European military technology and organization.

💰
Economic Weakness

Did not industrialize; fell behind Europe economically.

🔥
Nationalist Uprisings

Greeks, Serbs, Bulgarians, Arabs, and others demanded independence.

⚖️
Weak Central Government

Sultans lost control over distant provinces; local leaders ruled as warlords.

🏛️
Corruption

The Ottoman bureaucracy became corrupt and inefficient.

🌍
Great Power Pressure

Russia, Britain, France, and Austria all wanted Ottoman territory or influence.

❓
The Eastern Question
What to do with a collapsing empire?
📌 The Eastern Question

The Eastern Question was the diplomatic and strategic problem of what would happen to the Ottoman Empire's territories as it declined — and which great powers would gain influence or control over them. It dominated European diplomacy from the 1820s to 1914.

The Great Powers' Interests

PowerWhat it wanted
RussiaControl of the Straits (Bosphorus & Dardanelles) for access to the Mediterranean; protection of Orthodox Christians; Slavic brothers in the Balkans
BritainPreserve the Ottoman Empire as a buffer against Russian expansion toward India and the Suez Canal
FranceProtection of Catholics in the Holy Land; influence in the Levant and North Africa
AustriaPrevent Russian expansion in the Balkans; control of its own Slavic populations
📝 Why it mattered

The Eastern Question was a diplomatic powder keg. Every crisis — Greek independence (1830), the Crimean War (1853–56), the Balkan Wars (1912–13) — threatened to drag the great powers into war. When World War I broke out in 1914, the Eastern Question was still unresolved — and the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo (in the Balkans) was the spark.

💥
The Crimean War (1853–1856)
The Eastern Question in flames

The Crimean War was the first major conflict of the Eastern Question — fought on the Crimean Peninsula in the Black Sea. Russia went to war against the Ottoman Empire, and Britain, France, and Sardinia joined on the Ottoman side to stop Russian expansion.

Key Features

  • First "modern" war — railways, telegraph, ironclad ships, and photography were used
  • Famous for mismanagement — the Charge of the Light Brigade, disease and supply failures
  • Florence Nightingale — pioneered modern nursing during this war
  • Peace of Paris (1856) — Russia lost territory and rights; the Black Sea was neutralized; the Ottoman Empire temporarily "protected"
📝 Consequences

The Crimean War weakened Russia, strengthened the Ottoman Empire temporarily, and exposed the weaknesses of the old powers. It also highlighted the growing importance of public opinion and war reporting. But it did not solve the Eastern Question — it merely postponed the next crisis.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.3
What you should remember
  • American Civil War (1861–1865) — caused by slavery, westward expansion, states' rights, economic differences, and Lincoln's election
  • 11 Southern states seceded and formed the Confederate States of America
  • Key turning points: Emancipation Proclamation (1863) and Battle of Gettysburg (1863)
  • Lee surrendered at Appomattox, April 1865; Lincoln assassinated days later
  • 13th Amendment (1865) abolished slavery; 14th (1868) granted citizenship; 15th (1870) gave Black men the vote
  • The war preserved the Union, ended slavery, and set the US on a path to becoming a global power
  • Ottoman decline caused by military weakness, economic backwardness, nationalist uprisings, weak government, corruption, and great power pressure
  • Eastern Question = the problem of what to do with Ottoman territories as the empire collapsed
  • Great powers' interests: Russia (Straits + Orthodox protection), Britain (buffer against Russia), France (Catholics + Levant), Austria (Balkans)
  • Crimean War (1853–1856) — the first major Eastern Question conflict; Russia vs. Ottoman + Britain + France + Sardinia
  • Peace of Paris (1856) — Russia lost territory; Black Sea neutralized
  • Florence Nightingale pioneered modern nursing during the Crimean War
  • The Eastern Question was unresolved when WWI broke out in 1914
