🌐 Ethiopia's Foreign Policy & Global Relations

Ethiopia has pursued an independent foreign policy since 1991, balancing relations with global powers while protecting its national interests and contributing to international peace and security.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Ethiopia's foreign policy is rooted in its long history of independence and its commitment to sovereignty. After 1991, the country adopted a foreign policy based on mutual respect, non-interference, and economic diplomacy. This lesson examines the principles guiding Ethiopia's foreign policy and its relations with major global powers.

📜
Principles of Ethiopia's Foreign Policy
Section 9.1
📌 Foreign Policy

A government's strategy in dealing with other nations, designed to safeguard national interests, security, and prosperity.

  • Sovereignty and Territorial Integrity: Protecting Ethiopia's borders and independence is the top priority.
  • Non-Interference: Ethiopia respects the internal affairs of other states and expects the same in return.
  • Economic Diplomacy: Attracting foreign investment, trade, and development cooperation to support economic growth.
  • Peaceful Coexistence: Maintaining good relations with neighbors and resolving disputes through dialogue.
  • Multilateralism: Active participation in the UN, AU, and other international organizations.
🌍
Relations with Global Powers
Section 9.1 (continued)
  • United States: A long-standing partner in development, health (PEPFAR), and security cooperation, though relations have seen tensions over governance and the Nile dispute.
  • China: A major investor in infrastructure (railways, roads, industrial parks) and a key trade partner. China built the Addis Ababa–Djibouti railway.
  • European Union: A significant development and trade partner, providing aid and preferential market access.
  • Russia: Historical ties dating back to the imperial era; renewed cooperation in energy and security.
  • Gulf States: Growing economic and security ties, particularly with the UAE and Saudi Arabia, including investment and labor migration.
  • United Nations: Ethiopia is a founding member and a top contributor to UN peacekeeping missions.
📌 Note

Ethiopia's foreign policy emphasizes non-alignment — avoiding permanent alliances with any single power while cooperating with all on mutual interests.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 9.1
What you should remember
  • Ethiopia's foreign policy prioritizes sovereignty and territorial integrity.
  • Non-interference and peaceful coexistence guide its relations with other states.
  • Economic diplomacy aims to attract investment and trade.
  • Ethiopia maintains relations with the US, China, EU, Russia, and Gulf states.
  • China financed the Addis Ababa–Djibouti railway, a symbol of growing ties.
  • Ethiopia follows a policy of non-alignment and is a major UN peacekeeping contributor.
