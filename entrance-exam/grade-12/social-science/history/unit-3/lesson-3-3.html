⛪ Religious Reforms & Power Struggle (1889–1916)

After Adwa, Ethiopia entered a period of intense political and religious transformation. This lesson explores the church-state relationship, the rise and fall of Lij Iyasu, and the emergence of Ras Teferi Mekonnen — the future Haile Selassie I.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 3 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The period between 1889 and 1916 was one of the most turbulent in modern Ethiopian history. Menelik II's later years saw him weakened by illness, and a power struggle emerged between the church, the nobility, and his chosen successor, Lij Iyasu. This lesson traces the religious and political transformations that shaped the transition from Menelik's era to the reign of Haile Selassie I.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Describe religious reforms under Yohannes IV and Menelik II
  • Explain the church-state relationship in Ethiopia
  • Analyze the rise and fall of Lij Iyasu
  • Understand the emergence of Ras Teferi (Haile Selassie I)
⛪
Religious Policies
MoE Textbook §3.6
📌 Church-State in Ethiopia

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church was the official state church. The emperor was considered the protector of the church and had significant influence over its leadership. Religious policy was therefore inseparable from political policy — a central feature of Ethiopian governance from the medieval period onward.

Yohannes IV's Religious Policy

  • Promoted Orthodox Christianity as the unifying religion of Ethiopia
  • Convened the Council of Boru Meda (1878) — settled a theological dispute within the church
  • Required Muslims in Wollo to convert or face consequences
  • His religious policies were aimed at unifying the empire but also alienated some groups

Menelik II's Religious Policies

  • Continued state support for the Orthodox Church
  • Allowed the church to expand into newly incorporated territories
  • Land grants to the church in southern regions
  • More tolerant of religious diversity than Yohannes — allowed Muslim communities to practice
  • Encouraged the construction of churches in the newly incorporated south
📝 The Church as a Political Tool

Religious policy was never just about faith — it was about political control. The church provided legitimacy to the emperor, education to the elite, and a network of institutions across the country. Controlling the church meant controlling the intellectual and spiritual life of the empire.

📜
Church & State in the Post-Adwa Era
Institutional transformation

Adwa strengthened both the Ethiopian state and the Orthodox Church. Menelik used the prestige of victory to reorganize the church and extend its authority into new territories. But this also created new tensions — between the church and the state, and between religious and regional identities.

Key Developments

📖
Church Expansion

New churches and monasteries were built in the south, extending Orthodox influence.

🏛️
Land and Wealth

The church received extensive land grants, becoming one of the largest landowners.

👑
Political Power

Church leaders became important political figures at the imperial court.

🌍
Foreign Relations

The church played a role in Ethiopia's diplomatic relations with other Orthodox states (Russia, Greece).

👤
The Rise and Fall of Lij Iyasu
1913–1916
📌 Lij Iyasu

Lij Iyasu was the grandson of Menelik II and the designated heir to the throne. He became de facto ruler in 1909 (when Menelik became incapacitated) and de jure emperor in 1913. His reign was cut short in 1916 when he was deposed by a coalition of nobles and church leaders.

Iyasu's Policies and Controversies

  • Religious tolerance — he was accused of favoring Islam and even of converting
  • Integration of Muslim communities — sought to bring them into the political mainstream
  • Anti-noble reforms — tried to reduce the power of the nobility
  • Foreign policy — sought closer ties with the Ottoman Empire and Germany (during WWI)
  • His policies alarmed the church and the nobility, who saw them as threats to their power

The 1916 Coup

  • A coalition of nobles and church leaders deposed Iyasu in September 1916
  • Zewditu (Menelik's daughter) was proclaimed Empress
  • Ras Teferi Mekonnen (later Haile Selassie I) was appointed Regent and Heir to the Throne
  • Iyasu was excommunicated and eventually imprisoned; he died in 1935
📝 Why Iyasu's fall mattered

The 1916 coup was a turning point in modern Ethiopian history. It marked the end of the Shewan dynasty's direct line and the beginning of the rise of Ras Teferi, who would become Haile Selassie I — arguably the most consequential Ethiopian ruler of the 20th century.

🌅
The Rise of Ras Teferi
1916–1930

Ras Teferi Mekonnen was a member of the Shewan nobility with a direct line to Menelik's family. After the 1916 coup, he became Regent — and over the next 14 years, he steadily consolidated power, eventually becoming emperor in 1930.

Timeline: Teferi's Rise

1916
Regent and Heir to the Throne
Teferi is appointed Regent after Iyasu is deposed.
1917
Zewditu crowned Empress
Menelik's daughter becomes the first female head of state in modern Ethiopian history.
1923
Ethiopia joins the League of Nations
Teferi oversees Ethiopia's entry into the international body.
1924
Tour of Europe
Teferi visits Europe, gaining exposure to modernization and international diplomacy.
1928
Crowned King (Negus)
After a power struggle with conservative nobles, Teferi is crowned King.
1930
Crowned Emperor Haile Selassie I
Zewditu dies, and Teferi is crowned Emperor — beginning a 44-year reign.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 3.3
What you should remember
  • The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church was the official state church — inseparable from political power
  • Yohannes IV promoted Orthodox Christianity and convened the Council of Boru Meda (1878)
  • Menelik II continued state support for the church but was more tolerant of religious diversity
  • Lij Iyasu — Menelik's grandson — became emperor in 1913 but was deposed in 1916
  • Iyasu's alleged pro-Islam policies and anti-noble reforms alarmed the church and nobility
  • The 1916 coup installed Zewditu as Empress and Ras Teferi as Regent
  • Ras Teferi rose steadily through the 1920s — joining the League of Nations (1923), touring Europe (1924), becoming King (1928)
  • In 1930, he was crowned Emperor Haile Selassie I
