🤝 Pan-Africanism & the OAU

Pan-Africanism was the idea that all African people, both on the continent and in the diaspora, share a common history and destiny. This lesson explores the movement and its political expression: the Organization of African Unity (OAU).

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The dream of a united Africa has deep roots. Pan-Africanism began as a movement among intellectuals in the Americas and Europe, but it found its most powerful expression in the independence movements of the 1950s and 1960s. In 1963, African leaders founded the Organization of African Unity (OAU) to promote unity and cooperation. The OAU was later replaced by the African Union (AU) in 2002.

🌍
The Origins of Pan-Africanism
From the Diaspora to the Continent
  • Early Roots: Pan-Africanism emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, led by figures like W.E.B. Du Bois and Marcus Garvey, who fought for the rights of Black people worldwide.
  • The 1945 Manchester Congress: Key African leaders like Kwame Nkrumah (Ghana) and Jomo Kenyatta (Kenya) participated, calling for the end of colonialism and the unity of Africa.
  • African Unity: The idea gained strength as newly independent nations sought to end colonialism in the rest of the continent and promote economic development.
🏛️
The Organization of African Unity (OAU)
Section 7.5
📌 The OAU

Founded in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, on May 25, 1963, by 32 independent African states. Its goals were to promote unity, eradicate colonialism, defend the sovereignty of African states, and encourage economic development.

  • Successes: The OAU played a major role in coordinating support for liberation movements in Angola, Mozambique, and South Africa. It also created the African Development Bank.
  • Failures: The OAU was often divided and ineffective in dealing with internal conflicts and human rights abuses by African dictators. It also struggled to prevent coups.
  • The African Union (2002): To improve on the OAU's weaknesses, the OAU was replaced by the African Union (AU), which has a stronger focus on human rights, conflict resolution, and regional integration.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 7.3
What you should remember
  • Pan-Africanism is the belief in the unity and common destiny of all African people.
  • Key leaders like Kwame Nkrumah and W.E.B. Du Bois drove the movement forward.
  • The OAU was founded in Addis Ababa in 1963 to promote unity and end colonialism.
  • While it supported liberation movements, the OAU was often divided and unable to stop dictatorships.
  • In 2002, the OAU was replaced by the African Union (AU) to build a stronger, more unified Africa.
