🗺️ Road to Independence & Politics in Independent Africa

The mid-20th century saw a wave of independence sweep across Africa, dismantling European empires. This lesson covers how African nations achieved independence and the political challenges they faced in building new states.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

In 1950, only four African countries were independent (Egypt, Ethiopia, Liberia, and South Africa). By 1970, over 40 African nations had gained their independence. However, the transition from colonial rule to self-government was not without struggle. The new nations faced massive challenges: artificial borders, ethnic divisions, lack of infrastructure, and the difficult task of nation-building.

✊
The Road to Independence
Section 7.1
  • Ghana (1957): Under Kwame Nkrumah, Ghana became the first sub-Saharan African country to gain independence, inspiring other movements.
  • Peaceful vs. Armed Struggle: Some nations (like Ghana and Nigeria) achieved independence through negotiation and strikes. Others (like Algeria and Kenya) faced violent, protracted wars against European settlers.
  • The 1960 "Year of Africa": 17 African nations gained independence in a single year.
  • Legacy of Colonialism: European powers had drawn arbitrary borders, grouping rival ethnic groups together and splitting others apart.
🏛️
Politics in Independent Africa
Section 7.2
  • One-Party States: Many newly independent nations quickly became one-party states. Leaders argued that this prevented ethnic division and promoted national unity.
  • Military Coups: From the 1960s onward, dozens of African nations experienced military coups. Leaders like Kwame Nkrumah (Ghana) and Patrice Lumumba (Congo) were overthrown or assassinated.
  • Dictatorship and Corruption: Many leaders (e.g., Mobutu Sese Seko in Zaire, Idi Amin in Uganda) became brutal dictators, enriching themselves while their citizens suffered.
  • National Identity: Governments struggled to build a unified national identity out of diverse ethnic groups, often leading to civil wars (e.g., the Nigerian Civil War).

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 7.1
What you should remember
  • Africa's independence was achieved through both peaceful negotiations and armed struggles.
  • Ghana led the way in 1957, and 17 nations gained independence in 1960.
  • New states faced colonial legacies like artificial borders that caused ethnic tension.
  • Many nations fell into one-party rule and military coups shortly after independence.
  • Kwame Nkrumah of Ghana was a key figure in the independence and Pan-African movements.
