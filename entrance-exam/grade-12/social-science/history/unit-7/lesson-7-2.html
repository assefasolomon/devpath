🌍 Economy, Society & the Cold War

While newly independent African nations struggled to build their economies and societies, the continent became a major battleground in the global Cold War between the USA and the USSR.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

After independence, African leaders promised rapid economic development and social progress. However, they were hampered by colonial economic structures that relied on exporting raw materials. At the same time, the USA and the USSR competed for influence in Africa, funding proxy wars and supporting dictatorships that aligned with their side.

💱
Economy & Society
Section 7.3
  • Neocolonialism: Even after independence, European powers and multinational corporations controlled much of Africa's wealth, trade, and resources.
  • Debt Crisis: In the 1970s and 1980s, many African nations borrowed heavily from Western banks. When the loans came due, they could not repay, leading to a debt crisis.
  • Structural Adjustment Programs (SAPs): The IMF and World Bank forced African nations to cut government spending, privatize industries, and open their markets to foreign competition in exchange for loans.
  • Social Issues: Rapid urbanization, the spread of diseases (like HIV/AIDS), and a lack of quality education and healthcare were major challenges.
☢️
The Cold War in Africa
Section 7.4
  • Proxy Wars: Africa became an arena for the USA and USSR to fight each other indirectly. The superpowers supported rival factions in civil wars and conflicts.
  • Angola and Mozambique: After independence from Portugal in 1975, these nations were torn apart by civil wars, with the USA and USSR backing opposing sides.
  • The Horn of Africa: Ethiopia (backed by the USSR) and Somalia (backed by the USA) went to war in 1977-78 over the Ogaden region.
  • Supporting Dictators: Both superpowers often supported brutal dictators as long as they were anti-communist or anti-capitalist.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 7.2
What you should remember
  • Neocolonialism left African economies dependent on former colonial powers.
  • The debt crisis led to Structural Adjustment Programs (SAPs) imposed by the IMF.
  • Africa became a battleground in the Cold War, with the US and USSR supporting rival groups.
  • Angola and Mozambique suffered devastating civil wars fueled by superpower rivalry.
  • The Ogaden War between Ethiopia and Somalia drew in the US and USSR.
