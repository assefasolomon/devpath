👑 Restoration of Imperial Rule & Socio-economic Conditions

After the Italian occupation, Haile Selassie returned to rebuild Ethiopia. This lesson covers the post-war restoration of the monarchy, the socio-economic landscape, and the growing political opposition that eventually led to the 1974 revolution.

📖 Reading: ~30 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

In 1941, Haile Selassie I returned to Addis Ababa after five years of Italian occupation. He immediately began restoring the imperial government. However, the post-war era was a time of both modernization and growing internal conflict. While cities grew and education expanded, the vast majority of the rural population remained poor, and the emperor's absolute power began to face opposition from students, workers, and a new generation of military officers.

👑
Restoration of Imperial Rule
Section 6.1
  • Post-War Restoration: Haile Selassie re-established the absolute power of the emperor, supported by the nobility and the Orthodox Church.
  • Eritrea Federation (1950): The UN federated Eritrea with Ethiopia. In 1962, Haile Selassie annexed Eritrea as a province, sparking a decades-long war for independence.
  • Political Dominance: The emperor dissolved the parliament's independence, centralizing all power in his hands.
  • The 1960 Coup Attempt: Led by Germame Neway and his brother Mengistu, an attempted coup failed but shocked the emperor and revealed deep discontent within the educated elite and the military.
🏫
Socio-economic Conditions
Section 6.2
  • The Land Question: Land was owned by the emperor, the nobility, and the church. Peasants were tenants facing heavy taxation and feudal obligations.
  • Education: Modern schools and the Haile Selassie I University (1961) were established. This created a new class of educated and politically aware youth.
  • Inequality: Modernization was concentrated in cities like Addis Ababa. The rural areas remained stagnant, leading to rural unrest.
  • The 1960s: Student movements (like the "Voice of the Student") began demanding land reform, equality, and an end to the monarchy.
⚔️
Consolidation of Autocracy & Oppositions
Section 6.3
  • Absolute Rule: Haile Selassie governed by decree. The 1955 Revised Constitution gave the appearance of democracy but kept all power with the emperor.
  • Growing Opposition: Several groups began to oppose the regime:
    • Students: Demanded land reform and political freedom.
    • Workers: Suffered from low wages and high inflation.
    • Military: Officers were frustrated with corruption, low salaries, and the emperor's inability to address the famine (especially the 1972-73 Wollo famine).
  • The 1972-73 Famine: A devastating drought in Wollo province killed over 200,000 people. The government's slow and secretive response further eroded its legitimacy.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 6.1
What you should remember
  • After 1941, Haile Selassie restored the imperial government but faced growing opposition.
  • He annexed Eritrea in 1962, sparking a long independence war.
  • The economy was dominated by a feudal land system, leaving peasants in poverty.
  • The 1960 coup attempt shocked the emperor and revealed deep discontent in the military.
  • The 1972-73 famine and the government's failure to respond fueled the 1974 revolution.
