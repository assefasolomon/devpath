🌡️ Concepts & Trends of Climate Change

Before we can address climate change, we must understand what it is and how it is measured. This lesson covers the basic concepts of climate change and the observable trends in the global climate system.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns. While some climate change is natural, human activities have been the main driver of climate change since the 1800s, primarily due to the burning of fossil fuels. Understanding the trends helps us predict future impacts.

📖
Basic Concepts of Climate Change
Section 2.1
📌 Climate vs. Weather

Weather refers to the short-term state of the atmosphere (e.g., rainy today). Climate is the average weather over a long period of time (e.g., a tropical climate).

  • Global Warming: The gradual increase in the Earth's average temperature due to the greenhouse effect.
  • Greenhouse Effect: A natural process where gases in the atmosphere trap heat from the sun, keeping the Earth warm. However, human activities have intensified this effect.
  • Greenhouse Gases (GHGs): The primary gases include Carbon Dioxide (CO2), Methane (CH4), Nitrous Oxide (N2O), and water vapor.
📈
Trends in Global Climate Change
Section 2.2
  • Rising Temperatures: The Earth's average surface temperature has risen by about 1.1°C since the pre-industrial era.
  • Melting Ice: Glaciers and polar ice sheets are melting at an accelerating rate.
  • Sea Level Rise: As ice melts and water warms (thermal expansion), global sea levels are rising, threatening coastal communities.
  • Extreme Weather: There is an increase in the frequency and intensity of extreme weather events like droughts, floods, and heatwaves.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 2.1
What you should remember
  • Weather is short-term, while climate is the long-term average.
  • The greenhouse effect is a natural process, but human activities have intensified it.
  • The primary greenhouse gas from human activity is Carbon Dioxide (CO2).
  • Global temperatures have risen, causing melting ice and rising sea levels.
