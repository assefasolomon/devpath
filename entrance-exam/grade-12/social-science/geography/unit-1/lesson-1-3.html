↔️ Plate Movements & Boundaries

The Earth's surface is constantly changing because tectonic plates are moving. Where these plates meet, they create three main types of boundaries: divergent, convergent, and transform.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The interaction between tectonic plates at their boundaries is where the majority of Earth's seismic and volcanic activity occurs. Geologists classify these boundaries based on how the plates move relative to each other.

↔️
Divergent Boundaries (Constructive)
Section 1.3
📌 Divergent Boundary

A boundary where two tectonic plates move away from each other. Magma rises from the mantle to fill the gap, creating new crust.

  • Process: As plates pull apart, the lithosphere stretches and thins. Magma rises, cools, and solidifies, forming new oceanic crust.
  • Features: Mid-ocean ridges (underwater mountain ranges), rift valleys (like the East African Rift), and volcanic activity.
  • Example: The Mid-Atlantic Ridge, where the North American and Eurasian plates are moving apart.
➡️⬅️
Convergent Boundaries (Destructive)
Section 1.3 (continued)
📌 Convergent Boundary

A boundary where two tectonic plates move toward each other and collide. The denser plate is usually forced underneath the other in a process called subduction.

  • Oceanic-Continental: The denser oceanic plate subducts beneath the continental plate. This forms deep ocean trenches and volcanic mountain ranges (e.g., the Andes).
  • Oceanic-Oceanic: One oceanic plate subducts beneath another, forming volcanic island arcs (e.g., Japan).
  • Continental-Continental: Neither plate sinks, so they crumple together, forming massive mountain ranges (e.g., the Himalayas).
↕️
Transform Boundaries (Conservative)
Section 1.3 (continued)
📌 Transform Boundary

A boundary where two tectonic plates slide past each other horizontally. Crust is neither created nor destroyed here.

  • Process: The plates grind against each other, building up immense friction and pressure.
  • Features: Earthquakes are very common here, as the pressure is suddenly released when the plates slip.
  • Example: The San Andreas Fault in California, where the Pacific and North American plates slide past each other.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.3
What you should remember
  • Divergent boundaries occur where plates move apart, creating new crust (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge).
  • Convergent boundaries occur where plates collide. Subduction creates trenches and volcanoes.
  • Continental-Continental collisions create massive mountain ranges like the Himalayas.
  • Transform boundaries occur where plates slide past each other, causing earthquakes (e.g., San Andreas Fault).
