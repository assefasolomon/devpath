⛰️ Major Geological Processes

Plate tectonics drives the major geological processes that constantly reshape the Earth's surface. These include earthquakes, volcanic activity, and the formation of mountains and rift valleys.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The movement of tectonic plates creates immense stress on the Earth's crust. When this stress is released, it results in powerful geological processes. Understanding these processes helps humanity predict natural hazards and minimize their damage.

🌋
Earthquakes
Section 1.4
📌 Earthquake

A sudden shaking of the ground caused by the release of energy stored in the Earth's crust, usually due to the movement of tectonic plates along faults.

  • Focus: The point inside the Earth where the earthquake originates.
  • Epicenter: The point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus.
  • Seismograph: The instrument used to record and measure the magnitude of earthquakes.
  • Impact: Earthquakes can cause widespread destruction, tsunamis (if underwater), and loss of life.
🌋
Volcanism
Section 1.4 (continued)
📌 Volcanism

The process by which magma (molten rock), gases, and ash erupt onto the Earth's surface through a vent or fissure.

  • Magma vs. Lava: Magma is molten rock beneath the surface; lava is magma that has erupted onto the surface.
  • Types of Volcanoes: Shield volcanoes (broad, gentle slopes from fluid lava) and composite/stratovolcanoes (steep, explosive from thick, sticky lava).
  • Location: Most volcanism occurs at plate boundaries, particularly at divergent boundaries (mid-ocean ridges) and convergent boundaries (subduction zones).
  • Impact: Volcanic soil is highly fertile, but eruptions can be deadly and disrupt air travel.
🏔️
Mountain Building & Rift Valleys
Section 1.4 (continued)
  • Orogeny (Mountain Building): The process of mountain formation, primarily caused by the collision of continental plates (e.g., the formation of the Himalayas).
  • Rift Valleys: Formed when tectonic plates pull apart on land. The crust stretches, thins, and drops down, creating a valley. (e.g., the East African Rift Valley).
  • Faulting: The breaking and shifting of rocks along a fracture (fault line) in the Earth's crust, often creating fault-block mountains.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.4
What you should remember
  • Earthquakes occur when stress along faults is released. The epicenter is on the surface above the focus.
  • Volcanism is the eruption of magma (underground) as lava (on the surface).
  • Rift valleys form where plates pull apart on land, like the East African Rift Valley.
  • Mountain building (orogeny) occurs when plates collide, like the Himalayas.
