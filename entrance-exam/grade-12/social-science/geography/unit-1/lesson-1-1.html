🌍 Continental Drift Theory

The Earth's continents were once joined together in a single landmass called Pangaea before drifting apart over millions of years. This lesson covers the theory proposed by Alfred Wegener and the geological evidence that supports it.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction to Continental Drift
What you will learn

The Continental Drift Theory suggests that the Earth's continents have moved over geologic time relative to each other, appearing to have "drifted" across the ocean bed. Proposed by German meteorologist Alfred Wegener in 1912, it explains how the present continents were formed from a single supercontinent called Pangaea.

🗺️
Pangaea and Its Breakup
Section 1.1
📌 Pangaea

A supercontinent that existed about 350 million years ago during the Carboniferous period, located around the South Pole. In the Triassic period of the Mesozoic era, it started to break into several parts.

  • Laurasia: The northern part of Pangaea, which later subdivided into North America, Europe, and Asia.
  • Gondwanaland: The southern part of Pangaea, which later subdivided into South America, Africa, India, Australia, and Antarctica.
  • Drifting: Through the process of drifting over millions of years, the continents reached their present locations.
🔍
Geological Evidences Favoring Continental Drift
Section 1.1 (continued)

Wegener used several pieces of evidence to support his theory:

  • Coastline Similarity: The coastlines of Africa and South America have remarkable similarity and fit together like pieces of a jigsaw puzzle.
  • Rock Type and Structural Similarities: Similar rock types of the same age extend from one continent to the other. Mountain belts also extend from one continent to the next.
  • Fossil Evidence: Fossils of identical organisms, such as the Mesosaurus (a small reptile that lived 250 million years ago), are found on both sides of the Atlantic Ocean in South America and Africa. This implies the continents were once joined.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.1
What you should remember
  • Alfred Wegener proposed the Continental Drift Theory in 1912.
  • All continents were once joined in a supercontinent called Pangaea.
  • Pangaea broke into Laurasia (north) and Gondwanaland (south).
  • Evidence includes coastline similarities, matching rock structures, and identical fossils (like Mesosaurus) across oceans.
