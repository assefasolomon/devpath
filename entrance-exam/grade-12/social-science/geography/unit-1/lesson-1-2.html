🔄 Plate Tectonics Theory

While continental drift explained that the continents moved, it didn't explain how. The theory of Plate Tectonics provides the mechanism: the Earth's outer shell is broken into massive plates that float on the semi-fluid mantle.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Plate tectonics is the unifying theory of geology. It explains that the Earth's lithosphere is broken into a mosaic of large, rigid slabs called tectonic plates. These plates move relative to one another, and their interactions at boundaries are responsible for most of the Earth's earthquakes, volcanic activity, and mountain building.

🪨
The Earth's Layers and the Lithosphere
Section 1.2
📌 The Lithosphere

The rigid outer layer of the Earth, consisting of the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle. It is broken into several large and small tectonic plates.

  • The Asthenosphere: The semi-fluid layer of the mantle beneath the lithosphere. The tectonic plates float and slide on top of this ductile layer.
  • Major Plates: The largest plates include the Pacific, North American, Eurasian, African, Antarctic, Indo-Australian, and South American plates.
  • Minor Plates: Smaller plates like the Arabian, Nazca, and Philippine plates also play a crucial role.
🌊
Mechanisms of Plate Movement
Section 1.2 (continued)

What drives the plates to move? Geologists identify three main driving forces:

  • Mantle Convection Currents: Heat from the Earth's core causes molten rock in the mantle to rise. As it reaches the top, it cools and sinks back down, creating a circular current that drags the plates along.
  • Ridge Push: At mid-ocean ridges, magma rises and forms new crust. This new crust is hot and elevated, creating a slope. Gravity causes the plate to slide away from the ridge.
  • Slab Pull: At subduction zones, a dense, cold oceanic plate sinks into the mantle. The weight of the sinking plate pulls the rest of the plate along with it. (This is considered the strongest driving force).

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.2
What you should remember
  • The Earth's outer shell is broken into rigid plates called the Lithosphere.
  • These plates move over the semi-fluid Asthenosphere.
  • Plate movement is driven by mantle convection currents, ridge push, and slab pull.
  • Slab pull is considered the strongest driving force.
