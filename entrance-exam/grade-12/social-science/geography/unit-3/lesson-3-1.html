🤝 The Concept of Sustainable Development

Sustainable development is about meeting our needs today without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs. This lesson covers the principles, pillars, and importance of sustainability.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The concept of sustainable development emerged in the late 20th century as a response to growing concerns about environmental degradation, poverty, and inequality. The most widely accepted definition comes from the 1987 Brundtland Report: "Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."

⚖️
Principles of Sustainability
Section 3.1
  • Intergenerational Equity: Ensuring that future generations have access to the same natural resources we have today.
  • Intragenerational Equity: Fair distribution of resources and opportunities within the current generation (reducing poverty).
  • Precautionary Principle: Taking preventive action when there is a risk of serious or irreversible harm, even without full scientific certainty.
  • Integration: Balancing economic, social, and environmental goals in decision-making.
🏛️
The Three Pillars of Sustainability
Section 3.1 (continued)
📌 The Three Pillars

Sustainable development rests on three interconnected pillars: Economic, Social, and Environmental.

  • Economic Pillar: Promoting economic growth, efficiency, and innovation while decoupling growth from resource depletion.
  • Social Pillar: Ensuring social justice, equity, health, education, and cultural preservation.
  • Environmental Pillar: Protecting natural resources, biodiversity, and reducing pollution and greenhouse gas emissions.

A truly sustainable society achieves a balance between all three pillars.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 3.1
What you should remember
  • Sustainable development meets present needs without compromising the future.
  • Intergenerational equity is about fairness to future generations.
  • Intragenerational equity is about fairness within the current generation.
  • The three pillars of sustainability are Economic, Social, and Environmental.
  • The Precautionary Principle means acting to prevent harm even without absolute certainty.
