🌿 Green Economy and Green Growth

A green economy delivers improved human wellbeing and social equity while significantly reducing environmental risks and ecological scarcities. This lesson explores green economy, green growth, and the sources of renewable energy that make them possible.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

The world faces a fundamental question: can economies grow while preserving the environment? The concepts of green economy and green growth answer yes — but only with a fundamental restructuring of how we produce and consume energy.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define green economy
  • Explain green growth and why it matters
  • Identify the sources of renewable energy
  • Explain why green economy is relevant to developing countries
🌿
What is a Green Economy?
MoE Textbook §8.3, p. 199
📌 Green Economy

A green economy is an economy that results in improved human wellbeing and social equity, while significantly reducing environmental risks and ecological scarcities.

It uses low carbon. It is resource efficient. And it is socially inclusive.

Three defining characteristics

  • Low carbon — minimal greenhouse gas emissions
  • Resource efficient — maximizing output per unit of resource used
  • Socially inclusive — benefits shared across society

How growth is driven in a green economy

In a green economy, economic growth is driven by public and private investments that:

  • Reduce carbon emissions and pollution
  • Enhance energy and resource efficiency
  • Prevent the loss of biodiversity and ecosystem services

The combination of investment and innovation underpins sustained growth and gives rise to new economic opportunities.

📈
Green Growth
MoE Textbook §8.3, p. 199
📌 Green Growth (OECD, 2011)

Green growth is defined as "fostering economic growth and development, while ensuring that natural assets continue to provide the resources and environmental services on which our wellbeing relies" (OECD, 2011).

Why the OECD justifies green growth

Growing risks to development exist because growth continues to erode natural capital. This erosion leads to:

  • Increased water scarcity
  • Worsening resource bottlenecks
  • Greater pollution
  • Climate change
  • Unrecoverable biodiversity loss

The link to renewable energy

Green growth occurs with the use of renewable energy, which links low carbon to green growth. The motive for using renewable energy emerges from the perceived scarcity of fossil fuels. Even without climate change, the potential for peak oil (rising demand and declining fossil fuel production) requires alternative methods of operation.

📝 Untapped potential

Given technological support, countries can use untapped renewable energy potential. Hence, the green economy is said to be relevant not only to advanced economies but also a key catalyst for growth and poverty eradication in developing countries.

🌍
Relevance to Developing Countries
Why green growth matters for Ethiopia and beyond

For developing countries, the green economy framework offers a path that doesn't force a trade-off between growth and environmental protection:

  • Investment and innovation create new industries and jobs
  • Renewable energy reduces dependence on imported fossil fuels
  • Resource efficiency lowers production costs
  • Environmental protection preserves the natural assets that the poor depend on for their livelihoods
📝 The core insight

A green economy is not a constraint on growth — it is a new growth model. It reflects the recognition that lasting prosperity depends on preserving the natural capital that all economic activity ultimately rests upon.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 8.3
  • Green economy = improved human wellbeing and social equity, reduced environmental risks and ecological scarcities
  • Three characteristics: low carbon, resource efficient, socially inclusive
  • Growth driven by public and private investments that reduce emissions, enhance efficiency, and protect biodiversity
  • Green growth (OECD, 2011) = fostering growth while ensuring natural assets continue to provide resources and services
  • Risks from eroding natural capital: water scarcity, resource bottlenecks, pollution, climate change, biodiversity loss
  • Renewable energy links low carbon to green growth
  • Motive for renewables: scarcity of fossil fuels + peak oil concern
  • Sources of renewable energy: solar, biomass, geothermal, hydroelectricity, tidal, wind
  • Green economy is relevant to both advanced and developing countries
  • For developing countries: green economy is a key catalyst for growth and poverty eradication
