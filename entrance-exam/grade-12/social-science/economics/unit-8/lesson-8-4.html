🇪🇹 Environment and Climate Change in Ethiopia

Ethiopia is one of the most vulnerable countries to climate change — facing recurrent drought, soil erosion, and land degradation. This final lesson explores the country's climate challenges and the ambitious national policies designed to meet them.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Ethiopia is an East African country with immense geographical diversity and topographical variation. The country is cited as one of the most vulnerable countries to future climate change. For much of the last 100 years, the country has faced recurrent drought, famine, and recourse to food aid.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain why Ethiopia is vulnerable to climate change
  • Identify Ethiopia's key environmental issues
  • Describe the National Adaptation Programme of Action (NAPA) and NAMA
  • Explain the Climate Resilient Green Economy (CRGE) strategy
  • Describe the Green Legacy Initiative
⚠️
Ethiopia's Vulnerability
MoE Textbook §8.4, p. 200

Ethiopia has faced recurrent drought and famine for much of the last 100 years. The country has been forced to rely on food aid repeatedly. Climate change is expected to worsen these challenges.

Key underlying causes of vulnerability (per NAPA)

  • Dependence on rain-fed agriculture
  • Poor water resource development
  • High population growth rate

Most vulnerable sectors

  • Agriculture
  • Health
  • Transport
  • Natural resources
  • Energy
  • Industry
🌱
Key Environmental Issues
Beyond drought

Apart from drought, soil erosion and land degradation are among the most critical and far-ranging environmental issues affecting Ethiopia — caused by increased crop cultivation in marginal areas and livestock grazing pressure.

Studies indicate that agricultural landscapes in parts of Ethiopia have undergone unprecedented changes in an unsustainable direction, manifested in:

  • Land degradation
  • Biodiversity loss
  • Low agricultural productivity

National Policies — NAPA and NAMA
MoE Textbook §8.4, p. 200
PolicyYearPurpose
NAPA — National Adaptation Programme of Action 2007 Assess key underlying causes of the country's vulnerability to climate change
NAMA — Nationally Appropriate Mitigation Actions 2011 Identify mitigation actions to reduce greenhouse gas emissions

These policy documents assessed the key underlying causes of the country's vulnerability to climate change and identified the most vulnerable sectors.

🌿
Climate Resilient Green Economy (CRGE) Strategy
MoE Textbook §8.4, p. 200
📌 CRGE Strategy (2011)

In 2011, the Ethiopian government initiated the Climate Resilient Green Economy (CRGE) strategy. The strategy envisioned carbon-neutral growth based on an energy-efficient development trajectory, with a view to addressing both climate change adaptation (climate resilience) and mitigation (green economy) objectives (FDRE, 2011).

Goals of CRGE

  • Achieve middle-income status by 2025
  • Raise GDP per capita to $1,000 USD
  • Carbon-neutral / low-carbon growth path
  • Energy-efficient development trajectory
📝 The double objective

CRGE addresses both sides of the climate challenge at once:
Adaptation (climate resilience) — preparing for climate impacts
Mitigation (green economy) — reducing emissions

🌳
Green Legacy Initiative
The tree-planting campaign
📌 Green Legacy Initiative (June 2020)

In June 2020, the Ethiopian Government launched the Green Legacy Initiative — a massive tree-planting campaign aimed at curbing the effects of climate change and deforestation.

Aims of the initiative

  • Preventing flooding
  • Preventing food insecurity
  • Preventing environment-related conflicts
  • Preventing other adverse effects of climate change and deforestation
📝 Impact

An estimated 4.1 billion trees were planted by August 2020, according to the Ethiopian government.

Responsible agency

The agency responsible for climate change in Ethiopia is the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change — a lead government body legally mandated for environmental management.
