⚖️ Fiscal Decentralization

Fiscal decentralization transfers taxing and spending powers from the central government to regional and local governments. This final lesson of Unit 7 explores how Ethiopia's federal system shares revenue and the trade-offs involved.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Ethiopia is a federal country with 11 regional states and 2 chartered cities. Fiscal decentralization determines how tax and spending powers are divided between the federal government and these subnational governments. This lesson explains the concept, its advantages and disadvantages, and how it works in Ethiopia.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define fiscal decentralization
  • Explain the concept of fiscal equalization
  • Describe Ethiopia's revenue-sharing system
  • Identify the advantages of decentralization
  • Identify the disadvantages of decentralization

What is Fiscal Decentralization?
MoE Textbook §7.3, p. 175
📌 Definition

Fiscal decentralization refers to the devolution of taxing and spending powers from central government authorities to government authorities at sub-national levels (regional, provincial, municipal, etc.). It deals with studies of which functions/expenditure and which instruments/revenue are best centralized and which are best placed at decentralized levels of government.

Centralization Ratio

Countries differ in how centralized they are. This can be measured by the centralization ratio:

Centralization ratio = Direct government expenditure by central government ÷ Total government expenditure

(Note: direct government expenditure excludes federal grants/transfers.)

🇪🇹
Ethiopia's Fiscal System
Revenue sharing and fiscal equalization

In Ethiopia, the federal government makes fiscal equalization payments to local governments with the stated goal of equalizing the fiscal capacity of local governments to provide services.

Purpose of Fiscal Equalization

  • Help regional governments provide similar sets of public services to citizens with similar tax incidence
  • Reduce or eliminate differences in net fiscal benefits — the difference between the utility households derive from consuming public services and the taxes they pay for producing these services
  • Companion of fiscal decentralization: ensures equity, efficiency, and stability of fiscal policy

Recent Budgetary Support

In the past ten years (2010–2020), additional budgetary support has been provided to regional governments to finance capital projects tied to the MDGs and SDGs. For instance, in 2017/18, Birr 7 billion was allocated to regional states per the grant formula for SDG implementation.

📝 Constitutional basis

Ethiopia's federal constitution (1995) allows for the revenue sharing principle: the federal government levies and collects taxes on revenue sources reserved to it (Article 51(10)); regional states levy and collect taxes on their reserved sources and administer their own budgets (Article 52(2(e))).

Advantages of Decentralization
Why decentralized systems work better in some ways

1. Tailoring output to local tastes

Peoples in different regions and communities have different tastes and preferences. A centralized government tends to provide the same level of public services throughout the country regardless of local differences. Under a decentralized system, communities provide the type and quantities of public goods their inhabitants desire. Local governments are closer to the people and more responsive to citizen preferences.

2. Fostering intergovernmental competition

Public managers often lack incentives to produce at minimum possible cost — because they can't go out of business like private firms. But if citizens can move between communities, this creates incentives for government managers to produce efficiently and be responsive to citizens so they can attract productive residents.

3. Experimentation and innovation

For many policy questions, no one is certain what the right answer is. Letting each community choose its own way and comparing results is one way to find out. The case in Ethiopia is different — one policy used across all regions. Therefore, purely decentralized or centralized systems cannot maximize social welfare. There is some optimal level of federalism.

⚠️
Disadvantages of Decentralization
Where decentralized systems struggle

1. Interstate/local externalities

There are costs and benefits of local government goods and services to residents of other jurisdictions. These externalities (positive or negative) create problems for the efficient operation of a federal system.

2. Loss of scale economies in provision of public goods

For certain public services, the cost per person may fall as the number of users increases. For example, the more people who use a public library, the lower the cost per user. If each community constructs its own library (decentralization), costs per user are higher than necessary. Consolidation is one way for communities to take advantage of scale economies.

3. Inefficient tax system

Efficient taxation requires a higher tax rate on inelastically demanded/supplied goods. Goods that are inelastic at the national level may be elastic at local levels. Some goods — like capital — are inelastic nationally but elastic locally. Local governments usually impose little tax on capital to promote investment — which is inefficient from a national perspective.

4. Loss of scale economies in tax collection

Individual communities may not take advantage of scale economies in collecting taxes. Each community has to devote resources to its own tax administration. Some savings could be made with a joint taxing authority.

5. Ineffectiveness of redistributive programs

Suppose the tax and expenditure pattern in a community is favorable to the poor — it transfers income to the poor. This attracts more poor to the region and pushes out the rich (taxpayers). Eventually, the region is left with a small tax base, which leads to abandonment of the program.

6. Coordination of stabilization policies

Most economists agree that spending and taxing decisions meant to stabilize (price stability, reduce unemployment) should be made by the central government. Local/state governments are too small to affect overall economic activity.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 7.3
  • Fiscal decentralization = devolution of taxing and spending powers from central to subnational governments
  • Centralization ratio = direct central government expenditure ÷ total government expenditure
  • Ethiopia makes fiscal equalization payments to regional governments
  • Goal: reduce differences in net fiscal benefits across regions, ensure equity/efficiency/stability
  • In 2017/18, Birr 7 billion was allocated to regional states for SDG implementation
  • Advantages: tailoring to local tastes · intergovernmental competition · experimentation and innovation
  • Disadvantages: interjurisdictional externalities · loss of scale economies (provision + collection) · inefficient tax systems · ineffective redistributive programs · coordination of stabilization policy
  • Redistribution at the local level is ineffective because it attracts the poor and pushes out the rich, shrinking the tax base
  • Stabilization decisions should be made by the central government
  • Pure centralization or pure decentralization — neither maximizes social welfare. There is an optimal level of federalism.
