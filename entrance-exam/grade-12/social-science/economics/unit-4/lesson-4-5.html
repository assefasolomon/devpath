💱 Foreign Exchange Policies

Every country's currency has a value relative to others. This final lesson of Unit 4 explores how exchange rates are determined, the difference between fixed and floating regimes, and Ethiopia's exchange rate journey from 1945 to today.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Most countries have their own currencies. When people trade, travel, or invest across borders, they need to convert from one currency to another. The market where these conversions happen is the foreign exchange market — and the price at which currencies trade is the exchange rate.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define foreign exchange policy and exchange rate
  • Distinguish fixed from floating exchange rate regimes
  • Explain devaluation, revaluation, depreciation, appreciation
  • Describe the impacts of exchange rate fluctuations on trade
  • List the key factors affecting exchange rates
  • Describe Ethiopia's exchange rate history
📌
What is Foreign Exchange Policy?
MoE Textbook §4.5, p. 89
📌 Foreign Exchange Policy (MoE Textbook, p. 89)

Foreign exchange policy is the foreign exchange regime — including floating, fixed, managed, etc. — that regulates foreign exchange transactions in the financial system. It's concerned with how the value of the domestic currency, relative to other currencies, is determined.

📌 Exchange Rate

An exchange rate is nothing more than a price — the price of one currency in terms of another currency. It can be analyzed with the tools of supply and demand.

📝 Why it matters

The policy of the exchange rate affects aggregate demand through its effect on export and import prices of tradable goods and services — which in turn influence other prices in the economy. Policymakers can use this connection to influence macroeconomic trends.

🌐
The Foreign Exchange Market
Where currencies trade

We call the market in which people or firms use one currency to purchase another currency the foreign exchange market. In this market, demand and supply are closely interrelated — because a person who demands one currency must at the same time supply another currency.

Four Groups in the Forex Market

🏭
Firms in International Trade

An Ethiopian firm exporting abroad earns US dollars but needs Birr to pay workers at home — it supplies dollars and demands Birr.

✈️
International Tourists

An American visiting Ethiopia supplies US dollars into the forex market and demands Ethiopian Birr.

📈
International Investors (Ownership)

Buying ownership or part ownership of a foreign firm.

💵
Financial Investors

Investments that don't involve ownership — bonds, deposits, etc.

📝 Exchange rate formula

Starting amount (original currency) ÷ Ending amount (new currency) = Exchange rate.
Example: 1 USD = 30 Birr → rate 0.033 (in USD per Birr) or 30 (in Birr per USD).

📌
Fixed Exchange Rate Policy
MoE Textbook §4.5, p. 90
📌 Fixed Exchange Rate

In a fixed exchange rate regime, the exchange rate is determined by the government's political and economic decisions. An example of a problem associated with this policy is the creation of a parallel market (black market).

Two Goals & Effects

⬇️
Undervalued Exchange Rate

Acts as an import tax and an export subsidy. Fixing the rate at an artificially low level promotes domestic industries by encouraging exports and discouraging imports. It can hurt other industries by raising the price of imported inputs.

⬆️
Overvalued Exchange Rate

Acts as an import subsidy and an export tax. Fixing the rate at an artificially high level benefits domestic consumers by encouraging imports and discouraging exports, decreasing the price of imported inputs.

Fluctuations Under Fixed Regime

TermMeaningCause
DevaluationAn increase in the exchange ratePolitical & economic decisions of the government
RevaluationA decrease in the exchange ratePolitical & economic decisions of the government
🌊
Floating Exchange Rate Policy
MoE Textbook §4.5, p. 90
📌 Floating Exchange Rate

A flexible/floating exchange rate is a regime where the currency price of a nation is set by the foreign exchange market based on supply and demand relative to other currencies. Exchange rate determination is left for market forces.

Fluctuations Under Floating Regime

TermMeaningCause
DepreciationAn increase in the exchange rateMarket forces
AppreciationA decrease in the exchange rateMarket forces
In a floating exchange rate regime, the exchange rate (R₀) and quantity (Q₀) are determined where the supply of Birr meets the demand for Birr.
📊
Impacts of Exchange Rate Fluctuation
MoE Textbook §4.5, p. 91
📈
Devaluation / Depreciation

Improves the current account balance and/or overall balance of payment by making exports cheaper and imports more expensive. Foreigners find exports cheaper; residents find imports more expensive.

📉
Revaluation / Appreciation

Worsens the external balance by making exports more expensive and imports cheaper. Foreigners find exports more expensive; residents find imports cheaper.

📝 The general rule

An appreciation raises the relative price of the country's exports and decreases the relative price of its imports. Depreciation has the opposite effect.

🔍
Key Factors Affecting Exchange Rates
What moves the currency?
FactorEffect on Currency
Inflation rates Lower inflation → currency appreciates. Higher inflation → currency depreciates (usually with higher interest rates).
Interest rates Higher interest rates → attract foreign capital → currency appreciates.
Balance of payments Current account deficit → spending more on imports than earning from exports → currency depreciates.
Government debt High debt → foreign investors sell bonds → currency value decreases.
Terms of trade Export prices rise faster than import prices → higher revenue → higher demand for currency → appreciates.
Political stability Stable politics → attracts foreign investment → currency appreciates. Unrest → depreciates.
Recession Interest rates fall → less foreign capital → currency weakens.
Speculation If investors expect currency to rise, they demand more → currency rises.

Exchange Rate Structure in Ethiopia

📝 The gradualism principle

Ethiopia's approach has been gradualism in liberalization — slowly moving from fixed to floating. In 1992, the currency was devalued from 2.07 to 5.00 Birr/USD, then gradually liberalized through auction systems and finally the interbank market. Today, the exchange rate is determined through an interbank foreign exchange market on a daily basis.

Advantages & Disadvantages
Fixed vs floating regimes

Fixed Exchange Rate

✅ Advantages❌ Disadvantages
Certainty — firms always know the exchange rate → less risky trade and investment Unable to respond to shocks — no mechanism to quickly respond to BOP crises
No speculation if people believe the rate will stay fixed Reserve problems — requires large foreign exchange reserves
Constraint on government policy — prevents extreme or irresponsible macro policies Speculation — if markets believe a revaluation/devaluation is coming, speculation can cost the government reserves
Deflationary bias — countries with BOP deficits may deflate, hurting others
Policy conflicts — may conflict with growth, inflation, unemployment targets

Floating Exchange Rate

✅ Advantages❌ Disadvantages
Protection from external shocks — rate adjusts automatically Instability — large fluctuations cause uncertainty for firms
Lack of policy constraints — free to pursue domestic policies No constraints on domestic policy — may pursue inappropriate policies
Correction of BOP deficits — depreciation restores competitiveness Speculation — causes exchange rate changes unrelated to trade fundamentals

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 4.5
What you should remember
  • Foreign exchange policy = the regime governing how currency value is determined
  • Exchange rate = price of one currency in terms of another
  • Fixed regime: government-determined; fluctuations = devaluation (↑) and revaluation (↓)
  • Floating regime: market-determined; fluctuations = depreciation (↑) and appreciation (↓)
  • Depreciation/devaluation: exports cheaper, imports more expensive → improves current account
  • Appreciation/revaluation: exports more expensive, imports cheaper → worsens current account
  • Key factors affecting exchange rates: inflation, interest rates, BOP, government debt, terms of trade, political stability, recession, speculation
  • Ethiopia's exchange rate history: 1945 (2.48 Birr/USD) → 1992 devaluation to 5.00 → 1993 auction system → 1998 interbank market → today floating
  • Ethiopia follows a policy of gradualism in liberalizing the exchange rate
  • Fixed regimes provide certainty but lack flexibility; floating regimes adapt but cause uncertainty
