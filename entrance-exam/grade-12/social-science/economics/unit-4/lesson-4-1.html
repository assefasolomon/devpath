🎯 Definition & Types of Macroeconomic Policies

The economy doesn't always work smoothly — sometimes it's in recession, sometimes it's overheating with inflation. This lesson introduces the four main instruments governments use to stabilize the economy: fiscal, monetary, income, and foreign exchange policy.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Macroeconomic analysis deals with the behaviour of the economy as a whole — output, income, employment, price level, and other aggregate variables. To bring about desirable changes in these variables, nations adopt various macroeconomic policies. These policies and their instruments vary from one economy to another and according to prevailing economic conditions.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define macroeconomic policy instruments
  • List the general objectives of macroeconomic policy
  • Identify the four main types of macroeconomic policies
  • Distinguish the tools of each policy type
📊
General Objectives of Macroeconomic Policy
MoE Textbook §4.1, p. 72
📌 Macroeconomic Policy

Macroeconomic policy refers to the deliberate actions taken by a government or central bank to influence the economy's overall performance. The economy does not always work smoothly — fluctuations occur in the level of economic activity, and macroeconomic policies aim to smooth these fluctuations.

Two Economic Problems

📉
Recession

National income, output, and employment are far below full potential. Idle factories, rising unemployment, excess capital stock.

🔥
Overheating / Inflation

The economy is "overheated" — rising prices (inflation) occur. Free market economies show instability.

The Six General Objectives

  • Maximum feasible output — producing as much as possible
  • High rate of economic growth — raising saving, investment, and capital accumulation
  • Full employment — every willing worker finds a job
  • Price stability — controlling inflation and deflation
  • Equality in the distribution of income and wealth
  • A healthy balance of payments
📝 Why this matters

Classical economists believed an automatic mechanism would restore stability. But the 1930s Great Depression showed no such mechanism works. That's why Keynes argued for government intervention using appropriate tools of macroeconomic policy.

🔢
Four Types of Macroeconomic Policies
The four tools available
The four macroeconomic policy instruments: fiscal, monetary, income, and foreign exchange policy.
🏛️
Fiscal Policy
Government spending & taxation
📌 Fiscal Policy

Fiscal policy is the expenditure and revenue (tax) policy of the government to achieve desired objectives. It can be expansionary (to boost demand) or contractionary (to reduce demand) depending on prevailing economic conditions.

Two Key Tools

💰
Taxation

Direct and indirect taxes, capital gains — affect consumer income and hence real GDP.

💳
Government Spending

Welfare programmes, salaries, subsidies, infrastructure — has the power to raise or lower real GDP.

📝 Full details in Lesson 4.2

We'll cover the types of fiscal policy (expansionary vs contractionary) and their effects on the AD-AS model in Lesson 4.2.

💵
Monetary Policy
Money supply & interest rates
📌 Monetary Policy

Monetary policy is the process of drafting, announcing, and implementing the plan of actions taken by the National Bank (or other monetary authority) that controls the quantity of money in an economy and the channels by which new money is supplied.

The National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) is the government agency that oversees the banking system and conducts monetary policy. In the US, the equivalent is the Federal Reserve System.

Three Key Tools

📊
Open Market Operations (OMO)

Purchase and sale of government bonds by the National Bank — changes the monetary base and money supply.

🏦
Discount Rate (DR)

The interest rate the National Bank charges banks to borrow funds. In Ethiopia, the discount window facility started in March 2001.

📋
Required Reserve Ratio (RRR)

Funds banks must retain as a proportion of deposits. Lowering RRR releases more capital for loans; raising it curtails lending.

📝 NBE in action

The National Bank of Ethiopia cut the minimum deposit reserve from 15% to 10% in January 2012, and cut the liquid assets-to-deposits ratio to 20% from 25% — aimed at encouraging banks to lend, especially to the export sector.

💼
Income Policy
Wage & price controls
📌 Income Policy

Income policies are economy-wide wage and price controls, most commonly instituted as a response to inflation, and usually seeking to establish wages and prices below free market level.

Income policies vary from "voluntary" wage and price guidelines to mandatory controls such as price/wage freezes. They complement expansionary monetary and fiscal policies.

Two Main Tools

💰
Minimum Wages

A legally mandated price floor on hourly wages — the minimum amount an employer is required to pay.

📊
Price Ceilings & Floors

A price ceiling = maximum price (causes shortage). A price floor = minimum price (causes surplus).

📝 Real-world example

The most important example of a price floor is the minimum wage. Many agricultural goods also have price floors imposed by governments to protect farmers.

💱
Foreign Exchange Policy
Managing currency value
📌 Foreign Exchange Policy

Foreign exchange policy is concerned with how the value of the domestic currency, relative to other currencies, is determined. It includes the foreign exchange regime — floating, fixed, managed, etc.

The exchange rate affects aggregate demand through its effect on export and import prices of tradable goods and services, which in turn influences other prices in the economy.

Two Main Types

📌
Fixed Exchange Rate

Determined by government's political and economic decisions. Devaluation = increase in exchange rate; revaluation = decrease.

🌊
Floating Exchange Rate

Determined by market forces of supply and demand. Depreciation = increase in exchange rate; appreciation = decrease.

📝 Currency terms

Devaluation / Depreciation — the currency becomes less valuable (exports cheaper, imports more expensive)
Revaluation / Appreciation — the currency becomes more valuable (exports more expensive, imports cheaper)

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 4.1
What you should remember
  • Macroeconomic policy = deliberate actions to influence the economy's overall performance
  • Two problems: recession (below full employment) and overheating/inflation
  • Six objectives: max output, growth, full employment, price stability, income equality, healthy balance of payments
  • Four policy types: fiscal, monetary, income, foreign exchange
  • Fiscal policy = government expenditure + taxation (controlled by government)
  • Monetary policy = money supply + interest rates (controlled by National Bank)
  • Income policy = wage and price controls (minimum wage, price ceilings/floors)
  • Foreign exchange policy = currency value management (fixed vs floating)
  • Fixed: devaluation / revaluation (government decisions); Floating: depreciation / appreciation (market forces)
