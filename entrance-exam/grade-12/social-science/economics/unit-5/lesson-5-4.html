📊 Types of Tax & Tax Accounting in Ethiopia

Ethiopia's tax system is organized into five schedules — A, B, C, D, and E — plus several indirect taxes. This lesson covers all of them, including the actual employment income tax rate tables from Ethiopian law.

📖 Reading: ~30 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

According to the Tax Proclamation No. 286/2002 and Regulations No. 78/2002 of Ethiopia, income is taxed under four schedules plus an exempt category. Together with several indirect taxes, this forms the complete Ethiopian tax system.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Identify the 5 schedules of Ethiopian income tax
  • Explain Schedule A — employment income tax (with rates)
  • Explain Schedule B — rental income tax
  • Explain Schedule C — business income tax
  • Explain Schedule D — other income
  • Explain Schedule E — exempt income
  • Describe the main indirect taxes in Ethiopia
  • Explain tax accounting

The 5 Tax Schedules
MoE Textbook §5.4, p. 112
📌 Ethiopia's Income Tax Schedules

According to the Tax Proclamation No. 286/2002 and Regulations No. 78/2002, there are four schedules of income in addition to exempt income:

ScheduleType of Income
Schedule AIncome from employment
Schedule BIncome from rental of buildings
Schedule CIncome from business
Schedule DOther income (royalties, dividends, interest, etc.)
Schedule EExempt income
🅰️
Schedule A: Employment Income Tax
The most common tax for workers
A
Schedule A — Employment Income
Taxed at rates from 10% to 35%

Employment income tax is withheld by employers and is a final tax — meaning employees earning income exclusively from employment are not required to declare income to tax authorities.

What Counts as Employment Income?

  • Salary, wages, allowances, bonus, commission, gratuity, or other remuneration (past, current, or future employment)
  • The value of fringe benefits received by an employee
  • Amounts received on termination of employment — including redundancy compensation or a golden handshake payment

Tax Computation with Deductions

To calculate tax quickly, use the deduction method: EIT = (Income × Rate) − Deduction

📝 Worked example — 10,000 Birr monthly income

Income falls in the 7,801–10,900 bracket (30% rate, deduction = 955).
EIT = (10,000 × 30%) − 955 = 3,000 − 955 = 2,045 Birr

📝 What about 15,000 Birr income?

Falls in the top bracket (35%, deduction = 1,500).
EIT = (15,000 × 35%) − 1,500 = 5,250 − 1,500 = 3,750 Birr

🅱️
Schedule B: Rental Income Tax
Tax on building rentals
B
Schedule B — Rental Income
Taxed at 25% of taxable income

Rental income tax is imposed for each tax year on a person renting out a building or buildings who has taxable income for the year.

The taxable income for a tax year = gross rental income for the year − total allowed deductions for the year.

  • Rate: 25% of taxable rental income
  • Frequency: Annual
  • Deductions: Certain specified expenses can be deducted before calculating tax
🅲
Schedule C: Business Income Tax
Tax on business profits
C
Schedule C — Business Income
30% for bodies, 10–35% progressive for individuals

Business income tax is imposed for each tax year. The taxable income for a tax year = total business income − total deductions allowed to the taxpayer for the year.

Type of TaxpayerRate
Bodies (corporations)30% flat
Individuals (sole proprietors)Progressive 10% – 35%
📝 Frequency

Business income tax is filed and paid annually with monthly advance payments.

🅳
Schedule D: Other Income
Catch-all category
D
Schedule D — Other Income
A catch-all for income not in Schedules A, B, or C

Schedule D includes various types of income that don't fit into the other schedules.

What Falls Under Schedule D?

  • Income from royalties
  • Income paid for services rendered outside of Ethiopia
  • Income from games (gambling, lotteries, etc.)
  • Dividends
  • Income from casual rental of property (not buildings — that's Schedule B)
  • Interest income
  • Specified non-business capital gains
🅴
Schedule E: Exempt Income
Income not subject to tax
E
Schedule E — Exempt Income
Income types that are legally exempt from tax

Certain types of income are exempted from tax by law to serve social, health, or equity purposes.

Exempt Income Items

  • The cost of medical treatment of employees
  • Hardship allowance
  • Salary paid to domestic servants
  • Maintenance or child support payments
  • Travelling expenses paid to employees recruited from elsewhere than the place of employment
  • Pension contribution
  • Payments made to a person as compensation
📈
Indirect Taxes in Ethiopia
Taxes on consumption

Apart from the direct income taxes (Schedules A–E), the Ethiopian government also levies several indirect taxes:

🛒
Value Added Tax (VAT)

Rate: 15%. Introduced 1 January 2003. A destination-based (consumption) tax — only the consumption of goods and services is taxed.

🔄
Turnover Tax (TOT)

An equalization tax for persons not registered for VAT. Rates: 2% on goods, 10% on services. Threshold: annual transactions ≤ 500,000 Birr.

🎯
Excise Tax

Imposed on selected goods (luxury and demand-inelastic items). Applies to 19 groups of items and 378 goods. Rate range: 5% – 500%.

➕
Surtax

An additional 10% tax on imported goods except fertilizers, petroleum, motor vehicles, aircraft, and capital goods.

💼
Withholding Tax

All bodies and specified sole proprietors deduct 2% of transaction value on domestic transactions and remit monthly.

🏛️
Stamp Duty

A tax raised by requiring stamps sold by the government to be affixed to designated documents.

📦
Customs Duty

Tax on imported or exported goods. Best instrument to prevent or reduce importation. Can reach rates of 100% or above.

👴
Pension Contribution

Employer pays 11%; employee pays 7% of salary to the Private Organizations Pension Fund.

Tax Accounting
MoE Textbook §5.4, p. 117
📌 Tax Accounting

Tax accounting is the practice of calculating financial statements and figures used to make tax calculations. Tax and accounting are two separate entities that are also linked — all taxation involves accounting processes.

Who Uses Tax Accounting?

  • Individuals — focus on income, qualifying deductions, and investment gains or losses
  • Businesses — need to keep up-to-date financial records to determine tax liabilities
  • Other entities — partnerships, corporations, non-profits
📝 Ethiopia example

Individuals with employment in different public establishments in Ethiopia get their income tax paid by their employers. Businesses must keep financial records to help determine their tax liabilities.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 5.4
What you should remember
  • Ethiopia has 5 income tax schedules: A (employment), B (rental), C (business), D (other), E (exempt)
  • Schedule A — Employment income tax at 0–35%, withheld by employers, final tax
  • Employment income includes salary, wages, bonus, fringe benefits, and termination payments
  • EIT = (Income × Rate) − Deduction
  • Schedule B — Rental income at 25% of taxable income
  • Schedule C — Business income: 30% for bodies, progressive 10–35% for individuals
  • Schedule D — Other income: royalties, dividends, interest, capital gains
  • Schedule E — Exempt income: medical treatment, pensions, child support
  • Indirect taxes: VAT (15%), TOT (2%/10%), Excise (5–500%), Surtax (10%), Withholding (2%), Stamp Duty, Customs
  • Pension: employer pays 11%, employee pays 7%
  • Tax accounting = calculating financial statements for tax purposes
