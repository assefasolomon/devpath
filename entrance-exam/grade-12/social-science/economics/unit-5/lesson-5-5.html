⚠️ Problems of Taxation in Ethiopia

Ethiopia's tax system has many strengths — but also real challenges. This final lesson of Unit 5 explores the major problems: non-compliance, avoidance, evasion, bureaucracy, and the underground economy.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Ethiopia's tax system faces diverse problems — from tax non-compliance to bureaucratic inefficiency. Understanding these problems is the first step toward fixing them. This lesson maps the full landscape of tax challenges in Ethiopia and developing countries more broadly.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define tax compliance and non-compliance
  • Distinguish tax avoidance from tax evasion
  • Identify the major administrative problems in tax systems
  • Explain the underground economy and why it exists
  • Describe suggested remedies to tax problems
✅
Tax Compliance
MoE Textbook §5.5, p. 117
📌 Tax Compliance

Tax compliance refers to the degree to which taxpayers comply with the tax laws. It is the extent to which taxpayers accurately report their income, calculate their tax liability correctly, and pay on time.

📌 Tax Non-Compliance

Tax non-compliance takes two main forms: tax avoidance and tax evasion. These represent the two ways taxpayers can reduce or eliminate their tax obligations.

Taxpayer behaviour ranges from full compliance (left) through legal avoidance (middle) to illegal evasion (right).
⚠️
Tax Avoidance
Legal but problematic
📌 Tax Avoidance

Tax avoidance refers to arranging one's affairs so as to minimize the tax burden. It entails taking full advantage of the provisions of the tax code or schedule to reduce one's tax obligations.

  • An example is a reduction of one's tax burden through exemptions, deductions, or incentives approved in the tax schedule
  • From the perspective of law, tax avoidance is NOT illegal
  • However, it poses problems for tax administration — reduces revenue and undermines fairness
📝 The gray zone

Tax avoidance sits in a legal gray zone. It's technically permitted, but it's often against the spirit of the tax law. Many countries have introduced "anti-avoidance" rules to curb this practice.

❌
Tax Evasion
Illegal and punishable
📌 Tax Evasion

Tax evasion is falsifying information on a tax return to reduce one's tax liability, or not filing at all — failing to pay legally due taxes. This is illegal.

Why Tax Evasion Happens

Tax evasion is rooted in underground economic activities that exist for at least two reasons:

  • Because certain activities are illegal, and individuals don't want records of those activities having taken place
  • Because high marginal tax rates give people an incentive to obtain income without reporting it
📝 Consequences

Tax evasion reduces government revenue, shifts the tax burden to compliant taxpayers, and creates unfair competition between honest and dishonest businesses.

📋
Tax Avoidance vs Tax Evasion
The critical difference
Aspect Tax Avoidance Tax Evasion
Legality Legal — permitted by tax code Illegal — punishable by law
Method Uses exemptions, deductions, incentives Falsifies information, doesn't file, fails to pay
Intent Minimizes tax within legal means Avoids tax by illegal means
Consequences Reduces revenue; poses administrative problems Criminal penalties; undermines the entire tax system
Example Investing in a tax-exempt bond Underreporting income or not filing a return
📝 Memory trick

Avoidance = Allowed (legal)
Evasion = Eillegal (illegal)

🏢
Administrative Problems in Tax Systems
Beyond compliance

The major criticisms of tax systems around the world have focused on the complexity of their administration and the difficulty for taxpayers to understand and comply. These problems cause taxpayers to incur costs to correctly calculate their tax liability.

Problems in Developing Countries (Including Ethiopia)

🧩
Complexity of the Tax System

Tax codes are complex, with many provisions that taxpayers struggle to understand and apply correctly.

💼
Perception of Corruption

When taxpayers perceive corruption in tax administration, they are less willing to comply voluntarily.

💰
Cost of Compliance

Complying with tax rules requires time, money, and expertise — especially burdensome for small businesses.

⚖️
Fairness Perception

When taxpayers believe the system is unfair, they are less likely to comply voluntarily.

📚
Tax Knowledge

Low tax knowledge has a negative effect on voluntary tax compliance. Education is key.

📈
Growing Workload

Tax administrations face a steadily growing workload with limited resources.

📜
Complex Fiscal Legislation

Constantly changing tax laws make compliance even harder.

🎯
Customer Service Gap

In developing countries, taxpayer services often need modernization to improve efficiency.

📝 The "underground economy"

These problems encourage the growth of unreported transactions in the underground economy (tax evasion) and the growth of demand for tax shelters (tax avoidance). The underground economy is a major reason why developing countries struggle to raise sufficient tax revenue.

🛠️
Suggested Remedies
How to improve tax collection
🤝
Modernize Customer Service

In developed countries, one tool for improving efficiency in tax administration is to modernize customer services — based on new management techniques with "client orientation" or "customer orientation".

📚
Improve Tax Education

Higher tax knowledge has a positive effect on voluntary tax compliance. Public awareness campaigns are essential.

💻
Digitize Tax Systems

Electronic filing and payment reduce opportunities for evasion, lower compliance costs, and speed up processing.

⚖️
Ensure Fairness & Transparency

Taxpayers are more willing to pay when they perceive the system as fair and free from corruption.

🛡️
Strengthen Enforcement

Strict but fair enforcement of tax laws discourages evasion and increases compliance.

📊
Simplify Tax Structure

Simpler tax codes reduce compliance costs and make it easier for taxpayers to comply correctly.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 5.5
What you should remember
  • Tax compliance = the degree to which taxpayers comply with tax laws
  • Two forms of non-compliance: avoidance and evasion
  • Tax avoidance = arranging one's affairs to minimize tax burden — legal but problematic
  • Examples of avoidance: exemptions, deductions, tax-exempt bonds
  • Tax evasion = falsifying information or not filing — illegal
  • Tax evasion is rooted in the underground economy: illegal activities + high marginal tax rates
  • Administrative problems: complexity, corruption, cost of compliance, fairness perception, low tax knowledge, growing workload
  • Customer service modernization is a key tool in developed countries
  • Remedies: customer orientation, tax education, digitization, fairness, enforcement, simplification
  • A good tax system needs to balance compliance, fairness, and efficiency
