🏛️ Tax System and Structure in Ethiopia

Ethiopia is a federal country — and its tax system reflects that structure. In this lesson, you'll learn how tax powers are shared between the federal and regional governments, who collects taxes, and the legal framework behind it all.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Ethiopia is a federal country — and its tax system follows the same structure. Tax powers are shared between the federal government and the regional states, as established by the Ethiopian Constitution.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Describe the historical background of Ethiopian taxation
  • Explain the role of the Ministry of Revenues (MoR)
  • Identify the legal framework for taxation
  • Describe the federal government's tax powers
  • Describe the regional governments' tax powers
  • Name Ethiopia's 11 regional states + 2 autonomous administrations
📜
Historical Background
MoE Textbook §5.3, p. 111

Ethiopia introduced the concept of income taxation in 1944 when the Emperor issued a decree requiring all peasants to pay one-tenth of their agricultural products to tax officials.

📝 Since then...

The tax regimes have changed with associated changes in governments. Each regime — Imperial, Derg, and FDRE — has adapted the tax system to its economic philosophy.

Ethiopian tax timeline: 1944 income tax introduced → 1974 Derg → 1992 reform → 1995 FDRE Constitution.
🏢
The Ministry of Revenues (MoR)
The federal tax authority
📌 Ministry of Revenues (MoR)

Currently, the government agency responsible for tax collection is the federal Ministry of Revenues (MoR). The MoR prepares decrees, directives, and regulations on fiscal policy matters and submits them for parliament to legislate.

Responsibilities of MoR

  • Prepare decrees, directives, and regulations on fiscal policy matters
  • Submit them to parliament for legislation
  • Collect federal taxes and duties
  • Administer federal tax law
📖
Legal Framework for Taxation
The two foundational documents
📜
Proclamation No. 33/1992

Provided the initial legal basis for Ethiopia's current tax system after the change of government in 1991.

🇪🇹
FDRE Constitution (1995)

The Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia Constitution — promulgated the sharing of revenue between federal and regional governments.

📝 Federal system

As a federal country, the Ethiopian Constitution allows for the revenue sharing principle of federal and regional governments. This means the power to tax is divided between two levels of government.

🏛️
Federal Government Tax Powers
Constitution — Article 51(10)
📌 Article 51(10) of the FDRE Constitution

The Federal government shall levy taxes and collect duties on revenue sources reserved to the federal government. Moreover, it draws up, approves, and administers the federal government's budget.

Federal Revenue Sources

  • Customs duties on imported goods
  • Income tax on federal government employees
  • Corporate income tax (on large businesses)
  • Value Added Tax (VAT) — shared with regions
  • Excise taxes on selected goods
  • Taxes on federal services and properties
🗺️
Regional Government Tax Powers
Constitution — Article 52(2(e))
📌 Article 52(2(e)) of the FDRE Constitution

The regional states shall levy and collect taxes and duties on revenue sources reserved to the states and draw up and administer their respective state budgets.

Regional Revenue Sources

  • Land use fees and agricultural income tax
  • Rental income tax on buildings
  • Business income tax (on smaller businesses)
  • Rural land use fees
  • Regional service fees
  • Taxes on regional government employees
📝 Ethiopia's regional structure

As of April 2022, Ethiopia has 11 regional states and 2 autonomous administrations — Addis Ababa and Dire Dawa Cities.

🏗️
Ethiopia's Tax Structure at a Glance
Federal vs regional summary
Ethiopia's dual tax structure: federal government collects major taxes, regional states collect regional revenues.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 5.3
What you should remember
  • Ethiopia introduced income taxation in 1944 — one-tenth of agricultural products
  • The federal Ministry of Revenues (MoR) is responsible for tax collection
  • Legal framework: Proclamation No. 33/1992 + FDRE Constitution (1995)
  • Article 51(10) — federal government levies taxes on federal revenue sources
  • Article 52(2(e)) — regional states levy taxes on regional revenue sources
  • Federal taxes: customs, corporate income, VAT, excise, federal employee tax
  • Regional taxes: land use fees, rental income, business income, regional service fees
  • Ethiopia has 11 regional states + 2 autonomous administrations (Addis Ababa, Dire Dawa)
  • The system is based on revenue sharing — a key feature of federalism
