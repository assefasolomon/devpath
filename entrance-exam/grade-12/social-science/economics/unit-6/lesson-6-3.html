🌍 Global and Regional Poverty

Extreme poverty has declined dramatically since 1990 — but it remains concentrated in specific regions, especially Sub-Saharan Africa. This lesson covers global trends, regional patterns, the SDGs, and the Poverty Reduction Strategy Paper process.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

In previous lessons we defined poverty and inequality, and learned how to measure them. Now we zoom out to the global scale. Where is extreme poverty concentrated today? How has the world's poverty picture changed over the past 30 years? What is the international community doing about it?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Describe global poverty trends since 1990
  • Identify regional patterns — especially Sub-Saharan Africa
  • Explain the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and their 17 goals
  • Describe the Poverty Reduction Strategy Paper (PRSP) process
  • Discuss global inequality trends and their drivers
📈
Global Poverty Trends
MoE Textbook §6.3, pp. 135–136
📌 The Good News

The conditions of poverty have clearly improved over the past half century. The number of people living in extreme poverty declined from 36% in 1990 to 10% in 2015. Countries like China made significant progress in lifting millions of their citizens out of poverty.

📌 The Bad News

Extreme poverty remains widespread in the developing world, especially in Sub-Saharan Africa. In 2010:

  • More than 1.2 billion people lived on less than $1.25/day
  • Some 2.4 billion people — more than one-third of the world's population — lived on less than $2/day
📝 UN reports

The UN reported that more than 700 million people, or 10% of the world population, were living in extreme poverty, and the majority of people on less than $1.90 a day live in Sub-Saharan Africa.

The Sub-Saharan Africa Problem

Sub-Saharan Africa experienced virtually no rate reduction from 1950 to 2000. The gravity of the situation led developing countries to make the eradication of poverty their policy goal — reflected in the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), 2015–2030. Goal 1 aims to end poverty in all its forms.

🌏
Regional Patterns
Where is poverty concentrated?
🌍
Sub-Saharan Africa

The region with the highest concentration of extreme poverty today. Virtually no rate reduction from 1950 to 2000.

🇨🇳
East Asia (China)

The biggest success story — China lifted hundreds of millions out of poverty over the past 40 years.

🌏
South Asia

Large numbers of poor people, but poverty rates have been declining in recent decades.

🌎
Latin America

Higher income levels than Africa/Asia, but high inequality remains a major challenge.

📝 The key regional fact

Africa and South Asia are identified as the two most vulnerable regions to both current climate variation and future climate change. But for poverty specifically, Sub-Saharan Africa is the region where extreme poverty remains most stubbornly high.

📝 The two poverty-related goals

Goal 1 (No Poverty) — end poverty in all its forms everywhere.
Goal 10 (Reduced Inequality) — reduce inequality within and among countries.

📋
Poverty Reduction Strategy Paper (PRSP)
World Bank approach
📌 Poverty Reduction Strategy Paper (PRSP)

The World Bank is said to favour the Poverty Reduction Strategy Paper (PRSP) process to help countries think systematically about how the position of poor people may be improved, and to act accordingly (Haughton and Khandker, 2009). Many developing countries — including Ethiopia — have undergone this process in their development endeavours.

What PRSPs Do

  • Provide a country-led framework for poverty reduction
  • Encourage governments to think systematically about poverty
  • Link public policy to poverty outcomes
  • Serve as the basis for international development assistance
⚖️
Global Inequality
Rising trend, complex drivers
📌 Global inequality is rising

As far as inequality is concerned, it has been rising globally due to:

  • Globalization
  • Technical progress
  • Policies and institutions of different countries
📝 Why this matters

Even though global extreme poverty has declined, inequality has been rising within and between countries. The gap between rich and poor — globally and within nations — remains a major development challenge.
