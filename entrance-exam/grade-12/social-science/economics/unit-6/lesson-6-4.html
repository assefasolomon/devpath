👩 Women and Poverty

Globally, women make up a substantial majority of the world's poor. This lesson examines why women are disproportionately affected by poverty, the role of female-headed households, and the policy implications for development.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

In developing countries, women are disproportionately poor. This isn't an accident — it results from a combination of economic, social, and cultural factors that systematically disadvantage women. Understanding these factors is essential to designing effective anti-poverty policies.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain why women are disproportionately poor
  • Describe the role of female-headed households
  • Identify barriers women face in accessing resources
  • Explain the concept of intra-household bias
  • Discuss policy implications for integrating women into development
📌
Why Women Are Disproportionately Poor
MoE Textbook §6.4, p. 137
📌 The Global Reality

Globally, women make up a substantial majority of the world's poor. Among the poorest communities throughout the developing world, virtually everywhere women and children experience the harshest deprivation. They are more likely to be poor and malnourished and less likely to receive medical services, clean water, sanitation, and other benefits.

Three Key Contributing Factors

1️⃣
Female-Headed Households

The prevalence of female-headed households contributes to women's disproportionate poverty.

2️⃣
Lower Earning Capacity

Women's earning capacity is considerably below that of their male counterparts.

3️⃣
Limited Control Over Income

Women have limited control over their spouses' income.

📝 Key finding

A disproportionate number of the poorest people live in households headed by women who have lower incomes. A portion of the income disparity between male-headed and female-headed households can be explained by the large earnings differentials between men and women. Since the earning potential of women is considerably below that of their male counterparts, women are more likely to be among the very poor.

🏠
Female-Headed Households
More likely to be poor, more likely to stay poor

Why Female-Headed Households Face More Poverty

  • Higher proportion of female-headed households are situated in the poorest areas
  • These areas have little or no access to public services such as piped water, sanitation, and health care
  • Household members are more likely to fall ill and less likely to receive medical attention
  • Children in female-headed households are less likely to be enrolled in school
  • Children are more likely to be working in order to provide additional income
📝 What household income alone misses

Household income alone fails to describe the severity of women's relative deprivation. Even within households, income may be distributed unequally — and women often bear the brunt of that inequality.

🚧
Access to Resources
Structural barriers women face
📚
Education

Women have less access to education.

💼
Formal Employment

Women have less access to formal-sector employment.

🛡️
Social Security

Women have less access to social security.

🏛️
Government Programs

Women have less access to government employment programs.

🌾
Rural Resources

Rural women have less access to resources needed to generate stable incomes.

⚖️
Property Rights

Social customs and laws sometimes play a restrictive role in women owning property.

📝 Urban vs rural

In urban areas, women are much less likely to obtain formal employment in private companies or public agencies and are frequently restricted to illegal, low-productivity jobs. In rural areas, women have less access to the resources necessary to generate stable incomes.

⚖️
Intra-Household Bias
Inequality within families
📌 Intra-Household Resource Allocation

The degree of economic hardship may vary widely within a household depending on the distribution of income within the household, which may be unequal. In terms of intra-household resource allocation, there may exist a bias against females in areas such as nutrition, medical care, education, and inheritance.

📝 What the studies show

Studies have found that where women's share of income within the home is relatively high, there is less discrimination against girls, and women are better able to meet their own needs as well as those of their children.

📝 Nutrition and household spending

When household income is marginal, most of women's income is contributed towards household nutritional intake. Since this fraction is considerably smaller for men, a rise in male earnings leads to a less than proportionate increase in the funds available for daily needs. It is thus unsurprising that programs designed to increase nutrition and family health are more effective when targeting women than when targeting men.

Why Women's Control Over Resources Is Limited

  • A relatively large proportion of work performed by women is unremunerated (e.g., collecting firewood, cooking, parenting)
  • Many women from poor households are not paid for work performed in family agriculture or business
  • It is common for a male head of household to control all funds from cash crops or family business
  • These factors perpetuate the low economic status of women and limit their control over household resources
🎯
Policy Implications
Integrating women into development
📌 The Core Policy Principle

When public programmes to alleviate poverty work exclusively with men, they tend to exacerbate these inequalities. In urban areas, training programmes to increase earning potential and formal-sector employment are generally geared to men, while agricultural extension programmes promote male-dominated crops, frequently at the expense of women's vegetable plots.

Policy Recommendations

1️⃣
Integrate Women Into Development

Design development policy to integrate women into programmes, since these influence the welfare of women and children.

2️⃣
Increase Female Participation

Increase female participation rates in educational programmes, training programmes, formal-sector employment, and agricultural extension programmes.

3️⃣
Equal Access to Government Resources

Ensure women have equal access to government resources provided through schooling, health, and other services.

4️⃣
Formalise Informal Employment

Where the majority of female labour force is employed in the informal sector, formalising or legalising such employment would improve women's economic status.

📝 Why this matters for growth

The consequences of a decline in women's relative or absolute economic status have both ethical and long-term economic implications. Any process of growth that fails to improve the welfare of the people experiencing the greatest hardship has failed to accomplish one of the principal goals of development. Likewise, the low status of women, in the long run, is likely to translate into slower rates of economic growth.

📝 The human capital argument

Considering that human capital is perhaps the most important prerequisite for growth, education and enhanced economic status for women are critical to meeting long-term development objectives. Women-centred poverty strategies often require us to challenge basic assumptions. In general, women must be drawn into the economic mainstream in order to improve living conditions for the poorest individuals (Todaro and Smith, 2015).

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 6.4
What you should remember
  • Globally, women make up a substantial majority of the world's poor
  • Women and children experience the harshest deprivation in the poorest communities
  • Three key factors: female-headed households, lower earning capacity, limited control over income
  • Female-headed households are more likely to be poor, less likely to access services, and their children are less likely to be in school
  • Women have less access to education, formal employment, social security, government programs, and property
  • Intra-household bias against females affects nutrition, medical care, education, and inheritance
  • When women control more income, there is less discrimination against girls and better outcomes for children
  • Women's work is often unremunerated — collecting firewood, cooking, parenting, family agriculture
  • Poverty programmes working exclusively with men exacerbate inequalities
  • Policy recommendations: integrate women into development, increase participation, ensure equal access, formalise informal employment
  • Low status of women slows economic growth — human capital is the key prerequisite for development
