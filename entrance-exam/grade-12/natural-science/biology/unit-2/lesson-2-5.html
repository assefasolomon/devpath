🦠 Protozoa — The Animal-Like Microbes

Protozoa are single-celled organisms that behave like tiny animals — they move, hunt, and consume organic matter. Most are harmless and essential to ecosystems, but a few cause some of the world's most devastating diseases. This lesson explores their diversity, biology, and health impact.

Introduction
What you will learn

Protozoa are the "animal-like" protists — single-celled organisms that move and hunt like tiny animals. They live in water, soil, and inside other organisms. Most are harmless and help make the environment more productive. But a small number — like the malaria parasite — cause some of the deadliest diseases in human history.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define protozoa and describe their characteristics
  • Identify the four main groups of protozoa
  • Describe major protozoal diseases — malaria, trypanosomiasis, leishmaniasis, amoebiasis, giardiasis
  • Explain the life cycle of Plasmodium (malaria parasite)
  • Discuss the impact of protozoa on health in Ethiopia
📌
What are Protozoa?
MoE Textbook §2.2.4, p. 49
📌 Protozoa (MoE Textbook, p. 49)

Protozoa (singular: protozoan) are unicellular eukaryotic microscopic animals. They may be free-living or parasitic, and/or get their energy from organic carbon. All protozoa contain a true, membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bounded organelles within their cytoplasm.

Key Characteristics

  • Unicellular — made of a single cell
  • Eukaryotic — have a true membrane-bound nucleus
  • Motile — move using flagella, cilia, or pseudopodia
  • Free-living or parasitic — some live independently; others need a host
  • Heterotrophic — absorb or ingest organic compounds from their environment
  • Some (like Euglena) are photosynthetic — they use light as an energy source
  • Reproduce sexually or asexually

Three Modes of Movement

ModeDescriptionExample
PseudopodiaExtensions of cytoplasm — "false feet"Amoeba
FlagellaLong, whip-like appendagesEuglena, Trypanosoma, Giardia
CiliaNumerous short, hair-like appendagesParamecium
🔬
Four Main Groups
Classification of protozoa

Protozoa are traditionally classified into four main groups based on their mode of locomotion:

🦠
Rhizopoda (Amoebae)

Move by pseudopodia (false feet). Examples: Amoeba, Entamoeba histolytica (causes amoebiasis).

🌿
Mastigophora (Flagellates)

Move by flagella. Examples: Euglena (photosynthetic), Trypanosoma (sleeping sickness), Giardia (diarrhoeal disease).

👁️
Ciliata (Ciliates)

Move by cilia. Example: Paramecium — a classic example of a ciliated protozoan.

🩸
Sporozoa (Apicomplexa)

No movement organelles in adult stage; all are parasitic. Example: Plasmodium — causes malaria.

📝 The classic examples

The three most studied protozoa in school biology are:

• Amoeba — moves by pseudopodia; helps us understand cell structure
• Euglena — has both plant (chloroplast) and animal (flagellum) features
• Paramecium — moves by cilia; a classic model for studying single-celled life

⚠️
Protozoal Diseases
Major human and animal infections

Most protozoa are harmless, but several cause serious diseases in humans and domestic animals. Here are the most important ones:

DiseaseCausative agentVector/RouteSymptoms
Malaria Plasmodium falciparum, P. vivax, P. ovale, P. malariae Bite of female Anopheles mosquito Fever, shivering, cough, headache, vomiting, severe anemia
African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness) Trypanosoma brucei Tsetse fly bite Fever, joint pains, neurological disorder, somnolence (sleeping)
Chagas disease Trypanosoma cruzi Triatomine bug bite Acute: fever, splenomegaly; Chronic: heart and digestive damage
Leishmaniasis Leishmania donovani, L. major, etc. Sand fly bite Skin ulcers, mucocutaneous complications, visceral disease
Amoebiasis (amoebic dysentery) Entamoeba histolytica Contaminated water or food Diarrhoea, dysentery, abdominal pain
Giardiasis Giardia lamblia Contaminated water Diarrhoea, cramps, bloating
Trichomoniasis Trichomonas vaginalis Sexual contact Vaginal discharge, odor, edema

Diseases in Livestock

  • Nagana (Gandii in Afaan Oromo, Gendi in Amharic) — cattle trypanosomiasis; caused by Trypanosoma species; transmitted by tsetse flies; major constraint on livestock production in Ethiopia
  • Coccidiosis — intestinal disease of poultry and other animals caused by Eimeria species
  • Babesiosis — cattle disease caused by Babesia species; transmitted by ticks
  • Theileriosis — East Coast fever in cattle; caused by Theileria species
🦟
Malaria in Detail
The deadliest protozoal disease

Malaria is a serious and sometimes fatal disease caused by a parasite that commonly infects a certain type of mosquito which feeds on humans. It is caused by four species of Plasmodium:

  • Plasmodium falciparum — the most dangerous, can be fatal
  • Plasmodium vivax — most widespread
  • Plasmodium ovale — less common
  • Plasmodium malariae — least common

The Malaria Life Cycle

The malaria parasite life cycle involves two hosts — a mosquito and a human:

1. An infected female Anopheles mosquito bites a human and injects sporozoites into the bloodstream.
2. Sporozoites travel to the liver and infect liver cells, where they mature into schizonts.
3. Schizonts rupture and release merozoites into the bloodstream.
4. Merozoites infect red blood cells, multiply, and cause the red blood cells to burst — this causes the fever and chills of malaria.
5. Some parasites develop into gametocytes — sexual forms that can be picked up by another mosquito when it bites the infected human.
6. Inside the mosquito, gametocytes fuse to form a zygote, which develops into new sporozoites — ready to infect another human.

Symptoms of Malaria

  • High fever with shaking chills
  • Flu-like illness — headache, muscle pain, fatigue
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Severe anemia — from destruction of red blood cells
  • Respiratory distress in severe cases
  • Convulsions and organ failure in cerebral malaria

Prevention & Control

  • Insecticide-treated bed nets — prevent mosquito bites at night
  • Indoor residual spraying — kills mosquitoes resting indoors
  • Draining standing water — eliminates mosquito breeding sites
  • Antimalarial drugs — for prevention (prophylaxis) and treatment
  • Rapid diagnostic tests — detect the parasite early
📝 Malaria in Ethiopia

Malaria is a major public health problem in Ethiopia. It is endemic in many parts of the country, especially below 2,000 meters altitude. The most common species in Ethiopia are P. falciparum (causing the most severe cases) and P. vivax. Control efforts include distribution of bed nets, indoor spraying, and community education.

🇪🇹
Protozoa in Ethiopia
Local impact

Protozoal diseases have a significant impact on health and agriculture in Ethiopia:

  • Malaria — affects millions each year; leading cause of illness and death, especially in children under 5
  • Leishmaniasis — known locally as Kunchir (Amharic) or Sinbira halkanii (Afaan Oromo) for the cutaneous form; endemic in many regions
  • Nagana (Gandii/Gendi) — cattle trypanosomiasis; major constraint on livestock production in tsetse-infested areas
  • Amoebiasis and giardiasis — common waterborne infections, especially where clean water is scarce

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 2.5
What you should remember
  • Protozoa are unicellular eukaryotic microscopic animals — free-living or parasitic
  • They move by pseudopodia (Amoeba), flagella (Euglena, Trypanosoma), or cilia (Paramecium)
  • The four main groups: Rhizopoda, Mastigophora, Ciliata, Sporozoa
  • Major protozoal diseases: malaria, African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness), Chagas disease, leishmaniasis, amoebiasis, giardiasis
  • Malaria is caused by Plasmodium and transmitted by female Anopheles mosquitoes; its life cycle involves two hosts
  • In Ethiopia, protozoal diseases include malaria, leishmaniasis (Kunchir/Sinbira halkanii), Nagana (Gandii/Gendi) in cattle
