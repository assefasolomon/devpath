🔬 What are Microorganisms?

Microorganisms are the smallest and most abundant forms of life on Earth — invisible to the naked eye, but essential to every ecosystem, and even to your own body. This lesson introduces the invisible world that shapes our planet.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

You can't see them, but they're everywhere — in the air you breathe, the water you drink, the food you eat, and even inside your own body. Microorganisms are the invisible majority of life on Earth. They outnumber every other form of life by an enormous margin, and without them, life as we know it would be impossible.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define microorganisms and microbiology
  • Describe the diversity of microorganisms
  • Explain the beneficial roles of microorganisms in the environment and human life
  • Understand what normal microbiota is and why it matters
  • Explain the difference between harmless and pathogenic microorganisms
📌
What are Microorganisms?
MoE Textbook §2.1, p. 30
📌 Microorganisms (MoE Textbook, p. 30)

Microorganisms (also called microbes) are life forms including acellular viruses that are too small to be seen with the naked eye. These microscopic organisms are diverse in form and function and inhabit every environment on Earth that supports life.

📌 Key characteristics

• Many microbes are single-celled organisms
• Some can form complex structures
• Some are even multicellular
• They typically live in complex microbial communities
• Their activities are regulated by interactions with each other, with their environments, and with other organisms

📝 Microscopic vs macroscopic

Microscopic organisms are too small to be seen with the naked eye — they require a microscope to be observed. This distinguishes them from macroscopic organisms (like plants and animals) that are readily visible.

🔬
Microbiology
The science of the invisible
📌 Microbiology

Microbiology is the scientific study of microorganisms — what they are, how they work, and what they do.

📌 Biosphere

The biosphere is the region of the surface and atmosphere of the Earth occupied by living organisms.

Why Study Microorganisms?

Microorganisms are everywhere, and though small, their activities have tremendous impacts on everything in our biosphere. Many of our day-to-day interactions with the world are influenced — for better or worse — by microbial activities.

The Five Main Groups

Based on evolutionary lines, there are three main kinds of microorganisms: bacteria, archaea, and eukarya. In practice, we group microorganisms into five main groups:

🦠
Bacteria

Prokaryotes — single-celled organisms without a nucleus. Include cyanobacteria that photosynthesize.

🍄
Fungi

Eukaryotes — yeasts, molds, and mushrooms. Decomposers and fermenters.

🦠
Protozoa

Unicellular eukaryotes — amoeba, paramecium. Free-living or parasitic.

🌿
Some Algae

Photosynthetic microorganisms — produce oxygen and form the base of aquatic food chains.

🧬
Viruses

Acellular — not truly alive, but they infect all cellular life forms.

💚
Beneficial Roles of Microorganisms
The good news

Most microorganisms are harmless or indeed beneficial. Here are some of their most important beneficial roles:

  • Nutrient recycling — they are essential to the recycling of nutrients that form the bodies of all organisms and sustain all the metabolic cycles of life
  • Oxygen production — they produce about 50% of the oxygen gas we breathe
  • Food production — they are used to make many foods we eat (bread, cheese, yoghurt, injera, tej, tella)
  • Nutrient supply — they provide nutrients to many other organisms
  • Medicine — they are the source of antibiotics and other drugs
  • Bioremediation — they clean up pollution in the environment
  • Agriculture — nitrogen-fixing bacteria enrich the soil
  • Industry — used in fermentation, enzyme production, and bioplastics
📝 Astonishing fact

Microorganisms produce about 50% of the oxygen we breathe — mostly from ocean phytoplankton. Every second breath you take comes from a microorganism.

🧑
Our Normal Microbiota
The microbes that live on and inside us
📌 Normal Microbiota

The normal microbiota (or normal flora) are the microorganisms that live on or inside another living organism without causing disease. They are typically beneficial to their host.

Where do our microbiota live?

  • Skin — approximately 100 billion microorganisms live on/in our skin
  • Mouth — bacteria and fungi that help break down food
  • Ears and nose — protective communities that block pathogens
  • Throat — bacteria that prevent harmful microbes from taking hold
  • Digestive tract — the largest microbial community; helps digest food and produce vitamins

What Do Our Microbiota Do?

  • Help us resist diseases — they compete with harmful microbes for space and nutrients
  • Regulate development — they help train our immune system
  • Regulate nutrition — they help us digest food and synthesize vitamins (like vitamin K and B vitamins)
📝 The numbers are staggering

According to the MoE Textbook: "Some 100 billion microorganisms inhabit in/on our skin and grow in our mouth, ears, nose, throat, and digestive tract." In fact, there are roughly 10 bacterial cells in your body for every one human cell. You are more microbe than human!

⚠️
Pathogens — The Harmful Minority
The rare bad actors
📌 Pathogen

A pathogen is a microorganism that causes disease. Pathogens are rare — the vast majority of microorganisms are harmless or beneficial.

Common Misconception

When most of us hear the word "bacterium" or "virus", we think infection or disease — but such pathogens are rare. Out of the thousands of species of viruses, bacteria, fungi, and parasites, only a tiny portion are involved in diseases of any kind.

📝 Key insight

The vast majority of microorganisms are either harmless or beneficial. Only a small fraction cause disease. This is why understanding microbiology is important — not to fear microbes, but to understand how they help us and how a small minority can harm us.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 2.1
What you should remember
  • Microorganisms are life forms too small to be seen with the naked eye — including acellular viruses
  • Microbiology is the scientific study of microorganisms
  • The five main groups are: bacteria, fungi, protozoa, some algae, and viruses
  • Most microorganisms are harmless or beneficial — they produce 50% of our oxygen and recycle nutrients
  • Normal microbiota live on and inside our bodies — helping us resist disease, regulate development, and absorb nutrients
  • Pathogens (disease-causing microbes) are a tiny minority of all microorganisms
