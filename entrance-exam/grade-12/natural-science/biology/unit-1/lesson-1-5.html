🎓 Development of Careers

Biology is not just a school subject — it's a gateway to hundreds of meaningful careers. From medicine to agriculture, environmental science to biotechnology, biology opens doors to professions that improve lives and protect the planet. This lesson helps you think about your own career path.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Career development is a lifelong process — not just about choosing a job at 18, but about continually growing, learning, and adapting throughout your working life. Biology gives you a foundation for careers that matter: saving lives, feeding communities, protecting ecosystems, and discovering new knowledge.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define career development
  • Describe the stages of career development
  • Identify career paths related to biology
  • Understand the skills needed for biology-related careers
  • Begin planning your own career goals
📌
What is Career Development?
Key definitions
📌 Career Development

Career development is the lifelong process of managing your learning, work, and transitions to move toward your desired future. It involves understanding yourself, exploring opportunities, making decisions, and taking action.

📌 Career vs Job

A job is a specific position you hold for a period of time.
A career is the overall progression of your working life — a series of jobs, experiences, and learning that builds toward your long-term goals.

📝 Why career development matters

The world of work is changing fast. Careers that exist today may look different in ten years. Career development skills — self-awareness, adaptability, continuous learning — help you thrive no matter what changes come.

📈
Stages of Career Development
From awareness to achievement
StageTypical AgeWhat happens
1. AwarenessChildhood & early teensDiscovering interests, talents, and what kinds of work exist
2. ExplorationLate teens (Grade 11–12)Learning about careers, asking questions, choosing a stream
3. PreparationUniversity / collegeFormal education and training for your chosen field
4. EstablishmentEarly career (20s–30s)Getting your first jobs, building experience and skills
5. AdvancementMid-career (30s–50s)Growth, leadership, specialization, higher responsibility
6. Maintenance & transitionLater careerMentoring, adapting to changes, possibly pivoting or retiring
📝 You are here

As a Grade 12 student, you're mainly in the Exploration stage — with Preparation starting soon. This is a great time to research careers, talk to professionals, and set goals.

🧬
Biology Career Paths
Where biology can take you

Biology opens doors to careers in health, agriculture, environment, research, industry, and education. Here are just a few examples:

🩺
Medicine & Health

Physician, nurse, pharmacist, dentist, veterinarian, public health specialist, medical researcher.

🌾
Agriculture & Food

Agronomist, plant breeder, soil scientist, food scientist, agricultural extension officer.

🌿
Environmental Science

Ecologist, conservation biologist, environmental consultant, wildlife manager, climate scientist.

🔬
Research & Biotechnology

Geneticist, biochemist, microbiologist, biotech researcher, drug developer.

🏭
Industry

Food production, pharmaceutical manufacturing, brewing, quality control, biofuel production.

📚
Education & Communication

Biology teacher, science communicator, textbook author, museum educator, science journalist.

Emerging Biology Careers

  • Bioinformatics — combining biology, computer science, and statistics to analyze biological data
  • Genetic counseling — helping families understand genetic risks and testing options
  • Stem cell research — developing new treatments for diseases
  • Bioinformatics / genomics — studying entire genomes to understand disease and evolution
  • Synthetic biology — designing new biological systems for useful purposes
  • Climate biology — studying how species and ecosystems respond to climate change

Skills & Goals
Preparing for your career

Skills Needed for Biology Careers

TypeExamples
Scientific skillsLab techniques, data analysis, experimental design, observation
Technical skillsMicroscopy, computer modeling, bioinformatics tools, statistical software
Communication skillsWriting scientific reports, presenting results, explaining to non-specialists
Critical thinkingAnalyzing evidence, evaluating claims, solving problems
Soft skillsTeamwork, time management, resilience, curiosity, ethical judgment
Language & literacyStrong English (much scientific literature is in English), reading, writing

Setting Career Goals — SMART Method

  • S — Specific: "I want to become a research biologist specializing in plant genetics"
  • M — Measurable: "I will score 80%+ in biology and get a scholarship to study biology"
  • A — Achievable: The goal is realistic given your current situation
  • R — Relevant: It matches your interests, values, and skills
  • T — Time-bound: "I will achieve this by the end of my Grade 12 year"
