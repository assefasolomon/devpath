♻️ Waste Treatment

Every day, humans produce enormous amounts of waste — from household sewage to industrial byproducts. Biology provides powerful tools for treating this waste, turning pollution into resources through natural biological processes.

📖 Reading: ~15 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Waste treatment is the process of removing contaminants from waste materials before they are released back into the environment. Without treatment, sewage and industrial waste pollute rivers, kill aquatic life, and spread disease. Biology gives us the tools to clean up waste naturally and sustainably.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Identify types of waste and their environmental impacts
  • Describe the stages of wastewater treatment
  • Explain the role of activated sludge and microorganisms
  • Understand biogas production from organic waste
  • Describe bio-mining and its applications
📌
Types of Waste
Understanding what we produce
📌 Waste

Waste is any material that is discarded because it is no longer useful. It can be solid, liquid, or gaseous, and comes from households, industries, agriculture, and hospitals.

TypeExamplesEnvironmental impact
Domestic wasteFood scraps, paper, plastics, glass, metalsLandfill overflow, plastic pollution
SewageHuman waste, soap, food residue from homesWater pollution, disease spread
Industrial wasteChemicals, heavy metals, solventsToxic water and soil contamination
Agricultural wasteManure, crop residue, pesticidesEutrophication of waterways
Medical wasteUsed needles, bandages, biological samplesInfectious disease risk
Radioactive wasteSpent nuclear fuel, medical isotopesLong-term radiation hazards

Wastewater Treatment
Stages of biological cleaning

Wastewater treatment is a multi-stage process that removes contaminants from sewage and industrial water before it's released back into the environment.

StageWhat happensBiology involved
1. PreliminaryScreening removes large objects (rags, sticks, plastics)None — physical process
2. PrimarySedimentation tanks let solids settle as sludgeNone — physical process
3. SecondaryBiological treatment — microorganisms break down dissolved organic matterAerobic bacteria consume organic matter
4. TertiaryNutrient removal — nitrogen and phosphorus removedDenitrifying bacteria convert nitrates to N₂ gas
5. DisinfectionChlorination or UV kills remaining pathogensKills harmful microorganisms
📝 The key role of biology

The secondary stage is where biology does the heavy lifting. Microorganisms — mostly bacteria — consume the dissolved organic matter in wastewater, converting it into carbon dioxide, water, and new cell mass. Without these microbes, wastewater treatment would be impossible at scale.

🧫
Activated Sludge Process
Microbes at work
📌 Activated Sludge

Activated sludge is a process for treating sewage in which air or oxygen is pumped into a mixture of sewage and bacteria. The bacteria consume organic matter, multiply, and form a "sludge" that can be separated from the clean water.

How It Works

  • Aeration tank: Wastewater is mixed with a bacterial culture and air is bubbled through
  • Bacterial action: Aerobic bacteria consume organic matter, converting it to CO₂, water, and more bacteria
  • Settling tank: The mixture is allowed to settle — bacteria and organic matter form sludge at the bottom
  • Sludge return: Some sludge is returned to the aeration tank to keep the bacterial population active
  • Sludge disposal: Excess sludge is digested (anaerobically) to produce biogas or dried and used as fertilizer
  • Clean water: The treated water at the top is disinfected and released

Why This Matters

The activated sludge process is used in virtually every modern wastewater treatment plant. It's efficient, relatively low-cost, and uses naturally occurring microbes instead of harsh chemicals. Ethiopia is expanding its wastewater treatment capacity, with plants like the Kaliti Wastewater Treatment Plant in Addis Ababa.

🔥
Biogas Production
Turning waste into energy
📌 Biogas

Biogas is a renewable fuel produced by the anaerobic digestion of organic waste — manure, food scraps, sewage sludge, and crop residues. It is mainly composed of methane (CH₄) and carbon dioxide (CO₂).

How Biogas is Made

  • Feedstock: Organic waste (manure, food scraps, crop residues) is collected
  • Anaerobic digester: Waste is placed in an oxygen-free tank
  • Microbial breakdown: Anaerobic bacteria (methanogens) break down organic matter
  • Biogas production: Methane-rich gas is produced and collected
  • Use: The gas is burned for cooking, heating, or electricity
  • Byproduct: The digested material (bio-slurry) is an excellent fertilizer
📝 Biogas in Ethiopia

Ethiopia has been promoting household biogas systems, especially in rural areas. A family with just a few cows can produce enough biogas for daily cooking — replacing firewood, reducing deforestation, and improving indoor air quality. The bio-slurry byproduct enriches farm soil.

Advantages of Biogas

  • Renewable — organic waste is constantly produced
  • Clean — burns without smoke, unlike firewood
  • Reduces waste — treats organic waste and prevents pollution
  • Produces fertilizer — bio-slurry is nutrient-rich
  • Reduces deforestation — replaces firewood for cooking
  • Reduces greenhouse gases — captures methane that would otherwise escape
⛏️
Bio-mining
Using microbes to extract metals
📌 Bio-mining (Bioleaching)

Bio-mining (or bioleaching) is the extraction of metals from ores using microorganisms. Certain bacteria can dissolve metals from rocks, allowing them to be recovered from low-grade ores or mining waste.

How Bio-mining Works

  • Certain bacteria (e.g. Thiobacillus ferrooxidans) are added to crushed ore
  • The bacteria oxidize sulfur and iron compounds in the ore
  • This produces acid and other chemicals that dissolve the metals
  • The metal-rich solution is collected and processed to extract the metal

Applications

  • Copper extraction — bioleaching is used worldwide to recover copper from low-grade ores
  • Gold extraction — bio-oxidation of refractory gold ores
  • Uranium extraction — bioleaching can recover uranium from low-grade deposits
  • Mine waste cleanup — using microbes to treat acid mine drainage
  • Electronic waste recycling — recovering metals from old electronics

Advantages of Bio-mining

  • Environmentally friendly — much less energy and pollution than traditional smelting
  • Low cost — can extract metals from ores that would be uneconomical with traditional methods
  • Sustainable — uses naturally occurring microbes
  • Recovers metals from waste — e-waste and mine tailings can be reprocessed

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.7
What you should remember
  • Waste comes in many forms — domestic, sewage, industrial, agricultural, medical, and radioactive
  • Wastewater treatment has five stages: preliminary, primary, secondary, tertiary, disinfection
  • The secondary stage uses aerobic bacteria to consume organic matter
  • Activated sludge = sewage + bacteria + air — a key biological treatment method
  • Biogas = methane-rich fuel from anaerobic digestion of organic waste — renewable, clean, and produces fertilizer
  • Bio-mining (bioleaching) uses microorganisms to extract metals from ores — sustainable and low-cost
