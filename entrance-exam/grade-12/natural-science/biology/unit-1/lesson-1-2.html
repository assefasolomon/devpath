🌳 Conservation of Natural Resources

Ethiopia is blessed with extraordinary natural wealth — forests, rivers, wildlife, minerals, and fertile land. This lesson is about understanding these resources, why they're under threat, and how biology helps us conserve them for future generations.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Every country depends on its natural resources — the raw materials that come from nature and support human life and economic development. Ethiopia is one of the richest countries in Africa in terms of biodiversity and natural resources. But these resources are not unlimited. The way we use them today determines whether they will still be there for our children.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define natural resources and conservation
  • Distinguish between renewable and non-renewable resources
  • Identify Ethiopia's major natural resources and the threats they face
  • Explain the role of biology in conserving natural resources
  • Describe practical conservation strategies used in Ethiopia
📌
What is Conservation?
Key definitions
📌 Natural Resources

Natural resources are materials or substances that occur in nature and can be used for economic gain or to support life. They include air, water, soil, minerals, forests, wildlife, and energy sources.

📌 Conservation

Conservation is the sustainable use and protection of natural resources so that they are available for present and future generations. It is not about never using resources — it's about using them wisely.

📝 Conservation vs Preservation

Conservation = wise, sustainable use (e.g. selective logging, sustainable fishing).
Preservation = protecting nature from any human use (e.g. strict national parks, wilderness areas).
Both are important — different tools for different situations.

♻️
Renewable vs Non-renewable
Two fundamental categories
FeatureRenewable ResourcesNon-renewable Resources
DefinitionCan be replenished naturally over timeFormed over millions of years; cannot be replaced in human timescales
ExamplesForests, wildlife, water, soil, solar, wind, biomassCoal, petroleum, natural gas, minerals, metals
Rate of useCan be sustainable if used wiselyWill eventually run out
Conservation approachSustainable harvesting and managementRecycling, efficiency, substitutes
Ethiopia examplesForests (e.g. Bale, Menagesha), coffee, teff, livestockGold, tantalum, opals, potash
📝 Important warning

Even renewable resources can be destroyed if used faster than they can regenerate. For example, Ethiopia has lost a large portion of its forest cover over the last century. Once soil is eroded or a forest is cleared, recovery takes decades or centuries.

🇪🇹
Ethiopia's Natural Resources
What we have and what threatens it

Major Resources

🌳
Forests

Home to unique species like the Ethiopian wolf and gelada baboon. Historical forest cover has declined dramatically.

💧
Water

The Blue Nile, Awash, Omo, and other rivers. Ethiopia is often called the "water tower of East Africa".

🐘
Wildlife

Endemic species found only in Ethiopia — the walia ibex, mountain nyala, and Ethiopian wolf.

🌾
Soil & Agriculture

Fertile highlands grow coffee, teff, wheat, and more. Soil erosion is a serious threat.

⛏️
Minerals

Gold, tantalum, potash, opals, and more — valuable but non-renewable.

☀️
Renewable Energy

Abundant hydro, solar, wind, and geothermal potential.

Major Threats to Ethiopian Resources

  • Deforestation — cutting trees for fuel, farmland, and construction
  • Soil erosion — caused by deforestation and poor farming practices, especially on steep slopes
  • Overgrazing — too many livestock on limited pasture land
  • Pollution — industrial waste, agricultural runoff, and urban sewage
  • Climate change — changing rainfall patterns and increased drought
  • Overpopulation pressure — more people needing more land, food, and fuel
  • Illegal wildlife trade — poaching for ivory, skins, and exotic pets
🧬
Role of Biology in Conservation
How biology helps protect resources

Biology provides the scientific foundation for conservation. Without understanding how ecosystems work, we can't protect them effectively.

Biology ContributionHow It Helps Conservation
EcologyStudies how organisms interact with each other and their environment — informs ecosystem management
GeneticsHelps preserve genetic diversity in endangered species; informs captive breeding programs
BotanyIdentifies threatened plant species and guides reforestation efforts
ZoologyTracks animal populations; identifies endangered species
MicrobiologyDevelops biofertilizers and biopesticides; helps clean up pollution (bioremediation)
Environmental biologyMonitors pollution, assesses ecosystem health, models climate impacts

Conservation Strategies in Ethiopia

  • National Parks — Simien Mountains, Bale Mountains, Awash, Omo, Gambella (protect biodiversity)
  • Forest reserves — Menagesha, Chilimo, Adaba-Dodola (protect remaining forests)
  • Community-based conservation — local communities manage and benefit from natural resources
  • Terracing and soil conservation — reduces erosion on Ethiopia's highland farms
  • Agroforestry — combines trees with crops to improve soil and provide fuelwood
  • Reforestation programs — millions of trees planted annually
  • Legal protection — laws against illegal logging, poaching, and wildlife trade
  • Environmental education — teaching conservation in schools and communities

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.2
What you should remember
  • Natural resources are materials from nature that support life and economic activity
  • Conservation means sustainable use — not never using, but using wisely
  • Renewable resources can be replenished (forests, water, soil); non-renewable cannot (minerals, fossil fuels)
  • Ethiopia's resources include forests, water, wildlife, soil, minerals, and renewable energy
  • Major threats: deforestation, soil erosion, overgrazing, pollution, climate change
  • Biology provides the scientific basis for conservation — ecology, genetics, botany, zoology, microbiology
  • Conservation strategies: national parks, forest reserves, community management, terracing, agroforestry, reforestation
