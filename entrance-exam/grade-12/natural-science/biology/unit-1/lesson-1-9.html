🧬 Biotechnology — Applications

Biotechnology isn't just theory — it's transforming how we grow food, produce medicine, solve crimes, and clean up pollution. This lesson explores the major applications of biotechnology, from soil fertility to DNA forensics to bioengineering.

📖 Reading: ~22 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

In Lesson 1.8 you learned the what and how of biotechnology — its definition, tools, and gene-transfer methods. In this lesson, you'll see the where and why — the major real-world applications transforming agriculture, industry, medicine, and society.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Describe how biotechnology improves agriculture (soil, tissue culture, animal breeding)
  • Explain the role of biotechnology in food, beverages, and pharmaceuticals
  • Define single-cell protein and its role in food security
  • Explain how DNA analysis is used in forensic science
  • Discuss biological warfare and its ethical issues
🌾
Biotechnology in Agriculture
MoE Textbook §1.8, pp. 18–22

Food supply for a fast-growing population is threatened by loss of soil fertility and by crop and animal diseases. Biotechnology provides several powerful tools to address these.

1. Soil Fertility & Bio-fertilizers

  • Organic fertilizers (bio-fertilizers) — use beneficial microorganisms to enrich soil naturally
  • Vermicomposting — earthworms break down organic waste into nutrient-rich compost
  • Crop rotation with legumes — legume plants (beans, peas) host nitrogen-fixing bacteria in root nodules, restoring soil nitrogen
  • Improved germplasm — better seeds adapted to local conditions

2. Tissue Culture

Tissue culture is a technique that grows new plants from small pieces of plant tissue in a sterile, nutrient-rich medium. It allows rapid, large-scale propagation of identical plants.

  • Used for palm trees, orchids, bananas, and carrots
  • Produces large quantities of food in a small area
  • Enables seedless fruits (watermelon, papaya, orange, grape) through genetic engineering
  • Significantly reduces crop cycle time

3. Animal Breeding & Transgenic Animals

Animal breeding selects animals with superior traits — faster growth, more meat, milk, or eggs, or better disease resistance. Transgenic animals contain genes from another species.

  • Enhanced growth rate and feed utilization
  • Improved carcass composition (more meat, less fat)
  • Better milk production or composition
  • Improved disease resistance
  • Modified hair or fiber (e.g. wool quality)
  • Example: Borana Bull — an Ethiopian improved cattle breed
📝 Model organisms in research

A model organism is an organism well-suited for studying a particular trait or disease due to its short generation time, well-characterized genome, or similarity to humans. Examples: fruit fly (Drosophila), zebrafish, mouse, rat, and the bacterium E. coli.

Food, Industry & Pharmaceuticals
Industrial (white) biotechnology
📌 Industrial Biotechnology (White Biotechnology)

The modern use and application of biotechnology for the sustainable production of bio-products — food, chemicals, biomaterials, and fuels — from renewable sources using living cells and/or their enzymes.

Major Industrial Products

Product categoryExamples
Food supplementsVitamin B2, vitamin K, amino acids
PharmaceuticalsAntibiotics, vaccines, insulin, hormones
BioplasticsPolylactic acid (PLA)
Energy carriersBiogas, bioethanol, hydrogen
DetergentsEnzyme-based biological washing powders
Pulp & paperEnzymatic processing
TextilesBio-processing for quality fabrics
Ethanol productionFrom sugarcane, corn, lignocellulose

Food Preservation

Industrial biotechnology uses cultures of harmless microorganisms to prevent food spoilage. These cultures inhibit the growth of undesirable decomposing microorganisms — extending shelf life without harsh chemicals.

🍄
Single-Cell Protein (SCP)
A promising food of the future
📌 Single-Cell Protein (SCP)

Single-cell proteins (SCP) — also called microbial proteins — are edible unicellular microorganisms. The biomass or protein extract from pure or mixed cultures of algae, yeasts, fungi, or bacteria may be used as an ingredient or substitute for protein-rich foods, suitable for human consumption or as animal feed.

Common SCP Sources

🍄
Mushrooms

Fungal fruiting bodies — already widely eaten, rich in protein and B vitamins.

🟢
Spirulina

Blue-green algae — high in protein, vitamins, and minerals.

🍞
Yeast

Fungi grown in culture — used as food supplement and protein source.

🌿
Green Algae

Photosynthetic microorganisms — grow on light, produce protein efficiently.

📝 Why SCP matters for food security

SCP can be produced quickly on cheap substrates (waste materials, industrial byproducts), doesn't require farmland, and can be produced year-round. It's a promising tool for feeding a growing global population — especially in countries like Ethiopia where food security is a priority.

🔍
Forensic Science
Using DNA to solve crimes

Forensic biology uses biological evidence — blood, saliva, hair, and other tissues — to solve crimes and determine paternity. The technique relies on the fact that each individual has unique DNA fingerprints (except identical twins).

How DNA Forensics Works

  • Collect biological samples from the crime scene (blood, hair, saliva)
  • Extract DNA from the samples
  • Amplify specific DNA regions using PCR
  • Analyse the DNA using gel electrophoresis
  • Compare the pattern to suspects or other samples
  • Report findings in court as expert testimony

VNTR Analysis

Forensic scientists examine 5–10 different Variable Number of Tandem Repeat (VNTR) loci. The probability that two random individuals share the same genetic pattern at all these loci is approximately 1 in 10 billion — making DNA evidence extremely powerful.

Fingerprints

  • Each individual has unique fingerprints that don't change throughout life
  • Used to investigate crime scenes and determine paternity
  • Bioinformatics tools now make fingerprint and DNA analysis faster and more reliable
⚠️
Biological Warfare
The dark side of biotechnology
📌 Biological Warfare (Germ Warfare)

The use of biological toxins or infectious agents — bacteria, viruses, fungi, or their toxins — with the intent to kill or incapacitate humans, animals, or plants as an act of war.

Types of Biological Weapons

  • Bacteria — e.g. anthrax, plague, cholera
  • Viruses — e.g. smallpox, Ebola
  • Fungi — e.g. rice blast (crop destruction)
  • Toxins — poisonous compounds produced by microorganisms (e.g. botulinum toxin)
📝 Why biological warfare is prohibited

The Biological Weapons Convention (1972) bans the development, production, and stockpiling of biological weapons. However, concerns remain that some nations or terrorist groups may seek these agents. Ethical issues around biotechnology — including bioweapons, GMOs, and gene editing — are a critical part of modern biology education.

⚙️
Bioengineering
Where biology meets engineering
📌 Bioengineering (Biological Engineering)

The application of principles of biology and the tools of engineering to create usable, tangible, economically viable products.

Key Concepts

TermDefinition
Bio-process engineeringPlanning, construction, execution, and revision of biological and mechanical processes to create new products
BioinformaticsSub-discipline of biology and computer science for acquisition, storage, analysis, and dissemination of biological data (DNA, protein sequences)
Bio-catalysisUsing isolated enzymes (or enzymes inside living cells) to perform chemical conversions of organic compounds
BioreactorA manufactured device or system that supports a biologically active environment

Why Bioreactors Matter

A bioreactor provides the controlled conditions — temperature, pH, oxygen, nutrients — that let microorganisms or cells perform their desired function with limited production of impurities. This makes industrial-scale bioproduction (insulin, vaccines, enzymes, etc.) possible.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.9
What you should remember
  • Agriculture: bio-fertilizers, vermicomposting, crop rotation, tissue culture, animal breeding, transgenic animals
  • Industrial biotech produces food supplements, pharmaceuticals, bioplastics, biofuels, detergents, and enzymes
  • Single-Cell Protein (SCP) = edible unicellular microorganisms (mushrooms, spirulina, yeast, algae) — promising for food security
  • Forensic biology uses DNA analysis (via PCR, VNTR, gel electrophoresis) and fingerprints to solve crimes
  • Biological warfare uses pathogens or toxins as weapons — banned by the Biological Weapons Convention (1972)
  • Bioengineering applies engineering to biology — key concepts include bio-process engineering, bioinformatics, bio-catalysis, and bioreactors
