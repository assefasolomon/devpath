🧬 Biotechnology — Definition & Genetic Engineering

Biotechnology is the science of using living organisms to make useful products. It's one of the most powerful applications of biology — from the bread and beer we've made for thousands of years, to the genetic engineering that's transforming medicine and agriculture today.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Humans have been using living organisms to make useful products for more than 6,000 years — making bread with yeast, brewing beer, and fermenting milk into cheese and yoghurt. But in the last 50 years, our ability to manipulate life at the molecular level has transformed what's possible. This is biotechnology — and it's reshaping medicine, agriculture, industry, and our environment.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define biotechnology
  • Distinguish traditional vs modern biotechnology
  • Identify the main fields of biotechnology
  • Explain the principles of genetic engineering
  • Describe the methods used to transfer genes into organisms
📌
What is Biotechnology?
MoE Textbook §1.8, p. 14
📌 Biotechnology (MoE Textbook, p. 14)

Biotechnology is the "science and technology of harnessing the natural and biological capabilities of plants, animals, and microbes for the benefit of people."

📌 In simpler words

Biotechnology is the application of biological knowledge, techniques, and living organisms to make a product or run a process for human use.

📝 Key insight

Biotechnology is not just about modern genetic engineering — it includes any use of living things to make useful products. This means the yeast in your bread and the bacteria in your yoghurt are also examples of biotechnology.

Traditional vs Modern Biotechnology
Two eras of using life
FeatureTraditional BiotechnologyModern Biotechnology
Time period 6,000+ years ago → present 1970s → present
Core method Domestication, fermentation Genetic engineering, recombinant DNA
Examples Bread, wine, beer, cheese, yoghurt, injera, tej, tella Human insulin, GM crops, gene therapy, monoclonal antibodies
Precision Whole organisms — results are unpredictable Specific genes — results are targeted
Speed Slow — depends on natural breeding Fast — genes can be moved between species

Traditional Biotechnology — Ethiopian Examples

  • Injera — fermented teff dough using wild yeasts and bacteria
  • Tej — honey wine fermented with yeasts
  • Tella — traditional barley beer fermented with bacteria and yeasts
  • Areki — distilled spirit from fermented grains
  • Ergo — fermented milk (yoghurt) using lactic acid bacteria
  • Kocho — fermented enset (false banana) bread
📝 Back-slopping

In traditional fermentation, a small amount of the previous batch is saved and added to the new batch to start fermentation. This is called back-slopping. It keeps the same beneficial microorganisms alive across generations and gives consistent flavour and texture.

Fields of Biotechnology
Where biotechnology is applied

Modern biotechnology spans many fields. The textbook identifies the following key areas:

🩺
Medical Biotechnology

Vaccines, insulin, monoclonal antibodies, gene therapy, diagnostics.

🌾
Agricultural Biotechnology

GM crops, pest resistance, improved yield, drought tolerance.

🏭
Industrial Biotechnology

Enzymes, bioplastics, bioethanol, food processing, textiles.

🌊
Marine Biotechnology

Marine organisms used for drugs, food, and industrial products.

🌿
Environmental Biotechnology

Bioremediation, waste treatment, pollution cleanup.

💻
Bioinformatics

Uses computers to analyse DNA and protein sequences.

🍎
Nutritional Biotechnology

Golden rice, biofortified crops, single-cell proteins.

🏜️
Desert & Dry Regions

Drought-resistant crops and microorganisms for arid areas.

⚔️
Bioterrorism & Bioweapons

Misuse of biology — banned by international law.

⚖️
Ethical & Philosophical Issues

Debates about GMOs, gene editing, cloning, and privacy.

Genetic Engineering
The core of modern biotechnology
📌 Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering is the deliberate modification of an organism's genome using biotechnology techniques. It lets scientists take a gene from one organism and insert it into another — even across species.

Key Tools of Genetic Engineering

  • Restriction enzymes — molecular "scissors" that cut DNA at specific sequences
  • DNA ligase — molecular "glue" that joins DNA fragments together
  • Plasmids — small circular DNA molecules used as vectors to carry genes into cells
  • Vectors — carriers (plasmids, viruses) that deliver genes into host cells
  • Recombinant DNA — DNA that has been artificially combined from different sources
  • Transgenic organisms — organisms that contain genes from another species (GMOs)
🔧
Methods of Gene Transfer
How genes are inserted

Method 1 — Plasmid Method (Bacterial Transformation)

Used to insert genes into bacteria. The bacterium is a natural gene-transfer machine.

Step 1: A plasmid is extracted from a bacterium. Both plasmid DNA and the foreign DNA (containing the gene of interest) are cut with the same restriction enzyme.
Step 2: The foreign DNA is inserted into the plasmid using DNA ligase. The result is a recombinant plasmid.
Step 3: The recombinant plasmid is introduced into a bacterium, which becomes a transgenic bacterium.
Step 4: Bacteria are grown on a medium where only those that picked up the plasmid survive.
Step 5: The selected bacteria multiply and produce the protein the inserted gene codes for. This protein is then harvested and purified.

Method 2 — Agrobacterium Method (Plant Transformation)

Used to insert genes into plants. It exploits a natural plant pathogen called Agrobacterium tumefaciens.

Step 1: The Ti plasmid is removed from Agrobacterium. The T-DNA (transfer DNA) portion is cut with a restriction enzyme.
Step 2: The foreign DNA (gene of interest) is cut with the same enzyme.
Step 3: The foreign DNA is inserted into the T-DNA of the plasmid, forming a recombinant Ti plasmid.
Step 4: The recombinant plasmid is inserted back into Agrobacterium.
Step 5: The Agrobacterium infects plant cells, inserting the T-DNA (with the foreign gene) into the plant chromosome.
Step 6: The transformed plant cells are grown in culture.
Step 7: Whole plants are regenerated from the cultured cells. All their cells carry the foreign gene.

Other Gene Transfer Methods

  • Gene gun (biolistics) — DNA-coated metal particles are shot into plant cells
  • Microinjection — DNA is injected directly into a cell nucleus using a fine needle
  • Electroporation — an electric pulse opens temporary pores in cell membranes so DNA can enter
  • Viral vectors — modified viruses (e.g. retroviruses, adenoviruses) carry genes into cells

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 1.8
What you should remember
  • Biotechnology = using living organisms or their components to make useful products
  • Traditional biotechnology has been used for 6,000+ years (bread, beer, cheese, injera, tej, tella)
  • Modern biotechnology uses genetic engineering to move specific genes between organisms
  • Key tools: restriction enzymes (cut DNA), DNA ligase (join DNA), plasmids (vectors), recombinant DNA
  • Plasmid method = transfer genes into bacteria; Agrobacterium method = transfer genes into plants
  • Main fields: medical, agricultural, industrial, marine, environmental, bioinformatics, nutritional
