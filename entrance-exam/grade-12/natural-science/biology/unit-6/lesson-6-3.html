🛟 Safety Rules / Precautions During Natural Disasters

Natural disasters can strike without warning — but knowing what to do can save lives. This lesson covers the safety rules and precautions for earthquakes, tsunamis, cyclones, and floods, and explores how disaster risk management and mitigation measures can reduce the damage.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Natural disasters are catastrophic events that often occur without warning and disrupt the ecosystem, causing damage to personal lives, property, transportation, and livelihood. While it is never possible to prevent a disaster, the damage can be minimized through timely preparation. Every natural disaster has its own set of precautions to be taken — which must be followed to save precious lives.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define disaster risk management
  • List safety rules for earthquakes (indoor and outdoor)
  • Describe precautions during tsunamis
  • Describe precautions during cyclones and floods
  • Explain the three main climate change mitigation approaches
📌
Disaster Risk Management
MoE Textbook §6.3, p. 317
📌 Disaster Risk Management

Disaster risk management is a comprehensive approach involving the identification of threats through implementation of the proposed mitigation measures.

Key Facts

  • Natural disasters are catastrophic events that often occur without warning
  • They disrupt the ecosystem, causing damage to personal lives, property, transportation, and livelihood
  • It is never possible to prevent a disaster — but damage can be minimized through timely preparation
  • Every natural disaster has its own set of precautions to be taken
📝 Elements of Comprehensive Risk Management

Risk management involves a cycle of activities: prevention → mitigation → preparedness → response → recovery. Each stage reduces the impact of future disasters.

🏚️
Earthquakes
Safety rules during seismic activity
🌍
What Causes Earthquakes?
The shifting of tectonic plates under the earth's crust causes earthquakes, which are responsible for mass destruction.

🏠 If You Are Indoors

  • Take cover under a sturdy table or other piece of furniture, and hold on until the shaking stops
  • Stay away from glass, windows, outside doors and walls, and anything that could fall, such as lighting fixtures or furniture
  • Stay inside until the shaking stops and it is safe to go outside — most injuries occur to people trying to move to a different location inside or trying to leave
  • Do not use elevators

🌳 If You Are Outdoors

  • Stay away from buildings, streetlights, and utility wires
  • Stand in open ground until the shaking stops — it's dangerous to stay directly outside buildings, at exits, and alongside exterior walls
📝 Important Facts

Ground movement during an earthquake is seldom the direct cause of death or injury. Most earthquake-related casualties result from collapsing walls, flying glass, and falling objects.

🌊
Tsunamis
Enormous ocean waves
🌊
What Are Tsunamis?
Tsunamis are a series of enormous ocean waves caused by earthquakes, underwater landslides, or volcanic eruptions. Tsunami waves range from tens to hundreds of feet tall and can travel twenty to thirty miles per hour.

✅ Safety Rules During a Tsunami

  • Turn on your radio/TV to learn and follow precautionary instructions during a tsunami warning — especially if you live near a coastal area
  • Move inland to higher ground immediately and stay there
  • Check for a noticeable recession in water away from the shoreline — this is nature's tsunami warning and should be heeded
  • Stay away from flooded and damaged areas until officials say it is safe to return
  • Keep away from debris in the water — it may pose a safety hazard to boats and people
🌀
Cyclones
Tropical storms
🌀
What Are Cyclones?
Cyclones are tropical storms caused by atmospheric disturbances around a low-pressure area. They are accompanied by strong winds, moving at a speed of sixty-two kmph or more.

✅ Safety Rules During a Cyclone

  • Be alert to the changing weather conditions
  • Listen to radio/TV for the latest information
  • Look for approaching storms
  • Watch for warning signs: a dark, often greenish sky; large hailstones; a large, dark, low-lying cloud (particularly if rotating); and roars similar to a freight train
  • If you see approaching storms or any of the danger signs, be prepared to take shelter immediately
  • If you are under a tornado warning, seek shelter immediately
  • In places with designated cyclone shelters, take refuge there
💧
Floods
Among Earth's most common natural hazards
💧
What Are Floods?
Floods are among the Earth's most common and dangerous natural hazards, formed due to a flow of water on areas of land that are usually dry. Excessive rains can damage nearby dams.

✅ Safety Rules During a Flood

  • Do NOT attempt to walk, swim, or drive through floods — floodwater contains debris and contamination and can be deadly due to fallen electrical lines
  • Stay clear of bridges over fast-moving water
  • Keep an eye out for evacuation alerts
  • Move to higher ground
  • If your vehicle is trapped in flood water and water starts filling inside the car, seek refuge on the roof
🌱
Mitigation Measures
Three main climate change mitigation approaches

Beyond personal safety during disasters, there are three main climate change mitigation approaches that address the root cause of the problem.

⚡
1. Decarbonization

Reduce CO₂ emissions through renewable energy, fuel switching, efficiency gains, nuclear power, and carbon capture storage and utilization.

🌳
2. Carbon Sequestration

Capture CO₂ from the atmosphere through ocean fertilization, ocean alkalinity enhancement, soil carbon sequestration, afforestation and reforestation, and wetland construction.

☀️
3. Radiation Balance

Alter the Earth's radiation balance through the management of solar and terrestrial radiation.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 6.3
What you should remember
  • Disaster risk management = comprehensive approach to identifying threats and implementing mitigation measures
  • Earthquakes: Indoors — take cover under sturdy table, stay away from glass, don't use elevators. Outdoors — stay away from buildings and wires, stand in open ground
  • Tsunamis: Move inland to higher ground, watch for water recession (nature's warning), follow official warnings
  • Cyclones: Watch for warning signs (dark greenish sky, hailstones, low rotating cloud, train-like roar), take shelter immediately
  • Floods: Never walk, swim, or drive through floodwater — it contains debris, contamination, and hidden electrical lines
  • Mitigation approaches: (1) Decarbonization, (2) Carbon sequestration, (3) Managing Earth's radiation balance
  • Key principle: Disasters cannot be prevented, but damage can be minimized through timely preparation
