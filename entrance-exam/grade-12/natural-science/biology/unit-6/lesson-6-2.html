🌊 Effects of Climate Change & Natural Disasters

Climate change doesn't just raise temperatures — it destabilizes the entire Earth system. This lesson explores how a warming planet affects biodiversity, agriculture, forests, human health, and how it fuels more frequent and intense natural disasters.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Climate change destabilizes the Earth's temperature equilibrium and has far-reaching effects on human beings and the environment. As global warming continues, the energy balance of the planet changes — posing significant impacts on humans, animals, plants, and entire ecosystems. It also increases the probability of extreme weather events.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Identify the direct and indirect consequences of climate change
  • Explain how climate change affects biodiversity
  • Describe the effects on agriculture and food security
  • Explain how climate change affects forest productivity
  • Describe the risks of climate change for human health
  • Explain the link between climate change and natural disasters
📌
Direct Consequences of Climate Change
MoE Textbook §6.1.2, p. 311
📌 The Direct Consequences

The direct consequences of man-made climate change include: rising maximum temperatures, rising sea levels, higher ocean temperatures, an increase in heavy precipitation, shrinking of glaciers, and thawing of permafrost.

Direct Effects Explained

  • Rising maximum temperatures — heat waves, record-breaking summer temperatures
  • Rising sea levels — thermal expansion of water + melting ice sheets and glaciers
  • Higher ocean temperatures — affects marine life, coral bleaching, stronger storms
  • Heavy precipitation — more intense rainfall events, leading to floods and landslides
  • Shrinking glaciers — reduces freshwater supplies for millions of people
  • Thawing permafrost — releases additional methane and CO₂ into the atmosphere, creating a feedback loop
🌐
Indirect Consequences
Ripple effects on human societies and ecosystems

The indirect consequences of climate change affect humans and our environment in complex, interconnected ways:

🍞
Hunger & Water Crises

Especially in developing countries, climate change reduces crop yields and access to clean water.

🏥
Health Risks

Rising air temperatures and heat waves cause heat-related illnesses and deaths.

💸
Economic Crisis

Damage from disasters, lost productivity, and food insecurity all hurt economies.

🦠
Spread of Pests & Pathogens

Warmer conditions allow disease carriers (mosquitoes, etc.) to expand their range.

🐘
Loss of Biodiversity

Limited adaptability of flora and fauna means many species cannot survive the change.

🌊
Ocean Acidification

Increased CO₂ concentrations cause higher HCO₃ in water, harming shellfish and coral reefs.

🐘
Effects on Biodiversity
MoE Textbook §6.1.2

Biodiversity provides immense direct benefits to humans — at least 40% of the world's economy is derived from biological resources. Maintaining biodiversity provides:

  • Greater food security
  • Opportunities for economic development
  • A foundation for new pharmaceuticals and medical advances

How Climate Change Harms Biodiversity

  • Climate change is affecting the habitats of several species
  • Species must either adapt or migrate to areas with more favorable conditions
  • Climate change is likely to become the dominant direct driver of biodiversity loss by the end of the century
  • Projected changes in climate, combined with land use change and the spread of exotic/alien species, limit the capability of some species to migrate — accelerating their loss
🌾
Effects on Agriculture
Food security at risk
📌 Agricultural Biodiversity

Agricultural biodiversity refers to all components of biological diversity relevant to food and agriculture. It includes plant genetic resources, crops, wild plants harvested for food, trees on farms, pastures, rangeland species, medicinal plants, and ornamental plants. Animal genetic resources include domesticated animals, wild animals hunted for food, wild and farmed fish, insect pollinators, and microbial and fungal genetic resources.

How Climate Change Hurts Agriculture

Climate change can disrupt food availability, reduce access to food, and affect food quality. Projected increases in temperatures, changes in precipitation patterns, changes in extreme weather events, and reductions in water availability may all result in reduced agricultural productivity.

📝 Ethiopia Example

Failed crops as a result of climate change are already being observed in Ethiopia. Droughts, unpredictable rainfall, and shifting growing seasons directly threaten food security for millions.

🌲
Effects on Forest Productivity
Disturbances on the rise

Climate change could alter the frequency and intensity of forest disturbances:

  • Pest outbreaks — insects often defoliate, weaken, and kill trees
  • Invasive species — non-native species can outcompete native trees
  • Wildfires — warm temperatures and drought conditions trigger fires that can consume millions of acres of forest
  • Storms — more intense storms damage forests
📝 Forest Recovery

These disturbances can reduce forest productivity and change the distribution of tree species. In some cases, forests can recover. In others, existing species may shift their range or die out — and new species of vegetation that colonize the area create an entirely new type of forest.

🏥
Risks for Human Health
A growing concern

Climate change is already having an impact on human health:

  • An increase in the number of heat-related deaths in some regions
  • A decrease in cold-related deaths in other parts of the world
  • Changes in the spread of some water-borne illnesses
  • Shifts in the distribution of disease vectors (e.g., mosquitoes that carry malaria and dengue)
🌪️
Climate Change & Natural Disasters
MoE Textbook §6.2, p. 315
📌 Climate Hazards

Climate hazards are natural events in weather cycles. We have always had hurricanes, droughts, wildfires, high winds, and flooding. However, we are currently witnessing a scale of destruction and devastation that is new and terrifying.

Why Disasters Are Getting Worse

  • With increasing global surface temperatures, the possibility of more droughts and increased intensity of storms is likely to occur
  • As more water vapor evaporates into the atmosphere, it becomes fuel for more powerful storms
  • Warm ocean water provides more energy for hurricanes and cyclones

Key Disaster Types

DisasterCause / MechanismClimate Link
FloodsExcessive rainfall, dam failureHeavy precipitation increases with warming
DroughtsProlonged lack of rainfallShifting rainfall patterns, higher evaporation
WildfiresHeat + dry vegetation + ignitionWarmer temperatures + drought
Storms/CyclonesLow-pressure systems, warm ocean waterWarmer oceans = stronger storms
Sea-Level RiseThermal expansion + ice meltGlobal warming directly causes this
Dust StormsDry soil + high windsDrought conditions worsen dust storms

Melting Ice & Rising Seas

When water warms up, it expands. At the same time, global warming causes polar ice sheets and glaciers to melt. The combination of these changes is causing sea levels to rise — resulting in flooding and erosion of coastal and low-lying areas.

Extreme Weather & Shifting Rainfall

Heavy rain and other extreme weather events are becoming more frequent. This can lead not only to floods and decreasing water quality but also to decreasing availability of water resources in some regions.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 6.2
What you should remember
  • Direct consequences of climate change: rising temperatures, rising sea levels, higher ocean temperatures, heavy precipitation, shrinking glaciers, thawing permafrost
  • Indirect consequences: hunger and water crises, health risks, economic crisis, spread of pests and pathogens, loss of biodiversity, ocean acidification
  • Biodiversity: at least 40% of the world economy is derived from biological resources; climate change will become the dominant direct driver of biodiversity loss
  • Agriculture: climate change disrupts food availability, reduces access to food, and affects food quality
  • Forests: increased pest outbreaks, invasive species, wildfires, and storms reduce forest productivity and change species distribution
  • Human health: more heat-related deaths, changing spread of water-borne illnesses, shifting disease vectors
  • Natural disasters: more droughts and more intense storms; warmer air holds more water vapor which fuels storms
  • Sea-level rise = thermal expansion of water + melting of ice sheets and glaciers
