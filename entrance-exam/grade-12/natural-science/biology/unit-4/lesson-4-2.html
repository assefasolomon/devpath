🦒 Lamarckism

Before Darwin, a French naturalist named Jean-Baptiste Lamarck proposed one of the first comprehensive theories of evolution. His idea — that organisms pass on traits they acquire during their lifetime — was later disproved, but it was a crucial step toward understanding how evolution really works.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Jean-Baptiste de Lamarck was one of the first scientists to propose a naturalistic explanation for how species change over time. His theory — called Lamarckism — was the first comprehensive theory of biological evolution. Even though it was later shown to be wrong in its main mechanism, it stimulated other biologists (including Darwin) to look for the real mechanism of evolution.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Identify who Lamarck was and when he proposed his theory
  • Describe the four postulates of Lamarckism
  • Explain the classic giraffe example
  • List the significance of Lamarckism in the history of science
  • Explain the criticisms that led to its rejection
👨‍🔬
Who was Lamarck?
MoE Textbook §4.2.6, p. 160
📌 Jean-Baptiste De Lamarck (1744–1829)

Jean-Baptiste De Lamarck was a great French naturalist. Lamarck sought a naturalistic explanation for the diversity of modern organisms and the animals seen in the fossil record. He proposed 'the theory of inheritance of acquired characters' in 1809 — 50 years before Darwin published On the Origin of Species.

Historical Context

  • Lived 1744–1829 in France
  • Proposed his theory in 1809 — before Darwin
  • His theory was called "inheritance of acquired characters" or Lamarckism
  • He was trying to explain the diversity of life and the fossil record in naturalistic terms
📋
Four Postulates of Lamarckism
The core of Lamarck's theory

Lamarck's theory has four main postulates:

1
New Needs

Changes in environmental factors — like light, temperature, medium, food, or air — or migration leads to the origin of new needs in living organisms. To fulfil these new needs, organisms have to exert special efforts, changing their habits or behaviour.

2
Use and Disuse of Organs

"The new habits involve the greater use of certain organs to meet new needs, and the disuse or lesser use of certain other organs which are of no use in new conditions."

3
Inheritance of Acquired Characters

Favourable acquired characters are inheritable and are transmitted to the offspring, so that these are born fit to face the changed environmental conditions and their chances of survival increase.

4
Speciation

In every generation, new characters are acquired and transmitted to the next generation. New characters accumulate generation after generation, and after a number of generations, a new species is formed.

📝 The heart of Lamarckism

The core idea is that traits acquired during an organism's lifetime can be passed on to its offspring. For example, if a giraffe stretches its neck to reach high branches, its offspring would inherit a slightly longer neck. This type of inheritance is called Lamarckian inheritance.

🦒
The Giraffe Example
Lamarck's classic illustration

Lamarck used the elongated neck of the giraffe to illustrate his theory. Here's how it works in his model:

1. New Need: Ancestral giraffes lived in areas where the only available food was high up in trees.

2. Use and Disuse: To reach the leaves, giraffes had to stretch their necks — over and over, day after day. The neck became slightly longer through use.

3. Inheritance: The slightly longer neck was passed on to the offspring. Those offspring also stretched their necks, and their necks became even longer.

4. Speciation: Over many generations, the accumulation of longer and longer necks produced the modern giraffe with its characteristic long neck.

The Key Claim

  • A giraffe could, over a lifetime of straining to reach high branches, develop an elongated neck
  • Giraffes that had acquired long necks would have offspring with long necks rather than the short necks their parents were born with
  • Over many generations, this process accumulates
📝 The problem with this explanation

Lamarck could not explain how this might happen. What was the physical mechanism by which a stretched neck could produce genetic changes in the offspring's gametes? At the time, this was unknown — and it turned out to be impossible.

✨
Significance of Lamarckism
Why it mattered

Even though Lamarck's theory was ultimately disproved, it played an important role in the development of evolutionary thought:

  • First comprehensive theory: It was the first comprehensive theory of biological evolution
  • Explained vestigial organs: It nicely explains the existence of vestigial organs in animals due to their continuous disuse
  • Explained adaptive features: It explains the development of strong jaw muscles and claws in carnivores due to their continued extra use
  • Stimulated others: It stimulated other biologists to look for the mechanism of organic evolution
  • Paved the way: It set the stage for Darwin and Wallace's theory of natural selection
📝 The scientific method at work

Lamarck's theory was a scientific hypothesis — it made predictions that could be tested. When genetics was discovered (in the early 20th century), it became clear that acquired traits cannot change the DNA of gametes. This is how science progresses: by proposing, testing, and rejecting hypotheses.

❌
Criticisms of Lamarckism
Why it was disproved

Lamarckism was eventually rejected because of several fundamental problems:

  • No mechanism: Lamarck could not explain how acquired characters are transmitted to the offspring
  • Disproved by genetics: Genetics showed that traits are determined by genes in gametes, not by changes to the body during life
  • Weismann's experiment: August Weismann cut off the tails of mice for 22 generations — and the offspring still had long tails. If acquired traits were inherited, their tails should have become shorter
  • Circumcision: Jewish people have performed circumcision for thousands of years, yet boys are still born with a foreskin — a clear demonstration that acquired traits are not inherited
  • DNA evidence: Modern genetics confirms that only changes to DNA in the gametes (eggs and sperm) can be passed to the next generation

Lamarckism vs Darwinism — Quick Comparison

FeatureLamarckismDarwinism
Source of variationAcquired during lifeInherited from parents (random)
MechanismUse and disuse of organsNatural selection of pre-existing variation
InheritanceAcquired characters inheritedOnly inherited characters passed on

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 4.2
What you should remember
  • Jean-Baptiste De Lamarck (1744–1829) was a French naturalist who proposed the first comprehensive theory of evolution in 1809
  • His theory is called Lamarckism or inheritance of acquired characters
  • Four postulates: new needs, use and disuse, inheritance of acquired characters, speciation
  • The classic example is the giraffe's long neck — acquired by stretching to reach high branches
