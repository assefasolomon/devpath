🧠 The Brain & Spinal Cord

The brain is the most complex organ in the human body — the seat of intelligence, creativity, emotion, and memory. Together with the spinal cord, it forms the central nervous system. This lesson explores their structure, functions, and the protective layers that keep them safe.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

In the last lesson, you learned the nervous system has two main parts: the Central Nervous System (CNS) and the Peripheral Nervous System (PNS). In this lesson, we focus on the CNS — the brain and the spinal cord — the body's command and control centre.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Identify the two main parts of the CNS
  • Describe the structure and function of the brain
  • Explain the three areas of the brain — forebrain, midbrain, hindbrain
  • Describe the roles of the cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem
  • Describe the spinal cord and its functions
  • Explain the protective layers of the CNS — meninges and cerebrospinal fluid
  • Distinguish between grey matter and white matter
📌
The Central Nervous System
MoE Textbook §5.2.1
📌 The Central Nervous System (CNS)

The Central Nervous System (CNS) consists of two main parts: the brain and the spinal cord. It is the body's control centre — it receives information, processes it, and sends out instructions.

🧠
The Brain

Enclosed in the skull (cranium). Controls thoughts, memory, speech, movement, and the function of many organs.

🦴
The Spinal Cord

A column of nerve tissue within the backbone. Carries signals between the brain and the rest of the body; controls reflexes.

🧠
The Brain
Structure and protection

The human brain is a delicate mass of nervous tissue with the consistency of thick yoghurt. It is enclosed in membranes and protected by the bones of a skull in a space known as the cranium.

Cranial Nerves

  • The nerves that come out of the brain are known as the cranial nerves
  • They go mainly to structures in the head, neck, eyes, tongue, and jaws
  • There are 12 pairs of cranial nerves

Grey Matter vs White Matter

  • Grey matter — the darker outer portion in the brain; made up of cell bodies and short relay neurons
  • White matter — the lighter, inner section underneath the grey matter; made mostly of axons
  • Note: in the spinal cord, this is reversed — grey matter is in the middle, white matter on the outside
📝 The amazing brain

The brain is a three-pound organ that controls all functions of the body, interprets information from the outside world, and embodies the essence of the mind and soul. Intelligence, creativity, emotion, and memory are a few of the many things governed by the brain.

🔀
The Three Areas of the Brain
Forebrain, midbrain, hindbrain

As an embryo develops in the womb, the brain starts off as a tube in the head with three main areas. Each of these areas develops into different structures.

🧠
Forebrain

Develops into the olfactory lobes (which deal with smell) and the cerebral hemispheres (which are involved in all the higher levels of thought — coordination, interpretation, and control).

👁️
Midbrain

Develops into the areas of the brain that deal with vision (the optic lobes).

⚖️
Hindbrain

Forms the areas of the brain that deal with balance and orientation (the cerebellum) and the most fundamental reflexes of life (the medulla).

🧩
Cerebrum, Cerebellum, Brainstem
The three main parts of the adult brain

Protected within the skull, the brain is composed of three main parts:

🧠
Cerebrum

The largest part of the brain, composed of right and left hemispheres. It performs higher functions: interpreting touch, vision, and hearing; as well as speech, reasoning, emotions, learning, and fine control of movement.

⚖️
Cerebellum

Located under the cerebrum. Its function is to coordinate muscle movements, maintain posture, and balance.

❤️
Brainstem (Medulla)

Acts as a relay centre connecting the cerebrum and cerebellum to the spinal cord. Performs automatic functions: breathing, heart rate, body temperature, wake and sleep cycles, digestion, sneezing, coughing, vomiting, swallowing.

How the Brain Receives and Uses Information

  • The brain receives information through our five senses: sight, smell, touch, taste, and hearing
  • It assembles the messages in a way that has meaning for us
  • It can store that information in our memory
  • The brain controls our thoughts, memory, speech, movement of the arms and legs, and the function of many organs
📝 The brain has two hemispheres

The cerebrum is divided into left and right hemispheres. In general, the left hemisphere controls the right side of the body, and the right hemisphere controls the left side. The two hemispheres are connected by a bundle of nerve fibres called the corpus callosum.

🦴
The Spinal Cord
MoE Textbook §5.2.2

The spinal cord has a much simpler structure than the brain. It is a tubular structure composed of nervous tissue that extends from the brainstem and continues down the body.

Structure of the Spinal Cord

  • Extends from the brainstem down through the body
  • Tapers at the lower thoracic/upper lumbar region as the conus medullaris (the terminal end)
  • Encased and protected by the vertebrae making up the spine
  • The majority of nerves coming out of the spinal cord are known as the spinal nerves
  • Spinal nerves stretch to the arms, legs, trunk, and the rest of the body

Functions of the Spinal Cord

  • Conducts signals between the brain and the body
  • Contains entrance points for affector (sensory) nerves bringing information into the CNS
  • Contains exit points for effector (motor) nerves carrying instructions from the CNS
  • Controls reflexes — some reflex actions are controlled entirely by the spinal cord

Grey and White Matter in the Spinal Cord

  • In the spinal cord, the grey matter is located in the middle
  • The white matter is found on the outside
  • This is the opposite of the arrangement in the brain
📝 Why this arrangement matters

The outer white matter in the spinal cord carries signals up and down between the brain and the body. The inner grey matter contains the cell bodies and relay neurons that coordinate reflex actions — allowing reflexes to happen without needing the brain.

🛡️
Meninges & Cerebrospinal Fluid
Protecting the CNS

Both the brain and the spinal cord are protected by three layers of membrane called the meninges, plus a cushioning fluid called cerebrospinal fluid.

🛡️
Dura Mater

The thick outermost covering (meninges) of the brain and spinal cord. It's the layers of connective tissue that make up the outermost layer of the three meninges.

🕸️
Arachnoid Mater

Interposed between the dura mater and the pia mater. It is a delicate layer that is not attached to the inside of the dura but lies against it, surrounding the brain and spinal cord.

🎯
Pia Mater

The innermost covering of the spinal cord. It is intimately adhered to the surface and stabilizes the spinal cord through lateral extensions called the denticulate ligaments.

📌 Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF)

The cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is the fluid that flows in and around the hollow spaces of the brain and spinal cord, and between two of the meninges. It cushions the brain and spinal cord against physical shock. CSF is made by tissue called the choroid plexus in the ventricles (hollow spaces) in the brain.

Functions of the Meninges and CSF

  • Protection — cushion the brain and spinal cord against physical shock
  • Support — hold the brain and spinal cord in position
  • Nourishment — CSF supplies nutrients and removes waste products
  • Immunity — the meninges help protect against infections
⚪
Grey vs White Matter — Summary
A confusing but important distinction
FeatureGrey MatterWhite Matter
Made ofCell bodies and short relay neuronsMostly axons (often myelinated)
FunctionProcessing and integrating informationConducting signals over long distances
In the brainOuter layerInner layer
In the spinal cordInner (middle) portionOuter portion
📝 Why they look different

Grey matter looks grey because it contains mostly cell bodies and dendrites (no myelin). White matter looks white because it contains mostly myelinated axons — the myelin sheath gives it a whitish colour.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 5.2
What you should remember
  • The CNS consists of the brain and the spinal cord
  • The brain is protected by the skull (cranium) and the spinal cord by the vertebrae
  • The three embryonic areas of the brain are the forebrain (smell, higher thought), midbrain (vision), and hindbrain (balance, basic reflexes)
  • The adult brain has three main parts: cerebrum (higher functions), cerebellum (balance and coordination), and brainstem/medulla (automatic functions)
  • The spinal cord carries signals between the brain and body, and controls some reflexes
  • The CNS is protected by three meninges (dura mater, arachnoid mater, pia mater) and cushioned by cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)
  • In the brain: grey matter is outer, white matter is inner
  • In the spinal cord: grey matter is inner, white matter is outer (opposite)
  • Grey matter = cell bodies and short relay neurons; white matter = mostly myelinated axons
