⚠️ Drug Side Effects & Unit 5 Review

Effects on the Nervous System
MoE Textbook §5.9

Drugs that affect the nervous system work primarily by altering neurotransmitter levels at synapses, or by mimicking neurotransmitters. This disrupts the normal flow of nerve impulses and can lead to addiction, tolerance, and long-term damage.

How Drugs Affect the Nervous System

  • Stimulants — increase neural activity (e.g. nicotine, cathinone, cocaine, amphetamines)
  • Depressants — decrease neural activity (e.g. alcohol, benzodiazepines)
  • Hallucinogens — distort perception (e.g. THC in cannabis, LSD)

Specific Side Effects

SubstanceEffects on the nervous system
AlcoholSlows neural activity; impairs judgment, coordination, and memory; long-term: kills brain cells, causes memory loss and cognitive decline
NicotineStimulant; releases dopamine; highly addictive; increases heart rate and blood pressure
Khat (cathinone)Stimulant; euphoria and alertness; long-term: depression, fatigue, sleep problems, appetite loss
Cannabis (THC)Affects memory, emotion, and motivation; distorted perception; anxiety and paranoia
SolventsDepressants; brain damage, organ damage, sudden death from heart failure
📝 How addiction works

Repeated drug use causes biochemical changes in the brain. This leads to:

• Tolerance — needing more of the drug for the same effect
• Dependence — the brain can't function normally without it
• Withdrawal — unpleasant symptoms when stopped

⚗️
Effects on the Endocrine System
Hormonal disruption

Effects of Specific Substances

SubstanceEffects on the endocrine system
AlcoholDisrupts blood sugar regulation; lowers testosterone in men; disrupts menstrual cycles in women; causes fetal alcohol syndrome
NicotineAffects adrenal glands (increases adrenaline); reduces fertility in both sexes
KhatReduces appetite; affects the reproductive system; can lower sperm count
CannabisAffects growth, puberty, and reproduction; lowers testosterone; disrupts menstrual cycles
Anabolic steroidsLiver damage, reduced sperm production, masculine characteristics in women

Disruption of Homeostasis

  • Blood glucose — alcohol and other drugs disrupt insulin and glucagon balance
  • Body temperature — many drugs interfere with thermoregulation
  • Water balance — alcohol is a diuretic; increases urine production, leading to dehydration
  • Blood pressure — stimulants raise it; depressants lower it
📝 The take-home message

Drugs disrupt the body's two main control systems — the nervous system and the endocrine system. Because these systems regulate almost every aspect of body function, drug abuse has wide-ranging effects on health, mood, behavior, and long-term well-being.

Key takeaways
What you should remember from Unit 5
  • The nervous system has two main parts: the CNS (brain and spinal cord) and the PNS (nerves)
  • A reflex action is a sudden, automatic, uncontrolled response to a stimulus
  • The brain has three main parts: cerebrum (higher functions), cerebellum (balance), brainstem (automatic functions)
  • The PNS has somatic (voluntary) and autonomic (involuntary) divisions
  • The autonomic system has sympathetic (fight or flight) and parasympathetic (rest and digest) branches
  • Neurons have a cell body, axon, and dendrites; signals cross synapses via neurotransmitters
  • Drug abuse disrupts the nervous and endocrine systems; common substances in Ethiopia are alcohol and khat
  • Sense organs — skin, tongue, nose, eye, ear
  • The endocrine system uses hormones to control the body; major glands: pituitary, thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal, pancreas, gonads, pineal
  • Exocrine glands (sweat, salivary, sebaceous) have ducts; liver and pancreas are both exocrine and endocrine
  • Homeostasis maintains a stable internal environment; the kidney plays a central role
