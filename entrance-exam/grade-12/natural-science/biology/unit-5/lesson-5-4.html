💊 Drug Abuse

Drugs affect how the brain works, how we feel, and how we behave. When used improperly — or abused — they can cause serious health risks, addiction, and even death. This lesson explores the most commonly abused substances in Ethiopia and their effects on the body.

📖 Reading: ~22 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Drug abuse is becoming more of a public health problem in Ethiopia. School surveys have shown that alcohol, khat, tobacco, cannabis (marijuana), and solvents are the substances most widely used. Understanding what these drugs are and how they affect the body is the first step in making responsible choices.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define drug, drug use, drug abuse, and drug dependence
  • Identify the most commonly abused substances in Ethiopia
  • Describe the effects of smoking, alcohol, khat, and cannabis
  • Explain the health consequences of drug abuse
  • Understand the social and economic impacts of drug abuse
📌
What is Drug Abuse?
MoE Textbook §5.4, p. 241
📌 Drug

Drugs are substances that change a person's mental or physical state. They can affect the way the brain works, how you feel and behave, your understanding, and your senses. This makes people, especially young people, develop unpredictable and dangerous behaviour.

Key Terms

💊
Drug Use

When someone uses a drug to the extent that it affects the brain and/or body function and mental activity.

⚠️
Drug Abuse

When a person uses a substance to the point of excess and/or dependence. When drugs are taken in excess, they cause serious health risks and even death.

🔗
Drug Dependence

When a person uses a drug again and again and becomes addicted. Addiction is marked by a change in behaviour caused by biochemical changes in the brain after continued substance abuse.

🚫
Withdrawal Symptoms

A set of unpleasant effects upon the body caused by a sudden stopping of using a drug.

📌 Substance Use Disorder (SUD)

Substance use disorder (SUD) or drug addiction is a disease that negatively affects a person's brain and behaviour. A person can become obsessed with any legal or illegal drugs. Some people can get addicted to certain medications. This addiction gradually starts developing when the individual continues to consume the drug despite the impairment it causes.

📝 Legal vs illegal drugs

Some drugs are legal (e.g. alcohol, khat, tobacco in Ethiopia) and some are illegal (e.g. cannabis, cocaine, heroin). But legality does not mean safety. Legal drugs can still be abused, cause dependence, and lead to serious health problems.

📝 The pattern in Ethiopia

In Ethiopia, alcohol and khat are the most common substances of abuse, followed by cannabis and solvents. Hard drugs such as heroin and cocaine are rarely used. This makes alcohol and khat the main targets for prevention and education efforts.

🚬
Smoking
Tobacco and nicotine

Smoking is not as common in Ethiopia as it is in many parts of the world. However, documented evidence suggests that more and more young people are smoking across the world.

📌 Nicotine

The addictive drug in cigarette smoke is nicotine, which affects the brain and produces a sensation of calm, well-being, and being able to cope. However, it is very physically addictive. Unfortunately, cigarette smoke also contains many very harmful chemicals, and these are linked to a number of very serious health problems.

Harmful Chemicals in Cigarette Smoke

  • Nicotine — highly addictive
  • Tar — a sticky substance that coats and damages the lungs
  • Carbon monoxide — reduces oxygen in the blood
  • Formaldehyde — a preservative also used in embalming
  • Benzene — a known carcinogen (cancer-causing substance)
  • Ammonia — added to increase nicotine absorption
  • Hydrogen cyanide — a toxic gas
  • Heavy metals — including cadmium and lead

Diseases Caused by Smoking

  • Lung cancer — the most well-known consequence of smoking
  • Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) — emphysema and chronic bronchitis
  • Heart disease and stroke — smoking damages blood vessels
  • Cancers of the mouth, throat, oesophagus, bladder, and more
  • Reduced fertility in both men and women
  • Premature ageing of the skin
📝 Second-hand smoke

Smoking also harms non-smokers who breathe in second-hand smoke — particularly children, pregnant women, and the elderly. This is why many countries have banned smoking in public places.

🍷
Alcohol
The most commonly used drug

Alcohol is one of the drugs most commonly used by people of all ages in Ethiopia. Interestingly, however, Ethiopians still drink far less than many other countries.

Short-Term Effects

  • In small amounts, alcohol makes people feel relaxed and cheerful
  • It makes users less inhibited — shy people may feel more confident
  • Slows reaction time and impairs judgment
  • Difficulty walking and speaking
  • Memory loss and blackouts at higher doses

Long-Term Effects

  • Alcoholism — physical and psychological addiction
  • Liver damage — including cirrhosis (scarring of the liver)
  • Brain damage — alcohol kills brain cells and impairs memory
  • Heart disease — increased risk of high blood pressure and stroke
  • Cancers of the mouth, throat, oesophagus, liver, and breast
  • Weakened immune system — greater susceptibility to infections
  • Fetal alcohol syndrome — if a pregnant woman drinks, the baby may have birth defects and developmental problems
📝 Alcohol is addictive and poisonous

Although some religions ban the use of alcohol, many people all over the world drink in defiance of the ban. Perhaps this is because alcohol has been used for thousands of years. But it is important to remember that alcohol has a powerful effect on the human body. It is very addictive and it is also very poisonous.

🌿
Khat
Catha edulis
📌 Khat (Catha edulis)

Khat is a plant grown commonly in the Horn of Africa. The leaves of khat are chewed by people as a stimulant. Its young buds and tender leaves are chewed to attain a state of euphoria and stimulation. Khat contains a drug that affects the brain. It can also be made into a tea.

Effects of Khat

  • It is a mild stimulant that makes people feel happy and reduces their appetite
  • Often used in social situations
  • In Ethiopia, khat use is growing fast among young people
  • It is not yet prohibited legally in Ethiopia

How It Works

  • The drug cathinone from the khat leaves is absorbed into the bloodstream through the membranes lining the mouth and stomach
  • It acts quickly, within 30 minutes, before it is broken down and removed by the liver
  • When addicted people cannot get the drug, they feel depressed, tired, and unable to concentrate
📝 Khat and the economy

Khat has an economic dimension — it is an important cash crop for some Ethiopian farmers. But it also has negative effects on the economy — workers who chew khat may be less productive, and families may spend money on khat instead of food or education. Some studies also show a link between khat use and unprotected sex, which increases the risk of HIV/AIDS infection.

🍃
Cannabis (Marijuana)
Illegal in Ethiopia
📌 Cannabis (Cannabis sativa)

Cannabis is a plant that contains 400 known chemicals, 60 of which are the cannabinoids, unique to the plant. The most potent is delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). THC is known to affect the brain cells responsible for memory, emotion, and motivation.

How It's Used

  • Cannabis is usually smoked
  • It can also be eaten, when it has a much stronger effect because the liver converts it into a more powerful drug

Effects

  • Can make you feel a great sense of well-being and relaxation — happy and euphoric
  • It is a mild hallucinogenic drug
  • Hallucinogens are drugs that produce vivid waking dreams, where the user sees or hears things that are not really there, or has a distorted view of the world
  • However, many people find the effect of the drug very unpleasant and disturbing
  • The effect of cannabis is very variable — it affects different people in different ways
  • Even the same person can react very differently depending on how it is used
📝 Cannabis in Ethiopia

Cannabis is illegal in Ethiopia. In spite of this, there is a long tradition of using it. It is currently used in a number of social contexts. There are many other illegal drugs, but they are not widely used in Ethiopia, though in some places they are becoming more common.

⚠️
Effects on Health & Society
Why drug abuse matters

Health Effects

DrugMain health effects
TobaccoLung cancer, COPD, heart disease, stroke, cancers, reduced fertility, premature ageing
AlcoholLiver cirrhosis, brain damage, heart disease, cancers, fetal alcohol syndrome, weakened immunity
KhatDepression, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, appetite loss, sleep problems
CannabisMemory problems, distorted perceptions, anxiety, paranoia, potential psychological dependence
SolventsBrain damage, organ damage, sudden death (from heart failure)

Social Effects

  • Family breakdown — drug abuse strains relationships and leads to neglect of children
  • Loss of productivity — people who abuse drugs may be unable to work or study effectively
  • Financial problems — money spent on drugs is money not spent on food, education, or healthcare
  • Crime — drug abuse is often linked to criminal activity
  • Spread of disease — sharing needles and risky sexual behaviour under the influence of drugs increase the risk of HIV/AIDS and hepatitis
  • Accidents — driving or operating machinery under the influence of drugs is dangerous

  • Read from different books and other resources about drug addiction, drug dependence, and withdrawal symptoms. Reflect your findings to the class.

  • Can you list some of the relationships between unprotected sex, HIV/AIDS infection, and khat?
  • What are the health and social effects of drug abuse on individuals, families, and communities?

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 5.4
What you should remember
  • Drugs are substances that change a person's mental or physical state
  • Drug abuse = using a substance to the point of excess and/or dependence
  • Drug dependence = addiction; marked by biochemical changes in the brain
  • Most commonly abused substances in Ethiopia: alcohol and khat, followed by cannabis and solvents
  • Smoking — addictive drug = nicotine; causes lung cancer, COPD, heart disease, and other cancers
  • Alcohol — addictive and poisonous; causes liver cirrhosis, brain damage, heart disease, fetal alcohol syndrome
  • Khat — contains cathinone; acts within 30 minutes; causes depression, fatigue, and concentration problems
  • Cannabis — contains THC; affects memory, emotion, and motivation; illegal in Ethiopia
  • Drug abuse has serious health, social, and economic consequences for individuals, families, and communities
