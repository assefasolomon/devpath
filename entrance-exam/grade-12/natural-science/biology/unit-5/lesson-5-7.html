💧 Exocrine Glands

Unlike endocrine glands, which release hormones into the blood, exocrine glands release their products through ducts onto body surfaces. This short lesson introduces the major exocrine glands and how they differ from endocrine glands.

📖 Reading: ~12 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

In the last lesson, you learned about endocrine glands — the ductless glands that secrete hormones directly into the blood. But most glands in the body are actually exocrine glands. These glands secrete their products (enzymes, sweat, saliva, milk, etc.) through ducts onto an epithelial surface.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define exocrine glands
  • Describe the main types of exocrine glands and their functions
  • Explain how the liver and pancreas are both exocrine AND endocrine glands
  • Compare exocrine and endocrine glands
📌
What are Exocrine Glands?
MoE Textbook §5.7, p. 289
📌 Exocrine Glands (MoE Textbook, p. 289)

Many glands in the body are exocrine glands. These glands are glands that secrete substances onto an epithelial surface by way of a duct. This means that they have a special tube or duct that carries the secretion from the gland where it is made to the place where it is needed.

Key Features

  • Have ducts — tubes that carry secretions to where they are needed
  • Secrete onto an epithelial surface
  • Products include enzymes, sweat, saliva, milk, tears, and sebum
  • Secrete chemical substances through ducts into an open space

Endocrine vs Exocrine — Quick Comparison

FeatureEndocrine GlandsExocrine Glands
DuctsNo ducts (ductless)Have ducts
Secretion targetDirectly into the bloodOnto an epithelial surface (via ducts)
ProductsHormonesEnzymes, sweat, saliva, milk, sebum, tears
TransportThrough the bloodstreamThrough ducts to where they are needed
ExamplesPituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas (endocrine part)Salivary, sweat, sebaceous, lacrimal, mammary, pancreas (exocrine part)
📝 Why the difference matters

Endocrine glands use the bloodstream to send hormones to target cells anywhere in the body. Their effects are slow but long-lasting.

Exocrine glands use ducts to deliver their secretions to a specific location (a body surface or cavity). Their effects are local and immediate.

🔬
Major Exocrine Glands
Examples and functions
💧
Sweat Glands

Secrete sweat onto the skin surface through ducts. Help regulate body temperature (thermoregulation) and excrete small amounts of waste.

💦
Salivary Glands

Secrete saliva into the mouth. Saliva contains enzymes (like amylase) that begin the digestion of starch. Also lubricates food for swallowing.

🛢️
Sebaceous Glands

Secrete sebum (oil) into hair follicles. Lubricates skin and hair, and provides a waterproof barrier.

👁️
Lacrimal Glands

Produce tears. Lubricate and clean the surface of the eye, and protect against infection.

🍼
Mammary Glands

Produce milk for feeding infants. Present in both sexes, but only functional in females after pregnancy.

👂
Ceruminous Glands

Produce earwax (cerumen). Traps dust and germs, and lubricates the ear canal.

🔵
Prostate Gland

Secretes a fluid that is part of semen. Aids in the survival and motility of sperm.

🫧
Mucous Glands

Secrete mucus. Line the respiratory, digestive, and reproductive tracts; trap particles and lubricate surfaces.

📝 Sweat glands and temperature control

Sweat glands are a good example of how exocrine glands contribute to homeostasis. When the body gets too hot, sweat glands secrete sweat onto the skin surface. As the sweat evaporates, it carries heat away from the body — cooling the body down.

⚡
Special Cases — Liver & Pancreas
Both exocrine AND endocrine
📌 Dual-Function Glands (MoE Textbook, p. 289–290)

The liver and pancreas are both exocrine AND endocrine glands. They are exocrine because they secrete products such as bile and pancreatic juice into the gastrointestinal tract through a series of ducts. They are also endocrine because they secrete other substances directly into the bloodstream.

The Pancreas — A Perfect Example

FunctionWhat it secretesWhere it goes
Exocrine (99% of mass)Digestive enzymes and fluids (pancreatic juice)Into the small intestine through the pancreatic duct
Endocrine (1% of mass)Insulin and glucagonDirectly into the bloodstream

The Liver

  • Exocrine — produces bile, which is stored in the gallbladder and released into the small intestine via the bile duct to help digest fats
  • Endocrine — produces hormones and releases other substances directly into the blood
  • The liver is one of the most metabolically active organs in the body
📝 Why these glands are unusual

Most glands are either purely endocrine (like the thyroid) or purely exocrine (like the salivary glands). The liver and pancreas are unusual because they have both functions — they can send secretions through ducts (exocrine) AND release hormones directly into the blood (endocrine).

🔀
Types of Secretion
How exocrine glands release their products

Exocrine glands can be classified by how they release their secretions:

TypeHow it worksExample
MerocrineSecretes products via exocytosis — the cell remains intactSweat glands, salivary glands
ApocrineSecretes products by budding off a portion of the cell's cytoplasmMammary glands
HolocrineSecretes products by the entire cell rupturing and releasing its contentsSebaceous glands

Simple vs Compound Glands

  • Simple glands — have a single, unbranched duct
  • Compound glands — have a branched duct; the secretory portion is divided into multiple lobes
📝 Which type is most common?

Merocrine secretion is the most common type. It's the method used by the majority of exocrine glands, including sweat glands, salivary glands, and the pancreas. It has the advantage of being sustainable — the gland cell is not destroyed in the process, so secretion can continue indefinitely.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 5.7
What you should remember
  • Exocrine glands secrete substances onto an epithelial surface via ducts
  • They have ducts (unlike endocrine glands, which are ductless)
  • Their products include enzymes, sweat, saliva, milk, tears, and sebum
  • Examples: sweat glands, salivary glands, sebaceous glands, lacrimal glands, mammary glands, ceruminous glands, prostate gland, mucous glands
  • The liver and pancreas are both exocrine AND endocrine — they have ducts AND secrete hormones into the blood
  • Three types of exocrine secretion: merocrine (exocytosis; most common), apocrine (budding), holocrine (cell ruptures)
  • Exocrine glands can be simple (unbranched duct) or compound (branched duct)
