⚡ Energy & Metabolism

Every living thing needs energy. Energy powers the chemical reactions that keep cells alive, allows organisms to grow and reproduce, and drives the flow of nutrients through ecosystems. In this lesson, we begin exploring how energy is defined, transformed, and used by living systems.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

All living things require energy to carry out life processes. It may seem obvious that cells need energy to grow and reproduce, but even non-growing cells need energy simply to maintain themselves. Cells obtain energy in many forms, but that energy can seldom be used directly to power cell processes. For this reason, cells have mechanisms that convert energy from one form to another.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define energy and its relationship to work and heat
  • Distinguish between potential and kinetic energy
  • Describe the laws of thermodynamics
  • Compare exergonic and endergonic reactions
  • Explain what metabolism means in a cellular sense
📌
What is Energy?
The capacity to do work
📌 Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work or to produce heat. It is what makes things happen — whether it's a cell breaking down sugar, a muscle contracting, or a plant capturing sunlight.

📝 Energy and life

All living things require energy to carry out life processes. Even non-growing cells need energy to maintain themselves. Cells obtain energy in many forms, but energy can seldom be used directly — it must first be converted into a usable form (like ATP). Cells have mechanisms to convert energy from one form to another.

📌 The ordered systems of the cell

The ordered systems of the cell provide the information that makes these energy transformations possible. Because most components of these energy conversion systems evolved very early in the history of life, many aspects of energy metabolism tend to be similar in a wide range of organisms.

🔋
Forms of Energy
Potential vs kinetic

Energy exists in two fundamental forms: potential and kinetic.

🔋
Potential Energy

Stored energy — energy an object has because of its position or chemical structure. Example: chemical bonds in food, water behind a dam.

🏃
Kinetic Energy

Energy of motion — energy an object has because it is moving. Example: a moving muscle, a falling rock, heat (motion of molecules).

Types of Energy in Living Systems

TypeDescriptionExample in cells
Chemical energyStored in chemical bonds (a form of potential energy)Glucose, ATP
Heat (thermal) energyKinetic energy of moleculesBody temperature
Light energyEnergy from the sun (electromagnetic)Captured during photosynthesis
Mechanical energyEnergy of motionMuscle contraction
Electrical energyEnergy from charged particlesNerve impulses
📝 Energy can change form, but not be created or destroyed

In every energy transformation, some energy is converted into heat, which is released into the surroundings. This is why no energy transformation in a cell is 100% efficient.

🌡️
Laws of Thermodynamics
The rules that govern all energy in the universe

Thermodynamics is the study of energy transformations. Two laws govern all energy changes — including those inside living cells.

📌 First Law of Thermodynamics

Energy cannot be created or destroyed — only transformed from one form to another. This is also called the law of conservation of energy. In organisms, the energy in food is transformed into ATP, motion, heat, and other forms, but the total energy is conserved.

📌 Second Law of Thermodynamics

In any energy transformation, the total entropy (disorder) of the universe increases. Energy transformations are never 100% efficient — some energy is always lost as heat. This is why organisms must continuously take in energy to maintain their order.

Key Terms

TermMeaning
Enthalpy (H)Total heat content of a system
Entropy (S)Measure of disorder or randomness — always increases in the universe
Free energy (G)Energy available to do work in a cell; ΔG = ΔH − TΔS
📝 How living things defy disorder (temporarily)

The 2nd law says the universe tends toward disorder. But living things are highly ordered! They stay ordered only by continuously taking in energy and releasing heat to the environment. If energy input stops, an organism quickly falls apart.

🔬
Exergonic vs Endergonic
Energy-releasing vs energy-absorbing reactions

Chemical reactions in cells either release or absorb energy. This is one of the most important concepts in bioenergetics.

FeatureExergonic ReactionEndergonic Reaction
What happensReleases energyAbsorbs energy
ΔGNegative (energy released)Positive (energy absorbed)
SpontaneitySpontaneous (can happen on its own)Non-spontaneous (needs energy input)
Also calledCatabolic (breaking down)Anabolic (building up)
ExampleCellular respiration (glucose → CO₂ + H₂O + energy)Photosynthesis (CO₂ + H₂O + energy → glucose)

Coupled Reactions

In cells, exergonic and endergonic reactions are often "coupled" — the energy released by an exergonic reaction powers an endergonic one. This usually involves ATP as an energy carrier.

  • The first reaction must be exergonic (releases energy)
  • The second reaction is endergonic (absorbs energy)
  • The two occur nearly simultaneously
  • Example: ATP hydrolysis powers muscle contraction
🔄
Metabolism
The sum of all chemical reactions in a cell
📌 Metabolism

Metabolism (from the Greek word for "change") consists of the chemical reactions that change or transform energy in a cell. The reactions of metabolism are organized into step-by-step sequences called metabolic pathways, in which the product of one reaction becomes the starting point or substrate of another.

Metabolic Pathways

  • In a metabolic pathway, a specific molecule is altered in a series of defined steps, resulting in a certain product
  • A specific enzyme — a macromolecule that speeds up a chemical reaction — catalyzes each step
  • Enzymes enable metabolic reactions to proceed fast enough to sustain life
  • Feedback inhibition — where the end product of the pathway inhibits an upstream process — is an important regulatory mechanism in cells

Two Subcategories of Metabolism

FeatureAnabolismCatabolism
MeaningBuild up (synthesis)Break down (degradation)
DirectionSmall molecules → large moleculesLarge molecules → small molecules
EnergyRequires energy (endergonic)Releases energy (exergonic)
ExamplesMaking proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides from smaller unitsGlycolysis, Krebs cycle, breakdown of proteins into amino acids
GoalBuild cell componentsProvide energy and raw materials
📝 Nutrition and metabolism

To maintain a good metabolism rate, nutrition is required. The main goal of nutrition is to keep anabolism always greater than catabolism — that is, to build more than you break down, so the body can grow and repair itself.

Summary
Key takeaways from Lesson 3.1
What you should remember
  • Energy is the capacity to do work or produce heat; living things need it to maintain themselves
  • Potential energy is stored; kinetic energy is motion
  • First Law of Thermodynamics: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed
  • Second Law of Thermodynamics: energy transformations increase entropy (disorder) in the universe
  • Exergonic reactions release energy; endergonic reactions absorb energy
  • In cells, exergonic and endergonic reactions are coupled — often through ATP
  • Metabolism = all chemical reactions in a cell, organized into metabolic pathways where each step is catalyzed by an enzyme
  • Anabolism = build up (requires energy); catabolism = break down (releases energy)
