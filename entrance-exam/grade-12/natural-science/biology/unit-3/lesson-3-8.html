Unit Overview
What you learned

Unit 3 explored how living things capture, store, transform, and use energy. You learned about the molecules that store energy (carbohydrates and lipids), the laws that govern energy transformations (thermodynamics), and the two great pathways that power life on Earth: photosynthesis and cellular respiration.

📝 Critical distinction

α-1,4 bonds (α-glucose) form starch and glycogen → digestible by humans
β-1,4 bonds (β-glucose) form cellulose and chitin → NOT digestible by humans (we lack cellulase)

📝 Saturated vs unsaturated

Saturated fats — no double bonds; solid at room temperature; linked to heart disease
Unsaturated fats — at least one double bond; liquid at room temperature; generally healthier

Photosynthesis — Quick Review
From Lesson 3.5
StageLocationInputsOutputs
Light-dependent reactions Thylakoid membranes Light, H₂O, ADP, NADP⁺ ATP, NADPH, O₂
Calvin cycle (light-independent) Stroma CO₂, ATP, NADPH G3P → Glucose

Three Stages of the Calvin Cycle

  • Carbon fixation — CO₂ + RuBP → 3-PGA (enzyme: rubisco)
  • Reduction — 3-PGA → G3P (using ATP and NADPH)
  • Regeneration — RuBP is regenerated
📝 C3 vs C4 vs CAM

C3 plants (95%) — fix carbon directly (e.g. rice, wheat, teff)
C4 plants (~1%) — use bundle-sheath cells (e.g. corn, sugarcane)
CAM plants (3–4%) — open stomata at night (e.g. cacti, pineapple)

From Lesson 3.6
StageLocationProducts per glucose
GlycolysisCytosol2 Pyruvate + 2 ATP (net) + 2 NADH
Pyruvate oxidationMitochondrial matrix2 Acetyl-CoA + 2 NADH + 2 CO₂
Krebs cycleMitochondrial matrix6 NADH + 2 FADH₂ + 2 GTP + 4 CO₂
Electron transport chainInner mitochondrial membrane~32–34 ATP + H₂O

ATP Yield Summary

  • 1 NADH → ~3 ATP
  • 1 FADH₂ → ~2 ATP
  • 1 GTP → 1 ATP
  • Total per glucose ≈ 36–38 ATP (older textbooks); modern estimates ~30–32
📝 The role of oxygen

Oxygen is the terminal electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain. It combines with electrons and H⁺ to form water. Without O₂, the ETC stops — and the cell must switch to fermentation.

📝 Why fermentation matters

Fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue. Without it, cells would run out of NAD⁺ within seconds and could not produce any ATP anaerobically.

Key takeaways
What you should remember from Unit 3
  • Energy is the capacity to do work; it exists as potential (stored) or kinetic (motion)
  • First Law of Thermodynamics: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed
  • Second Law: energy transformations increase entropy (disorder)
  • Carbohydrates: monosaccharides, disaccharides, polysaccharides; α-1,4 bonds are digestible, β-1,4 bonds are not
  • Lipids: simple, compound, and derived; saturated fats are solid, unsaturated fats are liquid
  • Metabolism: all chemical reactions in a cell; anabolism builds up, catabolism breaks down
  • Photosynthesis: uses light to convert CO₂ + H₂O → glucose + O₂
  • Cellular respiration: breaks down glucose to release ATP (glycolysis → pyruvate oxidation → Krebs → ETC)
  • Fermentation: anaerobic pathway that regenerates NAD⁺; produces only 2 ATP per glucose
