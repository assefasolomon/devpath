📊 Faraday's First Law of Electrolysis

How much copper is deposited when a known current flows for a known time? Faraday's First Law answers this: the mass deposited is directly proportional to the charge passed. One formula, four variables, unlimited applications.

📖 Reading: ~22 min

Introduction
What you will learn

In 1834, Michael Faraday discovered the first quantitative relationship in electrolysis: the mass of a substance deposited or dissolved at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity passed through the electrolyte. This simple law turns electrolysis from a qualitative observation into an exact science.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • State Faraday's First Law
  • Write the formula m = zQ = MIt/nF
  • Define the Faraday constant (F = 96,500 C)
  • Calculate electrochemical equivalent (z) and equivalent weight
  • Solve problems involving mass, current, time, valence, and molar mass
  • Determine any one variable when the others are given
📜
The Law Statement
MoE Textbook, p. 81
📌 Faraday's First Law of Electrolysis

When an electrolyte, either in molten state or solution state, is electrolyzed, the mass of a substance (m) deposited or dissolved at the electrodes is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity (coulombs, Q) passed through the electrolyte.

m ∝ Q   →   m = z Q
where z is the electrochemical equivalent
📌 In words

Double the charge → double the mass deposited. Triple the charge → triple the mass. The relationship is linear with no intercept.

Direct Proportionality — Mass vs Charge
Mass deposited is a straight line through the origin, with slope equal to the electrochemical equivalent z.
🧮
All the Formulas
From simplest to most useful

Faraday's First Law can be written several equivalent ways. Learn the top one; understand the bottom one.

m = z Q
base form — mass = (electrochemical equivalent) × (charge)
m = z I t
since Q = I × t   (current × time)
m = (E / F) I t
since z = E / F   (E = equivalent weight)
m = (M I t) / (n F)
★ most useful — since E = M / n   (M = molar mass, n = valence)

The Four Key Relationships

FormulaUsed when…
m = z QYou know the electrochemical equivalent and total charge
m = z I tYou know current (A) and time (s)
m = E I t / FYou know the equivalent weight E
m = M I t / (n F)You know molar mass M and valency n (most common in exams)
📝 Choosing the right formula

In MoE exams, you're usually given M (molar mass), n (charge on the ion), I (current), t (time). So the formula m = M I t / (n F) is the one you'll use most often.

📌 The Faraday Constant F

F = 96,500 C/mol is the charge on one mole of electrons (Avogadro's number × charge on one electron). This is the fundamental constant of electrochemistry.

📌 Electrochemical Equivalent z

z = E / F = M / (n F)
It is the mass of substance deposited by 1 coulomb of charge. Its units are grams per coulomb (g/C).

📌 Equivalent Weight E

E = M / n
For example, Cu²⁺ (M = 63.5, n = 2) → E = 31.75 g/eq
For Ag⁺ (M = 108, n = 1) → E = 108 g/eq

📝 One mole of electrons = 96,500 C

If you pass 96,500 C through a solution of Ag⁺, you deposit 1 mole of Ag (108 g).
If you pass 96,500 C through a Cu²⁺ solution, you deposit half a mole of Cu (31.75 g) — because Cu²⁺ needs 2 electrons per atom.

📝
Problem-Solving Steps
A repeatable 5-step procedure
  1. Identify the ion being deposited and write its reduction/oxidation half-reaction. This tells you the value of n.
  2. Convert time to seconds — always. If time is given in minutes, multiply by 60; if in hours, multiply by 3600.
  3. Compute the charge Q = I × t (in coulombs).
  4. Apply the formula m = M I t / (n F) or m = z Q, substituting all known values. Use F = 96,500 C/mol.
  5. Check units and significant figures. The answer should be in grams and should be reasonable (a few grams for typical lab-scale currents).
📝 Common pitfalls

• Forgetting to convert time to seconds — the #1 mistake
• Using molar mass M when you should use equivalent weight E (or vice versa)
• Using the wrong value of n (e.g. n = 1 for Cu²⁺ when it should be 2)
• Forgetting that F = 96,500 C (not 96.5 or 965)

Worked Examples
Textbook Example 2.4 + more
Example 1Textbook Example 2.4 — CaCl₂ Electrolysis
Problem: Calculate the mass of calcium metal and chlorine gas that can be produced when a current of 0.452 A is passed through molten CaCl₂ for 1.5 hours.
Step 1
Identify half-reactions in molten CaCl₂ (only Ca²⁺ and Cl⁻ present):
Anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
Cathode: Ca²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Ca(s)
Both n = 2
Step 2
Time in seconds: t = 1.5 × 60 × 60 = 5400 s
Step 3
Charge: Q = I × t = 0.452 × 5400 = 2440.8 C
Step 4a — Ca
M(Ca) = 40.08 g/mol, n = 2
m(Ca) = (40.08 × 0.452 × 5400) / (2 × 96500)
m(Ca) = 97,777 / 193,000 ≈ 0.507 g
Step 4b — Cl₂
M(Cl₂) = 71 g/mol, n = 2
m(Cl₂) = (71 × 0.452 × 5400) / (2 × 96500)
m(Cl₂) = 173,207 / 193,000 ≈ 0.898 g
✅ Mass of Ca = 0.507 g; Mass of Cl₂ = 0.898 g
Example 2Ag Deposition from AgNO₃
Problem: What mass of silver is deposited at the cathode when a current of 2.0 A flows through AgNO₃ solution for 30 minutes? (M(Ag) = 108 g/mol.)
n value
Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag(s) → n = 1
Time
t = 30 × 60 = 1800 s
Formula
m = M I t / (n F)
m = (108 × 2.0 × 1800) / (1 × 96500)
m = 388,800 / 96,500 ≈ 4.03 g
✅ Mass of silver deposited = 4.03 g
Example 3Copper from CuSO₄
Problem: Calculate the mass of copper deposited when 0.5 A is passed through CuSO₄ solution for 3 hours. (M(Cu) = 63.5 g/mol.)
n value
Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu(s) → n = 2
Time
t = 3 × 3600 = 10,800 s
Formula
m = (63.5 × 0.5 × 10800) / (2 × 96500)
m = 342,900 / 193,000 ≈ 1.78 g
✅ Mass of copper deposited = 1.78 g
Example 4Find Current from Mass
Problem: A constant current is passed through CuSO₄ solution for 5.00 hours and deposits 404 mg of copper. What was the current? (M(Cu) = 63.5 g/mol.)
Given
m = 0.404 g, t = 5.00 × 3600 = 18,000 s, M = 63.5, n = 2
Rearrange
I = m n F / (M t)
Substitute
I = (0.404 × 2 × 96500) / (63.5 × 18000)
I = 77,972 / 1,143,000 ≈ 0.0682 A
✅ Current ≈ 0.0682 A (or 68.2 mA)
Example 5Find Time from Mass
Problem: How long must a current of 2.5 A flow through AgNO₃ solution to deposit 10.8 g of silver? (M(Ag) = 108 g/mol.)
n value
Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag → n = 1
Rearrange
t = m n F / (M I)
Substitute
t = (10.8 × 1 × 96500) / (108 × 2.5)
t = 1,042,200 / 270 ≈ 3860 s
Convert
t = 3860 / 60 ≈ 64.3 minutes ≈ 1.07 hours
✅ Time required ≈ 3860 s ≈ 64 minutes

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 2.7
✅ Key Points — Faraday's First Law
  • Faraday's First Law: mass deposited ∝ quantity of electricity passed
  • Base formula: m = z Q   where z = electrochemical equivalent
  • Master formula: m = M I t / (n F)
  • Q = I × t — time must always be in seconds
  • z = E / F = M / (n F) — electrochemical equivalent
  • E = M / n — equivalent weight
  • F = 96,500 C/mol — the Faraday constant
  • n = valence = electrons transferred per ion (1 for Ag⁺, 2 for Cu²⁺, 2 for Ca²⁺, 2 for Cl⁻ → Cl₂)
  • Typical applications: electroplating, refining, calculating deposition mass
