🌡️ Global Warming & Climate Change

Burning fossil fuels releases CO₂. CO₂ traps heat. Heat warms the planet. This is the greenhouse effect — and human activity is amplifying it. This lesson explores the chemistry behind climate change.

Introduction
What you will learn

Climate change is one of the main current concerns and challenges for mankind. Data in our hands reveals the existence of a global warming process, and urgent actions are needed before dramatic and irreversible climate changes could take place. In this lesson we'll explore the chemistry behind global warming and climate change.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain what global warming and climate change are
  • Describe the greenhouse effect — mechanism and significance
  • List the greenhouse gases — CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, O₃, H₂O, fluorinated gases
  • Explain the chemistry of greenhouse gases
  • Identify the main sources and % contribution of each greenhouse gas
  • Understand Ethiopia's Green Legacy initiative and climate action
📌
What is Global Warming?
Definitions
📌 Global Warming

Global warming is the increase in the average temperature of our planet. It's a consequence of the greenhouse effect — the warming effect caused when certain gases accumulate in Earth's atmosphere.

📌 Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns. Many of these shifts are caused by human activities — particularly the burning of fossil fuels — which release greenhouse gases.

📌 The chemistry perspective

Any human activity has a definite impact on the environment. The emission of certain substances to the atmosphere produces a greenhouse effect contributing to global warming. Many of these emitted gases result from:

• Chemical reactions — mainly combustion of fuels: CₓHᵧ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + heat
• Manufactured products — CFCs, solvents, VOCs
• Natural origin — methane from living organisms (especially in oceans)
• Human activities — most well-known is CO₂, from fossil fuel combustion

📝 The scale of the problem

According to data from the US Department of Energy, global emissions of CO₂ resulting from combustion of fossil fuels increased by more than a factor of 10 since 1900.

⚗️
Chemistry & Climate Change
Why chemistry matters
📌 Why chemistry is important in the fight against climate change

Climate change is one of the main current concerns and challenges for mankind. Chemistry plays a central role because:

• Chemistry explains the reactions that produce greenhouse gases
• Chemistry identifies the sources of emissions
• Chemistry develops green alternatives — renewable energy, cleaner fuels, safer chemicals
• Chemistry quantifies the impact through measurements and models

📌 Types of emissions contributing to greenhouse effect

Emissions come from diverse origins:
• Combustion reactions — burning fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas)
• Manufactured products — CFCs, solvents, VOCs
• Natural processes — methane from living organisms
• Human activities — most important: CO₂ from fossil fuel combustion

📝 Some gases have greater effects than CO₂

A variety of other gases contributes to the greenhouse effect, including solvents, chlorofluorocarbons, other volatile organic compounds (VOCs), and nitrogen and sulfur oxides. Some of them can have, intrinsically, an even greater greenhouse effect than CO₂ — but they are released to the atmosphere in much smaller amounts.

🌡️
The Greenhouse Effect
How it works
📌 The greenhouse effect (MoE Textbook, p. 267)

The greenhouse effect happens when certain gases, known as greenhouse gases, accumulate in Earth's atmosphere. These gases warm our planet by absorbing infrared (IR) light radiation that would otherwise escape into space.

The Greenhouse Effect
Solar radiation reaches Earth. Some IR heat escapes; most is absorbed by greenhouse gases and re-radiated back — keeping Earth warm enough for life.

The Numbers That Matter

📌 Earth's temperature with and without greenhouse effect

• Without the greenhouse effect: Average temperature would be 255 K (−18 °C, or on an extremely cold winter day)

• With the greenhouse effect: Average temperature is 288 K (+15 °C, about the temperature on a slightly cool spring day)

• Result: Earth is 33 K warmer than it would be without the greenhouse effect

📌 Which gases contribute to the greenhouse effect?

• CO₂(g) and H₂O(g) absorb IR light and gain energy, transferred to the rest of Earth. Even though present in small amounts, they are very good at absorbing IR — the main contributors

• N₂(g) and O₂(g) are present in very large amounts, but they do not absorb IR light — so they do NOT contribute to the greenhouse effect

📝 The goldilocks zone

33 K (33 °C) may sound like a small number — but it's the difference between a frozen, uninhabitable Earth and one with liquid water, active biology, and human civilization. Without the greenhouse effect, Earth would be uninhabitable. The problem is not the greenhouse effect itself — it's that we're amplifying it.

Greenhouse Gases
The six main players

Greenhouse gases include:

🌫️
Carbon Dioxide
CO₂
From fuel combustion, deforestation, and cement production. Largest single contributor to global warming.
🐄
Methane
CH₄
From livestock, agriculture, sewage treatment, natural gas and oil distribution, coal mining, waste tips.
🌱
Nitrous Oxide
N₂O
From fertilizers, fuel use, chemical production, and sewage treatment. Lasts up to 114 years in the atmosphere.
⚡
Tropospheric Ozone
O₃
Product of reactions between CO, NO₂, and VOCs from burning fuels. Short-lived in atmosphere (months at most).
💧
Water Vapor
H₂O
Natural greenhouse gas. Very effective at absorbing IR. Its concentration is largely controlled by temperature.
🧊
Fluorinated Gases
CFCs & HCFCs
From refrigeration, air conditioning, electrical equipment, medicine, metallurgy. Duration ranges from months to tens of thousands of years, depending on the compound.
📝 Important distinction

Natural greenhouse gases — CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, O₃, H₂O — exist as part of the natural carbon and nitrogen cycles. Their concentrations are increasing due to human activity, which is the problem.

Man-made gases — CFCs, HCFCs, and other fluorinated gases — do not occur in nature and are entirely produced by humans.

📝 Why CO₂ dominates

CO₂ accounts for over half (52.92%) of the human contribution to global warming. Reasons:
• It's produced by every combustion of fossil fuels
• It has a long atmospheric lifetime — most of it lasts 200 years
• It's released in enormous quantities — over 20 billion tonnes per year

Methane has a stronger warming effect per molecule — but its short lifetime (12 years) and lower emission volumes make its total contribution smaller.

🔥
Combustion Chemistry
Where the CO₂ comes from

The main source of anthropogenic CO₂ is the combustion of fossil fuels — coal, oil, and natural gas. All combustion reactions follow the same general pattern:

General Combustion of a Hydrocarbon
CₓHᵧ + (x + y/4)O₂ → x CO₂ + (y/2) H₂O + heat energy
Combustion of coal (carbon)
C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)
Combustion of methane (natural gas)
CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(g)
Combustion of octane (gasoline)
2C₈H₁₈(l) + 25O₂(g) → 16CO₂(g) + 18H₂O(g)
📝 Why combustion is exothermic

Combustion releases energy in the form of heat because the products (CO₂ + H₂O) have lower chemical potential energy than the reactants (fuel + O₂). That energy is exactly what makes fossil fuels so useful — and exactly what makes them so dangerous to the climate.

Ethiopia & Climate Action
The Green Legacy
📌 Ethiopia's Green Legacy Initiative

In recent years, Ethiopia has launched the Green Legacy program — a massive national tree-planting campaign. Its goal is to combat desertification, restore degraded land, and capture CO₂ from the atmosphere.

📌 How tree-planting fights climate change

Trees absorb CO₂ from the atmosphere through photosynthesis:
6CO₂ + 6H₂O —[sunlight]→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

The carbon is stored in the tree's biomass — trunk, branches, roots — and in the soil. This is called a carbon sink. As long as the trees live, the carbon stays out of the atmosphere.

📌 Other methods to capture CO₂

• Afforestation and reforestation — planting trees
• Soil carbon sequestration — no-till farming, cover crops
• Ocean absorption — oceans dissolve CO₂ naturally (but become more acidic)
• Direct air capture (DAC) — industrial machines that pull CO₂ from air
• Carbon capture and storage (CCS) — capturing CO₂ at power plants before release and storing underground
• Blue carbon — coastal ecosystems like mangroves that store carbon

📝 Ethiopia's climate leadership

Ethiopia has been a vocal voice in international climate negotiations (including the Copenhagen summit), advocating for developing countries that are often the most vulnerable to climate change while contributing the least to global emissions. Ethiopia's 2015 Climate Commitment set a high bar for national climate action.

Worked Examples
5 practice problems
Example 1Greenhouse vs Non-Greenhouse
Problem: Explain why N₂ and O₂ — despite making up 99% of the atmosphere — do NOT contribute to the greenhouse effect, while trace gases like CO₂ and H₂O do.
Key principle
The greenhouse effect requires gases that absorb IR radiation
N₂ and O₂
Both are diatomic homonuclear molecules — they do NOT absorb IR light (no change in dipole moment during vibration)
CO₂ and H₂O
Both are polar molecules with IR-active vibrations — they readily absorb and re-emit IR radiation
Conclusion
Even though CO₂ and H₂O are present in smaller amounts, they matter far more for the greenhouse effect than N₂ or O₂
✅ Greenhouse effect requires IR-absorbing molecules. N₂ and O₂ do not absorb IR; CO₂ and H₂O do.
Example 2The 33 K Difference
Problem: Earth's actual average temperature is 288 K (15 °C). Without the greenhouse effect, it would be 255 K (−18 °C). Calculate the difference and explain what it means.
Difference
288 − 255 = 33 K
What it means
The greenhouse effect warms Earth by 33 °C — making liquid water and life possible
If amplified
Adding more GHGs → more warming → climate change and disruption
✅ 33 K difference — the greenhouse effect is essential, but amplification is dangerous

Example 4Balance a Combustion Reaction
Problem: Write and balance the complete combustion of methane (CH₄). Explain why this reaction contributes to global warming.
Reactants
CH₄ + O₂ → ?
Products
Complete combustion of hydrocarbons gives CO₂ + H₂O
Balance C
1 C on left → 1 CO₂ on right
Balance H
4 H on left → 2 H₂O on right
Balance O
4 O on right → 2 O₂ on left
✅ CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. The CO₂ produced accumulates in the atmosphere, adding to global warming.

Problem: Explain how Ethiopia's Green Legacy (tree-planting) helps combat global warming, using the chemistry of photosynthesis.
Photosynthesis
6CO₂ + 6H₂O —[sunlight]→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
Carbon capture
