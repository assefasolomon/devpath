🏭 Environmental Pollution

Pollution takes three main forms: air, water, and land. Each has its own sources, its own chemistry, and its own remedies. This lesson breaks down the pollutants, the damage they cause, and the methods we use to reduce them.

📖 Reading: ~25 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources

Introduction
What you will learn

Pollution is the dark side of industrial progress. When chemicals enter the environment faster than natural cycles can process them, they accumulate and cause harm. In this lesson we'll examine the three main types — air, water, and land pollution — and the methods we use to fight each.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define pollution and pollutant
  • Identify the major air pollutants and their effects
  • Explain water pollution — eutrophication, sewage, fertilizers, pesticides
  • Explain land pollution — plastics, garbage, mercury
  • List methods to reduce each type of pollution
  • Recognize how the three types of pollution are interconnected
📌
What is Pollution?
Definitions
📌 Pollution (MoE Textbook, p. 256)

Pollution is any discharge of a solid, liquid, or gaseous substance or radiation (energy) into an environment that causes unwanted changes. It causes short-term or long-term harm that affects the Earth's ecological balance and lowers the quality of life in the environment.

📌 Pollutant

A pollutant is any substance that changes air, water, or any other natural resource in a way that impairs the use of the resource. Pollutants are discharged into the environment as a result of:

• Natural events — like a volcanic eruption
• Human activities — like chemical industries, agriculture, transportation

📌 Three types of pollution

Pollutants can be classified by the type of pollution they cause:
1. Air pollution
2. Water pollution
3. Land pollution

📝 Pollution anywhere threatens everywhere

Air pollution released over one country can fall as acid rain in another. Water pollution in a river can contaminate the ocean. Land pollution can leach into groundwater. Air pollution anywhere is a potential threat elsewhere.

🌬️
Air Pollution
MoE Textbook, p. 256
📌 Definition

Air pollution is caused by the presence of contaminant gaseous substances in the air that affect the lives of plants and animals on Earth.

📌 Composition of the atmosphere as a "solution"

The atmosphere is considered a solution. Since N₂ is the molecule present in greatest amount, N₂ is the "solvent" — and O₂ is the solute present in largest amount.

Atmospheric Gases by Volume

GasPercent by volume
Nitrogen (N₂)78%
Oxygen (O₂)20.95%
Argon (Ar)0.93%
Gaseous water (H₂O vapor)0.08%
Carbon dioxide (CO₂)0.04% and increasing
📝 Why CO₂ is a worry

CO₂ is only 0.04% of the atmosphere — but it's increasing. Because CO₂ is a greenhouse gas, that small percentage matters enormously for global climate.

⚠️
Major Air Pollutants in Detail
Sources, effects, chemistry
🟡
Sulfur Dioxide
SO₂
From combustion of coal and petroleum, and metal extraction from sulfide ores. Causes coughing, chest pains, shortness of breath. Thought to cause bronchitis and lung diseases. Slowly oxidizes to SO₃ in air.
🟠
Nitrogen Oxides
NO and NO₂
Formed by natural processes (thunderstorms) and by combustion of fossil fuels. NO catalyzes ozone decomposition. Contributes to acid rain and photochemical smog.
⚫
Carbon Monoxide
CO
From incomplete combustion of fuel. Prolonged exposure impairs vision, produces headaches, strains the heart. Reduces oxygen-carrying capacity of blood by reacting with hemoglobin.
💨
Hydrocarbons & Ozone
CₓHᵧ, O₃
Responsible for photochemical smog — a reddish-brown haze irritating to eyes, nose, and lungs. Causes extensive damage to vegetation.
🌫️
Particulates
Dust, ash, smoke
From dust storms, forest fires, volcanic eruptions, mining, and burning coal/oil. Industrial areas contain particles of limestone, fertilizers, coal, cement, silica. Irritate the lungs and affect breathing.
🧊
Chlorofluorocarbons
CFCs
Used as refrigerants, solvents, plastic foam-blowing agents. Release Cl· free radicals that destroy ozone. Depletion of ozone layer → allows dangerous UV radiation → skin cancer.
🧴
Volatile Organic Compounds
VOCs
Released from burning gasoline, wood, natural gas, coal. Also from thinners, paints, cigarettes, solvents, air fresheners, cleaners, pesticides.
⚡
PCBs
Polychlorinated biphenyls
Organic chlorine compounds once widely used as coolants, dielectric fluids in electrical equipment, heat transfer fluids. Persistent and toxic.
🔥
Excess CO₂
CO₂
From burning coal and petroleum for electricity, vehicles, heating, industry. Releases about 20 billion tonnes of CO₂ annually. Causes rise in global temperature via greenhouse effect.
🧪
Heavy Metals (Lead)
Pb
From vehicle engines using fuels with tetraethyl lead, and from lead paints. High levels damage brain, kidneys, and liver.

Key Atmospheric Reactions

NO catalyzes ozone decomposition in upper atmosphere
NO + O₃ → O₂ + NO₂ (net: 2O₃ → 3O₂)
NO oxidized by O₂ in UV light
2NO(g) + O₂(g) —[UV]→ 2NO₂(g)
Acid rain formation
SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃; SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄; NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃
CFC decomposition and ozone destruction
CF₂Cl₂ —[UV]→ CF₂Cl· + Cl·   |   Cl· + O₃ → ClO + O₂
📝 Effects of acid rain

• Accelerates corrosion and deterioration of metals, buildings, statues
• Causes tree defoliation (loss of leaves)
• Releases heavy metal ions from soil into water courses
• Causes drop in pH of rivers and lakes → kills fish

📝 The logic behind these methods

All these methods aim to either reduce emissions at the source (public transport, filters on chimneys, less electricity) or absorb existing pollutants (afforestation). Filters on chimneys capture particulates before they leave the factory.

Water Pollution
MoE Textbook, p. 260
📌 Definition

Water pollution is the degradation of the quality of water brought about by the discharge of untreated sewage, industrial and agricultural waste, and oil spillage. Water is polluted if it contains matter that affects the health of living things or causes damage to property.

📌 Major water pollutants

• Nitrate and phosphate fertilizers washed out of the soil
• Phosphate detergents
• Untreated sewage
• Insecticides and herbicides
• Heavy metal ions
• Acidic and/or basic residues released by industrial processes

The Eutrophication Sequence

Phosphate and nitrate fertilizers washed from soil and phosphate detergents from untreated water enter streams, rivers, lakes, and seas. These dissolved minerals are valuable nutrients for plants — and their discharge accelerates the growth of surface-water plants, such as algae.

Eutrophication — A Chain of Events
📌 Eutrophication

This sequence of events — nutrient enrichment → algal bloom → decomposition → oxygen depletion → aquatic animal death — is called eutrophication.

Major Water Pollutants

PollutantEffects
Domestic sewageMajor source of plant nutrients (nitrates and phosphates). Excess nutrients promote algae growth → oxygen decline during decomposition.
Solid wasteGarbage, rubbish, e-waste, trash, construction waste — from residential, commercial, institutional, and industrial activities.
FertilizersExcess nitrates and phosphates washed out from food and animal waste, and from inorganic fertilizers.
Insecticides & herbicidesWashed into lakes, rivers, streams, seas. Many do not decompose easily — persistent in the environment.
Lead (Pb)Dissolves readily in water; upon uptake causes devastating health problems — brain, kidney, and liver damage.

Methods of Reducing Water Pollution
MoE Textbook, p. 261
🏭

Treat water before discharge into rivers and lakes

🌡️

Avoid unnatural temperature changes — don't discharge heat-laden water

♻️

Recycle industrial and agricultural wastes

🌱

🪨
Land Pollution
MoE Textbook, p. 262
📌 Definition

Land pollution is caused by things we put into it. It results from the spillage of oil, leaching of harmful chemicals and heavy metal ions, and dumping of non-biodegradable wastes such as plastics.

Causes of Land Pollution

📌 Five major causes

• Spillages of oil from leaking pipelines
• Harmful heavy metal ions from buried waste leaching into water systems
• Leaching of harmful chemicals from corroded metal drums buried underground
• Dumping of non-biodegradable wastes (plastics) — remain unchanged in soil for decades or hundreds of years. Accumulation hinders air and water movement and affects plant growth.
• Excessive use of synthetic fertilizers in agricultural activities

Major Land Pollutants

PollutantDescription
GarbageOne of the most significant land pollution causes. Tons produced by households annually. When disposal falls short, garbage is dumped onto land — known as a landfill.
PlasticOne of the most common environmental pollutants and bears one of the greatest impacts. The abundance of plastics led to coining of the term "plastic pollution."
MercuryReleased into the environment due to mining activities and poor disposal of items made with mercury. Batteries are the main source of mercury — dispose of them carefully.
📝 Why non-biodegradable waste is the hardest problem

Non-biodegradable wastes cannot be broken down by decomposers. Examples: glass, medical waste, plastic, man-made synthetic materials, electronic components, cans, nuclear waste, man-made polymers, artificial rubber. Because they persist for centuries, their disposal poses a major problem.

🛡️
Methods of Reducing Land Pollution
Focusing on non-biodegradable waste

When we deal with methods of reducing land pollution, we deal especially with reducing non-biodegradable wastes. This is because non-biodegradable wastes cannot be broken down by decomposers — their disposal poses a big problem.

📌 Why non-biodegradable waste matters

Non-biodegradable waste is a major source of soil, air, and water pollution. It's cited as the primary cause of serious diseases such as cancer.

📌 Examples of non-biodegradable waste

Glass · Medical waste · Plastic · Man-made synthetic materials · Electronic components · Cans · Nuclear waste · Man-made polymers · Artificial rubber

Worked Examples
5 practice problems
Example 1Classify a Pollutant
Problem: Classify each of the following as an air, water, or land pollutant: (a) SO₂, (b) nitrate fertilizers, (c) plastic bags, (d) CO, (e) mercury.
(a) SO₂
Air pollutant — from burning coal
(b) Nitrate fertilizers
Water pollutant — washes into streams, causes eutrophication
(c) Plastic bags
Land pollutant — non-biodegradable, persists for centuries
(d) CO
Air pollutant — from incomplete combustion
(e) Mercury
Land pollutant — from mining and batteries
✅ Air: SO₂, CO | Water: nitrate fertilizers | Land: plastics, mercury
Example 2Why CO is Dangerous
Problem: Explain why carbon monoxide (CO) is dangerous to human health. Why is it more dangerous than CO₂?
Mechanism
CO binds to hemoglobin in blood — the same molecule that carries oxygen
Effect
This reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood → impairs vision, produces headaches, strains the heart
Why worse than CO₂
CO₂ is a natural part of the carbon cycle at low levels. CO directly blocks oxygen transport and is toxic at much lower concentrations
✅ CO binds hemoglobin and blocks O₂ transport — far more immediately toxic than CO₂
Example 3Eutrophication Sequence
Problem: List the five steps of eutrophication in order, starting with fertilizer runoff.
Step 1
Fertilizers (nitrates + phosphates) washed from soil into water bodies
Step 2
Dissolved nutrients accelerate growth of surface-water plants (algal blooms)
Step 3
Less light reaches bottom plants → they die off
Step 4
Dead plants rot; bacteria multiply and consume dissolved oxygen
Step 5
Oxygen depletes → fish and aquatic animals die
✅ Fertilizers → Algal bloom → Plants die → Decomposition consumes O₂ → Fish die
Example 4Why Non-Biodegradable Waste is Worst
Problem: Explain why non-biodegradable waste is considered the most serious form of land pollution.
Definition
Non-biodegradable = cannot be broken down by decomposers
Consequence
Persists in soil for decades or hundreds of years
Physical effect
Accumulation hinders air and water movement in soil → affects plant growth
Multi-sphere effect
Also causes soil, air, AND water pollution. Cited as the primary cause of serious diseases like cancer.
✅ Non-biodegradable waste is the hardest because it never breaks down — it just accumulates
Example 5How the Three Types Interconnect
Problem: Give an example showing how air, water, and land pollution are interconnected.
Start — Air
Coal power plant releases SO₂ into the air
To Water/Land
SO₂ oxidizes to SO₃ → forms H₂SO₄ → falls as acid rain
Water effects
Acid rain lowers pH of lakes/rivers → fish die
Land effects
Acid rain dissolves limestone in buildings and statues; releases heavy metal ions from soil
Biological effects
Trees defoliate; heavy metals enter food chain
✅ A single pollutant (SO₂) can affect all three spheres — air, water, and land

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 5.2
✅ Key Points — Environmental Pollution
  • Pollution = discharge of solid, liquid, gaseous substance or radiation causing unwanted changes
  • Pollutants come from natural events (volcanoes) or human activities (industry, agriculture, transport)
  • Three types of pollution: air, water, and land
  • Atmosphere composition: 78% N₂, 20.95% O₂, 0.93% Ar, 0.04% CO₂ (and increasing)
  • Air pollutants: SO₂ (bronchitis, lung disease), NOₓ (ozone destruction, smog), CO (binds hemoglobin, reduces O₂ transport), hydrocarbons + O₃ (photochemical smog), particulates (lung irritation), CFCs (ozone depletion), VOCs, PCBs, excess CO₂ (global warming), lead (brain, kidney, liver damage)
  • Acid rain: SO₂ → H₂SO₃; SO₃ → H₂SO₄; NO₂ → HNO₃. Corrodes buildings, defoliates trees, kills fish.
  • Reducing air pollution: public transport, turn off lights, recycle, avoid plastic bags, filters on chimneys, afforestation, use fans not AC
  • Water pollutants: nitrates + phosphates (fertilizers), detergents, sewage, pesticides, heavy metals, industrial residues
  • Eutrophication sequence: nutrients → algal bloom → plants die → decomposition consumes O₂ → fish die
  • Reducing water pollution: treat water before discharge, avoid thermal pollution, recycle wastes, use moderate chemicals
  • Land pollutants: garbage (landfills), plastics (plastic pollution), mercury (batteries)
  • Non-biodegradable waste is the hardest problem — never decomposes, causes soil/air/water pollution
  • Reference: MoE Chemistry Grade 12 Student Textbook, §5.2 (pp. 255–264)
