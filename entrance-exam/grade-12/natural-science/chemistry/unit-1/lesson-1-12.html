12.1 Acid-Base Chemistry in Biology & Medicine
Why pH control keeps us alive

Blood pH — Tightly Buffered at 7.4

📌 The Blood Buffer System

Human blood is buffered at pH 7.35–7.45. Deviations of just 0.4 units can be fatal. The principal buffer is the carbonic acid/bicarbonate system:

H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻

🫁
Respiration Control
Breathing rate controls CO₂ levels → adjusts H₂CO₃ concentration → keeps pH stable.
🫘
Kidney Regulation
Kidneys excrete excess H⁺ or HCO₃⁻ to fine-tune blood pH over hours/days.
🩸
Hemoglobin Buffer
Hemoglobin protein also helps buffer pH inside red blood cells.
📝 When things go wrong

Acidosis: blood pH < 7.35 — caused by diabetes, kidney disease, or respiratory failure.
Alkalosis: blood pH > 7.45 — caused by hyperventilation or excessive antacid use.

Stomach Acid — pH 1.5

📌 Why So Acidic?

The stomach secretes HCl, creating a pH of ~1.5. This extreme acidity:

  • Kills most bacteria in food
  • Activates the enzyme pepsin (needs pH 1–3 to work)
  • Denatures proteins for digestion
📝 Antacids — Chemistry in Action

Antacids neutralize excess stomach acid. Common antacids and their reactions:

• Mg(OH)₂: Mg(OH)₂ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + 2H₂O
• CaCO₃: CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
• NaHCO₃: NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂

Cell Cytoplasm — pH 7.2

📌 The Phosphate Buffer System

Inside cells, the H₂PO₄⁻ / HPO₄²⁻ buffer maintains pH around 7.2:

H₂PO₄⁻ ⇌ H⁺ + HPO₄²⁻

Medical Applications

💉
IV Fluids
Saline and lactate solutions are pH-buffered to match blood pH.
🧪
Blood Tests
Doctors monitor blood pH to detect respiratory or metabolic disorders.
💊
Drug Delivery
Many drugs are weak acids/bases — their absorption depends on body pH.

12.2 Environmental Acid-Base Chemistry
Acid rain and its effects

Acid Rain Formation

📌 What is Acid Rain?

Acid rain is precipitation with pH < 5.6 (natural rain is ~pH 5.6 due to dissolved CO₂). It forms when sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and nitrogen oxides (NOₓ) from burning fossil fuels react with water vapor in the atmosphere.

Chemical Reactions of Acid Rain

Step 1 — SO₂ oxidation:
2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃

Step 2 — SO₃ reaction with water:
SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid)

From NOₓ:
2NO₂ + H₂O → HNO₃ + HNO₂ (nitric + nitrous acids)

Sources of Acid Rain

SourcePollutant Released
Coal power plantsSO₂ and NOₓ
Vehicle exhaustNOₓ
Industrial smeltingSO₂
Volcanic eruptionsSO₂ (natural)

Effects of Acid Rain

🌳
Forest Damage
Leaches nutrients from soil, damages leaves, weakens trees.
🐟
Lake Acidification
pH below 5 kills fish and aquatic life.
🏛️
Building Corrosion
Dissolves limestone (CaCO₃) in statues and buildings.
📝 Limestone corrosion reaction

CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂
This reaction slowly dissolves marble statues, limestone buildings, and monuments.

12.3 Industrial, Agricultural & Household Applications
Chemistry you use every day
🧼
Soap Making (Saponification)
Fat + NaOH → soap + glycerol. The soap's –COO⁻Na⁺ makes it a weak base in water.
🌾
Fertilizers
Ammonium salts (NH₄NO₃, (NH₄)₂SO₄) make soil acidic through hydrolysis of NH₄⁺.
🥫
Food Preservation
Vinegar (acetic acid), citric acid, and lactic acid lower pH to inhibit bacteria.
🧴
Cleaning Products
Toilet bowl cleaners (HCl), drain cleaners (NaOH), and window cleaners (NH₃).
🧪
Chemical Industry
Sulfuric acid is the world's most produced chemical — used in fertilizers, dyes, batteries.
🍷
Wine Making
Yeast fermentation produces ethanol; pH control prevents spoilage and controls taste.
🧂
Pool Chemistry
pH of pool water kept at 7.2–7.8 — chlorine works best in this range.
🔋
Batteries
Car batteries use sulfuric acid as the electrolyte (H₂SO₄, ~35% concentration).

Ethiopian Context

📝 Acid-base chemistry in Ethiopia

• Rift Valley soda lakes (Lake Shala, Lake Abijata) have pH 9–10 due to dissolved Na₂CO₃ and NaHCO₃
• Tella and Tej (traditional Ethiopian beverages) rely on fermentation chemistry and pH control
• Ethiopian agriculture uses lime (CaCO₃) to neutralize acidic soils
• Cement industry uses CaCO₃ as a key raw material

Buffers

📌 Henderson-Hasselbalch Equations

• Acidic buffer: pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])
• Basic buffer: pOH = pKb + log([HB⁺]/[B])
• Maximum buffer capacity when pH = pKa

Titration Essentials

📌 Key Titration Formulas

• N₁V₁ = N₂V₂ (normality-based)
• M₁V₁/n₁ = M₂V₂/n₂ (molarity-based)
• Equivalence point: mol H⁺ = mol OH⁻

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Blood pH and the Buffer System
Problem: A patient's blood pH is measured at 7.25, indicating acidosis. Explain what could cause this and how the body would respond.
What is acidosis?
Blood pH below 7.35 — too many H⁺ ions in the blood
Causes
• Diabetic ketoacidosis (excess keto acids)
• Kidney failure (can't excrete H⁺)
• Respiratory depression (CO₂ builds up)
Body response
• Increase breathing rate (blows off CO₂, shifts equilibrium left)
• Kidneys excrete H⁺ and reabsorb HCO₃⁻
• Buffer: H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ → H₂CO₃ → CO₂ + H₂O
✅ Acidosis is treated by addressing the cause and supporting the buffer system (e.g., IV bicarbonate in severe cases)
Example 2Acid Rain Chemistry
Problem: Explain how SO₂ from coal burning leads to acid rain, including all chemical reactions.
Step 1
S + O₂ → SO₂ (burning coal with sulfur impurities)
Step 2
2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃ (catalyzed by atmospheric particles)
Step 3
SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid forms in clouds)
Final
H₂SO₄ falls as acid rain, corroding limestone: CaCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂
✅ Acid rain pH can reach 3–4, damaging ecosystems and buildings
Example 3Antacid Neutralization
Problem: An antacid tablet contains 500 mg of CaCO₃. What volume of 0.10 M HCl can it neutralize? (Molar mass CaCO₃ = 100 g/mol.)
Reaction
CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
Moles CaCO₃
n = 0.500 g / 100 g/mol = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol
Moles HCl
2 HCl per CaCO₃ → n(HCl) = 2 × 5.00×10⁻³ = 1.00 × 10⁻² mol
Volume
V = n / M = 1.00×10⁻² / 0.10 = 0.10 L = 100 mL
✅ The tablet neutralizes 100 mL of 0.10 M HCl
Example 4Fertilizer and Soil pH
Problem: Why does the use of ammonium-based fertilizers (like (NH₄)₂SO₄) acidify soil over time?
Dissociation
(NH₄)₂SO₄ → 2NH₄⁺ + SO₄²⁻
Hydrolysis
NH₄⁺ + H₂O ⇌ NH₃ + H₃O⁺ (NH₄⁺ is a weak acid)
Result
Repeated application produces excess H₃O⁺ → soil pH drops
Solution
Farmers add lime (CaCO₃) to neutralize the acid
✅ NH₄⁺ hydrolysis produces H₃O⁺, acidifying the soil
Example 5Soap as a Weak Base
Problem: Why are soap solutions basic (pH ~9–10)?
Structure
Soap is a sodium salt of a long-chain fatty acid: R-COO⁻Na⁺
In water
R-COO⁻Na⁺ → R-COO⁻ + Na⁺ (complete dissociation)
R-COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ R-COOH + OH⁻ (hydrolysis!)
Result
The carboxylate ion (R-COO⁻) is the conjugate base of a weak acid → it hydrolyzes to give OH⁻ → basic solution
✅ Soap is basic because its anion is a weak base that hydrolyzes water
