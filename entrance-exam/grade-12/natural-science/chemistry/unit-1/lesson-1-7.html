🧪 Hydrolysis of Salts

Not all salts give neutral solutions. Depending on the acid and base that formed them, salt solutions can be acidic, basic, or neutral. Learn how to predict which — with a simple framework you can apply to any salt.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Dissolve table salt (NaCl) in water — the solution is neutral (pH 7). Dissolve baking soda (NaHCO₃) — the solution is basic. Dissolve ammonium chloride (NH₄Cl) — the solution is acidic. Same solvent, different salts, different pHs. Why?

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define hydrolysis of salts
  • Predict whether a salt solution is acidic, basic, or neutral
  • Explain why salts of weak acids/bases react with water
  • Use Ka and Kb to compare strengths in weak-weak systems
  • Write hydrolysis equations
  • Identify hydrolysis in everyday situations
⚖️
7.1 Salts of Strong Acids + Strong Bases
Neutral solutions
📌 Definition of Hydrolysis

Hydrolysis (from Greek: hydro = water, lysis = break) is a reaction where water molecules interact with the ions of a salt. Depending on the strengths of the parent acid and base, the cation, the anion, or both may react with water.

Case 1 — Strong Acid + Strong Base → Neutral Salt

Example: HCl (strong acid) + NaOH (strong base) → NaCl (neutral salt) + H₂O

Net Ionic Equation

H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)

📌 Why Neutral?

• The anion (Cl⁻) comes from a strong acid (HCl) → Cl⁻ is a very weak conjugate base → does NOT hydrolyze
• The cation (Na⁺) comes from a strong base (NaOH) → Na⁺ is a very weak conjugate acid → does NOT hydrolyze
• Only water ionizes → solution stays neutral

Result

pH = 7 (neutral)

📝 Examples of neutral salts

NaCl, KBr, KNO₃, NaI, KClO₄ — all from strong acid + strong base combinations.

🔵
7.2 Salts of Weak Acids + Strong Bases
Basic solutions

Example: CH₃COOH (weak acid) + NaOH (strong base) → CH₃COONa (basic salt) + H₂O

📌 Why Basic?

• The cation (Na⁺) comes from a strong base → does NOT hydrolyze
• The anion (CH₃COO⁻) is the conjugate base of a weak acid → it IS a moderately strong base → it hydrolyzes water

Hydrolysis Reaction

Acetate Ion Hydrolysis

CH₃COO⁻(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ CH₃COOH(aq) + OH⁻(aq)

The acetate ion accepts a proton from water, producing OH⁻. The solution becomes basic.

Result

pH > 7 (basic)

📝 Examples of basic salts

CH₃COONa, Na₂CO₃, NaHCO₃, NaF, KCN, Na₃PO₄ — all from weak acid + strong base combinations.

🔴
7.3 Salts of Strong Acids + Weak Bases
Acidic solutions

Example: NH₃ (weak base) + HCl (strong acid) → NH₄Cl (acidic salt)

📌 Why Acidic?

• The anion (Cl⁻) comes from a strong acid → does NOT hydrolyze
• The cation (NH₄⁺) is the conjugate acid of a weak base → it IS a weak acid → it hydrolyzes water

Hydrolysis Reaction

Ammonium Ion Hydrolysis

NH₄⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₃(aq) + H₃O⁺(aq)

The ammonium ion donates a proton to water, producing H₃O⁺. The solution becomes acidic.

Result

pH < 7 (acidic)

📝 Examples of acidic salts

NH₄Cl, NH₄NO₃, (NH₄)₂SO₄, AlCl₃, FeCl₃ — all from strong acid + weak base combinations.

🟣
7.4 Salts of Weak Acids + Weak Bases
Compare Ka and Kb

When both the acid and base that formed the salt are weak, BOTH the cation and anion hydrolyze. The overall pH depends on which is stronger.

📌 The Rule

Compare Ka of the cation (conjugate acid of the weak base) vs Kb of the anion (conjugate base of the weak acid):

• Ka > Kb → solution is acidic (pH < 7)
• Kb > Ka → solution is basic (pH > 7)
• Ka = Kb → solution is neutral (pH = 7) — very rare

Example — Ammonium Formate (NH₄CHO₂)

Two Hydrolysis Reactions

NH₄⁺ + H₂O ⇌ NH₃ + H₃O⁺   (Ka = 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁰)
CHO₂⁻ + H₂O ⇌ HCHO₂ + OH⁻   (Kb = 5.9 × 10⁻¹¹)

Comparison

Ka(NH₄⁺) = 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ > Kb(CHO₂⁻) = 5.9 × 10⁻¹¹
Solution is slightly acidic

📝 The general rule

In a salt of weak acid + weak base:
• Compare Ka of the conjugate acid (cation) to Kb of the conjugate base (anion)
• The larger value "wins" and determines whether the solution is acidic or basic

The Four Cases — Summary

🔴 Acidic Salt

Strong acid + Weak base
Cation hydrolyzes → H₃O⁺
Example: NH₄Cl, FeCl₃
pH < 7

🔵 Basic Salt

Weak acid + Strong base
Anion hydrolyzes → OH⁻
Example: CH₃COONa, Na₂CO₃
pH > 7

🟢 Neutral Salt

Strong acid + Strong base
Neither ion hydrolyzes
Example: NaCl, KNO₃
pH = 7

🟣 Weak acid + Weak base

Compare Ka vs Kb:
Ka > Kb → acidic
Kb > Ka → basic
Ka = Kb → neutral

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Classify NaCl
Problem: Will an aqueous solution of NaCl be acidic, basic, or neutral?
Identify parents
NaCl is made from NaOH (strong base) + HCl (strong acid)
Check ions
• Na⁺ = conjugate acid of strong base → does NOT hydrolyze
• Cl⁻ = conjugate base of strong acid → does NOT hydrolyze
Conclusion
No hydrolysis — only water ionizes
✅ NaCl solution is neutral (pH = 7)
Example 2Classify Na₂CO₃
Problem: Is Na₂CO₃ solution acidic, basic, or neutral?
Identify parents
Na₂CO₃ is from NaOH (strong base) + H₂CO₃ (weak acid)
Which ion hydrolyzes?
• Na⁺ — does NOT hydrolyze (strong base)
• CO₃²⁻ — conjugate base of weak acid → WILL hydrolyze
Hydrolysis
CO₃²⁻ + H₂O ⇌ HCO₃⁻ + OH⁻
✅ Na₂CO₃ solution is basic (pH > 7)
Example 3Classify NH₄Cl
Problem: Is NH₄Cl solution acidic, basic, or neutral?
Identify parents
NH₄Cl is from NH₃ (weak base) + HCl (strong acid)
Which ion hydrolyzes?
• Cl⁻ — does NOT hydrolyze (strong acid)
• NH₄⁺ — conjugate acid of weak base → WILL hydrolyze
Hydrolysis
NH₄⁺ + H₂O ⇌ NH₃ + H₃O⁺
✅ NH₄Cl solution is acidic (pH < 7)
Example 4Determine pH of NH₄F
Problem: NH₄F is a salt of a weak acid (HF, Ka = 6.8×10⁻⁴) and a weak base (NH₃, Kb = 1.8×10⁻⁵). Is the solution acidic or basic?
Identify
Both ions hydrolyze — must compare Ka and Kb
Ka of cation
Ka(NH₄⁺) = Kw / Kb(NH₃) = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ / 1.8×10⁻⁵ = 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁰
Kb of anion
Kb(F⁻) = Kw / Ka(HF) = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ / 6.8×10⁻⁴ = 1.5 × 10⁻¹¹
Compare
Ka(NH₄⁺) = 5.6×10⁻¹⁰ > Kb(F⁻) = 1.5×10⁻¹¹ → acidic
✅ NH₄F solution is acidic (pH < 7)
Example 5Identify Salt Type from pH
Problem: A 0.1 M aqueous solution of an unknown salt has pH = 8.5. What type of salt is it?
Note pH
pH = 8.5 > 7 → basic solution
Deduce
Basic solution means the anion hydrolyzed (produced OH⁻)
Conclusion
Salt is from a weak acid + strong base — e.g., Na₂CO₃, CH₃COONa, KCN
✅ Salt is a weak acid + strong base type

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 1.7
✅ Key Points — Hydrolysis of Salts
  • Hydrolysis: reaction of salt ions with water
  • Strong acid + Strong base → neutral salt (pH = 7), neither ion hydrolyzes
  • Weak acid + Strong base → basic salt (pH > 7), anion hydrolyzes
  • Strong acid + Weak base → acidic salt (pH < 7), cation hydrolyzes
  • Weak acid + Weak base → compare Ka and Kb to determine pH
  • Anion hydrolysis: A⁻ + H₂O ⇌ HA + OH⁻
  • Cation hydrolysis: HB⁺ + H₂O ⇌ B + H₃O⁺
  • Examples: NaCl neutral; CH₃COONa basic; NH₄Cl acidic
  • The stronger hydrolysis reaction "wins" for weak-weak salts
