Titration Curves — Quick Guide

  • SA + SB → equivalence pH 7, sharp jump, bromothymol blue
  • WA + SB → equivalence pH > 7, use phenolphthalein
  • SA + WB → equivalence pH < 7, use methyl orange or methyl red
  • WA + WB → no sharp jump, use pH meter
  • At half-equivalence: pH = pKa

Ethiopian Industries — Quick Reference

  • Glass: silica (SiO₂) + Na₂CO₃ + CaCO₃, melted at 1600 °C; soda-lime = 90% of glass; borosilicate = Pyrex
  • Cement: CaCO₃ + clay + alumina → 1450 °C → clinker; ground with 2–3% gypsum → OPC
  • Sugar: 8 steps from sugarcane; by-product = bagasse (fuel)
  • Paper: wood pulp; Kraft process (NaOH + Na₂S at 170 °C) is dominant
  • Soap: saponification — Fat + 3NaOH → 3RCOONa + glycerol
  • Ethanol: fermentation (sugar → ethanol + CO₂) or catalytic hydration of ethene
  • Araki (Ethiopian): traditional distilled beverage; by-product = Atela (cattle feed)
  • Detergents: R–C₆H₄–SO₃Na; work in hard water (Ca/Mg salts are soluble)

Key Concepts

  • Pollutant — substance whose concentration increased due to human activity, causing harm
  • Contaminant — introduced by humans but not yet causing harm
  • Sink — medium that retains pollutants (water, soil)
  • DO — Dissolved Oxygen (optimum 4–8 mg/L; below 4 = polluted)
  • BOD — Biological Oxygen Demand; DO consumed by DOM over 5 days
  • TLV — Threshold Limit Value; permissible 8-hour exposure level

12 Principles of Green Chemistry — Quick List

  1. Prevention — prevent waste rather than treat it
  2. Atom economy — maximize atoms incorporated into product
  3. Less hazardous synthesis — use/generate low-toxicity chemicals
  4. Safer chemicals — design benign products
  5. Safer solvents — avoid solvents or use innocuous ones
  6. Energy efficiency — run at ambient conditions
  7. Renewable feedstock — use plant-based raw materials
  8. Reduce derivatives — avoid protecting groups
  9. Catalysis — use catalysts instead of stoichiometric reagents
  10. Design for degradation — products should break down safely
  11. Real-time analysis — monitor reactions in-process
  12. Inherently safer chemistry — prevent accidents by design
📐 Atom Economy Formula
% Atom Economy = (mass of desired product / total mass of all products) × 100%

Example: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ → (112 / 244) × 100% = 45.9%
Reaction with only ONE product = 100% atom economy (e.g., N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃)

Ibuprofen case study: Old (1960s) = 6 steps, 40% AE; BHC (1990s) = 3 steps, ~80% AE
