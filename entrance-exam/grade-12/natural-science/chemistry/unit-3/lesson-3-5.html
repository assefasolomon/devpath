💧 Sulphuric Acid & the Contact Process

Sulphuric acid is the single most produced chemical in the world — its consumption rate measures a nation's industrial strength. This lesson covers its properties, uses, and the 4-step Contact Process that makes it.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
The most important industrial chemical

Which chemical do you think is produced in the largest volume in the world? The answer is sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄). More of it is produced each year than any other industrial chemical, and the amount a country consumes is often used as a rough measure of its industrial development.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • List the physical and chemical properties of H₂SO₄
  • Describe the four steps of the Contact Process
  • Explain the optimal conditions (temperature, pressure, catalyst)
  • Define oleum (fuming sulphuric acid)
  • List the major uses of H₂SO₄
  • Explain safety precautions when diluting H₂SO₄
🔬
Properties of Sulphuric Acid
MoE Textbook, p. 170
📌 Physical Properties

• Anhydrous, 100% H₂SO₄ is a colorless, odorless, heavy, oily liquid
• Heavier than water (molar mass = 98 g/mol)
• Melting point = 10.5 °C
• Boiling point = 338 °C
• Soluble in all proportions with water
• Commercial forms: 33.5% "battery acid", 62.18% "chamber acid" (used for fertilizers)

📌 Chemical Properties

• Highly corrosive, reactive
• Strong acid — dissociates completely in dilute solution
• Strong oxidizing agent — oxidizes both metals and non-metals
• Strong dehydrating agent — removes water from substances
• Very exothermic when mixed with water

Oxidizing a Metal (Copper)
Cu + 2H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + SO₂ + 2H₂O
Oxidizing a Non-Metal (Carbon)
2H₂SO₄ + C → 2SO₂ + CO₂ + 2H₂O
📝 Two commercial concentrations to remember

• 33.5% H₂SO₄ — "battery acid" — used in lead-acid car batteries
• 62.18% H₂SO₄ — "chamber acid" — used for fertilizer production

🌍
Uses of Sulphuric Acid
Why so much of it is produced

Sulphuric acid is used in a huge range of industrial applications. Its main use is in the production of fertilizers, but it's also critical for many other industries.

🌾
Fertilizers
Biggest use — to make phosphate fertilizers (reacts with phosphate rock) and ammonium sulfate.
🔋
Car Batteries
The electrolyte in lead-acid batteries — 33.5% H₂SO₄ solution.
🧴
Detergents
Used to sulfonate organic compounds in detergent manufacturing.
🎨
Paints & Pigments
Used in the production of titanium dioxide (TiO₂) and other pigments.
💥
Explosives
Used to make nitroglycerin, TNT, and other explosives.
🛢️
Petroleum Refining
Used to refine petroleum products and remove impurities.
🧪
Chemical Catalyst
Used as a catalyst in many chemical processes (e.g., esterification).
🥫
Metal Pickling
Removes oxide layers from steel and other metals before processing.
📝 A measure of industrialization

Because H₂SO₄ is used in so many industries, its rate of consumption is often used as a rough measure of a country's industrialization. More sulfuric acid consumed = more industry.

🏭
The Contact Process
Industrial production — 4 major steps
📌 The Contact Process

Sulphuric acid is manufactured industrially by the Contact Process, which involves four major steps. It's called "contact" because the SO₂ and O₂ molecules react on the surface (contact) of the V₂O₅ catalyst.

1
Burn Sulphur
S + O₂ → SO₂
2
Oxidize SO₂
2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃
3
Absorb in H₂SO₄
SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇
4
Dilute Oleum
H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄

Step 1 — Preparation of Sulphur Dioxide

Sulphur is burned in air to produce sulphur dioxide:

Step 1 — Oxidation of Sulphur
S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g)

Step 2 — Oxidation of SO₂ to SO₃

This is the key step — the conversion of SO₂ to SO₃. The reaction is slow, but it's accelerated by heating to 400 °C in the presence of a V₂O₅ catalyst. Because SO₂ and O₂ molecules react on the surface of the V₂O₅, this is called the "contact" process.

Step 2 — The Key Catalytic Reaction
2SO₂(g) + O₂(g)  ⇌  2SO₃(g)    [V₂O₅, 400 °C]

Step 3 — Absorption in Concentrated H₂SO₄

Sulphur trioxide is absorbed into 98% sulphuric acid to form oleum (also known as fuming sulphuric acid):

Step 3 — Formation of Oleum
SO₃(g) + H₂SO₄(l) → H₂S₂O₇(l)
📝 Why not dissolve SO₃ directly in water?

If SO₃ were bubbled into water directly, the reaction would be violently exothermic and would produce a dense mist of sulfuric acid that's very difficult to condense. Absorbing SO₃ in 98% H₂SO₄ avoids this problem.

Step 4 — Dilution of Oleum

Oleum is diluted with water to form concentrated sulphuric acid:

Step 4 — Formation of Concentrated H₂SO₄
H₂S₂O₇(l) + H₂O(l) → 2H₂SO₄(l)
Contact Process — Full Flow
⚖️
Optimal Conditions for Step 2
Le Chatelier applied

Step 2 is a reversible, exothermic reaction. The industrial conditions represent a compromise between equilibrium yield and reaction rate.

The Key Equilibrium
2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)    ΔH = −197 kJ/mol
FactorOptimal ConditionWhy
Temperature ~400 °C Lower T favors SO₃ (exothermic), but too low = too slow. 400 °C is the compromise with the catalyst.
Pressure 1–2 atm (near atmospheric) High P would favor SO₃ (3 mol → 2 mol gas), but the equilibrium already lies far to the right — extra pressure isn't worth the cost.
Catalyst Vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅) Speeds up the reaction so equilibrium is reached quickly at 400 °C.
Excess O₂ Air (excess) Adding more O₂ pushes equilibrium to the right (Le Chatelier).
📝 Why only ~1–2 atm?

The forward reaction produces fewer gas moles (3 → 2), so higher pressure would favor SO₃. But because the equilibrium constant is already very favorable at 400 °C, industry doesn't need high pressure — saving equipment cost and energy. This is a good example of economic optimization in industrial chemistry.

💠
What is Oleum?
Fuming sulphuric acid
📌 Definition

Oleum is a solution of SO₃ dissolved in H₂SO₄ — its chemical formula is H₂S₂O₇ (pyrosulphuric acid). It's also called fuming sulphuric acid because it releases SO₃ fumes when exposed to air.

📌 Why does industry make oleum first?

The Contact Process doesn't make H₂SO₄ directly from SO₃ + H₂O — instead, it makes oleum first, then dilutes it with water. This is because:

• Direct SO₃ + H₂O → violent, produces a fine mist of H₂SO₄ that's very hard to condense
• SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → oleum → controlled, safer, no mist formation
• Then oleum + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄ → cleanly makes the acid

Oleum Formation & Dilution
SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇   (formation)
Dilution of Oleum
H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄
⚠️
Safety & Dilution
The most important rule
📌 The golden rule

When diluting sulphuric acid, always add the acid to water — NOT water to acid.

📝 Why?

Dissolving H₂SO₄ in water is highly exothermic — it releases a large amount of heat. If you add water to concentrated acid, the water can boil instantly and splash concentrated acid back at you. By adding acid to water:

• The acid is diluted slowly into the larger volume of water
• Heat is absorbed by the water (water has high specific heat)
• The mixture stays well below boiling point

📌 Other safety notes

• Concentrated H₂SO₄ causes severe burns — wear goggles, gloves, and lab coat
• It also dehydrates organic materials — a drop on paper or skin will char it
• Never taste or smell directly; use wafting technique
• If spilled on skin: flush with plenty of water, then treat as a chemical burn

Worked Examples
5 practice problems
Example 1Identify the Contact Process Steps
Problem: List the four steps of the Contact Process and give the chemical equation for each.
Step 1
S + O₂ → SO₂ — burning sulphur in air
Step 2
2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ — oxidation over V₂O₅ at 400 °C (the key step)
Step 3
SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇ — absorption in 98% H₂SO₄ to form oleum
Step 4
H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄ — dilution of oleum to give concentrated H₂SO₄
✅ Four steps: S→SO₂ → SO₂→SO₃ → SO₃→oleum → oleum→H₂SO₄
Example 2Why is V₂O₅ Used?
Problem: Explain the role of V₂O₅ in the Contact Process and why 400 °C is chosen.
Role of V₂O₅
It is the catalyst — speeds up the slow conversion of SO₂ to SO₃ by providing a surface for reaction
Temperature choice
At 400 °C, the reaction proceeds quickly enough with V₂O₅ while still giving good SO₃ yield
Why not lower?
Lower temperature favors equilibrium yield (exothermic), but reaction would be too slow even with a catalyst
Why not higher?
Higher T would be faster but shift equilibrium to the left, giving less SO₃
✅ V₂O₅ is the catalyst; 400 °C is the compromise between rate and equilibrium yield
Example 3Why Make Oleum First?
Problem: Why does the Contact Process produce oleum as an intermediate rather than dissolving SO₃ directly in water?
Direct method problem
SO₃ + H₂O is violently exothermic and produces a fine acid mist that's very hard to condense
Oleum solution
Absorbing SO₃ in 98% H₂SO₄ gives a liquid (oleum) with no mist formed
Then dilution
Oleum + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄ gives clean concentrated acid
✅ Oleum formation avoids the violent SO₃ + H₂O reaction and the mist problem
Example 4Why Add Acid to Water?
Problem: Explain why one must always add concentrated H₂SO₄ to water and never the reverse.
Exothermic process
Dissolving concentrated H₂SO₄ in water releases a large amount of heat
Water to acid (WRONG)
If water is added to acid, the small amount of water can boil instantly and splash concentrated acid out
Acid to water (RIGHT)
Adding acid to a large volume of water dilutes the acid slowly; the water absorbs the heat and stays below boiling
✅ Always add acid to water — this prevents boiling and dangerous splashing
Example 5Why H₂SO₄ Measures Industrialization
Problem: Explain why the consumption of sulphuric acid is used as an indicator of a country's industrial development.
Wide use
H₂SO₄ is used in fertilizers, batteries, detergents, paints, explosives, petroleum, and many more
Indicator
Because it is used in so many industries, its consumption reflects the level of industrial activity in a country
Comparison
Countries with high H₂SO₄ consumption generally have large manufacturing sectors
✅ H₂SO₄ is used across so many industries that its consumption indicates overall industrial output

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 3.5
✅ Key Points — Sulphuric Acid & the Contact Process
  • H₂SO₄ is the most produced industrial chemical in the world
  • Physical: colorless, odorless, oily liquid; mp 10.5 °C; bp 338 °C; heavier than water
  • Chemical: strong acid, strong oxidizing agent, strong dehydrating agent
  • Commercial forms: 33.5% (battery acid) and 62.18% (chamber acid for fertilizers)
  • Major uses: fertilizers (biggest), car batteries, detergents, paints, explosives, petroleum refining, metal pickling
  • Its consumption rate is used as a measure of a country's industrialization
  • Contact Process — 4 steps:
    Step 1: S + O₂ → SO₂
    Step 2: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (V₂O₅, 400 °C)
    Step 3: SO₃ + H₂SO₄ → H₂S₂O₇ (oleum)
    Step 4: H₂S₂O₇ + H₂O → 2H₂SO₄
  • Oleum = H₂S₂O₇ = fuming sulphuric acid; made first to avoid violent SO₃ + H₂O reaction
  • Optimal conditions for Step 2: 400 °C, 1–2 atm, V₂O₅ catalyst, excess O₂
  • Dilution rule: Always add acid to water — never the reverse (highly exothermic)
