Introduction
Why arithmetic sequences matter

Imagine a worker who earns 500 ETB on the first day and gets a raise of 50 ETB every day. Or a farmer who plants 10 trees the first week and adds 5 more trees each week. These are everyday examples of arithmetic sequences.

An arithmetic sequence is one of the most important topics in the Ethiopian Grade 12 curriculum — it appears in almost every entrance exam, often in multiple questions. Mastering it gives you a significant advantage.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define an arithmetic sequence and identify its common difference
  • Use the nth term formula: aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d
  • Find any term when given a₁ and d
  • Find a₁ or d when given other information
  • Find the number of terms in a finite arithmetic sequence
  • Insert arithmetic means between two numbers
  • Solve real-world word problems involving arithmetic sequences

Explanation
Complete theory with visual aids
📌 Definition — Arithmetic Sequence

An arithmetic sequence (also called an arithmetic progression or AP) is a sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by adding a fixed constant d to the preceding term. This constant is called the common difference.

2.1 The Common Difference

The common difference d is found by subtracting any term from the next term:

d = a₂ − a₁ = a₃ − a₂ = a₄ − a₃ = aₙ₊₁ − aₙ

The common difference can be positive (sequence increases), negative (sequence decreases), or zero (constant sequence).

Increasing (d > 0)
2, 5, 8, 11, ...
d = +3
Decreasing (d < 0)
20, 15, 10, 5, ...
d = −5
Constant (d = 0)
7, 7, 7, 7, ...
d = 0

2.2 The nth Term Formula

The most important formula for arithmetic sequences. Starting from a₁ and adding d repeatedly:

a₁
+d
a₁+d
+d
a₁+2d
+d
a₁+3d
...
a₁+(n−1)d
aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

Where: aₙ = nth term, a₁ = first term, n = position, d = common difference

2.3 Alternative Form Using Any Term

Sometimes you are not given the first term but another term. The formula can be extended:

aₙ = aₘ + (n − m)d

This lets you find any term starting from any other known term aₘ at position m.

⚠ Exam Tip

On the entrance exam you may be given two non-consecutive terms (e.g., a₃ and a₇) and asked to find d. Set up two equations and subtract them to eliminate a₁.

2.4 Arithmetic Means

The terms inserted between two numbers to form an arithmetic sequence are called arithmetic means.

Method — Inserting k Arithmetic Means between a and b

If you insert k terms between a and b, the total number of terms is k + 2.
The common difference is: d = (b − a) / (k + 1)

Example: Insert 3 arithmetic means between 2 and 18.
d = (18 − 2) / (3 + 1) = 16 / 4 = 4
Sequence: 2, 6, 10, 14, 18

2.5 Finding Number of Terms

For a finite arithmetic sequence with first term a₁, last term aₙ, and common difference d:

n = (aₙ − a₁) / d + 1

Example: How many terms in 5, 8, 11, ..., 50?
n = (50 − 5) / 3 + 1 = 45/3 + 1 = 15 + 1 = 16 terms

2.6 Real-World Applications

The entrance exam frequently tests arithmetic sequences through word problems. Common scenarios include:

  • Salary increases: Starting salary + fixed annual raise
  • Seat arrangements: Theatre rows with fixed extra seats
  • Construction: Layers of bricks decreasing by fixed amount
  • Saving money: Increasing fixed amount deposited each month
  • Population: Linear growth by fixed number per year
⚠ Strategy for Word Problems

Step 1: Identify a₁ (first value). Step 2: Find d (the fixed increase or decrease). Step 3: Write the formula aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d. Step 4: Solve for what is asked.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1 Finding the nth Term
Problem: The first term of an arithmetic sequence is 7 and the common difference is 4. Find the 15th term.
Given
a₁ = 7,   d = 4,   n = 15
Formula
aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d
Substitute
a₁₅ = 7 + (15−1)(4) = 7 + 14(4) = 7 + 56 = 63
✅ The 15th term is 63
Example 2 Finding d and a₁ from Two Terms
Problem: In an arithmetic sequence, a₄ = 13 and a₉ = 33. Find the common difference and the first term.
Step 1
Write equations using aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d:
a₄ = a₁ + 3d = 13  ...(i)
a₉ = a₁ + 8d = 33  ...(ii)
Step 2
Subtract equation (i) from (ii):
(a₁ + 8d) − (a₁ + 3d) = 33 − 13
5d = 20  ⟹  d = 4
Step 3
Substitute d = 4 into equation (i):
a₁ + 3(4) = 13  ⟹  a₁ + 12 = 13  ⟹  a₁ = 1
✅ Common difference d = 4, first term a₁ = 1
Verify: 1, 5, 9, 13, 17, 21, 25, 29, 33 ✓
Example 3 Inserting Arithmetic Means
Problem: Insert 4 arithmetic means between 3 and 23.
Step 1
Total terms = 4 means + 2 endpoints = 6 terms.
Here a₁ = 3, a₆ = 23, n = 6.
Step 2
Find d using d = (b − a) / (k + 1):
d = (23 − 3) / (4 + 1) = 20 / 5 = 4
Step 3
Build the sequence: a₁ = 3, d = 4
3,   7,   11,   15,   19,   23
✅ The 4 arithmetic means are 7, 11, 15, 19
Example 4 Number of Terms — Entrance Exam Style
Problem: How many terms are in the arithmetic sequence 8, 13, 18, ..., 98?
Step 1
Identify: a₁ = 8,   d = 5,   aₙ = 98
Step 2
Use aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d:
98 = 8 + (n−1)(5)
90 = (n−1)(5)
n−1 = 18  ⟹  n = 19
✅ There are 19 terms in the sequence
Example 5 Real-World Word Problem
Problem: A teacher earns 4,500 ETB per month in the first year and receives a monthly raise of 150 ETB each year. What will the teacher earn per month in the 8th year?
Step 1
Identify the arithmetic sequence:
a₁ = 4,500 ETB  (first year salary)
d = 150 ETB  (annual raise)
n = 8  (8th year)
Step 2
Apply the formula:
a₈ = 4,500 + (8−1)(150)
a₈ = 4,500 + 7 × 150
a₈ = 4,500 + 1,050 = 5,550 ETB
✅ The teacher will earn 5,550 ETB per month in the 8th year

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 2
✅ Key Points — Arithmetic Sequences
  • An arithmetic sequence has a constant common difference d between consecutive terms: d = aₙ₊₁ − aₙ
  • The nth term formula is aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d — this is the most used formula on the exam.
  • If d > 0 the sequence increases; if d < 0 it decreases; if d = 0 it is constant.
  • To find d from two terms: subtract their equations to eliminate a₁, then solve for d.
  • To insert k arithmetic means between a and b, use d = (b − a) / (k + 1).
  • To count terms in a finite sequence: n = (aₙ − a₁) / d + 1.
  • For word problems: identify a₁ (starting value) and d (fixed change), then apply the formula.
  • The middle term of any arithmetic sequence equals the average of any two equidistant terms.
