∞ Geometric Series and Infinite Series

Learn to find the sum of geometric series — both finite and infinite. Understand convergence, one of the most beautiful ideas in mathematics, and apply it to real-world problems like compound interest and bouncing balls.

Introduction
From geometric sequences to their sums

You have already learned how geometric sequences work — each term multiplied by a constant ratio r. Now the question is: what happens when you add all those terms together?

More interestingly — what if the sequence goes on forever? Can an infinite sum of numbers give a finite answer? The surprising answer is: yes, but only under certain conditions. This idea of infinite series is one of the most powerful concepts in all of mathematics.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Find the sum of a finite geometric series using Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)
  • Determine whether an infinite geometric series converges or diverges
  • Find the sum of a convergent infinite series: S∞ = a₁/(1 − r) when |r| < 1
  • Convert repeating decimals to fractions using infinite series
  • Solve real-world problems involving geometric series

Explanation
Complete theory with visual aids
📌 Definition — Geometric Series

A geometric series is the sum of the terms of a geometric sequence. If the sequence is a₁, a₁r, a₁r², ..., a₁rⁿ⁻¹ then the series is:

Sₙ = a₁ + a₁r + a₁r² + a₁r³ + ... + a₁rⁿ⁻¹

2.1 Deriving the Finite Sum Formula

Write Sₙ, then multiply both sides by r, then subtract:

Sₙ  = a₁ + a₁r + a₁r² + ... + a₁rⁿ⁻¹
rSₙ =       a₁r + a₁r² + ... + a₁rⁿ⁻¹ + a₁rⁿ
Sₙ − rSₙ = a₁ − a₁rⁿ
Sₙ(1 − r) = a₁(1 − rⁿ)
Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r),   r ≠ 1
Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ) / (1 − r)    (r ≠ 1)
⚠ Alternative Form

When r > 1, it is more convenient to write: Sₙ = a₁(rⁿ − 1) / (r − 1). Both forms give the same answer — use whichever avoids negative denominators.

2.2 Special Case: r = 1

When r = 1, all terms are equal to a₁. The formula above does not apply (division by zero). Instead:

Sₙ = n · a₁    (when r = 1)

2.3 Infinite Geometric Series — Convergence

What happens when n → ∞? The key is the behavior of rⁿ:

Converges (|r| < 1)
1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ...
r = 1/2. As n→∞, rⁿ→0.
Sum approaches a finite value.
S∞ = 1
Diverges (|r| ≥ 1)
2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + ...
r = 2. As n→∞, rⁿ→∞.
Sum grows without bound.
S∞ = ∞ (no sum)
📌 Convergence Rule

An infinite geometric series converges (has a finite sum) if and only if |r| < 1.
When |r| < 1, as n→∞, rⁿ→0, so:

S∞ = a₁ / (1 − r)    (only valid when |r| < 1)

2.4 Repeating Decimals as Infinite Series

Every repeating decimal can be written as an infinite geometric series and converted to a fraction — a classic entrance exam topic.

Example — Convert 0.333... to a fraction

0.333... = 0.3 + 0.03 + 0.003 + ...
This is a geometric series with a₁ = 0.3 and r = 0.1
Since |r| = 0.1 < 1, it converges:
S∞ = 0.3 / (1 − 0.1) = 0.3 / 0.9 = 1/3 ✓

⚠ Exam Tip

Converting repeating decimals to fractions using infinite geometric series appears frequently on the Ethiopian entrance exam. Always check |r| < 1 before applying S∞ = a₁/(1−r).

2.5 Comparison of All Series Formulas

Series Type Formula When to use
Arithmetic Sₙ = n/2·(a₁+aₙ) First and last term known
Arithmetic Sₙ = n/2·[2a₁+(n−1)d] d known, last term not
Geometric (finite) Sₙ = a₁(1−rⁿ)/(1−r) Any r ≠ 1, finite n
Geometric (infinite) S∞ = a₁/(1−r) Only when |r| < 1

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1 Finite Geometric Series
Problem: Find the sum of the first 6 terms of the geometric series 2 + 6 + 18 + 54 + ...
Identify
a₁ = 2,   r = 3,   n = 6
Apply
Since r = 3 > 1, use Sₙ = a₁(rⁿ−1)/(r−1):
S₆ = 2(3⁶ − 1)/(3 − 1) = 2(729 − 1)/2 = 728
✅ S₆ = 728
Example 2 Finite Series with Fractional Ratio
Problem: Find the sum of the first 5 terms of 16 + 8 + 4 + 2 + ...
Identify
a₁ = 16,   r = 1/2,   n = 5
Apply
Since r = 1/2 < 1, use Sₙ = a₁(1−rⁿ)/(1−r):
S₅ = 16(1 − (1/2)⁵)/(1 − 1/2)
= 16(1 − 1/32)/(1/2)
= 16 · (31/32) · 2 = 16 · 31/16 = 31
✅ S₅ = 31 — Verify: 16+8+4+2+1 = 31 ✓
Example 3 Infinite Geometric Series
Problem: Find the sum of the infinite series: 12 + 4 + 4/3 + 4/9 + ...
Identify
a₁ = 12,   r = 4/12 = 1/3
Check
|r| = 1/3 < 1 ✓ — series converges
Apply
S∞ = a₁/(1−r) = 12/(1 − 1/3) = 12/(2/3) = 12 × 3/2 = 18
✅ S∞ = 18
Example 4 Repeating Decimal to Fraction
Problem: Express 0.272727... as a fraction.
Step 1
Write as infinite series:
0.272727... = 0.27 + 0.0027 + 0.000027 + ...
Step 2
Identify: a₁ = 0.27,   r = 0.01
|r| = 0.01 < 1 ✓ — converges
Step 3
Apply S∞ formula:
S∞ = 0.27/(1 − 0.01) = 0.27/0.99 = 27/99 = 3/11
✅ 0.272727... = 3/11 — Verify: 3 ÷ 11 = 0.2727... ✓
Example 5 Real-World — Bouncing Ball
Problem: A ball is dropped from a height of 10 m. Each time it bounces it rises to 3/5 of its previous height. What is the total distance traveled by the ball?
Step 1
The ball falls 10m, then bounces up and down repeatedly.
Total distance = 10 (first fall) + 2 × (sum of all bounce heights)
Each bounce: heights are 6, 3.6, 2.16, ... (geometric with a₁=6, r=3/5)
Step 2
Sum of infinite bounces (each counted twice — up and down):
S∞ = 6/(1 − 3/5) = 6/(2/5) = 6 × 5/2 = 15
Total bounce distance = 2 × 15 = 30m
Step 3
Add initial fall:
Total = 10 + 30 = 40m
✅ Total distance traveled = 40 metres

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 5
✅ Key Points — Geometric Series
  • A geometric series is the sum of a geometric sequence: Sₙ = a₁ + a₁r + a₁r² + ... + a₁rⁿ⁻¹
  • Finite sum formula: Sₙ = a₁(1−rⁿ)/(1−r) for r ≠ 1, or a₁(rⁿ−1)/(r−1) when r > 1.
  • When r = 1, all terms are equal: Sₙ = n·a₁.
  • An infinite geometric series converges only when |r| < 1.
  • Infinite sum formula: S∞ = a₁/(1−r) — only apply when |r| < 1.
  • Every repeating decimal is an infinite geometric series and can be converted to a fraction.
  • For repeating decimals: use a₁ = first repeating block as decimal, r = 10⁻ᵏ where k = length of block.
  • Always check convergence before applying S∞. If |r| ≥ 1 the series has no finite sum.
