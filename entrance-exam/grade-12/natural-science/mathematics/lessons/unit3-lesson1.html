d/dx The Derivative

Discover the most powerful tool in calculus — the derivative. Learn what it means geometrically as the slope of a tangent line, define it formally using limits, and evaluate derivatives from first principles.

✏️ Examples: 5 worked

Introduction
What is a derivative and why does it matter?

How fast is a car accelerating at exactly this moment? How steep is a hill at a specific point? How quickly is a population growing right now? All of these questions ask about instantaneous rate of change — and the answer in every case is the derivative.

The derivative is the single most important concept in differential calculus. It connects the algebra of functions to their geometric behavior, and it appears in physics, engineering, economics, and biology. On the Ethiopian entrance exam, derivative questions make up a large portion of the calculus section.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Understand the derivative as a slope of a tangent line
  • Write the formal limit definition: f'(x) = lim(h→0) [f(x+h)−f(x)]/h
  • Use the definition to find derivatives from first principles
  • Read and write derivative notation correctly
  • Understand differentiability and its relationship to continuity
  • Interpret the derivative as a rate of change in real-world contexts
📖
Explanation
From secant lines to tangent lines — building the derivative

2.1 From Average Rate of Change to Instantaneous Rate

The average rate of change of f between x and x+h is the slope of the secant line:

Average rate of change = [f(x+h) − f(x)] / h

As h → 0, the second point slides toward the first, and the secant line becomes the tangent line. The slope of the tangent line is the derivative:

As h → 0, the secant line through P and Q rotates and becomes the tangent line at P. The slope of this tangent line is the derivative f'(x).
📌 Formal Definition — The Derivative

The derivative of f at x, written f'(x), is defined as:

f'(x) = lim(h→0) [f(x+h) − f(x)] / h

provided this limit exists. If the limit exists at x = a, we say f is differentiable at x = a.

An alternative form at a specific point x = a:
f'(a) = lim(x→a) [f(x) − f(a)] / (x − a)

2.2 Derivative Notation — Five Ways to Write It

All of the following mean exactly the same thing — the derivative of f with respect to x:

f'(x)
Lagrange notation — most common
dy/dx
Leibniz notation — used in physics
d/dx [f(x)]
Operator notation
ẏ
Newton notation — dot above y
Df(x)
D-notation (operator form)
⚠ Exam Tip

The Ethiopian entrance exam uses f'(x) and dy/dx most frequently. Make sure you are comfortable with both. When asked to "differentiate" or "find the derivative", use the rules in Lesson 2. When asked to use the "definition" or "first principles", use the limit formula above.

2.3 Geometric Meaning — Slope of Tangent Line

The derivative f'(a) gives the slope of the tangent line to the graph of f at x = a.

The sign of f'(a) tells you whether the function is rising, flat, or falling at x = a.

2.4 Differentiability and Continuity

Key Theorem

If f is differentiable at x = a, then f is continuous at x = a.

Differentiable → Continuous

Important Warning

The converse is NOT always true.
Continuous does NOT mean differentiable.

Example: f(x) = |x| is continuous but NOT differentiable at x = 0.

⚠ When is a Function NOT Differentiable?

A function is not differentiable at x = a when: the graph has a sharp corner (like |x| at 0), a cusp, a vertical tangent, or a discontinuity.

2.5 Derivative as Rate of Change

The derivative measures how fast a quantity changes. In real-world problems:

Context f(x) represents f'(x) represents
Motion Position at time t Velocity (speed)
Economics Total cost Marginal cost
Biology Population size Growth rate
Geometry Area or volume Rate of expansion

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems — derivatives from first principles
Example 1 Derivative of a Linear Function from First Principles
Problem: Find f'(x) using the definition if f(x) = 3x + 5.
Step 1
Write f(x+h):
f(x+h) = 3(x+h)+5 = 3x+3h+5
Step 2
Compute f(x+h)−f(x):
(3x+3h+5)−(3x+5) = 3h
Step 3
Divide by h:
3h/h = 3
Step 4
Take limit as h→0:
lim(h→0) 3 = 3
✅ f'(x) = 3 — the slope of a line is its own derivative
Example 2 Derivative of x² from First Principles
Problem: Find f'(x) using the definition if f(x) = x².
Step 1
f(x+h) = (x+h)² = x²+2xh+h²
Step 2
f(x+h)−f(x) = x²+2xh+h²−x² = 2xh+h²
Step 3
[f(x+h)−f(x)]/h = (2xh+h²)/h = 2x+h
Step 4
lim(h→0)(2x+h) = 2x
✅ f'(x) = 2x
Example 3 Derivative of x³ from First Principles
Problem: Find f'(x) if f(x) = x³.
Step 1
f(x+h) = (x+h)³ = x³+3x²h+3xh²+h³
Step 2
f(x+h)−f(x) = 3x²h+3xh²+h³
Step 3
Divide by h: 3x²+3xh+h²
Step 4
lim(h→0)(3x²+3xh+h²) = 3x²
✅ f'(x) = 3x²
Example 4 Derivative at a Specific Point + Tangent Line
Problem: Find f'(2) for f(x) = x² and write the equation of the tangent line at x = 2.
Find f'(2)
From Example 2: f'(x) = 2x
f'(2) = 2(2) = 4
Find f(2)
f(2) = 2² = 4
Tangent line
y − f(a) = f'(a)(x − a):
y − 4 = 4(x − 2)  ⟹  y = 4x − 4
✅ f'(2) = 4. Tangent line: y = 4x − 4
Example 5 Derivative of 1/x from First Principles
Problem: Find f'(x) using first principles if f(x) = 1/x.
Step 1
f(x+h) = 1/(x+h)
Step 2
f(x+h)−f(x) = 1/(x+h) − 1/x = [x−(x+h)] / [x(x+h)] = −h / [x(x+h)]
Step 3
Divide by h: −1 / [x(x+h)]
Step 4
lim(h→0) −1/[x(x+h)] = −1/x²
✅ f'(x) = −1/x²

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Unit 3 Lesson 1
✅ Key Points — The Derivative
  • The derivative f'(x) = lim(h→0) [f(x+h)−f(x)]/h measures the instantaneous rate of change.
  • Geometrically, f'(a) is the slope of the tangent line to the graph at x = a.
  • To find a derivative from first principles: write f(x+h), subtract f(x), divide by h, then take limit as h→0.
  • Tangent line equation at x = a: y − f(a) = f'(a)(x − a).
  • Notation: f'(x), dy/dx, d/dx[f(x)], ẏ and Df(x) all mean the same thing.
  • f'(a) > 0 means f is increasing at a. f'(a) < 0 means decreasing. f'(a) = 0 means flat.
  • Differentiable → Continuous. But Continuous does NOT imply Differentiable.
  • f(x) = |x| is the classic example: continuous everywhere but not differentiable at x = 0.
