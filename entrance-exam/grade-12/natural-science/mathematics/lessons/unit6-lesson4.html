⚖️ Midpoint of a Line Segment in Space

Extend the 2D midpoint formula into three dimensions. Learn to find the exact midpoint of any line segment in space, derive an unknown endpoint given the midpoint, and apply the midpoint formula to solve geometric problems on the entrance exam.

📖 Reading: ~16 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

The midpoint of a line segment is the point exactly halfway between the two endpoints. In 2D you average the x-coordinates and average the y-coordinates. In 3D the same idea extends naturally — you simply average all three coordinates.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • State and apply the 3D midpoint formula: M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2, (z₁+z₂)/2)
  • Find the midpoint of any line segment given its two endpoints in space
  • Find an unknown endpoint given the midpoint and the other endpoint
  • Verify that the midpoint is equidistant from both endpoints using the distance formula
  • Apply the midpoint formula to find the centre of a segment and solve geometric problems
  • Combine the midpoint and distance formulas in multi-step problems
📖
Explanation
Complete theory with visual aids

2.1 Review — Midpoint Formula in 2D

In 2D, the midpoint M of the segment joining P₁(x₁, y₁) and P₂(x₂, y₂) is:

M = ((x₁+x₂)/2 , (y₁+y₂)/2)

The idea: the midpoint's x-coordinate is the average of the two x-coordinates, and similarly for y. In 3D we add the same rule for z.

2.2 The 3D Midpoint Formula

📌 Midpoint Formula in Space

The midpoint M of the line segment joining P₁(x₁, y₁, z₁) and P₂(x₂, y₂, z₂) is:

M = ( (x₁+x₂)/2 , (y₁+y₂)/2 , (z₁+z₂)/2 )

Each coordinate of M is the average of the corresponding coordinates of P₁ and P₂.

M is equidistant from P₁ and P₂. Each segment has the same length d.

📝 Why averages?

The midpoint must be halfway between P₁ and P₂ in every direction. Moving halfway from x₁ to x₂ means going to (x₁+x₂)/2. The same logic applies to y and z independently. The three averages together give the one point that lies exactly in the middle.

2.3 Finding an Unknown Endpoint

A very common exam question: given the midpoint M and one endpoint P₁, find the other endpoint P₂. Rearrange the midpoint formula for each coordinate:

📌 Finding the Unknown Endpoint P₂

Given M(mₓ, m_y, m_z) and P₁(x₁, y₁, z₁), the other endpoint P₂(x₂, y₂, z₂) is:

x₂ = 2mₓ − x₁
y₂ = 2m_y − y₁
z₂ = 2m_z − z₁

Derivation: From mₓ = (x₁+x₂)/2, multiply both sides by 2: x₁+x₂ = 2mₓ, so x₂ = 2mₓ − x₁.

Step-by-step approach
x₂
x₂ = 2·mₓ − x₁
Double midpoint x, subtract P₁'s x
y₂
y₂ = 2·m_y − y₁
Double midpoint y, subtract P₁'s y
z₂
z₂ = 2·m_z − z₁
Double midpoint z, subtract P₁'s z

2.4 Verifying the Midpoint with the Distance Formula

You can always verify that M is the correct midpoint by checking that it is equidistant from both endpoints:

📌 Verification condition

M is the midpoint of P₁P₂ if and only if:

d(P₁, M) = d(M, P₂) = ½ · d(P₁, P₂)

Each half-segment has length equal to half the total length. If both distances equal each other and equal half the total, M is confirmed as the midpoint.

⚠ Most common exam mistake

Subtracting instead of averaging. The midpoint is (x₁+x₂)/2, not (x₂−x₁)/2. Also watch for sign errors when coordinates are negative — substitute carefully.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Basic Midpoint Calculation
Problem: Find the midpoint of the segment joining A(2, 4, 6) and B(8, 2, 4).
x
mₓ = (2+8)/2 = 10/2 = 5
y
m_y = (4+2)/2 = 6/2 = 3
z
m_z = (6+4)/2 = 10/2 = 5
✅ Midpoint M = (5, 3, 5)
Example 2Midpoint with Negative Coordinates
Problem: Find the midpoint of P(−3, 5, −1) and Q(7, −1, 9).
x
mₓ = (−3+7)/2 = 4/2 = 2
y
m_y = (5+(−1))/2 = 4/2 = 2
z
m_z = (−1+9)/2 = 8/2 = 4
✅ Midpoint M = (2, 2, 4)
Example 3Finding an Unknown Endpoint
Problem: The midpoint of segment P₁P₂ is M(3, 1, 5). One endpoint is P₁(1, −3, 7). Find P₂.
x₂
x₂ = 2(3) − 1 = 6 − 1 = 5
y₂
y₂ = 2(1) − (−3) = 2 + 3 = 5
z₂
z₂ = 2(5) − 7 = 10 − 7 = 3
Verify
Check: midpoint of P₁(1,−3,7) and P₂(5,5,3):
((1+5)/2, (−3+5)/2, (7+3)/2) = (3, 1, 5) = M ✓
✅ P₂ = (5, 5, 3)
Example 4Verifying with the Distance Formula
Problem: For A(0, 0, 0) and B(4, 6, 12), find midpoint M and verify d(A, M) = d(M, B).
Midpoint
M = (4/2, 6/2, 12/2) = (2, 3, 6)
d(A, M)
d = √(4+9+36) = √49 = 7
d(M, B)
d = √((4−2)²+(6−3)²+(12−6)²) = √(4+9+36) = √49 = 7
d(A, B)
d = √(16+36+144) = √196 = 14 = 2 × 7 ✓
✅ M = (2, 3, 6). Both half-segments have length 7 = ½ × 14 ✓
Example 5Finding an Unknown Coordinate of an Endpoint
Problem: The midpoint of AB is M(4, 2, 3). If A = (2, k, 1), find k given that B = (6, 4, 5). Verify that M is correct.
Setup
Use the midpoint formula for the y-coordinate:
m_y = (k + 4)/2 = 2
Solve
k + 4 = 4 → k = 0
Verify
A = (2, 0, 1), B = (6, 4, 5):
M = ((2+6)/2, (0+4)/2, (1+5)/2) = (4, 2, 3) ✓
✅ k = 0. A = (2, 0, 1) gives midpoint M = (4, 2, 3) ✓

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 4
✅ Key Points — Midpoint of a Line Segment in Space
  • The 3D midpoint formula: M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2, (z₁+z₂)/2). Average each coordinate independently.
  • The midpoint is equidistant from both endpoints: d(P₁, M) = d(M, P₂) = ½ d(P₁, P₂).
  • To find an unknown endpoint P₂ given M and P₁: x₂ = 2mₓ − x₁, y₂ = 2m_y − y₁, z₂ = 2m_z − z₁.
  • To find an unknown coordinate of an endpoint: set up the midpoint equation for that coordinate and solve algebraically.
  • Always verify your answer by substituting both endpoints back into the midpoint formula.
  • The 3D formula reduces to the 2D formula when z₁ = z₂ = 0.
  • The midpoint of a segment from P to the origin is (x/2, y/2, z/2).
