Unit 6 · Three Dimensional Geometry and Vectors in Space

🌐 Equation of a Sphere

A sphere is the 3D analogue of a circle. Learn to write its standard equation directly from the distance formula, read off its centre and radius, convert from general form using completing the square, and determine relationships between points and spheres.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

A circle is the set of all points in a plane at a fixed distance (radius) from a centre. A sphere is exactly the same idea in three dimensions — the set of all points in space at a fixed distance r from a fixed centre C(a, b, c). Because the distance formula is already known, the equation of a sphere follows immediately.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define a sphere and derive its equation from the distance formula
  • Write the standard form equation: (x−a)²+(y−b)²+(z−c)² = r²
  • Read the centre C(a,b,c) and radius r from a standard form equation
  • Write the general form and convert to standard form by completing the square
  • Determine whether a point is inside, on or outside a given sphere
  • Find the equation of a sphere given the endpoints of a diameter

Explanation
Complete theory with visual aids

2.1 Definition and Standard Form

📌 Definition — Sphere

A sphere with centre C(a, b, c) and radius r is the set of all points P(x, y, z) in space such that d(P, C) = r.

Applying the distance formula: √((x−a)²+(y−b)²+(z−c)²) = r
Squaring both sides gives the standard equation of a sphere:

(x − a)² + (y − b)² + (z − c)² = r²
Standard form · Centre C(a, b, c) · Radius r > 0

Every point P on the sphere is exactly r units from the centre C.

Circle (2D)
(x−a)²+(y−b)²=r²
Centre (a,b) · Radius r · Flat plane
Sphere (3D)
(x−a)²+(y−b)²+(z−c)²=r²
Centre (a,b,c) · Radius r · 3D space
📝 Special case — sphere centred at origin

When the centre is the origin C(0,0,0), the equation simplifies to x²+y²+z²=r². This is the most common special case on the entrance exam.

2.2 Reading Centre and Radius from Standard Form

Given the equation (x−a)²+(y−b)²+(z−c)²=r², identify the centre and radius by inspection:

Centre
The centre is (a, b, c) — the values being subtracted inside each bracket. Watch the signs carefully: (x+3)² means a = −3.
Radius
The right-hand side is r². Always take the positive square root: r = √(RHS). Never leave r as r².
Equation Centre Radius
(x−1)²+(y−2)²+(z−3)²=25 (1, 2, 3) 5
(x+2)²+(y−1)²+z²=9 (−2, 1, 0) 3
x²+y²+z²=16 (0, 0, 0) 4

2.3 General Form and Completing the Square

Expanding the standard form gives the general form:

x² + y² + z² + Dx + Ey + Fz + G = 0

To convert back to standard form, complete the square for each variable. Steps:

1
Group x terms, y terms and z terms together. Move the constant G to the right side.
2
For each group, add (half the coefficient)² to both sides. Example: x²+Dx → add (D/2)² to both sides → (x+D/2)².
3
Write each group as a perfect square. The right side is now r². Read off centre and radius.
⚠ Validity check

After completing the square, the right side must be positive for a real sphere. If it equals zero the "sphere" is a single point; if negative there is no real sphere.

2.4 Position of a Point Relative to a Sphere

📌 Inside, On or Outside

For a sphere with centre C(a,b,c) and radius r, and a point P(x₀,y₀,z₀), compute d = d(P,C):

• d < r: P is inside the sphere
• d = r: P is on the sphere (surface)
• d > r: P is outside the sphere

Equivalently, substitute (x₀,y₀,z₀) into the left side of the standard equation and compare to r²:

(x₀−a)²+(y₀−b)²+(z₀−c)² < r²
Inside the sphere
(x₀−a)²+(y₀−b)²+(z₀−c)² = r²
On the sphere
(x₀−a)²+(y₀−b)²+(z₀−c)² > r²
Outside the sphere

2.5 Equation of a Sphere Given a Diameter

If A(x₁,y₁,z₁) and B(x₂,y₂,z₂) are endpoints of a diameter:

Step 1 — Centre
C = midpoint of AB = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2, (z₁+z₂)/2)
Step 2 — Radius
r = ½ d(A,B) — half the length of the diameter segment.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Writing the Equation of a Sphere
Problem: Write the equation of the sphere with centre C(2, −1, 3) and radius 5.
Substitute
Use standard form with a=2, b=−1, c=3, r=5:
(x−2)²+(y−(−1))²+(z−3)² = 5²
Simplify
(x−2)²+(y+1)²+(z−3)² = 25
✅ Equation: (x−2)²+(y+1)²+(z−3)²=25
Example 2Reading Centre and Radius
Problem: Find the centre and radius of (x+3)²+(y−2)²+(z+1)²=49.
Centre
(x+3)²=(x−(−3))², so a=−3.
(y−2)², so b=2.
(z+1)²=(z−(−1))², so c=−1.
Centre = (−3, 2, −1)
Radius
r² = 49, so r = 7
✅ Centre = (−3, 2, −1)   Radius = 7
Example 3Converting from General Form
Problem: Find the centre and radius of x²+y²+z²−4x+6y−2z+5=0.
Group
Move constant: (x²−4x)+(y²+6y)+(z²−2z)=−5
Complete x
x²−4x: half of −4 is −2, (−2)²=4. Add 4 to both sides:
(x−2)² on left, +4 on right.
Complete y
y²+6y: half of 6 is 3, 3²=9. Add 9 to both sides:
(y+3)² on left, +9 on right.
Complete z
z²−2z: half of −2 is −1, (−1)²=1. Add 1 to both sides:
(z−1)² on left, +1 on right.
Result
(x−2)²+(y+3)²+(z−1)² = −5+4+9+1 = 9
✅ Centre = (2, −3, 1)   Radius = 3
Example 4Point Inside, On or Outside a Sphere
Problem: For the sphere (x−1)²+(y−2)²+(z−3)²=16, determine whether each point is inside, on or outside: (a) P(1,2,7)   (b) Q(3,2,3)   (c) R(5,2,3).
(a)
Substitute P(1,2,7): (1−1)²+(2−2)²+(7−3)²=0+0+16=16=r²
Equal to r² → P is on the sphere.
(b)
Substitute Q(3,2,3): (3−1)²+(2−2)²+(3−3)²=4+0+0=4 < 16
Less than r² → Q is inside the sphere.
(c)
Substitute R(5,2,3): (5−1)²+(2−2)²+(3−3)²=16+0+0=16=r²
Equal to r² → R is on the sphere.
✅ (a) On  |  (b) Inside  |  (c) On
Example 5Sphere from Endpoints of a Diameter
Problem: Find the equation of the sphere with diameter endpoints A(1, 2, 3) and B(5, 6, 7).
Centre
C = midpoint of AB:
C = ((1+5)/2, (2+6)/2, (3+7)/2) = (3, 4, 5)
Radius
r = ½ d(A,B):
d(A,B) = √((5−1)²+(6−2)²+(7−3)²) = √(16+16+16) = √48 = 4√3
r = 4√3/2 = 2√3, so r² = 12
Equation
(x−3)²+(y−4)²+(z−5)²=12
✅ (x−3)²+(y−4)²+(z−5)²=12

✅ Key Points — Equation of a Sphere
  • A sphere is the set of all points in space at a fixed distance r (radius) from a fixed point C(a,b,c) (centre).
  • Standard form: (x−a)²+(y−b)²+(z−c)²=r². Derived directly from the 3D distance formula.
  • Special case — sphere at the origin: x²+y²+z²=r².
  • To read centre: the values subtracted inside the brackets give (a,b,c). Watch signs: (x+3)² means a=−3.
  • To read radius: take the positive square root of the right side. r=√(RHS).
  • General form: x²+y²+z²+Dx+Ey+Fz+G=0. Convert to standard form by completing the square for each variable.
  • Point P relative to sphere: substitute into LHS. If result <r² → inside; =r² → on; >r² → outside.
  • Sphere from diameter AB: C=midpoint(A,B), r=½d(A,B), then write standard form.
