✖️ Geometric Sequences

Master geometric sequences — a key topic on the Ethiopian entrance exam. Learn to find any term, identify common ratios, insert geometric means, and solve real-world exponential growth problems.

Introduction
Why geometric sequences matter

Imagine a bacterial colony that doubles every hour. Or an investment that grows 10% every year. Or a bouncing ball that reaches 80% of its previous height on each bounce. These are all examples of geometric sequences — sequences that grow or shrink by a constant multiplicative factor.

While arithmetic sequences involve adding a constant difference, geometric sequences involve multiplying by a constant ratio. Both types are heavily tested on the Ethiopian entrance exam.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define a geometric sequence and identify its common ratio r
  • Use the nth term formula: aₙ = a₁ · rⁿ⁻¹
  • Find any term when given a₁ and r
  • Find a₁ or r from two given terms
  • Insert geometric means between two numbers
  • Identify the effect of r on sequence behavior
  • Solve real-world problems involving geometric growth and decay

Explanation
Complete theory with visual aids
📌 Definition — Geometric Sequence

A geometric sequence (also called a geometric progression or GP) is a sequence in which each term after the first is obtained by multiplying the preceding term by a fixed non-zero constant r, called the common ratio.

2.1 The Common Ratio

The common ratio r is found by dividing any term by the previous term:

r = a₂/a₁ = a₃/a₂ = a₄/a₃ = aₙ₊₁/aₙ
r > 1 (grows)
2, 6, 18, 54, ...
r = 3
0 < r < 1 (shrinks)
64, 32, 16, 8, ...
r = 1/2
r < 0 (alternates)
2, −6, 18, −54, ...
r = −3

2.2 The nth Term Formula

Starting from a₁ and multiplying by r repeatedly:

a₁
×r
a₁r
×r
a₁r²
×r
a₁r³
...
a₁rⁿ⁻¹
aₙ = a₁ · rⁿ⁻¹

Where: aₙ = nth term, a₁ = first term, r = common ratio, n = position

⚠ Most Common Mistake

Students often write aₙ = a₁ · rⁿ instead of a₁ · rⁿ⁻¹. The exponent is n MINUS 1, because the first term has r⁰ = 1 (multiply by r zero times).

2.3 Finding r from Two Non-Consecutive Terms

If you know two terms aₘ and aₙ you can find r by dividing their equations:

aₙ / aₘ = rⁿ⁻ᵐ  ⟹  r = ⁽ⁿ⁻ᵐ⁾√(aₙ/aₘ)

2.4 Geometric Means

Method — Inserting k Geometric Means between a and b

Total terms = k + 2. Common ratio: r = (b/a)^(1/(k+1))

Example: Insert 2 geometric means between 3 and 24.
r = (24/3)^(1/3) = 8^(1/3) = 2
Sequence: 3, 6, 12, 24

2.5 Arithmetic vs Geometric Comparison

Property Arithmetic Geometric
Operation Add d each time Multiply by r each time
Constant d = aₙ₊₁ − aₙ r = aₙ₊₁ / aₙ
nth term a₁ + (n−1)d a₁ · rⁿ⁻¹
Example use Salary raises, rows of seats Population growth, compound interest

2.6 Real-World Applications

  • Population growth: Population × constant factor each year (r = 1 + growth rate)
  • Compound interest: Money × (1 + rate) each period
  • Bacterial growth: Colony doubles every hour (r = 2)
  • Radioactive decay: Amount halved every half-life (r = 0.5)
  • Bouncing ball: Height × constant fraction each bounce
⚠ Strategy for Word Problems

Step 1: Find a₁ (initial value). Step 2: Find r (multiplication factor). Step 3: Write aₙ = a₁ · rⁿ⁻¹. Step 4: Substitute and solve. For growth: r = 1 + rate. For decay: r = 1 − rate.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1 Finding the nth Term
Problem: The first term of a geometric sequence is 3 and the common ratio is 2. Find the 6th term.
Given
a₁ = 3, r = 2, n = 6
Apply
a₆ = 3 · 2⁶⁻¹ = 3 · 2⁵ = 3 · 32 = 96
✅ The 6th term is 96 — Sequence: 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 96 ✓
Example 2 Finding r and a₁ from Two Terms
Problem: In a geometric sequence, a₂ = 6 and a₅ = 48. Find the common ratio and first term.
Step 1
Write equations:
a₂ = a₁ · r = 6  ...(i)
a₅ = a₁ · r⁴ = 48  ...(ii)
Step 2
Divide (ii) by (i):
r³ = 48/6 = 8  ⟹  r = 2
Step 3
Substitute into (i):
a₁ · 2 = 6  ⟹  a₁ = 3
✅ r = 2 and a₁ = 3 — Sequence: 3, 6, 12, 24, 48 ✓
Example 3 Inserting Geometric Means
Problem: Insert 3 geometric means between 2 and 162.
Step 1
Total terms = 3 + 2 = 5. So a₁ = 2, a₅ = 162.
Step 2
Find r:
r = (162/2)^(1/4) = 81^(1/4) = (3⁴)^(1/4) = 3
Step 3
Sequence with a₁ = 2, r = 3:
2, 6, 18, 54, 162
✅ The 3 geometric means are 6, 18, 54
Example 4 Negative Common Ratio
Problem: A geometric sequence has a₁ = 4 and r = −2. Find the first 5 terms and the 8th term.
Step 1
Generate terms by multiplying by −2:
4, −8, 16, −32, 64
Step 2
Find a₈:
a₈ = 4 · (−2)⁷ = 4 · (−128) = −512
✅ First 5 terms: 4, −8, 16, −32, 64  |  8th term: −512
Example 5 Real-World — Population Growth
Problem: A town has a population of 10,000. The population grows at 5% per year. What is the population after 4 years?
Identify
a₁ = 10,000,   r = 1.05,   n = 5 (after 4 years = 5th term)
Apply
a₅ = 10,000 · (1.05)⁴ = 10,000 · 1.2155 ≈ 12,155
✅ Population after 4 years ≈ 12,155

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 3
✅ Key Points — Geometric Sequences
  • A geometric sequence multiplies each term by a constant ratio r: found by r = aₙ₊₁ / aₙ
  • The nth term formula is aₙ = a₁ · rⁿ⁻¹ — the exponent is n MINUS 1, not n.
  • If r > 1 the sequence grows; if 0 < r < 1 it shrinks; if r < 0 signs alternate.
  • To find r from two terms: divide their equations to eliminate a₁, then take the appropriate root.
  • To insert k geometric means between a and b: r = (b/a)^(1/(k+1)).
  • The middle term squared equals the product of equidistant terms: aₘ² = aₘ₋ₖ · aₘ₊ₖ.
  • For real-world growth/decay: r = 1 + rate (growth) or r = 1 − rate (decay).
  • Key difference: arithmetic ADDS d every step, geometric MULTIPLIES by r every step.
