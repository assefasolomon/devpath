🌊 Fluid Flow

When fluids move, fascinating physics happens. Learn the difference between laminar and turbulent flow, the equation of continuity, and Bernoulli's principle — the physics behind why airplanes fly and blood flows.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked

Introduction
What you will learn

When fluid is in motion, its flow can be characterized as either laminar (smooth, orderly) or turbulent (chaotic, irregular). Understanding how fluids flow is essential for understanding everything from garden hoses to blood circulation.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Differentiate laminar and turbulent flow
  • Define flow rate and apply the continuity equation
  • Explain the Bernoulli principle and its applications
  • Understand viscosity and its effects
  • Apply fluid flow concepts to real-world problems
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

4.1 Types of Fluid Flow

📌 Laminar vs Turbulent Flow

Laminar (steady) flow:
• Each particle follows a smooth path — paths never cross
• Every particle arriving at a given point has the same velocity
• Occurs at low velocity, small diameter pipes

Turbulent flow:
• Irregular flow with whirlpools
• Adjacent layers cross each other
• Occurs at high velocity, large diameter pipes

4.2 Streamlines and Flow Rate

📌 Streamlines

A streamline is the path taken by a fluid particle under steady flow. The velocity of the particle is always tangent to the streamline.

A set of streamlines forms a tube of flow. In laminar flow, particles cannot flow into or out of the sides of this tube.

📌 Flow Rate (Q)

Q = V / t

The volume of fluid passing a point per unit time. Units: m³/s

Can also be written: Q = A · v

4.3 Equation of Continuity

For an incompressible fluid flowing through a pipe of varying cross-section, the same amount of fluid must pass every point per unit time.

📌 Continuity Equation

A₁ v₁ = A₂ v₂

Key insight: When a pipe narrows, fluid speeds up. When it widens, fluid slows down.

Valid for liquids (incompressible). Use with caution for gases.

4.4 Bernoulli's Principle

Discovered by Daniel Bernoulli, this principle connects pressure and speed in a moving fluid.

📌 Bernoulli's Principle

As a fluid's speed increases, the pressure it exerts on a surface decreases.

Applications:
• Airplane wings (lift)
• Blood pressure in vessels
• Snoring (soft palate)
• Wind instruments
• Atomizers and sprayers

📝 Why airplane wings create lift

Air moves faster over the top of the wing than the bottom. By Bernoulli's principle, pressure is lower on top. The higher pressure below pushes the wing upward — creating lift.

4.5 Viscosity

📌 Viscosity — Internal Friction

Viscosity is the internal friction between adjacent layers of fluid moving relative to each other.

• Causes part of the fluid's kinetic energy to be transformed into internal energy
• Similar to friction between sliding solid surfaces
• Blood has significant viscosity — important for circulation
• Honey is more viscous than water

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Blood Pumped in a Lifetime
Problem: How many cubic meters of blood does the heart pump in a 75-year lifetime, assuming average flow rate = 5.00 L/min?
Formula
V = Q × t
Convert time
75 years × 5.26 × 10⁵ min/year = 3.945 × 10⁷ min
Substitute
V = 5 × 3.945×10⁷ L = 1.97 × 10⁸ L
Convert to m³
V = 1.97 × 10⁸ / 1000 ≈ 2.0 × 10⁵ m³
✅ Total blood ≈ 200,000 m³ (about 80 Olympic swimming pools!)
Example 2Garden Hose and Nozzle
Problem: A nozzle with radius 0.250 cm is attached to a hose of radius 0.900 cm. Flow rate is 0.500 L/s. Find the speed in (a) the hose and (b) the nozzle.
Part a
v₁ = Q / A₁ = Q / (πr₁²)
v₁ = (0.500 × 10⁻³) / (π(9.00×10⁻³)²) ≈ 1.96 m/s
Part b — Continuity
v₂ = v₁ × (r₁/r₂)²
v₂ = 1.96 × (0.900/0.250)² ≈ 25.4 m/s
Interpret
The narrow nozzle increases speed 13× — this is why a hose with a nozzle sprays much farther.
✅ (a) v₁ ≈ 1.96 m/s   (b) v₂ ≈ 25.5 m/s
Example 3Why Paper Lifts When You Blow
Problem: Explain why a piece of paper held at its corners lifts up when you blow air across its top surface.
Bernoulli
Moving air has lower pressure than still air.
Pressure difference
Air above the paper is moving → lower pressure. Air below is still → higher pressure.
Result
The higher pressure below pushes the paper upward.
✅ The paper lifts because of Bernoulli's principle — faster air = lower pressure.
Example 4Blood Speed in Constricted Artery
Problem: Blood flows at 0.40 m/s in an artery of radius 1.0 cm. It passes into a constriction where the radius is 0.30 cm. Find the speed in the constriction.
Continuity
A₁ v₁ = A₂ v₂ → v₂ = v₁ × (r₁/r₂)²
Substitute
v₂ = 0.40 × (1.0/0.30)² = 0.40 × 11.1 ≈ 4.4 m/s
✅ Blood speed ≈ 4.4 m/s (11× faster!)
Example 5Airplane Wing Lift
Problem: Explain how the shape of an airplane wing produces lift.
Shape
Wing is curved — air travels farther over the top than the bottom.
Speed
Air over the top must move faster to meet air from bottom.
Bernoulli
Faster air → lower pressure on top.
Net force
Higher pressure below pushes wing upward → lift.
✅ Bernoulli's principle explains wing lift: faster air over top → lower pressure → lift.

  • Applications: airplane lift, snoring, wind instruments, sprayers, blood flow.
  • Viscosity — internal friction between fluid layers; slows flow.
  • Blood has significant viscosity — matters for circulation.
