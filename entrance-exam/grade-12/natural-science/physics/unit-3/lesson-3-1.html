💧 Fluid Statics

Fluids are everywhere — water in a glass, air in a room, blood in your veins. This lesson introduces the physics of fluids at rest: pressure, density, and why some objects float while others sink.

📖 Reading: ~22 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Matter exists as solids, liquids, or gases. Liquids and gases are both fluids — they lack the ability to resist deformation and flow under force. Fluid mechanics studies fluids at rest and in motion.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Compare the properties of solids, liquids, and gases
  • Define pressure and its various units
  • Distinguish between absolute, atmospheric, and gauge pressure
  • Calculate density and specific gravity
  • Solve problems involving pressure and density
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

1.1 Solids, Liquids, and Gases

📌 Molecular Structure

Solids: Atoms in close contact, arranged in a repeating pattern. Forces between them are like springs — allow vibration but not free movement. Solids resist all types of stress.

Liquids: Molecules close together but free to slide and rotate. Intermolecular forces weaker than solids but still strong compared to gases. Liquids flow but maintain volume.

Gases: Atoms separated by large distances. Forces between them very weak except when they collide. Gases flow, are compressible, and expand to fill their container.

1.2 Pressure

Pressure is defined as the normal force exerted per unit area.

📌 Pressure

P = F / A

Unit: pascal (Pa) = N/m²
Other units:
• 1 atm = 760 mmHg = 760 torr = 101.3 kPa = 14.7 psi

📝 Why nails penetrate easily

A nail tip has a very small area. Even a modest force produces huge pressure (P = F/A). That's why a hammer can drive a nail into wood.

1.3 Absolute, Gauge, and Vacuum Pressure

📌 Three Types of Pressure

• Absolute pressure — measured relative to absolute vacuum (zero)
• Gauge pressure — measured relative to atmospheric pressure (can be + or −)
• Vacuum pressure — when gauge pressure is negative

P_gauge = P_abs − P_atm
P_vac = P_atm − P_abs

1.4 Density and Specific Gravity

📌 Density (ρ)

ρ = m / V

Unit: kg/m³

Density measures the mass of one cubic meter of a substance.

📌 Specific Gravity (SG)

SG = ρ_substance / ρ_water

Reference: water at 4°C, ρ = 1000 kg/m³
Specific gravity is dimensionless — just a number.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Pressure from a Woman's Shoe
Problem: A 55 kg woman stands on one shoe of area 30 cm × 10 cm. Find the pressure. Express in kPa.
Area
A = 0.30 × 0.10 = 0.03 m²
Force
F = mg = 55 × 9.8 = 539 N
Pressure
P = 539 / 0.03 = 17,967 Pa ≈ 18 kPa
✅ Pressure ≈ 18 kPa
Example 2Nail Tip Pressure
Problem: What force on a nail with circular tip of 1 mm diameter creates a pressure of 3 × 10⁹ N/m²?
Area
d = 1 mm = 1 × 10⁻³ m
A = π(d/2)² = π(5×10⁻⁴)² ≈ 7.85 × 10⁻⁷ m²
Force
F = PA = 3×10⁹ × 7.85×10⁻⁷ ≈ 2.36 × 10³ N
✅ Force ≈ 2,360 N — shows why tiny areas create huge pressures
Example 3Person's Density
Problem: A person of 80 kg has dimensions 1.8 m tall × 0.3 m wide × 0.1 m thick. Estimate their density.
Volume
V = 1.8 × 0.3 × 0.1 = 0.054 m³
Density
ρ = 80 / 0.054 ≈ 1,481 kg/m³
✅ Density ≈ 1,500 kg/m³ (about 1.5× water density)
Example 4Absolute Pressure from Vacuum
Problem: A vacuum gauge reads 5.8 psi where P_atm = 14.5 psi. Find absolute pressure.
Formula
P_abs = P_atm − P_vac
Substitute
P_abs = 14.5 − 5.8 = 8.7 psi
✅ Absolute pressure = 8.7 psi
Example 5Converting Pressure Units
Problem: Convert 25 kPa to atm.
Relation
1 atm = 101.3 kPa
Solve
P_atm = 25 / 101.3 = 0.247 atm
✅ 25 kPa = 0.247 atm

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 3.1
✅ Key Points — Fluid Statics
  • Fluids = liquids + gases; they flow under force.
  • Solids resist all types of stress; liquids and gases don't.
  • Pressure = F/A, measured in pascals (Pa = N/m²).
  • 1 atm = 101.3 kPa = 760 mmHg = 14.7 psi.
  • Absolute pressure is measured from vacuum; gauge pressure from atmospheric.
  • Density ρ = m/V, in kg/m³.
  • Specific gravity = ρ/ρ_water — dimensionless.
  • Water density at 4°C = 1000 kg/m³ (reference).
  • Substances with SG < 1 float on water; SG > 1 sink.
