Unit 3 · Fluid Mechanics · Natural Science

🚢 Archimedes' Principle

Why do ships float? Why does an iceberg hide 89% of its mass underwater? The answer lies in Archimedes' Principle — the buoyant force that lifts objects in fluids.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

It's hard to push a beach ball underwater because water pushes back. This upward force — the buoyant force — comes from the fact that pressure increases with depth. The bottom of a submerged object experiences more force than the top.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • State Archimedes' Principle
  • Calculate buoyant force from fluid displacement
  • Determine whether an object floats, sinks, or suspends
  • Find the fraction submerged of a floating object
  • Solve problems on density from apparent mass
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

3.1 Archimedes' Principle

Discovered by the Greek mathematician Archimedes (287–212 BC), this principle is one of the oldest known laws of physics.

📌 Archimedes' Principle

The buoyant force on an object equals the weight of the fluid it displaces.

F_B = W_fluid = ρ_fluid · g · V_displaced

3.2 Origin of Buoyant Force

📌 Why Does Buoyancy Exist?

Pressure increases with depth. So the pressure on the bottom of an immersed object is greater than the pressure on the top.

Net upward force: F_B = P₂A − P₁A = ρ_fluid · g · h · A = ρ_fluid · g · V

where V = Ah is the displaced volume.

3.3 Floating, Sinking, and Suspending

📌 Three Cases

• ρ_object < ρ_fluid → object floats (buoyant force > weight)
• ρ_object = ρ_fluid → object suspends (buoyant force = weight)
• ρ_object > ρ_fluid → object sinks (buoyant force < weight)

3.4 Fraction Submerged

📌 Floating Objects

For a floating object, weight = buoyant force:
ρ_object · V_object = ρ_fluid · V_displaced

V_displaced / V_object = ρ_object / ρ_fluid

The fraction submerged equals the ratio of densities.

📝 Icebergs

Ice has ρ ≈ 917 kg/m³; seawater ≈ 1030 kg/m³.
Fraction submerged = 917/1030 ≈ 0.89 = 89% hidden below water. Only 11% is visible!

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Iceberg Floating
Problem: An iceberg floats in seawater. ρ_ice = 917 kg/m³, ρ_sea water = 1030 kg/m³. What fraction of the iceberg is below water?
Formula
V_sub / V_ice = ρ_ice / ρ_seawater
Substitute
V_sub / V_ice = 917 / 1030 ≈ 0.89
✅ 89% is submerged — only 11% visible above water!
Example 2Buoyant Force on Submerged Steel
Problem: 10,000 metric tons of steel (1.00 × 10⁷ kg) submerged in water. Find the buoyant force. (ρ_steel = 7,800 kg/m³)
Volume
V = m / ρ = 10⁷ / 7,800 ≈ 1.28 × 10³ m³
Buoyant force
F_B = ρ_w g V = 1000 × 9.8 × 1.28×10³ ≈ 1.26 × 10⁷ N
Compare
Weight = 10⁷ × 9.8 = 9.8 × 10⁷ N → weight ≫ buoyant force → sinks
✅ Buoyant force ≈ 1.26 × 10⁷ N — much less than weight.
Example 3Maximum Load of a Steel Boat
Problem: Same steel shaped into a boat displacing 1.00 × 10⁵ m³ of water. What is the maximum buoyant force?
Formula
F_B = ρ_w g V_displaced
Substitute
F_B = 1000 × 9.8 × 1.00 × 10⁵ = 9.8 × 10⁸ N
Interpret
This is 10× the weight of the steel → boat can carry a load of 9× its own weight without sinking.
✅ Max buoyant force ≈ 9.8 × 10⁸ N — enough to float the ship plus cargo.
Example 4Density of an Ancient Coin
Problem: A coin's mass in air is 8.630 g; when submerged in water its apparent mass is 7.800 g. Find the density.
Displaced water
m_w = 8.630 − 7.800 = 0.830 g
Volume
V_w = m_w / ρ_w = 0.830 / 1.000 = 0.830 cm³
Density of coin
ρ_coin = 8.630 / 0.830 ≈ 10.4 g/cm³
Identify
Density ~10.4 g/cm³ matches silver (10.5 g/cm³).
✅ Density ≈ 10.4 g/cm³ — the coin is silver.
Example 5Wooden Block Floating
Problem: A wooden block floats with 75% of its volume submerged in water. Find its density.
Formula
V_sub / V = ρ_wood / ρ_water
Solve
0.75 = ρ_wood / 1000 → ρ_wood = 750 kg/m³
✅ Density of the wood = 750 kg/m³

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 3.3
✅ Key Points — Archimedes' Principle
  • Archimedes' Principle: buoyant force equals the weight of the fluid displaced.
  • F_B = ρ_fluid g V_displaced
  • Buoyancy arises from the difference in pressure at the bottom vs top of an object.
  • ρ_object < ρ_fluid → floats; = → suspends; > → sinks.
  • Fraction submerged = ρ_object / ρ_fluid for floating objects.
  • Icebergs: ~89% submerged because ρ_ice < ρ_seawater.
  • Apparent mass method: ρ_object = m / (m − m_app) × ρ_fluid.
  • Steel ships float because their shape displaces more water than solid steel would.
  • Buoyant force exists whether the object floats, sinks, or suspends.
