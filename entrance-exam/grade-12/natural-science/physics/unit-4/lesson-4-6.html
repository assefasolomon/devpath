🔋 Transformers

Transformers are the unsung heroes of the electrical grid — they step voltage up for transmission and down for safe use at home. Learn how they work using Faraday's law of induction.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

When you charge your phone from a wall outlet, a small transformer inside the charger steps high voltage (220 V) down to a safe level (5 V). Transformers use electromagnetic induction to change AC voltage without changing frequency.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain what a transformer does
  • Distinguish step-up from step-down transformers
  • Apply the turns ratio equation
  • Understand why transformers need AC, not DC
  • Calculate efficiency and power in transformers
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

6.1 What is a Transformer?

📌 Definition

A transformer is an electrical device that transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another through electromagnetic induction — most commonly to increase (step up) or decrease (step down) voltage without changing frequency.

Transformer Structure
Two coils wrapped around a common iron core. AC current in the primary creates a changing magnetic flux in the core, inducing voltage in the secondary.

6.2 Step-up vs Step-down Transformers

⬆️ Step-up Transformer
  • More turns on secondary (Nₛ > Nₚ)
  • Secondary voltage > primary voltage (Vₛ > Vₚ)
  • Used at power plants to raise voltage for transmission
  • Secondary current is lower
⬇️ Step-down Transformer
  • Fewer turns on secondary (Nₛ < Nₚ)
  • Secondary voltage < primary voltage (Vₛ < Vₚ)
  • Used in phone chargers, laptop adapters, home appliances
  • Secondary current is higher

6.3 Why AC, Not DC?

📌 The Key Requirement

Transformers only work with alternating current (AC).

Reason: The magnetic flux in the core must be changing to induce emf (Faraday's law). AC changes direction and magnitude continuously → flux continuously changes → emf is induced. DC produces constant flux → no induced emf.

6.4 The Turns Ratio

📌 Transformer Equation

Nₚ / Nₛ = Vₚ / Vₛ

Where:
• Nₚ = number of primary turns
• Nₛ = number of secondary turns
• Vₚ = primary voltage
• Vₛ = secondary voltage

📝 Energy conservation

Ideal transformer: Power in = Power out
Vₚ Iₚ = Vₛ Iₛ

If voltage is stepped up, current is stepped down proportionally.

6.5 Efficiency

📌 Efficiency of a Transformer

η = (output power / input power) × 100%

Real transformers achieve up to 98% efficiency. Losses come from:
• Resistance in the coils (I²R loss)
• Eddy currents in the core
• Hysteresis (magnetic friction)
• Flux leakage

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Output Voltage from Turns Ratio
Problem: A transformer has 500 primary turns and 5000 secondary turns. Input voltage = 220 V. Find output voltage.
Formula
Vₛ = Vₚ × (Nₛ/Nₚ)
Substitute
Vₛ = 220 × (5000/500) = 220 × 10
Result
Vₛ = 2200 V — this is a step-up transformer.
✅ Output voltage = 2200 V
Example 2Secondary Current
Problem: A transformer has 1200 primary turns and 1000 secondary turns. Primary current = 4 A. Find secondary current.
Power conservation
VₚIₚ = VₛIₛ → Iₛ = Iₚ × (Nₚ/Nₛ)
Substitute
Iₛ = 4 × (1200/1000) = 4.8 A
✅ Secondary current = 4.8 A
Example 3Phone Charger Transformer
Problem: A phone charger steps 240 V down to 5 V. If the primary has 1200 turns, how many turns does the secondary have?
Rearrange
Nₛ = Nₚ × (Vₛ/Vₚ)
Substitute
Nₛ = 1200 × (5/240) = 25 turns
✅ Secondary has 25 turns
Example 4Transformer Efficiency
Problem: A transformer takes in 200 W and outputs 190 W. What is its efficiency?
Formula
η = (P_out / P_in) × 100%
Substitute
η = (190/200) × 100% = 95%
✅ Efficiency = 95%
Example 5Why DC Fails
Problem: Explain why a transformer connected to a DC source (like a battery) produces no output voltage.
Faraday's law
Induced emf requires a changing magnetic flux.
DC behaviour
DC produces a constant flux → ΔΦ/Δt = 0 → ε = 0.
✅ No changing flux → no induced emf, no output.

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 4.6
✅ Key Points — Transformers
  • A transformer changes AC voltage without changing frequency.
  • Works on electromagnetic induction (Faraday's law).
  • Step-up: Nₛ > Nₚ, voltage increases.
  • Step-down: Nₛ < Nₚ, voltage decreases.
  • Turns ratio: Nₚ/Nₛ = Vₚ/Vₛ.
  • Power is conserved: VₚIₚ = VₛIₛ.
  • Transformers only work with AC — DC produces no changing flux.
  • Efficiency: η = (P_out/P_in) × 100%; up to 98% real.
  • Applications: phone chargers, laptop adapters, power grid transmission, microwaves.
