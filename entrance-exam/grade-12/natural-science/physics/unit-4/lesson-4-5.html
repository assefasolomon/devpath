📉 Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction

Faraday's law quantifies the exact relationship between changing flux and induced emf. Lenz's law tells us the direction of the induced current — always opposing the change that created it.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Faraday's experiments established that a changing flux induces an emf. But how much emf? And in which direction does the induced current flow? Two laws answer these questions: Faraday's law and Lenz's law.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • State and apply Faraday's law
  • State and apply Lenz's law
  • Calculate induced emf for coils of N turns
  • Determine the direction of induced current
  • Apply conservation of energy to electromagnetic induction
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

5.1 Faraday's Law

📌 Faraday's Law

The magnitude of the induced emf is directly proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux in a closed coil.

ε = − ΔΦ / Δt

For a coil of N turns (each turn experiences the same flux change):
ε = − N ΔΦ / Δt

• ε = induced emf (volts)
• N = number of turns
• ΔΦ = change in magnetic flux (Wb)
• Δt = time interval (s)
• The negative sign is Lenz's law — see below

5.2 Lenz's Law

📌 Lenz's Law

The induced current always flows in a direction that opposes the change that caused it.

Consequences:
• Magnet approaching coil → induced current creates field that repels the magnet
• Magnet receding from coil → induced current creates field that attracts the magnet
• This is a manifestation of the conservation of energy

📝 Why Lenz's law matters

If induced current helped the change instead of opposing it, energy would be created from nothing. Lenz's law ensures energy is conserved.

5.3 Ways to Increase Induced emf

📌 Practical Tips

From ε = −N ΔΦ/Δt, we can increase emf by:

• Using a stronger magnet (larger B)
• Moving the magnet faster (larger ΔΦ/Δt)
• Using more turns N in the coil
• Changing orientation faster (rotating coil)
• Increasing the area of the coil

5.4 Applications

📌 Faraday's Law in Action

• Electric generators — rotating coil in a magnetic field
• Transformers — change AC voltage
• Induction cookers — changing flux heats the pan
• Wireless chargers — inductive coupling
• Metal detectors — detect eddy currents
• Credit card readers — magnetic strip reading
• Microphones — convert sound to electrical signals

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Simple Faraday Calculation
Problem: A coil of 40 turns has a magnetic flux through each turn changing from 0.10 Wb to 0.02 Wb in 0.20 s. Find the induced emf.
Flux change
ΔΦ = 0.10 − 0.02 = 0.08 Wb
Formula
ε = N ΔΦ / Δt
Solve
ε = 40 × 0.08 / 0.20 = 16 V
✅ Induced emf = 16 V
Example 2Square Loop in a Field
Problem: A square loop of side 10 cm and resistance 0.5 Ω is placed vertically in a field of 0.10 T. The field decreases to zero in 0.70 s. Find the induced emf and current.
Area
A = 0.10² = 0.01 m²
Initial flux
Φ₀ = BA cos θ = 0.10 × 0.01 × cos 45° ≈ 7.07 × 10⁻⁴ Wb
Emf
ε = ΔΦ / Δt = 7.07×10⁻⁴ / 0.70 ≈ 1.0 × 10⁻³ V
Current
I = ε / R = 10⁻³ / 0.5 = 2 × 10⁻³ A = 2 mA
✅ ε ≈ 1 mV   I = 2 mA
Example 3N-Turn Coil Emf
Problem: A coil of 1250 turns has flux 5 mWb reversed in 0.125 s. Find the average induced emf.
Flux change
Flux is reversed — from +5 mWb to −5 mWb → ΔΦ = 10 mWb = 1 × 10⁻² Wb
Formula
ε = N ΔΦ / Δt
Solve
ε = 1250 × 0.01 / 0.125 = 100 V
✅ Induced emf = 100 V
Example 4Direction of Induced Current
Problem: A magnet with its N pole facing a coil is moved toward the coil. What direction does the induced current flow?
Lenz's law
The induced current must oppose the incoming N pole.
Field direction
It creates an N pole on the side facing the magnet — this repels the incoming magnet.
Current direction
By the right-hand rule, current flows counter-clockwise when viewed from the magnet's side.
✅ Induced current is counter-clockwise (from magnet's view).
Example 5Generator Principle
Problem: A generator has 500 turns of area 0.01 m² in a 0.5 T field. If the coil rotates at 50 Hz, estimate the peak emf.
Flux
Φ_max = BA = 0.5 × 0.01 = 5 × 10⁻³ Wb
Rate of change
Coil rotates 50 times per second → flux completes one cycle in 1/50 s
Peak emf
ε_peak ≈ N × Φ_max × ω ≈ 500 × 5×10⁻³ × 2π×50 ≈ 785 V
✅ Peak emf ≈ 785 V

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 4.5
✅ Key Points — Faraday's Law
  • Faraday's law: ε = −N ΔΦ/Δt — induced emf proportional to rate of flux change.
  • For a single coil: ε = −ΔΦ/Δt.
  • Lenz's law: induced current opposes the change that caused it.
  • Lenz's law is a consequence of conservation of energy.
  • Magnet approaching a coil → induced current creates a field that repels it.
  • Magnet receding → induced current creates a field that attracts it.
  • To increase induced emf: stronger magnet, faster motion, more turns, larger area.
  • Applications: generators, transformers, induction cookers, wireless chargers, metal detectors.
  • Induced current: I = ε / R.
