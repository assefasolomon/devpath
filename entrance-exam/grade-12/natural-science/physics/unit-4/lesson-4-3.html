⚡ Current and Magnetism

In 1819, Hans Christian Oersted accidentally discovered that an electric current could deflect a compass needle. This discovery connected electricity and magnetism forever — and gave us Ampere's law, the right-hand rule, and modern electromagnets.

📖 Reading: ~20 min

Introduction
What you will learn

When current flows through a conductor, it produces heat and light energies — but also a magnetic field. This magnetic effect of current is the basis of every electromagnet, motor, and generator.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain the relationship between current and magnetism
  • State and apply Ampere's law
  • Calculate the magnetic field around a straight wire (B = μ₀I/2πr)
  • Apply the right-hand rule for current-carrying wires
  • Describe applications: electromagnets, relays, electric bells, DC motors

Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

3.1 Current Produces a Magnetic Field

Whenever current flows through a conductor, a magnetic field is produced around it. This was discovered by Hans Christian Oersted in 1819.

📌 Key Observation

A compass placed near a current-carrying wire deflects — its needle aligns with the magnetic field around the wire.

• The compass returns to normal when the current is turned off
• The magnetic field is generated by moving charges

3.2 Ampere's Law

📌 Ampere's Law

The magnetic field around an electric current is proportional to the current.

Each segment of current produces a magnetic field. The total field of any shaped current is the vector sum of the fields from each segment.

3.3 Magnetic Field Around a Long Straight Wire

📌 Magnetic Field of a Straight Wire

B = μ₀ I / (2 π r)

• μ₀ = permeability of free space = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A
• I = current (amperes)
• r = perpendicular distance from the wire (meters)
• B = magnetic field strength (Tesla)

📌 Properties of the Field

• Field lines form a circular pattern around the wire
• Field strength increases with current
• Field is stronger near the wire, weaker farther away
• Reversing current reverses the direction of B

3.4 The Right-Hand Rule

📌 Direction of B Around a Wire

Fleming's Right-Hand Rule:

1. Point your right thumb in the direction of the current
2. Curl your fingers around the wire
3. Your fingers point in the direction of the magnetic field lines

📝 Mnemonic

Thumb = current direction, fingers = field direction.
This rule works for any current-carrying conductor.

3.6 Applications of Electromagnetism

📌 Electromagnets at Home & in Industry

• Electric bells — magnet pulls a hammer to strike the bell
• Magnetic relays — used as switches in circuits
• DC electric motors — convert electrical energy to mechanical
• Generators & transformers — power distribution
• Loudspeakers & headphones — convert electrical signals to sound
• MRI machines — medical imaging
• Electric fans, doorbells, induction cookers, magnetic locks

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Current for Earth-strength Field
Problem: Find the current in a long straight wire that would produce a magnetic field twice the strength of Earth's field (5.0 × 10⁻⁵ T) at a distance of 5.0 cm.
Target B
B = 2 × 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ T
Rearrange
I = 2πrB / μ₀
Substitute
I = (2π × 0.05 × 1.0 × 10⁻⁴) / (4π × 10⁻⁷) = 25 A
✅ Current = 25 A
Example 2Magnetic Field Near a Wire
Problem: A wire carries 10 A. Find the magnetic field at 5 cm from the wire.
Formula
B = μ₀I / (2πr)
Substitute
B = (4π × 10⁻⁷ × 10) / (2π × 0.05) = 4 × 10⁻⁵ T
✅ B = 4 × 10⁻⁵ T (0.4 Gauss)
Example 3Distance for Given B
Problem: A long straight wire carries 10 A. At what distance is the magnetic field 8 × 10⁻⁴ T?
Rearrange
r = μ₀I / (2πB)
Substitute
r = (4π×10⁻⁷ × 10) / (2π × 8×10⁻⁴) = 2.5 × 10⁻³ m
✅ Distance = 2.5 mm
Example 4Reverse Current Direction
Problem: A wire carries current upward. A compass is placed east of the wire. Which way does the field point?
Rule
Right thumb points upward (current direction).
Fingers
Fingers curl — on the east side, they point toward the south.
Reverse
If current is reversed (down), the field reverses direction — points north.
✅ With current up: field at east side points south.
Example 5Electromagnet Strength
Problem: What happens to the magnetic field of an electromagnet if you double the current through the coil?
Ampere's law
B is directly proportional to I.
Result
Doubling current → doubling the magnetic field strength.
✅ Magnetic field doubles when current doubles.
