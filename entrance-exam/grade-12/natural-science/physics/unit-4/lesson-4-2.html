🧭 Magnetic Field Lines

Field lines are an elegant visual tool that lets us "see" invisible magnetic fields. Learn how to draw them, interpret them, and use them to predict how magnets and currents behave.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions

Introduction
What you will learn

Magnetic fields are invisible, but they can be mapped with field lines. Just like iron filings scattered around a magnet reveal the field, field lines help us visualize its strength and direction.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Describe the properties of magnetic field lines
  • Draw field patterns for bar magnets and like/opposite poles
  • Compare magnetic and electric field lines
  • Explain why magnetic field lines are closed curves
  • Interpret field line density as field strength
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

2.1 What Are Magnetic Field Lines?

📌 Magnetic Field Lines

Imaginary lines used to represent magnetic fields. They show:
• Direction — where a compass would point
• Strength — indicated by the density of lines

When iron filings are sprinkled around a magnet, they orient along these field lines — making them visible.

2.2 Properties of Magnetic Field Lines

📌 Six Key Properties

1. Field lines have both direction and magnitude at every point
2. The direction of B is tangent to the field line
3. Field strength is proportional to the closeness of lines (denser = stronger)
4. Field lines never cross — the field is unique at each point
5. Field lines are continuous closed loops — they have no beginning or end
6. Outside the magnet, lines go N → S; inside, S → N

2.3 Field Patterns — Common Configurations

Single Bar Magnet
Lines emerge from N pole and enter S pole. Inside the magnet, they travel from S to N — forming closed loops.
Opposite Poles (N–S Attract)
Field lines connect the two poles directly — from N to S. Opposite poles strongly attract.
Like Poles (N–N Repel)
Field lines curve away from each other — they do NOT connect. Like poles strongly repel.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems
Example 1Predicting Force Direction
Problem: Two bar magnets are placed with their N poles facing each other. What happens?
Analyze
Like poles (N–N) — the field lines curve away from each other.
Result
The magnets experience a repulsive force — they push apart.
✅ The two magnets repel each other.
Example 2Reading Field Strength
Problem: In a magnetic field diagram, one region has very dense field lines; another has sparse lines. Which region has a stronger field?
Rule
Field strength is proportional to the density of field lines.
Conclusion
Dense region = stronger field. Sparse region = weaker field.
✅ The region with denser lines has the stronger field.
Example 3Why Lines Never Cross
Problem: Explain why two magnetic field lines can never cross each other.
Reason
If they crossed, the field would have two directions at the same point.
Contradiction
The magnetic field direction is unique at each point — so crossing is impossible.
✅ Field lines never cross because the field has only one direction at any point.
Example 4Compass Direction
Problem: A compass needle is placed near a bar magnet. Which way does the N pole of the needle point?
Rule
The compass needle aligns with the local magnetic field.
Direction
The N pole of the needle points in the direction of the field line at that point.
✅ The N pole points along the field line at the compass location.
Example 5Field Inside a Magnet
Problem: What is the direction of the magnetic field inside a bar magnet?
Rule
Outside the magnet: N → S.
Inside
Inside the magnet: S → N — completing the closed loop.
✅ Inside a bar magnet, field lines go from S pole to N pole.
