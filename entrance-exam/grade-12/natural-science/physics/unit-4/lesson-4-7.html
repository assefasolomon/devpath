Unit 4 · Electromagnetism · Natural Science

⚡ Application and Safety

Electromagnetism powers modern life — from generators to MRI machines. But electric and magnetic fields also carry risks. Learn real-world applications, the four main uses of magnets, and essential safety guidelines for EMF exposure.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Over the last 200 years, physicists have discovered an enormous amount about electromagnetism. These discoveries now power nearly every device we use. But with power comes responsibility — electromagnetic fields can harm living tissue, and electrical systems can cause fires, shocks, and explosions.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Identify real-world applications of electromagnetism
  • Recognize the four common uses of magnets
  • Understand EMF safety guidelines
  • List safety measures for electromagnet use
  • Apply EMF exposure limits to practical situations
📖
Explanation
Real-world applications of electromagnetism

7.1 Common Uses of Magnets

🔔
Electric Bells
An electromagnet pulls a hammer to strike a bell, then releases it — creating the ringing sound.
⚙️
Generators & Motors
Magnets create the field that converts mechanical ↔ electrical energy in power plants and appliances.
🧭
Navigation
Compasses use Earth's magnetic field to find direction — essential for ships, planes, and hikers.
♻️
Recycling & Separation
Strong magnets separate magnetic metals from non-magnetic materials in scrapyards.
📌 Medical Uses of Magnets

Magnets are widely used in the medical field for treating pain in different body parts. MRI machines use enormous magnetic fields (~1.5 T) to image the body without radiation.

7.2 Everyday Devices Using Electromagnetism

🚪
Doorbells & Locks
Electromagnets release or engage locks, ring bells, and control garage doors.
📻
Speakers & Headphones
Coils in a magnetic field vibrate to produce sound waves from electrical signals.
💾
Data Storage
Hard drives write/read data using magnetic fields on spinning disks (magnetic induction).
🔋
Wireless Chargers
Use electromagnetic induction to transfer power from charger to phone without wires.

7.3 Medical Imaging with EMF

📌 MRI — Magnetic Resonance Imaging

MRI uses powerful magnetic fields (typically 1.5 T) plus radio waves to image soft tissue. It doesn't use harmful ionizing radiation like X-rays or CT scans.

⚠️
EMF Safety
Protecting yourself from electromagnetic fields

7.4 What are EMFs?

📌 Electric, Magnetic, and Electromagnetic Fields (EMF)

EMFs include static fields and low-frequency waves. They are classified as non-ionizing radiation — different from gamma/X-rays or UV/IR lasers.

EMFs are classified by frequency or wavelength.

EMF Warning Symbol
Non-ionizing EMF exposure must be limited by keeping a safe distance from strong sources.

7.5 Safety Tips for Electromagnets

🔍
1. Define the type of plates
  • Know what you're lifting
  • Match magnet strength to load
⚠️
2. Check potential hazards
  • Falling elements (common lifting risk)
  • Active body implants affected by EMF
  • Keep a safe distance
✅
3. Check equipment before use
  • Full overhaul of lift system
  • Create a pre-use checklist
  • Don't use if any check fails
🎯
4. Choose the right magnet
  • Each project has specific needs
  • Don't over- or under-specify
  • Match to intended load

7.6 General EMF Safety Precautions

⚠️ High-risk groups

• People with pacemakers or other active implants
• Pregnant women near industrial equipment
• Workers near high-voltage transmission lines

📌 Best Practices

✓ Keep a safe distance from strong magnetic fields
✓ Use proper shielding when necessary
✓ Follow manufacturer guidelines for equipment
✓ Never disable safety features (valves, shields)
✓ Report any unusual sensations near high-power equipment
✓ Take regular safety training

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1MRI Safety Distance
Problem: A hospital MRI machine produces B = 1.5 T at its center. At what distance does the field drop to safe levels below 5 mT? (Assume B ∝ 1/r³ outside the bore.)
Ratio
B₁r₁³ = B₂r₂³ (same field source)
Estimate
If 1.5 T is at r = 1 m, then 5 mT (5×10⁻³ T) is at:
r₂ = r₁ × (B₁/B₂)^(1/3) = 1 × (1.5/0.005)^(1/3) ≈ 6.7 m
✅ Safe distance ≈ 7 m from the bore.
Example 2Power Grid Transformer
Problem: A power station transformer steps 11 kV up to 220 kV. If the input current is 1000 A, what is the output current? (Assume 100% efficiency.)
Power conservation
VₚIₚ = VₛIₛ
Solve
Iₛ = (11,000 × 1000) / 220,000 = 50 A
Insight
Stepping voltage UP steps current DOWN — reducing losses in transmission wires.
✅ Output current = 50 A
Example 3Induction Cooker
Problem: Explain how an induction cooker heats a pot without heating the surrounding air.
Setup
A coil beneath the surface carries alternating current.
Physics
Changing current → changing magnetic flux → eddy currents induced in the pot's metal base.
Result
Eddy currents heat the pot directly via resistance. The cooker surface stays cool.
✅ Induction heating works via electromagnetic induction — the pot heats itself.
Example 4Wireless Phone Charging
Problem: Explain why wireless chargers cannot charge through a metal phone case.
Physics
Wireless charging uses a changing magnetic field between charger and phone coil.
Metal effect
Metal blocks changing magnetic fields — eddy currents circulate in the metal instead of reaching the phone.
Result
Energy is wasted as heat in the metal case. Charging becomes inefficient or fails.
✅ Metal cases block the changing magnetic flux required for induction.
Example 5Pacemaker Warning
Problem: Why are people with pacemakers warned to stay away from strong magnetic fields?
Pacemaker
A pacemaker is an electronic device that regulates heartbeat using electrical signals.
Interaction
Strong magnetic fields can interfere with its electronics or reprogram it.
Risk
Malfunction could cause irregular heartbeat — a serious medical emergency.
✅ Magnetic fields can disrupt pacemaker electronics, so patients must keep a safe distance.
