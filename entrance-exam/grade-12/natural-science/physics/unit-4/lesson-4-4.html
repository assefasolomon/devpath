🔌 Electromagnetic Induction

Faraday's great discovery: a changing magnetic field can produce an electric current. This one insight led to the invention of the generator and transformed the modern world.

Introduction
What you will learn

Just as current produces a magnetic field, can a magnetic field produce current? The answer is yes — discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831. This phenomenon, electromagnetic induction, is how power plants generate electricity.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define electromagnetic induction
  • Explain how changing magnetic flux induces current
  • Describe Faraday's experiments
  • Calculate magnetic flux through a surface
  • Understand the difference between induced emf and induced current

Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

4.1 The Discovery of Electromagnetic Induction

📌 Historical Background

• 1820 — Oersted: moving charges produce magnetic fields
• 1831 — Faraday asked: can magnetic fields produce current?
• Faraday's answer: YES — with a changing magnetic field

This discovery led to the invention of the dynamo (generator) and transformer — which together power the modern world.

4.2 What is Electromagnetic Induction?

📌 Electromagnetic Induction

The process by which a changing magnetic field produces an electromotive force (emf) and hence an induced current in a closed circuit.

Key word: changing — a steady magnetic field does NOT induce current.

4.3 Faraday's Experiments

Faraday connected a coil to a galvanometer and moved a magnet toward and away from the coil.

📌 What He Observed

• Magnet moving toward the coil → galvanometer deflects one way
• Magnet held stationary → no deflection
• Magnet moving away → galvanometer deflects the other way
• Faster motion → larger deflection

Conclusion: The induced current depends on relative motion between the coil and the magnetic field. Either the magnet, the coil, or both can move.

4.4 Magnetic Flux (Φ)

Magnetic flux is a measure of the total magnetic lines of force passing through a given area.

📌 Magnetic Flux Equation

Φ = B · A = B A cos θ

• B = magnetic field strength (T)
• A = area (m²)
• θ = angle between B and the area vector (perpendicular to surface)
• Unit: weber (Wb), where 1 Wb = 1 T·m²

📝 Special cases

• When B ⟂ surface (θ = 0): Φ = BA (maximum)
• When B ∥ surface (θ = 90°): Φ = 0 (no flux through surface)

4.5 When is Current Induced?

📌 Conditions for Induced Current

Current is induced whenever the magnetic flux through the circuit changes. Flux changes when:

• The magnetic field strength B changes
• The area of the circuit A changes
• The orientation θ changes
• The magnet or coil moves

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Magnetic Flux Through a Square Loop
Problem: A square loop of side 3 cm is positioned in a uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T. The plane of the loop makes an angle of 60° with the field. Find the flux through the loop.
Area
A = (0.03)² = 9 × 10⁻⁴ m²
Angle
Angle between B and A (normal) = 90° − 60° = 30°
Formula
Φ = BA cos θ = 0.5 × 9×10⁻⁴ × cos 30°
Solve
Φ ≈ 3.9 × 10⁻⁴ Wb
✅ Flux ≈ 3.9 × 10⁻⁴ Wb
Example 2Flux Through a Circular Loop
Problem: A circular loop of area 200 cm² sits perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field of 0.5 T. Find the magnetic flux.
Area
A = 200 cm² = 2 × 10⁻² m²
Angle
θ = 0 (perpendicular) → cos θ = 1
Solve
Φ = 0.5 × 2×10⁻² = 1 × 10⁻² Wb
✅ Flux = 0.01 Wb
Example 3Why No Current When Magnet is Still
Problem: Why does a galvanometer show no deflection when a magnet is held stationary inside a coil?
Physics
Induction requires a changing magnetic flux.
Reason
A stationary magnet produces constant flux — no change → no induced emf.
✅ No current is induced without a change in flux.
Example 4Effect of Angle on Flux
Problem: A coil of area 0.5 m² is in a 0.2 T field. Find the flux when (a) B ⟂ A, (b) B ∥ A, (c) angle is 60°.
Case a
Φ = BA = 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.1 Wb
Case b
Φ = 0 (no flux through surface)
Case c
Φ = 0.2 × 0.5 × cos 60° = 0.05 Wb
✅ (a) 0.1 Wb, (b) 0, (c) 0.05 Wb
Example 5Real-world Application
Problem: Explain how a bicycle dynamo uses electromagnetic induction to light a lamp.
Setup
The dynamo contains a coil and a permanent magnet.
Action
As the wheel turns, the magnet rotates past the coil, changing the flux through it.
Result
Changing flux induces an emf → current flows → lamp lights.
✅ The dynamo converts mechanical energy → electrical energy via electromagnetic induction.

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 4.4
✅ Key Points — Electromagnetic Induction
  • Electromagnetic induction = producing emf/current via a changing magnetic field.
  • Discovered by Michael Faraday (1831).
  • A steady magnetic field produces no current — only changes do.
  • Magnetic flux: Φ = BA cos θ, unit is weber (Wb).
  • Flux is maximum when B ⟂ surface (θ = 0) and zero when B ∥ surface.
  • Moving a magnet toward a coil induces current in one direction; moving away reverses it.
  • Faster motion → larger induced emf.
  • Applications: generators (dynamos), transformers, induction cookers, wireless chargers.
  • This discovery enabled the modern electrical grid.
