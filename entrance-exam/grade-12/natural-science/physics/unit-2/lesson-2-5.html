🌍 Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

Every object in the universe attracts every other object. Learn how Newton's law of gravitation explains falling apples, orbiting moons, planetary motion — and why Kepler's laws work.

📖 Reading: ~22 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked

Introduction
What you will learn

In 1666, Sir Isaac Newton realized that the force making an apple fall is the same force that keeps the Moon in orbit. This universal force acts between every pair of masses in the universe — that's why it's called universal gravitation.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • State and apply Newton's law of universal gravitation
  • Use the gravitational constant G
  • Calculate the force between two masses
  • Derive g from gravitation
  • Understand centripetal force and orbital velocity
  • Derive Kepler's third law from Newton's laws
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

5.1 The Universal Law of Gravitation

Newton defined this attraction mathematically: the force of attraction between two masses is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.

📌 Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

F_g = G m₁ m₂ / r²

• G = universal gravitational constant = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
• m₁, m₂ = two masses
• r = distance between their centres

The force is attractive, along the line joining the two masses. F₁₂ = −F₂₁ (Newton's third law).

5.2 Gravitational Acceleration (g)

Using F = mg and F_g = GMm/r², we can derive the value of g at the surface of any planet:

📌 Derivation of g

mg = G M m / r²
Cancel m:
g = G M / r²

For Earth (M = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, r = 6.38 × 10⁶ m):
g ≈ 9.8 m/s²

5.3 Centripetal Force and Orbital Motion

Gravity provides the centripetal force that keeps planets, moons, and satellites in orbit. Without it, they'd travel in straight lines.

📌 Centripetal Force & Orbital Velocity

Centripetal force: F_c = m v² / r

Setting F_c = F_g and solving for v:
m v² / r = G M_s m / r²
v = √(G M_s / r)

And since v = 2πr / T, we get:
T² / r³ = 4π² / (G M_s)

This is Kepler's third law — derived from Newton's law of gravitation!

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Force Between Two Masses
Problem: A 10 kg mass and a 100 kg mass are 1 meter apart. What is the force of attraction between them?
Formula
F = G m₁ m₂ / r²
Substitute
F = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 10 × 100) / 1²
Solve
F = 6.67 × 10⁻⁸ N
✅ Force = 6.67 × 10⁻⁸ N (tiny, but real)
Example 2Weight on Earth
Problem: A person has mass 60 kg. What is the gravitational force on them at Earth's surface? (M_E = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, r_E = 6.38 × 10⁶ m)
Formula
F = G M_E m / r_E²
Substitute
F = (6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 5.97×10²⁴ × 60) / (6.38×10⁶)²
Solve
F ≈ 584 N
Check
Using W = mg: 60 × 9.8 = 588 N ✓ (small diff due to rounding)
✅ Weight ≈ 588 N
Example 3Mass of the Sun
Problem: Calculate the mass of the Sun. Earth's orbit: T = 3.156 × 10⁷ s, r = 1.496 × 10¹¹ m.
Formula
T² / r³ = 4π² / (G M_s)
Rearrange
M_s = 4π² r³ / (G T²)
Substitute
M_s = 4π² (1.496×10¹¹)³ / (6.67×10⁻¹¹ × (3.156×10⁷)²)
Solve
M_s ≈ 1.99 × 10³⁰ kg
✅ Mass of Sun ≈ 1.99 × 10³⁰ kg
Example 4Weight Change with Altitude
Problem: If Earth had the same mass but 8 times its present volume, by what factor would a person's weight at the surface change?
Volume
V = (4/3)πr³. If V increases 8×, then r³ increases 8× → r increases 2×
Gravity
g = GM / r². If r doubles, g decreases by factor 4 (2²).
Weight
W = mg. So weight would be 1/4 of current.
✅ Weight would drop to 1/4 of its current value
Example 5Orbital Velocity
Problem: Find the orbital velocity of a satellite in low Earth orbit (r ≈ 6.6 × 10⁶ m). (M_E = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg)
Formula
v = √(G M / r)
Substitute
v = √((6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 5.97×10²⁴) / 6.6×10⁶)
Solve
v = √(6.03 × 10⁷) ≈ 7,770 m/s
✅ Orbital velocity ≈ 7.8 km/s (about 28,000 km/h!)
