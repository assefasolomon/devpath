Unit 2 · Two Dimensional Motion · Natural Science

🔄 Rotational Motion

When a body rotates about a fixed axis, every point moves in a circle. Learn how to describe rotation with angular quantities — displacement, velocity, and acceleration — and how they relate to linear motion.

📖 Reading: ~22 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Rotational motion is the motion of a body in which all its particles move in a circle about a common axis with the same angular velocity. Examples: Earth's rotation, a spinning fan, wheels of a car, a rotating disc.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define angular displacement, velocity, and acceleration
  • Apply the right-hand rule for angular vectors
  • Use the equations of rotational motion with constant α
  • Relate angular and translational quantities (v = ωr, a = αr)
  • Solve rotational kinematics problems
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

2.1 Rigid Body and Angular Displacement

A rigid body is a body with a perfectly defined, unchanging shape — the distance between any two particles within the body is constant.

📌 Angular Displacement (Δθ)

For a particle at distance r moving through an arc length s:
s = r θ

Angular displacement: Δθ = θ_f − θ_0

Units: radians (rad) or degrees
1 revolution = 2π rad = 360°

2.2 Angular Velocity and Angular Acceleration

📌 Angular Velocity (ω)

ω_av = Δθ / Δt

Units: rad/s

📌 Angular Acceleration (α)

α_av = Δω / Δt

Units: rad/s²

📝 Right-Hand Rule

Wrap your right hand's fingers in the direction of rotation. Your thumb points in the direction of ω (along the axis). The direction of α follows from α = Δω/Δt.

2.3 Equations of Rotational Motion

For rotation with constant angular acceleration, we have kinematic equations analogous to linear motion:

Linear (constant a) Angular (constant α)
v = v₀ + a·tω = ω₀ + α·t
Δs = ½(v₀ + v)tΔθ = ½(ω₀ + ω)t
Δs = v₀t + ½at²Δθ = ω₀t + ½αt²
v² = v₀² + 2aΔsω² = ω₀² + 2αΔθ

2.4 Relationship Between Angular and Translational Quantities

📌 Key Relations

v = ω r    (tangential speed)
a = α r    (tangential acceleration)
Δs = r Δθ

📝 Important insight

Every point on a rigid body has the same ω and α, but different v and a because r differs. Points farther from the axis move faster tangentially.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Average Angular Velocity
Problem: A rotating wheel's angular speed changes from 30 rad/s to 50 rad/s in 2 seconds. What is the average angular velocity?
Given
ω₀ = 30 rad/s, ω_f = 50 rad/s, t = 2 s
Formula
ω_av = (ω₀ + ω_f) / 2
Solve
ω_av = (30 + 50) / 2 = 40 rad/s
✅ Average angular velocity = 40 rad/s
Example 2Revolutions and Final Speed
Problem: A rotating wheel has initial angular velocity 10 rad/s and accelerates at 2.5 rad/s². (a) How many revolutions in 30 s? (b) What is its speed at t = 20 s?
Part a
Δθ = ω₀t + ½αt² = 10×30 + ½×2.5×900 = 300 + 1125 = 1425 rad
Convert
Δθ = 1425 / (2π) ≈ 226.9 rev
Part b
ω = ω₀ + αt = 10 + 2.5×20 = 60 rad/s
✅ (a) Δθ = ~226.9 revolutions   (b) ω = 60 rad/s
Example 3Linear Speed from Rotation
Problem: A bicycle wheel has angular velocity 18 rad/s and radius 40 cm. What is the bicycle's speed?
Given
ω = 18 rad/s, r = 0.4 m
Formula
v = ω r
Solve
v = 18 × 0.4 = 7.2 m/s
✅ Bicycle speed = 7.2 m/s
Example 4Pulley Wrapping a Rope
Problem: A rope is wrapped around a pulley of radius 50 cm. How many revolutions are needed to raise a bucket 20 m?
Arc length
Δs = 20 m, r = 0.5 m
Formula
Δθ = Δs / r = 20 / 0.5 = 40 rad
Convert
Δθ = 40 / (2π) ≈ 6.34 revolutions
✅ About 6.34 revolutions
Example 5Tangential Acceleration from Car
Problem: A car accelerates from 20 m/s to 24 m/s in 5 s. Radius of wheels = 40 cm. Find angular acceleration.
Tangential a
a_t = Δv / Δt = (24 − 20) / 5 = 0.8 m/s²
Angular α
α = a_t / r = 0.8 / 0.4 = 2 rad/s²
✅ Angular acceleration = 2 rad/s²

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 2.2
✅ Key Points — Rotational Motion
  • A rigid body has fixed shape; all its points share the same ω and α.
  • Angular displacement: Δθ = θ_f − θ₀. Units: radians.
  • 1 revolution = 2π rad = 360°.
  • Angular velocity: ω = Δθ/Δt (rad/s).
  • Angular acceleration: α = Δω/Δt (rad/s²).
  • Equations of rotational motion mirror linear motion:
    ω = ω₀ + αt, Δθ = ω₀t + ½αt², ω² = ω₀² + 2αΔθ.
  • Right-hand rule gives the direction of ω.
  • Relations to linear motion: v = ωr, a = αr, Δs = rΔθ.
  • Points farther from the axis move faster tangentially — same ω, larger v.
