⚙️ Rotational Dynamics

Newton's laws of motion have rotational equivalents. Learn about torque (rotational force), moment of inertia (rotational mass), and how they combine to produce angular acceleration: τ = Iα.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources

Introduction
What you will learn

Rotational dynamics answers the question: what causes a change in rotational motion? The answer is torque — the rotational equivalent of force. The resistance to that change is the moment of inertia. Together: τ = Iα.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Define torque as τ = rF sin θ
  • Calculate moment of inertia for point masses and systems
  • Apply τ = Iα (rotational Newton's second law)
  • Find net torque from multiple forces
  • Solve problems involving rotational dynamics
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

3.1 Torque — The Rotational Force

Torque is the rotational effect of force — what causes an object to acquire angular acceleration.

📌 Torque (τ)

τ = r F sin θ

• r = distance from axis to point of force application
• F = applied force
• θ = angle between r and F

Units: N·m (newton-meters)
Direction: Along the axis of rotation, determined by the right-hand rule

3.2 Moment of Inertia (I)

The moment of inertia is the rotational analog of mass — it measures the resistance of a body to changes in its rotational motion.

📌 Moment of Inertia

For a single point mass:
I = m r²

For multiple point masses:
I = m₁r₁² + m₂r₂² + m₃r₃² + ...

Units: kg·m²
Depends on: mass, distribution of mass, and axis of rotation

📝 What it means

The greater the moment of inertia, the more resistance to change in angular velocity. A flywheel with large I is hard to start or stop — which is exactly what makes it useful for storing energy.

3.3 Torque and Angular Acceleration

📌 Newton's Second Law for Rotation

τ = I α

This is the rotational analog of F = ma. The angular acceleration α is proportional to the net torque τ.

📝 Analogy table

• Force F ↔ Torque τ
• Mass m ↔ Moment of inertia I
• Acceleration a ↔ Angular acceleration α
• F = ma ↔ τ = Iα

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Net Torque on a Body
Problem: The body is pivoted at O. Three forces act: F₁ = 10 N at 8 m (120°), F₂ = 16 N at 4 m (150°), F₃ = 19 N at 3 m (45°). Find the net torque.
Torque 1
τ₁ = 8 × 10 × sin 120° = −69.3 N·m (clockwise)
Torque 2
τ₂ = 4 × 16 × sin 150° = 32 N·m (counterclockwise)
Torque 3
τ₃ = 3 × 19 × sin 45° = 40.3 N·m (counterclockwise)
Net
τ_net = −69.3 + 32 + 40.3 = 3 N·m (counterclockwise)
✅ Net torque = 3 N·m counterclockwise
Example 2Moment of Inertia of Three Particles
Problem: Three particles on the y-axis: 4 kg at y = 3 m, 2 kg at y = −2 m, 3 kg at y = −4 m. Find I about the x-axis.
Formula
I = m₁r₁² + m₂r₂² + m₃r₃²
Substitute
I = 4(3)² + 2(2)² + 3(4)²
Solve
I = 36 + 8 + 48 = 92 kg·m²
✅ I = 92 kg·m²
Example 3Rotational Inertia from Torque
Problem: A torque of 36 N·m applied to a wheel causes an angular acceleration of 24 rad/s². Find the moment of inertia.
Formula
τ = I α → I = τ / α
Substitute
I = 36 / 24 = 1.5 kg·m²
✅ I = 1.5 kg·m²
Example 4Flywheel Acceleration
Problem: A motor produces constant torque 100 N·m and drives a flywheel with I = 0.1 kg·m². (a) Find α. (b) How long to reach 150 rad/s from rest?
Part a
α = τ / I = 100 / 0.1 = 1000 rad/s²
Part b
t = (ω_f − ω₀) / α = 150 / 1000 = 0.15 s
✅ (a) α = 1000 rad/s²   (b) t = 0.15 s
Example 5Force Applied to a Beam
Problem: A force of 400 N is applied to a beam at 5 m from the pivot, at an angle of 30°. Find the torque.
Formula
τ = r F sin θ
Substitute
τ = 5 × 400 × sin 30° = 5 × 400 × 0.5 = 1000 N·m
✅ Torque = 1000 N·m

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 2.3
✅ Key Points — Rotational Dynamics
  • Torque (τ = rF sin θ) is the rotational effect of a force.
  • Torque is a vector with direction along the rotation axis, given by the right-hand rule.
  • Moment of inertia (I) is the rotational analog of mass — measures resistance to rotational change.
  • For a point mass: I = mr².
  • For multiple masses: I = Σ mᵢ rᵢ².
  • Newton's second law for rotation: τ = Iα.
  • Analogy: force ↔ torque, mass ↔ moment of inertia, a ↔ α.
  • Larger I means more resistance to angular acceleration.
