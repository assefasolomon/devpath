🎯 Physics and Defense Technology

Modern defense technology runs on physics. From RADAR to missiles, from night vision to laser guidance — this lesson explores how physics principles shape the modern battlefield.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources

Introduction
What you will learn

Every modern defense force — Air Force, Army, Navy, Space Force — relies on physics: laser guidance, satellite technology, electronics, optics, sensing systems, high-energy-density physics, atomic/nuclear physics, hydrodynamics, and advanced materials.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain the working principle of RADAR
  • Describe how missiles are propelled and guided
  • Understand infrared night vision technology
  • Identify physics concepts used in defense systems
  • Appreciate the applications of RADAR beyond military (aviation, weather)
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

8.1 RADAR — RAdio Detection And Ranging

RADAR uses electromagnetic waves to detect and track objects (ships, aircraft, missiles, etc.).

📌 How RADAR Works

1. Transmitter sends out a radio pulse
2. Pulse hits the target and reflects back as an echo
3. Receiver detects the echo
4. Time delay t gives the range: R = c·t / 2

The same antenna usually transmits and receives.

📝 Why divide by 2?

The signal travels to the target and back — that's twice the distance. So the distance one way is half of c·t.

8.2 Missiles

A missile is a rocket-propelled or jet-propelled weapon designed to deliver an explosive payload at high speed and accuracy.

📌 Types of Missiles

• Cruise missiles — jet-propelled throughout flight
• Ballistic missiles — rocket-powered only initially, then free-fall under gravity and air resistance

Physics involved:
• Newtonian mechanics — trajectory, momentum conservation
• Jet propulsion — oxygen from air; rocket — carries its own oxygen
• Electronics — guidance, tracking, control

📝 Missile subsystems

A missile contains: guidance unit, flight stabilizer, propelling unit, internal control unit, target-tracking unit, warhead, exploding unit, electrical system. All coordinated by continuous radio communication with the launcher.

8.3 Infrared Night Vision

Human eyes detect visible light (400–700 nm). Infrared (IR) waves are just outside this range — but every object emits IR in proportion to its temperature.

📌 How Night Vision Works

• IR devices detect heat emissions from objects
• Thermal imaging creates images from temperature differences
• Hotter objects appear brighter
• Colors in the image represent temperatures, not actual colors
• Night vision images appear green because green is best for human night vision

8.4 Other Defense Applications of Physics

📌 Physics in Each Branch

• Air Force — turbulent fluid flows, navigation, long-range observation
• Army — night vision, all-weather vision, detection avoidance
• Navy — oceanographic physics, sound propagation in water, meteorology
• Space Force — satellites, orbital mechanics, space surveillance
• All branches — lasers, electronics, optics, materials physics, high-energy-density physics

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems
Example 1RADAR Range Calculation
Problem: A RADAR signal takes 300 μs to travel to a target and back. How far is the target? (c = 3×10⁸ m/s)
Formula
R = c·t / 2
Convert
t = 300 μs = 3 × 10⁻⁴ s
Substitute
R = (3×10⁸ × 3×10⁻⁴) / 2 = 9×10⁴ / 2 = 4.5 × 10⁴ m
✅ Target is 45 km away
Example 2Detecting Missile Speed
Problem: A missile was at 60 km. After 10 s it is at 75 km. What is its average speed?
Change in range
ΔR = 75 − 60 = 15 km = 15,000 m
Formula
v = ΔR / Δt
Solve
v = 15,000 / 10 = 1,500 m/s
✅ Average speed = 1,500 m/s (1.5 km/s)
Example 3Why Cruise Missiles Are Slower
Problem: Why is a cruise missile slower than a ballistic missile?
Cruise
Jet-propelled throughout flight — uses air-breathing engines, limited speed.
Ballistic
Rocket-powered initially, then free-fall under gravity from very high altitude → reaches hypersonic speeds.
✅ Ballistic missiles reach higher speeds because they travel through space (no air resistance) and use gravity to accelerate.
Example 4Infrared Night Vision Image
Problem: A soldier uses night vision and sees a warm car engine glowing. Why?
Physics
All objects emit IR radiation proportional to their temperature.
Detection
The warm engine emits more IR than the cooler surroundings.
Display
The IR detector converts the intensity to visible light — engine appears bright.
✅ Night vision detects heat differences; the warm engine appears brightest.
Example 5RADAR Beyond Military
Problem: List three civilian uses of RADAR.
Uses
1. Air traffic control — tracking commercial aircraft
2. Weather RADAR — detecting precipitation
3. Navigation RADAR — ships and boats
4. Speed guns — police enforcing speed limits
✅ RADAR serves aviation, meteorology, marine navigation, and law enforcement.
