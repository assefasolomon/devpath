🧬 Physics and Biology

Life itself is a physics phenomenon. Every heartbeat, muscle movement, breath, and thought follows the laws of physics. In this lesson you'll discover how Newtonian mechanics explains animal motion, how fluid dynamics drives blood circulation, and how electricity carries nerve signals.

📖 Reading: ~22 min

Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

To truly understand how life works, you must understand physics. The human body is a remarkable physical system — it obeys Newton's laws, uses fluid mechanics to circulate blood, generates sound waves to speak, and transmits electrical pulses to think.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Apply Newton's laws to the motion and equilibrium of living systems
  • Explain blood flow using physics of fluid dynamics
  • Describe sound production in the human body in terms of wave physics
  • Relate electrical phenomena to nerve signaling and life processes
  • Understand how optics explains vision and light-sensitive organs

Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

3.1 Newtonian Mechanics in Living Organisms

Newtonian mechanics tells us how animals and their body parts move. The combination of physics and biology explains how a cheetah runs at 100 km/h, how a person walks without falling, and why we can carry heavy loads.

📌 Center of Gravity and Stability

A body is in stable equilibrium under gravity if its center of mass (c.g.) is directly over its base of support. Under this condition, the reaction force at the base cancels gravity and the torque it produces.

• If the c.g. is outside the base → torque causes the body to fall
• The wider the base and lower the c.g. → the more stable the body
• That's why we're more stable lying down than standing on one foot

📝 Example: Person carrying a load

When you carry a heavy load on one side, your body automatically bends to shift the center of gravity back over your feet. This is an unconscious application of Newtonian mechanics — your body is doing physics!

3.2 Fluid Flow and Blood Circulation

Blood circulation is a fluid dynamics problem. The physics of viscosity, continuity, and turbulent flow explains how blood moves through vessels and how pressure varies with position in the body.

📌 Physics of Blood Flow

• Viscosity — internal friction in blood slows flow
• Continuity equation — blood speeds up in narrow vessels (A₁v₁ = A₂v₂)
• Pascal's principle — pressure transmission helps push blood from the heart
• Valves + muscle action — prevent backflow and assist upward flow from feet

📝 How does blood go up against gravity?

When you stand, blood from your feet must travel up to the heart. Physics helps in three ways:
1. Pressure from heart pushes blood through arteries
2. One-way valves in veins prevent backflow
3. Skeletal muscle contraction squeezes veins, pushing blood upward (the "muscle pump")

3.3 Sound Production and Hearing

Sound is a mechanical wave produced by vibrating bodies. In humans, the vocal cords produce sound when they vibrate as air passes through them during exhalation.

📌 How Speech Works

1. Vocal folds come together in the larynx
2. Air from lungs passes through them during exhalation
3. The folds vibrate, disturbing surrounding air molecules
4. The vibrational disturbance propagates as a sound wave
5. When it reaches the ear, it causes the eardrum to vibrate
6. Nerve impulses travel to the brain, which interprets the sound

3.4 Electrical Phenomena in Living Systems

Many life processes involve electrical phenomena. The nervous system, muscle control, and even some animal senses are governed by electrical interactions.

📌 The Nervous System — A Living Circuit

• Specialized cells called neurons form a network throughout the body
• They receive, process, and transmit information
• Signals are electrical pulses — not chemical wires
• The brain is the control center, storing and analyzing information
• When a neuron receives a stimulus, it produces an electrical pulse that propagates along its length

📝 Fascinating: Sharks detect electricity

Sharks have small organs along their skin that are sensitive to electric fields in water. A shark can respond to a field as small as 1 μV/m — a millionth of a volt per meter! They use this to find prey buried in sand and possibly even to communicate.

3.5 Optical Physics and Vision

Light plays a fundamental role in living systems. Plants use light in photosynthesis; animals use light-sensitive organs like eyes to gather information about their environment.

📌 Light in Biology

• Visible light = 400 nm to 700 nm — where most solar radiation reaches Earth
• Photosynthesis — plants convert CO₂ and water into organic materials using light
• Eyes — main source of information about surroundings
• Bioluminescence — some bacteria and insects produce light through chemical reactions
• Optics tools — microscopes, telescopes, lasers for biology

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Stability of a Standing Person
Problem: Why is a person less stable standing on one foot than on two?
Physics
Stability depends on the base of support and the position of the center of gravity.
Compare
On two feet: base area ≈ 200 cm². On one foot: base area ≈ 100 cm².
Analyze
Smaller base → smaller tolerance for c.g. movement → less stable.
✅ Smaller base of support = less stable. Two feet give a wider base than one foot.
Example 2Blood Flow Speed in a Narrow Artery
Problem: Blood flows at 0.4 m/s in an artery of radius 1.0 cm. It passes into a constricted section of radius 0.5 cm. What is the speed in the constricted section?
Formula
Continuity equation: A₁v₁ = A₂v₂
Areas
A₁ = π(0.01)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁴ m²
A₂ = π(0.005)² = 7.85 × 10⁻⁵ m²
Solve
v₂ = v₁ × (A₁/A₂) = 0.4 × (3.14 × 10⁻⁴ / 7.85 × 10⁻⁵) = 0.4 × 4 = 1.6 m/s
✅ The blood speeds up to 1.6 m/s in the constricted section.
Example 3Sound Production in Vocal Cords
Problem: Explain, using wave physics, how your voice produces sound.
Step 1
Air from lungs is pushed through the larynx.
Step 2
The vocal cords come together and vibrate as air passes through.
Step 3
The vibration disturbs surrounding air molecules → sound wave propagates outward.
Step 4
The wave reaches a listener's ear → eardrum vibrates → nerve signals → brain interprets sound.
✅ Sound is produced by vibrating vocal cords creating a mechanical wave in air.
Example 4Nerve Signals as Electrical Pulses
Problem: You touch a hot stove and instantly pull your hand back. Explain using physics.
Reception
Heat sensors in your skin trigger electrical pulses in sensory neurons.
Transmission
The pulses travel along the neuron to the spinal cord — at speeds up to 120 m/s.
Response
The spinal cord sends a signal to muscle neurons → muscles contract → hand pulls back.
Physics
The signal is an electrical pulse, not a chemical or mechanical one. This is why the reaction is so fast.
✅ Nerve signals are electrical pulses that travel through neurons, allowing rapid reflex actions.
Example 5Why is Vision Limited to Visible Light?
Problem: Human eyes detect wavelengths only between 400 nm and 700 nm. Why this range?
Solar radiation
Most electromagnetic radiation from the Sun reaching Earth's surface is in the 400–700 nm range.
Evolution
Life evolved to utilize this abundant radiation — natural selection favored eyes sensitive to visible light.
Physics
Other wavelengths (UV, IR) are either absorbed by the atmosphere or don't provide enough useful information for vision.
✅ Human vision is tuned to visible light (400–700 nm) because that's where most solar radiation reaches Earth's surface.
