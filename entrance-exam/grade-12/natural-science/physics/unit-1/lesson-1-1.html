🔬 Physics and Other Sciences

Physics is the most fundamental of all sciences — every other discipline builds on its laws. In this lesson you'll discover how physics connects with chemistry, biology, astronomy, geology, engineering, medicine, and more. You'll see why physics truly is the foundation of modern science.

Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

Science is not a collection of separate islands — it is one connected continent. Physics sits at the centre. Every other scientific field, from chemistry to medicine, uses the laws of physics to explain how the world works.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain why physics is called the most fundamental science
  • Describe how physics connects with chemistry, biology, astronomy, geology
  • Identify physics concepts applied in other sciences
  • Recognize physics in engineering, medicine, defense, and communication
  • Appreciate how physics has shaped modern technology

Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

1.1 Physics — The Foundation of All Sciences

Physics studies the fundamental forces of nature — gravity, electromagnetism, the strong and weak nuclear forces. Every other scientific discipline is built on these forces. Think of physics as the trunk of a tree; chemistry, biology, geology, and astronomy are the branches.

📌 Why Physics is Fundamental

The laws of physics (Newton's laws, thermodynamics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics) apply universally — from the motion of galaxies to the vibration of atoms. No other science can claim such universal scope. That's why physics is called the most basic and all-inclusive science.

📝 Key idea

Physics doesn't just describe the world — it underpins every other science. Chemists study atoms using physics; biologists study motion and electricity using physics; geologists study the Earth using physics; engineers design machines using physics.

1.2 Physics and Chemistry

Physics and chemistry overlap when we study matter composed of electrons and nuclei. The rules of atomic and subatomic physics explain why atoms bond, why reactions happen, and how molecules form.

📌 How Physics Supports Chemistry

• Atomic physics explains covalent and ionic bonding
• Thermodynamics tells chemists if a reaction is energetically possible
• Spectroscopy reveals atomic and molecular structure
• Electricity & magnetism explain X-ray diffraction and radioactivity

In short: chemistry is rooted in atomic and molecular physics.

1.3 Physics and Biology

Life itself is a physics phenomenon. Every movement of a muscle, every heartbeat, every thought involves physical forces and energy.

Biology Process Physics Concept Used
Animal movement (walking, running)Newton's laws of motion
Blood circulationFluid dynamics, viscosity, Pascal's principle
Speech and hearingSound waves, vibration, resonance
Nerve signalsElectricity, electrical pulses
Vision (eye)Optics, light, refraction
📝 Fascinating fact

Sharks can detect electric fields as small as 1 μV/m — that's a millionth of a volt per meter! They use this to find prey buried in sand.

1.4 Physics and Astronomy

Astronomers can't touch stars or visit galaxies. They study distant objects using light and other electromagnetic waves — all governed by physics.

📌 Tools of Astrophysics

• Newton's laws — describe planetary orbits and Kepler's laws
• Electromagnetic spectrum — radio, IR, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma-ray telescopes
• Atomic physics — emission and absorption spectra reveal composition and temperature
• Inverse square law — apparent brightness ∝ true brightness / distance²

📝 Light year

A light year is the distance light travels in one year:
9.46 × 10¹² km (about 9.46 trillion km).

1.5 Physics and Geology

Geology is the study of the solid Earth. Geophysicists use physics to study rocks, minerals, earthquakes, and the Earth's interior.

📌 Physics Concepts Used in Geology

• Force, stress, strain — for rock deformation and earthquakes
• Optics & spectroscopy — to identify minerals
• Electromagnetic radiation — for seismic and radar surveys
• Heat and heat flow — for studying the Earth's interior
• Radioactivity — for radiometric dating of rocks
• Wave propagation — for seismic exploration (finding oil, gas, minerals)

1.6 Physics and Engineering

Engineers apply physics to build the modern world. Every bridge, engine, and electronic circuit uses physical laws.

Engineering Branch Physics Concepts Used
CivilForces, fluid pressure, gravity, elasticity
MechanicalMechanics, thermodynamics, aerodynamics
ElectricalElectromagnetism, circuits, semiconductors
ChemicalMolecular physics, thermodynamics, energy changes
📝 Ethiopian engineering heritage

Ancient Ethiopian civilizations applied physics principles to build the Axumite obelisks, the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela, and the Harar Jugol walls — long before modern engineering existed.

1.7 Medical Physics

Medical physics applies physics principles to diagnose and treat disease. It began in 1895 when Wilhelm Röntgen discovered X-rays.

📌 Major Medical Imaging Techniques

• X-ray — based on absorption of X-rays by body tissue
• CT scan — rotating X-ray source + detectors create cross-sectional images
• MRI — uses magnetic resonance of hydrogen protons (Bloch & Purcell, 1946)
• Ultrasound — high-frequency sound waves (>20 kHz) reflect off tissues
• Radiation therapy — controlled X-ray or gamma-ray doses destroy cancer cells

📝 Stethoscope

Invented in 1816 by French physician René Laennec, the stethoscope uses sound wave conduction to let doctors hear the heart, lungs, and intestines.

1.8 Physics in Defense and Communication

Modern defense and communication technologies are built on physics.

📌 Defense Applications

• RADAR (RAdio Detection And Ranging) — uses electromagnetic echoes to detect targets. Range: R = ct/2
• Missiles — guided by Newtonian mechanics and electronics
• Night vision — detects infrared (heat) radiation

📌 Communication Technologies

• Wired: telephone, fiber optics
• Wireless: radio, microwave, infrared, satellite
• All rely on electromagnetic theory and wave phenomena

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Identifying Physics in Chemistry
Problem: A chemist wants to determine the structure of an unknown molecule. Which physics technique would be most useful?
Analyze
The chemist needs to identify atomic/molecular structure without destroying the sample.
Physics concept
Spectroscopy — the study of interaction between matter and electromagnetic radiation.
Reason
Each molecule absorbs/emits unique wavelengths, revealing its structure.
✅ Use spectroscopy (e.g., IR, NMR, or mass spectrometry) to identify the molecule's structure.
Example 2Blood Flow and Physics
Problem: Explain, using physics concepts, how blood can flow upward from your feet to your heart when you stand.
Physics concept 1
Pressure gradient — blood flows from higher to lower pressure.
Physics concept 2
Pascal's principle — pressure transmitted through fluid assists circulation.
Physics concept 3
Valves + muscle contraction — one-way valves prevent backflow; muscles pump blood via compression.
✅ Blood rises due to pressure gradients created by the heart, assisted by one-way valves and skeletal muscle compression of veins.
Example 3Distance to a Star
Problem: Star A has the same true brightness as Star B, but Star A appears 9 times fainter. How many times farther is Star A?
Law
Inverse square law: Apparent brightness ∝ 1 / d²
Set up
Brightness_A / Brightness_B = (d_B / d_A)²
1/9 = (d_B / d_A)²
Solve
d_B / d_A = 1/3 → d_A = 3 × d_B
✅ Star A is 3 times farther than Star B.
Example 4RADAR Range Calculation
Problem: A RADAR signal takes 200 μs to travel to a target and back. How far is the target? (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)
Formula
R = c · t / 2 (divide by 2 for round trip)
Convert
t = 200 μs = 200 × 10⁻⁶ s = 2 × 10⁻⁴ s
Substitute
R = (3 × 10⁸ × 2 × 10⁻⁴) / 2 = 6 × 10⁴ / 2 = 3 × 10⁴ m
✅ Target is 30 km (3 × 10⁴ m) away.
Example 5Light Year Distance
Problem: How far does light travel in one year? (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)
Setup
1 year = 365 × 24 × 3600 s = 3.156 × 10⁷ s
Formula
d = c · t
Substitute
d = (3 × 10⁸)(3.156 × 10⁷) = 9.47 × 10¹⁵ m = 9.47 × 10¹² km
✅ One light year ≈ 9.46 × 10¹² km (about 9.46 trillion km).

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 1.1
✅ Key Points — Physics and Other Sciences
  • Physics is the most fundamental science — its laws apply universally, from atoms to galaxies.
  • Chemistry is applied molecular physics: atomic structure, bonding, reactions, and spectra all rely on physics.
  • Biology uses physics for motion, blood flow, sound production, nerve signals, and vision.
  • Astronomy uses Newtonian mechanics, electromagnetism, and atomic physics to study distant objects.
  • Geology applies stress, waves, electromagnetism, and radioactivity to study the Earth.
  • Engineering is the practical application of physics — civil, mechanical, electrical, and chemical.
  • Medical physics gave us X-rays, CT scans, MRI, ultrasound, and radiation therapy.
  • RADAR uses radio waves to detect targets; range R = c·t/2.
  • A light year = 9.46 × 10¹² km — the distance light travels in one year.
  • Physics is not isolated — it is the hub that connects every branch of science and engineering.
