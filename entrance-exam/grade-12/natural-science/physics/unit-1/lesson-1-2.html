⚗️ Physics and Chemistry

Chemistry is often called "applied molecular physics" — and for good reason. In this lesson you'll see how atomic and subatomic physics explains bonding, reactions, and the periodic table. You'll also learn how thermodynamics and spectroscopy are shared tools of both sciences.

Introduction
What you will learn in this lesson

Where does chemistry end and physics begin? The truth is: they overlap. Both study matter and its interaction with energy. When we zoom in to the level of atoms, electrons, and nuclei, we cross from chemistry into atomic and molecular physics.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain the overlap between physics and chemistry
  • Describe how atomic physics explains bonding
  • Explain how thermodynamics tells us if reactions are possible
  • Understand the role of spectroscopy in identifying molecules
  • Recognize shared tools and concepts between both sciences

2.1 Where Physics and Chemistry Overlap

Physics and chemistry overlap when the system under study involves matter composed of electrons and nuclei. The fundamental laws that govern the behavior of matter apply equally to both.

📌 Key Overlap Areas

• Both study matter and its interaction with energy
• Both use similar methods to study large numbers of interacting particles
• Chemistry's rules about atoms are ultimately explained by atomic and subatomic physics
• Shared tools: spectroscopy, thermodynamics, electromagnetism

2.2 Atomic Physics Explains Bonding

Why do atoms bond? Why do some bonds release energy while others absorb it? The answers come from the physics of atoms and subatomic particles.

📌 Physics Behind Chemical Bonds

• Covalent bonds — electrons shared between atoms; the physics of electron orbitals explains which bonds form
• Ionic bonds — electrons transferred; electrical attraction holds ions together
• Energy magnitudes — physics gives exact quantitative values for bond energies
• Molecular stability — physics tells us which molecules are energetically favorable

2.3 Thermodynamics and Chemical Reactions

Not every reaction a chemist imagines can actually happen. Physics — specifically thermodynamics — tells us which reactions are energetically possible.

📌 What Thermodynamics Tells Chemists

• Whether a reaction is energetically possible in the given direction
• The composition at equilibrium — how much product vs reactant
• Heat energy exchanges during reaction (exothermic / endothermic)
• Links macroscopic properties (temperature, pressure) to microscopic behavior (molecular motion)

📝 Bridging macro and micro

The physics of heat energy provides a bridge between the macroscopic properties of a substance (what we measure in a lab) and the microscopic properties of its constituent molecules and atoms (what we can't see directly).

2.4 Spectroscopy — The Shared Tool

Spectroscopy is the study of the interaction between matter and electromagnetic radiation as a function of wavelength or frequency. It's one of the most powerful tools in both physics and chemistry.

📌 What Spectroscopy Reveals

• Atomic structure — how electrons are arranged in atoms
• Molecular structure — how atoms are bonded together
• Composition — what elements are present
• Temperature — how hot an object is (via emitted light)

📝 Historical note

Most of what we know about the structure of atoms and molecules comes from spectroscopic study. Spectroscopic techniques were developed by collaborative work of physicists and chemists — a perfect example of interdisciplinary science.

2.5 Chemistry Concepts Rooted in Physics

Many fundamental chemistry concepts are explained by physics:

📝 Big idea

Chemistry is rooted in atomic and molecular physics. Every chemical rule has a physical foundation.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems solved step by step
Example 1Identifying the Physics Behind a Bond
Problem: Two hydrogen atoms combine to form H₂. What physics principle explains why this bond forms?
Identify
Two hydrogen atoms each have one electron. They share electrons in a covalent bond.
Physics
The electromagnetic force between electrons and nuclei, combined with quantum mechanics (electron orbitals), determines the bond.
Energy
The H₂ molecule is lower in energy than two separate H atoms — that's why the bond forms.
✅ Electromagnetism + quantum mechanics explains H₂ formation — a chemistry fact rooted in physics.
Example 2Can This Reaction Happen?
Problem: A chemist wants to know if a certain reaction will proceed spontaneously at room temperature. Which physics branch answers this?
Identify
The question is about spontaneity and energy.
Physics branch
Thermodynamics — using Gibbs free energy ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
Rule
If ΔG < 0, the reaction is spontaneous. If ΔG > 0, it won't happen spontaneously.
✅ Use thermodynamics — if ΔG < 0, the reaction proceeds spontaneously.
Example 3Atomic Structure from Spectroscopy
Problem: A hydrogen lamp emits light at specific wavelengths (e.g., 656 nm, 486 nm, 434 nm). What does this tell us about the atom?
Observation
Only certain wavelengths are emitted — not a continuous spectrum.
Physics
Electrons can only occupy discrete energy levels (quantum mechanics). When an electron drops from higher to lower level, a photon is emitted with energy E = hf = hc/λ.
Conclusion
The distinct wavelengths reveal the atom's quantized energy structure.
✅ The spectrum proves that electron energy levels in atoms are quantized — a fundamental physics fact.
Example 4Radioactivity — Physics or Chemistry?
Problem: A chemist studies a radioactive isotope of carbon (C-14). Is this more physics or chemistry?
Analyze
Radioactivity involves the nucleus — protons and neutrons.
Physics side
Nuclear physics explains decay, half-life, and emitted radiation.
Chemistry side
Chemistry uses C-14 for radiometric dating and studies its chemical behavior.
✅ Both — the physics explains the decay mechanism; the chemistry uses it as a tool. This is a perfect example of overlap.
Example 5X-ray Diffraction and Crystal Structure
Problem: How can we determine the arrangement of atoms in a crystal without seeing them directly?
Technique
Use X-ray diffraction — X-rays have wavelengths comparable to atomic spacing.
Physics
X-rays scattered by atoms interfere — constructive/destructive interference pattern emerges.
Result
The diffraction pattern reveals the crystal lattice structure with atomic precision.
✅ X-ray diffraction uses electromagnetic wave interference — a physics technique applied to chemistry.

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 1.2
✅ Key Points — Physics and Chemistry
  • Physics and chemistry overlap when studying matter composed of electrons and nuclei.
  • Both sciences study matter and its interaction with energy.
  • Atomic and subatomic physics explains why atoms bond, why reactions happen, and which molecules are stable.
  • Thermodynamics tells chemists whether a reaction is energetically possible and what composition it will reach at equilibrium.
  • Spectroscopy — the study of light-matter interaction — reveals the structure of atoms and molecules.
  • Chemistry concepts like atomic structure, molecular structure, valency, radioactivity, and crystal structure are all rooted in physics.
  • Chemistry is essentially applied molecular physics.
