📡 Physics in Communication

Every phone call, video stream, and satellite link is powered by physics. This lesson explores how electromagnetic waves, wave phenomena, and electronics make modern communication possible.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Communication is transferring information (a message) from one point to another. Every communication technology — telephone, mobile phone, computers — relies on physics. The exact physics depends on the message type and the transmission medium.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Differentiate wired and wireless communication
  • Describe how electromagnetic waves carry information
  • Explain the role of fiber optics
  • Understand satellite communication
  • Identify the wave phenomena used in communication
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

9.1 Two Types of Communication Systems

📌 Wired vs Wireless

Wired communication:
• Uses wires and optical fibers
• Examples: telephone (landline), cable internet, fiber optics

Wireless communication:
• Uses radio waves, microwaves, infrared waves
• Examples: satellite communication, ground wave communication, WiFi, cellular

9.2 Physics of Communication Technologies

Every communication technology relies on physics concepts.

📌 Physics Concepts Used

• Electromagnetic theory — radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light
• Electricity and magnetism — circuits, signals
• Electrical circuits — filters, amplifiers
• Energy — transmission efficiency
• Electronics — semiconductor devices
• Wave phenomena:
  – Reflection, refraction
  – Diffraction, interference
  – Rarefaction and compression

9.3 Fiber Optics — Light-Based Communication

Fiber optics uses total internal reflection of light to transmit data over long distances.

📌 How Fiber Optics Works

• Light travels through a thin glass or plastic fiber
• Light bounces at shallow angles, staying inside the fiber — total internal reflection
• No signal loss over long distances (unlike copper wires)
• Very high bandwidth — can carry huge data volumes
• Immune to electrical interference

9.4 Satellite Communication

Satellites in geostationary orbit (about 36,000 km altitude) relay signals between distant points on Earth.

📌 How Satellite Communication Works

1. Ground station sends signal up to satellite (uplink)
2. Satellite amplifies and re-transmits the signal
3. Signal comes back down to another ground station (downlink)

Uses microwaves (1–30 GHz). The satellite "sees" a large fraction of Earth — enabling global communication.

9.5 Major Communication Technologies

📌 Technologies and Their Physics

• Radio — modulates carrier waves with audio signal
• Television — modulates both audio and video signals
• Internet — digital packets over wired/wireless channels
• Mobile phones — cellular network of microwave transceivers
• Satellite — microwave relay through space

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems
Example 1Wavelength of a Radio Wave
Problem: A radio station broadcasts at 100 MHz. Find the wavelength. (c = 3×10⁸ m/s)
Formula
λ = c / f
Convert
f = 100 MHz = 1×10⁸ Hz
Substitute
λ = 3×10⁸ / 1×10⁸ = 3 m
✅ Wavelength = 3 m (typical FM radio)
Example 2Satellite Signal Delay
Problem: A geostationary satellite is 36,000 km up. How long does a signal take to travel up and back down?
Distance
d = 2 × 36,000 km = 72,000 km = 7.2 × 10⁷ m
Formula
t = d / c
Substitute
t = 7.2×10⁷ / 3×10⁸ = 0.24 s
✅ Total delay ≈ 0.24 s (240 ms)
Example 3Why Fiber Optics Is Superior
Problem: Why is fiber optics better than copper wire for long-distance communication?
Reason 1
Lower signal loss — light travels far without weakening.
Reason 2
Higher bandwidth — more data per second.
Reason 3
Immune to interference — light isn't affected by electrical noise.
✅ Fiber optics: low loss, high bandwidth, no interference — ideal for long distance.
Example 4Cellular Frequency
Problem: A cellular phone operates at 1.8 GHz. What wavelength?
Formula
λ = c / f
Substitute
λ = 3×10⁸ / 1.8×10⁹ = 0.167 m ≈ 16.7 cm
✅ Wavelength ≈ 16.7 cm (a microwave)
Example 5Two-Way Radio vs Cell Phone
Problem: Why do cell phones need many towers while radio broadcasts use one transmitter?
Radio
Broadcasts one-way, high power, covers large area.
Cell phone
Two-way, each cell tower covers a small area, but towers are networked together.
Result
Many low-power towers enable frequency reuse and continuous coverage.
✅ Cellular systems use many small cells networked together for two-way coverage.
