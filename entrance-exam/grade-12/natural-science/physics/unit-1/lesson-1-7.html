🏥 Medical Physics

Modern medicine runs on physics. From the X-ray to the MRI, from ultrasound to radiation therapy, this lesson explores how physics principles have transformed diagnosis and treatment.

📖 Reading: ~22 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources

Introduction
What you will learn

Medical physics applies physics principles to diagnose and treat diseases. It began in 1895 when Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen discovered X-rays — opening a new interdisciplinary field that has saved millions of lives.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Explain how X-rays and CT scans work
  • Describe the physics of MRI
  • Understand ultrasound imaging
  • Differentiate conventional X-ray vs CT scan
  • Explain radiation therapy for cancer treatment
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

7.1 X-rays and CT Scans

X-rays are high-energy electromagnetic radiation that pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by dense materials like bone and metal.

📌 How X-ray Imaging Works

• X-rays are absorbed differently by different tissues
• Higher atomic number → more absorption → whiter on image
• Bone (calcium, Z=20) blocks X-rays → appears white
• Lungs (nitrogen, Z=7) lets X-rays through → appears dark
• A shadowgram is created on film or detector

📝 CT scan vs X-ray

Conventional X-ray: single stationary picture (shadowgram).
CT (Computed Tomography) scan: rotating X-ray source and detectors create cross-sectional images (tomograms) — much more detailed.

7.2 MRI — Magnetic Resonance Imaging

MRI uses the magnetic properties of hydrogen protons in the body's water molecules to create detailed images of soft tissue.

📌 How MRI Works

1. Body's hydrogen protons align in a strong magnetic field
2. A radio-frequency pulse disturbs the alignment
3. When the pulse stops, protons relax and release energy
4. Different tissues release different amounts → computer builds image

Proposed by Felix Bloch and Edward Purcell (1946). First MRI images by Paul Lauterbur and Peter Mansfield.

📝 Advantages of MRI

• No ionizing radiation (safer than X-ray/CT)
• Excellent for soft tissue imaging (brain, spinal cord)
• Resolution as fine as 0.5 mm

7.3 Ultrasound Imaging

Ultrasound uses high-frequency sound waves (>20 kHz) to image internal organs.

📌 Physics of Ultrasound

• Typical frequencies used: 3.5–10 MHz
• Waves penetrate tissue and reflect off boundaries
• Detector measures reflected waves and converts to image

Terms:
• Anechoic — black (no echoes, fluid-filled)
• Hypoechoic — dark gray (few echoes)
• Hyperechoic — light gray (many echoes)

7.4 Radiation Therapy

High-energy radiation (X-rays, gamma rays, or particles) can destroy cancer cells. The physics: radiation rips electrons off biological molecules, damaging their structure.

📌 Radiotherapy Methods

• Implanted sources — radium or cobalt-60 placed near tumor
• External beam — gamma or X-ray beam aimed at tumor
• Beam rotated to minimize damage to healthy tissue
• The tumor is always in the path; healthy tissue gets smaller dose

7.5 Stethoscope — The Oldest Medical Physics Tool

Invented in 1816 by René Laennec, the stethoscope is a simple sound conduction device.

📌 How the Stethoscope Works

• Bell-shaped cavity placed on skin picks up body sounds
• Hollow flexible tube conducts sound to examiner's ears
• Can detect sounds from tens to thousands of Hz
• Used to listen to heart, lungs, intestines, blood flow

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems
Example 1Identify the Imaging Technique
Problem: A patient needs detailed images of the brain's soft tissue. Which technique is best — X-ray, CT, or MRI?
Compare
X-ray: poor for soft tissue. CT: better but uses ionizing radiation.
Reason
MRI gives best soft tissue contrast with no ionizing radiation.
✅ MRI — best for soft tissue detail with no radiation risk.
Example 2X-ray Absorption
Problem: Why does bone appear white on an X-ray while lungs appear dark?
Bone
Contains calcium — high atomic number (Z=20) → absorbs X-rays.
Lungs
Contain nitrogen/oxygen — low atomic number (Z=7) → X-rays pass through.
Result
Bone blocks X-rays → appears white. Lungs transmit X-rays → appears dark.
✅ Atomic number determines X-ray absorption.
Example 3Ultrasound Frequency
Problem: Is 5 MHz sound considered ultrasound? Why?
Threshold
Ultrasound = frequencies above 20 kHz = 0.02 MHz.
Compare
5 MHz = 5,000,000 Hz, which is 250× higher than the ultrasound threshold.
✅ Yes — 5 MHz is well within the ultrasound range (3.5–10 MHz is typical in medical imaging).
Example 4Radiation Therapy — Rotating Beam
Problem: Why is the radiation beam rotated around the patient during therapy?
Tumor
Tumor stays in the beam path — receives full dose.
Healthy tissue
Different tissue sections are in the beam at different times — each gets only a fraction of the dose.
✅ Rotation concentrates the dose at the tumor while sparing healthy tissue.
Example 5Why MRI Needs Hydrogen
Problem: Why is MRI based on hydrogen nuclei rather than, say, carbon?
Abundance
The body is mostly water (H₂O) → hydrogen is the most abundant nucleus.
Property
Hydrogen nucleus = single proton with strong magnetic moment.
Signal
More nuclei → stronger signal → better image.
✅ Hydrogen's abundance and magnetic property make it perfect for MRI.
