🪨 Relation of Physics with Geology

Geology is the study of the solid Earth — and physics is the toolkit. Learn how stress, waves, magnetism, and radioactivity let geologists explore deep into the planet without ever digging.

📖 Reading: ~18 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources

Introduction
What you will learn

Geology is a branch of Earth science that studies the solid and liquid matter that makes up Earth, along with the processes acting on it. To do this, geologists rely heavily on physics.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Describe how geologists use force, stress, and strain
  • Explain how seismic waves reveal Earth's interior
  • Identify physics-based methods for finding minerals and oil
  • Understand the role of radioactivity in dating rocks
  • Recognize electrical and magnetic methods in geophysics
📖
Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

5.1 Physics Concepts Used in Geology

Geology uses physics to study the properties of rocks and minerals.

📌 Physics Concepts in Geology

• Force, stress, strain — for rock deformation and earthquakes
• Optics & spectroscopy — for identifying minerals
• Atomic structure — for radioactive dating
• Electromagnetic radiation — for surface and subsurface surveys
• Heat and heat flow — for studying Earth's interior
• Electricity & magnetism — for magnetic surveys
• Waves (sound and seismic) — for seismic exploration
• Fluid flow — for groundwater and oil movement

5.2 Studying Rocks and Minerals

Geologists study several physical properties of rocks:

📌 Properties Studied

• Electrical properties — conductivity, resistivity
• Density — mass per unit volume
• Magnetization — magnetic susceptibility
• Radioactivity — for dating and mineral ID
• Elasticity — how rocks deform under stress
• Waves — propagation of seismic waves

📝 Two ways to study rocks

1. Excavation — take samples from different layers
2. Remote sensing — send waves from the surface (no digging!)

5.3 Geophysical Methods

Geologists use different physics-based methods to explore the Earth's subsurface.

📌 Common Geophysical Methods

• Seismic reflection/refraction — send sound waves into the ground, measure echoes
• Magnetic survey — detect anomalies in Earth's magnetic field
• Gravity survey — measure tiny variations in gravitational pull
• Electrical resistivity — measure resistance to electrical flow
• Radiometric dating — use radioactive decay to determine ages

📝 Why these methods matter

These techniques let geologists find ore minerals, fossil fuels, hydrocarbons, geothermal reservoirs, and groundwater — without excavating huge areas.

5.4 Branches of Geology

Geology has many specialized branches. For example:

📌 Mineralogists

Mineralogists study the composition and structure of minerals such as halite (rock salt), quartz, calcite, and magnetite. They use X-ray diffraction, spectroscopy, and other physics-based techniques.

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems
Example 1Stress and Strain
Problem: A 500 N force is applied to a rock sample of cross-section 0.05 m². Find the stress.
Formula
σ = F / A
Substitute
σ = 500 N / 0.05 m² = 10,000 Pa = 10 kPa
✅ Stress = 10 kPa
Example 2Young's Modulus
Problem: A rock has Young's modulus E = 5 × 10¹⁰ Pa and density ρ = 2,500 kg/m³. Estimate the speed of seismic P-waves.
Formula
v = √(E/ρ)
Substitute
v = √(5×10¹⁰ / 2,500) = √(2×10⁷) ≈ 4472 m/s
✅ Wave speed ≈ 4.5 km/s
Example 3Radiometric Dating
Problem: A rock sample contains 25% of its original Carbon-14 (half-life = 5730 years). How old is it?
Number of half-lives
25% = (1/2)² → 2 half-lives have passed.
Multiply
Age = 2 × 5730 = 11,460 years
✅ The rock is about 11,460 years old
Example 4Density of a Mineral
Problem: A mineral sample of mass 540 g occupies 200 cm³. What is its density? What mineral might it be?
Formula
ρ = m / V
Substitute
ρ = 540 g / 200 cm³ = 2.7 g/cm³ = 2700 kg/m³
Identify
Density 2.7 g/cm³ matches aluminium or the mineral quartz.
✅ Density = 2.7 g/cm³ — likely quartz.
Example 5Seismic Wave Travel Time
Problem: A seismic wave travels at 6 km/s. How long does it take to reach a detector 120 km away?
Formula
t = d / v
Substitute
t = 120 / 6 = 20 s
✅ Takes 20 seconds

Lesson Summary
Everything you learned in Lesson 1.5
✅ Key Points — Physics and Geology
  • Geology studies the solid and liquid matter of Earth — physics is the tool.
  • Force, stress, strain, and elasticity describe how rocks deform.
  • Seismic waves (sound waves in rock) reveal the Earth's interior.
  • Radioactive decay is used to date rocks (radiometric dating).
  • X-ray diffraction and spectroscopy identify minerals.
  • Geologists use magnetic, gravity, and electrical surveys to find oil, minerals, and groundwater.
  • Seismic reflection works because waves reflect differently at boundaries between rock layers.
  • Branches like mineralogy study specific minerals using physics-based techniques.
