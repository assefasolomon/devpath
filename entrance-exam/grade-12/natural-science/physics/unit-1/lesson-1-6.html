Unit 1 · Application of Physics · Natural Science

🏗️ Physics and Engineering

Every bridge, engine, circuit, and chemical plant is designed using physics. This lesson explores how the four major branches of engineering apply physics principles — and how technology, in turn, drives new physics discoveries.

📖 Reading: ~20 min
✏️ Examples: 5 worked
🎯 Quiz: 5 questions
🎥 Videos: 4 resources
🎯
Introduction
What you will learn

Engineering is the practical application of physics. It's what turns equations into bridges, engines, circuits, and products. Without physics, no engineering is possible. Without engineering, physics stays on paper.

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Relate Newtonian mechanics to civil engineering
  • Identify physics concepts used in mechanical engineering
  • Explain how electromagnetism underpins electrical engineering
  • Describe the role of molecular physics and thermodynamics in chemical engineering
  • Explain how technology generates new physics

Explanation
Complete theory from the textbook

6.1 Civil Engineering

Civil engineering concerns the design and construction of skyscrapers, roads, bridges, dams, and railways. It uses physics knowledge of forces, fluid pressure, gravity, and elasticity.

📌 Physics in Civil Engineering

• Forces, tension, compression — for structural design
• Harmonic vibrations and oscillations — for earthquake resistance
• Tensile strength, elasticity — for material selection
• Fluid pressure — for dams and pipelines

📝 Ethiopian engineering heritage

Ancient Ethiopians built remarkable structures using practical physics: the Axumite obelisks, the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela, and the Harar Jugol walls. Modern Ethiopia continues this tradition with projects like the Hedase Bridge, Africa Union HQ, and the Gotera Interchange.

6.2 Mechanical Engineering

Mechanical engineering uses physics to create engines, manufacturing equipment, and vehicles.

📌 Physics in Mechanical Engineering

• Mechanics — motion of parts
• Dynamics — forces during operation
• Thermodynamics — heat engines, refrigeration
• Stresses and strain — materials under load
• Aerodynamics — aircraft, cars, turbines

Applications: aircraft, watercraft, engines, robotics, weapons, cars, hydraulics, pneumatics.

6.3 Electrical and Electronic Engineering

Electrical engineering designs circuits, motors, appliances, fiber networks, computers, and communication systems.

📌 Physics in Electrical Engineering

• Electromagnetism — motors, generators, transformers
• Mechanics — moving parts in devices
• Thermodynamics — heat management in power systems
• Semiconductors — transistors, diodes, ICs
• Circuit theory — designing electronics

6.4 Chemical Engineering

Chemical engineering produces materials through chemical processes — designing equipment, systems, and processes for refining and mixing chemicals.

📌 Physics in Chemical Engineering

• Physical properties of molecules
• Chemical bonds between atoms — from molecular physics
• Molecular dynamics — how molecules move
• Energy changes from thermodynamics

Products: plastics, petroleum products, detergents, paints.

6.5 Technology Generating New Physics

Physics and technology are deeply interwoven. Physics leads to technology; technology enables new physics.

📌 Technology → New Physics

• Rocket technology → space measurements
• X-ray technology → study of atomic structure
• Lasers → spectroscopy, quantum optics
• Particle accelerators → particle physics discoveries

The cycle: Physics → Engineering → Technology → New Physics

Worked Examples
5 exam-style problems
Example 1Bridge Beam Stress
Problem: A bridge beam of cross-section 0.02 m² supports a 60,000 N load. Calculate the stress.
Formula
σ = F / A
Substitute
σ = 60,000 / 0.02 = 3 × 10⁶ Pa = 3 MPa
✅ Stress = 3 MPa
Example 2Dam Fluid Pressure
Problem: A dam holds back water 30 m deep. What is the gauge pressure at the bottom? (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²)
Formula
P = ρgh
Substitute
P = 1000 × 9.8 × 30 = 2.94 × 10⁵ Pa = 294 kPa
✅ Gauge pressure = 294 kPa
Example 3Engine Efficiency
Problem: A car engine takes in 200 kJ of chemical energy and does 60 kJ of useful work. Find its efficiency.
Formula
η = W_out / Q_in
Substitute
η = 60 / 200 = 0.30 = 30%
✅ Efficiency = 30%
Example 4Power Transmission
Problem: A transmission line carries 5 A at 240 V. How much power is transmitted?
Formula
P = VI
Substitute
P = 240 × 5 = 1200 W = 1.2 kW
✅ Power = 1.2 kW
Example 5Technology Driving Physics
Problem: Explain how the invention of the X-ray tube advanced physics research.
Direct use
X-rays used in medicine for imaging.
Indirect use
X-ray diffraction reveals atomic structure of crystals.
Further
X-ray spectroscopy helped develop quantum mechanics (Moseley's law, Bohr model verification).
✅ Technology (X-ray tubes) → new physics (quantum theory, atomic structure)
